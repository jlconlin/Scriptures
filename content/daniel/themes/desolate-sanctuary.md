Daniel tells of the house of God lost in several ways: vessels carried off and profaned, an offering taken away, the sanctuary and city destroyed. The passages describe events at different times and do not use one Hebrew word for all of them. In chapter 9 Daniel prays for “thy sanctuary that is desolate,” and the answer he is given speaks of iniquity reconciled and of the Messiah.

| Step | Passage | The words |
|---|---|---|
| Carried off | [[Dan. 1:2]] | “part of the vessels of the house of God” |
| Profaned | [[Dan. 5:3]] | “the golden vessels… drank in them” |
| Cast down | [[Dan. 8:11]], [[Dan. 8:14]] | “the place of his sanctuary was cast down”; “then shall the sanctuary be cleansed” |
| Prayed for | [[Dan. 9:17]] | “cause thy face to shine upon thy sanctuary that is desolate” |
| Answered | [[Dan. 9:24–26]] | “to make reconciliation for iniquity”; “shall Messiah be cut off, but not for himself” |
| Destroyed | [[Dan. 9:26]] | “shall destroy the city and the sanctuary” |
| Polluted | [[Dan. 11:31]] | “the abomination that maketh desolate” |

<h2 id="vessels">The vessels</h2>

The Lord gives Jehoiakim into Nebuchadnezzar's hand “with part of the vessels of the house of God,” which go into “the treasure house of his god” ([[Dan. 1:2]]). Belshazzar later has them brought, and “the king, and his princes, his wives, and his concubines, drank in them” ([[Dan. 5:3]]). Daniel says, “But hast lifted up thyself against the Lord of heaven; and they have brought the vessels of his house before thee” ([[Dan. 5:23]]). See the notes on [[Dan. 1:2]] and [[Dan. 5:3]].

<h2 id="cast-down">The sanctuary cast down</h2>

In the vision of chapter 8 a horn takes away “the daily sacrifice,” and “the place of his sanctuary was cast down” ([[Dan. 8:11]]). Asked how long, he is told, “Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed” ([[Dan. 8:13–14]]). No reckoning is made here; see the note on [[Dan. 8:14]]. The horn “has generally been interpreted to represent Antiochus IV”[@ot-manual-28] (see also the note on [[Dan. 8:9]]). Antiochus polluted the temple, which “was left desolate for three years” until Judas Maccabaeus “restored it to use”;[@bd-temple-of-zerubbabel] the Bible Dictionary does not connect this to the “cleansed” of 8:14.

**Connection made by this page:** the loss of the vessels in chapters 1 and 5 is set beside the losses of the sanctuary in chapters 8–11, as what befalls the house of God. The book does not join them: the vessels are not mentioned in the sanctuary passages, and the words differ. “Sanctuary” is *miqdash* at 8:11, 9:17, and 11:31, and *qodesh* at 8:13–14 and 9:26.[@oshb-wlc]

<h2 id="prayer">Daniel's prayer</h2>

Daniel understands from Jeremiah “seventy years in the desolations of Jerusalem” ([[Dan. 9:2]]). Chronicles tells the same seventy years: “they burnt the house of God,” and the land, “as long as she lay desolate… kept sabbath, to fulfil threescore and ten years” ([[2 Chr. 36:19]], [[2 Chr. 36:21]]).[@lds-scriptures] Daniel prays, “cause thy face to shine upon thy sanctuary that is desolate, for the Lord's sake” ([[Dan. 9:17]]). The desolation in Daniel's prayer is Babylon's, not the horn's.

<h2 id="answer">The answer</h2>

**Connection made by this page:** set beside Daniel's request, Gabriel's answer speaks of the street and wall “built again” ([[Dan. 9:25]]); the King James text has the word “sanctuary” in it once, where it is destroyed ([[Dan. 9:26]]). “To anoint the most Holy” may be a place, a thing, or a person; see the note on [[Dan. 9:24]]. The answer includes “to make reconciliation for iniquity” ([[Dan. 9:24]]), “the Messiah the Prince” ([[Dan. 9:25]]), and “shall Messiah be cut off, but not for himself” ([[Dan. 9:26]]).

Gabriel reveals “the time of the coming of the Messiah, who will make reconciliation for iniquity.”[@lds-scriptures] Verse 24 is “undoubtedly” a reference to Christ and His Atonement, “cut off” in 9:26 “seems to be an allusion to His Crucifixion.”[@ot-manual-28] See the notes on [[Dan. 9:24]], [[Dan. 9:26]], and [[Dan. 9:27]].

<h2 id="spoken-of-by-daniel">“Spoken of by Daniel the prophet”</h2>

The last vision returns to the sanctuary: forces “shall pollute the sanctuary of strength, and shall take away the daily sacrifice, and they shall place the abomination that maketh desolate” ([[Dan. 11:31]]). The Savior named it: “the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet” ([[Matt. 24:15]]). Joseph Smith—Matthew applies it “concerning the destruction of Jerusalem” ([[JS—M 1:12]]).[@lds-scriptures]

These passages describe separate desolations:

- Babylon's ([[Dan. 9:2]], [[Dan. 9:17]]).
- Antiochus's, read at [[Dan. 8:11]] and [[Dan. 11:31]]; 11:31 is given as an example of things that “seem to be dualistic, having application to more than one period of time.”[@ot-manual-28]
- Rome's, in A.D. 70, when the legions “laid siege to Jerusalem.”[@bd-abomination-of-desolation] The rest of chapter 9 “describes the destruction of Jerusalem and the temple” and parallels [[Matt. 24:15]] and [[JS—M 1:12]].[@ot-manual-28]
- One still to come: “again shall the abomination of desolation… be fulfilled” ([[JS—M 1:32]]).[@lds-scriptures]

Set beside the prayer for the sanctuary, Gabriel's answer speaks of the Messiah, who “will make reconciliation for iniquity.”[@lds-scriptures]
