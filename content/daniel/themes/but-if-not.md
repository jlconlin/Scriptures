This page sets four passages side by side: the rescue from the furnace and from the den (chapters 3 and 6), the fall of those who understand (“they shall fall by the sword, and by flame,” [[Dan. 11:33]]), and the promise that “thy people shall be delivered” and that many of the sleepers “shall awake” ([[Dan. 12:1–2]]). The comparison is the page's own. The book “illustrates the blessings of the Lord upon the faithful,” and “a testimony of mankind's resurrection is given in Dan. 12.”[@bd-daniel-book]

## “He will deliver us… But if not”

The king asks, “who is that God that shall deliver you out of my hands?” ([[Dan. 3:15]]). The three answer: “our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king. But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods” ([[Dan. 3:17–18]]). They say God is able and will; even if He chose not to save them, they would not bow.[@scripture-helps-dan] “But if not” refuses the worship; the three do not say what will become of them if He does not. Elder Dennis E. Simmons calls it “a statement of true faith.”[@simmons-but-if-not] See the note on [[Dan. 3:18]].

## The furnace and the den

Both end in rescue, in nearly the same words. Nebuchadnezzar: God “hath sent his angel, and delivered his servants” ([[Dan. 3:28]]). Daniel: “My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me” ([[Dan. 6:22]]). The Aramaic verb *shezib*, “deliver,” is in the king's question ([[Dan. 3:15]]), the three's answer ([[Dan. 3:17]]), Nebuchadnezzar's blessing ([[Dan. 3:28]]), Darius's words to Daniel ([[Dan. 6:16]], [[Dan. 6:20]]) and his decree ([[Dan. 6:27]]): nine times in seven verses.[@oshb-wlc] A second Aramaic verb, also “deliver” in the King James, stands in [[Dan. 3:29]] and beside the first in [[Dan. 6:14]] and [[Dan. 6:27]].[@oshb-wlc]

## Those who fall

In the last vision some of the faithful are not brought out. “They that understand among the people shall instruct many: yet they shall fall by the sword, and by flame, by captivity, and by spoil, many days.” Some fall “to try them, and to purge, and to make them white, even to the time of the end” ([[Dan. 11:35]]). They are “holpen with a little help” ([[Dan. 11:34]]), but no angel is sent and no rescue is told. See the note on [[Dan. 11:33]].

**Connection made by this page:** set the “flame” of 11:33 beside “the flame of the fire” in the furnace of [[Dan. 3:22]]. (Chapter 3 is Aramaic, 11:33 Hebrew.)[@oshb-wlc]

## Delivered, and the dead awake

“At that time thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book. And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life” ([[Dan. 12:1–2]]). Daniel himself is told he will “rest, and stand in thy lot at the end of the days” ([[Dan. 12:13]]). Two limits: “delivered” here is the Hebrew *malat*, not the Aramaic *shezib*, so the link is the English word;[@oshb-wlc] and the promise is to those “found written in the book.”

**Connection made by this page:** set the deliverance of 12:1–2 beside the “but if not” of 3:18 and the fall of 11:33–35. The sequence begins with rescue from death and ends with a promise of deliverance and, in the next verse, of the dead awaking.[@lds-scriptures]

## Through Jesus Christ

Hebrews joins the two outcomes in one passage. Faith “stopped the mouths of lions, Quenched the violence of fire” ([[Heb. 11:33–34]]), which the Church's footnotes tie to [[Dan. 6:22]] and [[Dan. 3:27]]; then “others were tortured, not accepting deliverance; that they might obtain a better resurrection” ([[Heb. 11:35]]), which the Joseph Smith Translation gives as “the first resurrection.”[@lds-scriptures] Elder Simmons sets the same two halves of Hebrews 11 side by side: besides the glorious outcomes “there were always the but if nots”; and he closes with faith in Jesus Christ, that “if we do all we can do, we will, in His time and in His way, be delivered.”[@simmons-but-if-not] Elder Lance B. Wickman brings “but if not” to grieving parents: faith is “a confidence in the Lord.”[@wickman-but-if-not]

The Church's footnotes at [[Dan. 12:2]] join it to [[John 5:28–29]] and to [[Morm. 9:13]]: “the death of Christ bringeth to pass the resurrection” ([[Morm. 9:13]]).[@lds-scriptures]
