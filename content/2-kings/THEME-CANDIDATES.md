# 2 Kings: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.** The candidates were Fable's and Codex's lists (`.cache/batch/2-kings/reports/themes-fable.md` and `themes-codex.md`), shown to the author combined on 2026-11-06; only the author's choice is recorded here.

## Author's selection (2026-11-06)

**Two pages** (the author: “I want 1 and 2.”).

### “Out of His Sight,” and brought back

**The arc.** One Hebrew word, *pānîm* (face, presence), rendered several ways in the King James. The prophet stands before the LORD (3:14; 5:16; 1 Kgs. 17:1); Israel is spared, not cast “from his presence as yet” (13:23); then “removed … out of his sight” (17:18, 20, 23); Hezekiah spreads the letter “before the LORD” and walked “before thee” (19:14; 20:3); Josiah “wept before me” (22:19); Judah removed in the same words (23:27; 24:20); and the book ends with a son of David brought out of prison to eat bread “continually before” the king of Babylon (25:29), with *tāmîd* the word of Mephibosheth at David's table (2 Sam. 9:7, 13). To Christ: Alma 42:9; 2 Ne. 9:6; Hel. 14:15–17 (brought back into the presence of the Lord); 3 Ne. 17:4; D&C 110:11; Heb. 9:24. Combines Fable's “Before Whom I Stand” and Codex's “Not Lost unto the Father.”

**Must not claim:** where the lost tribes are now; that Jehoiachin's release fulfils a prophecy (reading 25:27–30 as a figure of the return is the page's own, and says so).

### “Innocent Blood, Which the LORD Would Not Pardon”

**The arc.** Blood seen and requited in Ahab's house (9:7, 26; Naboth); the avenger's own bloodshed visited on his house (Hos. 1:4); then Manasseh's “innocent blood” (21:16), which the LORD “would not pardon” (24:4), outweighing even Josiah (23:25–26). To Christ: “I have betrayed the innocent blood” (Matt. 27:4); Heb. 12:24; Alma 34:10–14; Mosiah 4:2; D&C 20:79. Fable's proposal.

**Must not claim:** that the link from 24:4 to Matt. 27:4 is scripture's (it is the page's own); anything on the pardon of a murderer (D&C 42:18); the page is about the nation's sentence and the Savior's blood. It must also account for the occurrences of *dām* that don't fit (3:22–23; 16:13, 15).

**Not chosen:** “A Light for David,” “Take Up Thy Son,” “Redeemed Without Money,” and the rest of both lists.
