Before the world was, the Father’s plan was known, and the question was whom He would send. The Beloved Son answered, “Father, thy will be done, and the glory be thine forever”; Satan demanded the Father’s honor and rebelled. The book then follows both: Satan claims the Son’s title before Moses, lies to the first family, and holds a chain over the earth, while the Chosen is shown to Enoch suffering for sins and as King of Zion. The table puts the council first and then follows the book, which tells the council in chapter 4; that chapter’s first verse looks back to chapter 1: “Satan, whom thou hast commanded in the name of mine Only Begotten.”[@schade-bowen-council]

<h2 id="council">Whom shall I send</h2>

In Abraham’s vision the Lord asks, “Whom shall I send?” and two answer, “Here am I, send me”; the Lord says, “I will send the first” ([[Abr. 3:27]]). Moses 4 gives the words. Satan said: “I will be thy son, and I will redeem all mankind, that one soul shall not be lost, and surely I will do it; wherefore give me thine honor” ([[Moses 4:1]]). Of His Son the Father says: “my Beloved Son, which was my Beloved and Chosen from the beginning, said unto me—Father, thy will be done, and the glory be thine forever” ([[Moses 4:2]]).

The Father presented His plan of salvation,[@scripture-helps-gen-3-4] and “only one plan was presented in the premortal council—the Father’s plan.”[@bednar-divine-nature-2026] The question was who should be sent to carry it out; Lucifer presented no plan that was voted down. He rebelled.[@bednar-divine-nature-2026] The book’s one use of “plan” is “the plan of salvation unto all men, through the blood of mine Only Begotten” ([[Moses 6:62]]).[@lds-scriptures]

<h2 id="rebelled">Rebelled</h2>

The Lord gives the reason Satan was cast down: “Satan rebelled against me, and sought to destroy the agency of man, which I, the Lord God, had given him, and also, that I should give unto him mine own power” ([[Moses 4:3]]). So “he became Satan, yea, even the devil, the father of all lies, to deceive and to blind men, and to lead them captive at his will” ([[Moses 4:4]]).

These verses say what Satan sought and do not clearly say how he proposed to do it.[@mathews-satans-rebellion] One Church manual describes his proposal as “based on compulsion”;[@pgp-manual-moses-4] the note on [[Moses 4:3]] gives a second possibility, and says that what he proposed was a lie that would not have worked.[@mathews-satans-rebellion]

<h2 id="only-begotten">Satan claims the Son’s title</h2>

Satan comes to Moses: “Moses, son of man, worship me” ([[Moses 1:12]]). Satan cries, “I am the Only Begotten, worship me” ([[Moses 1:19]]), a claim that shows his aim “to supplant and usurp the role of Jesus Christ.”[@pgp-manual-moses-1-12-23] Moses answers: “In the name of the Only Begotten, depart hence, Satan” ([[Moses 1:21]]). See [Moses 1](/moses/1/).

<h2 id="first-family">The first family</h2>

Satan “sought also to beguile Eve, for he knew not the mind of God” ([[Moses 4:6]]). To Adam and Eve’s children he says, “I am also a son of God… Believe it not; and they believed it not” ([[Moses 5:13]]). Satan tells Cain, “Swear unto me by thy throat” ([[Moses 5:29]]), and “all these things were done in secret” ([[Moses 5:30]]). See [Moses 5](/moses/5/).

<h2 id="chain">A chain over the earth</h2>

In vision Enoch “beheld Satan; and he had a great chain in his hand, and it veiled the whole face of the earth with darkness; and he looked up and laughed” ([[Moses 7:26]]). Of the people Enoch is shown the Lord says, “Satan shall be their father, and misery shall be their doom” ([[Moses 7:37]]). The chain and the fatherhood are both tied to “lead them captive at his will” ([[Moses 4:4]]).[@pgp-manual-moses-7-1-41]

<h2 id="chosen">The Chosen</h2>

“That which I have chosen hath plead before my face. Wherefore, he suffereth for their sins” ([[Moses 7:39]]). The word “Chosen” takes the reader back to [[Moses 4:2]], and “that” is the Savior.[@lds-scriptures] Enoch says, “the Lamb is slain from the foundation of the world” ([[Moses 7:47]]), and the Lord says, “I am Messiah, the King of Zion, the Rock of Heaven” ([[Moses 7:53]]). See [Moses 7](/moses/7/).

<h2 id="son-father">Son and father</h2>

Set side by side, the words “son” and “father” follow Satan through these chapters; the comparison is this page’s. He says, “I will be thy son” ([[Moses 4:1]]) and “I am also a son of God” ([[Moses 5:13]]). The Lord names him “the father of all lies” ([[Moses 4:4]]) and says of those who follow him, “Satan shall be their father” ([[Moses 7:37]]).[@pgp-manual-moses-7-1-41] Cain is to be “the father of his lies” ([[Moses 5:24]]). (Satan’s other uses are of men: “son of man,” [[Moses 1:12]]; “thy father,” [[Moses 5:29]].) The Beloved Son’s reply begins, “Father.”

<h2 id="thy-will">“Thy will be done”</h2>

The Son’s words in the council are His words in Gethsemane: “not as I will, but as thou wilt… thy will be done” ([[Matt. 26:39]], [[Matt. 26:42]]). The risen Christ says He has “suffered the will of the Father in all things from the beginning” ([[3 Ne. 11:11]]). The three passages are set together to teach that He submitted to His Father’s will “in the premortal world and in mortality,” and bore witness of it after the Resurrection.[@nt-teacher-manual-matt-26] See also [The Plan of Salvation, Taught from the First Parents](/moses/themes/plan-from-the-beginning/).
