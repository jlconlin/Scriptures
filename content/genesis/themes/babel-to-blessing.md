Genesis 10 lists the families of the earth as they divide. At Babel the people build to make themselves a name and to avoid being scattered, and they are scattered. The Lord’s next act in the record is to call one man out of one of those families and promise to make his name great and to bless “all families of the earth.” Paul calls that promise the gospel preached beforehand. This page follows the world-sized problem and its answer; the covenant’s renewals are in [The Covenant with Abraham](/genesis/themes/covenant-with-abraham/).

## The families divided

Japheth’s sons are “divided in their lands; every one after his tongue, after their families, in their nations” ([[Gen. 10:5]]), and the chapter ends: “by these were the nations divided in the earth after the flood” ([[Gen. 10:32]]). “Families” is *mishpachah*, which occurs in twelve verses of Genesis, five of them in this chapter ([[Gen. 10:5]], [[Gen. 10:18]], [[Gen. 10:20]], [[Gen. 10:31]], [[Gen. 10:32]]).[@oshb-wlc] See [Genesis 10](/genesis/10/).

## A name, and a scattering

The builders say, “let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth” ([[Gen. 11:4]]). Then “the LORD scattered them abroad from thence upon the face of all the earth” ([[Gen. 11:8]]). The verb for “scatter” (*puts*) occurs five times in Genesis: [[Gen. 10:18]], three times in this story, and [[Gen. 49:7]].[@oshb-wlc] See [Genesis 11](/genesis/11/).

## From Shem to Abram

After the scattering the record follows one line from Shem to Abram ([[Gen. 11:10]], [[Gen. 11:26]]). *Shem* means “name” or “reputation.”[@scripture-helps-gen-6-11] One Church article calls the tower “the transitional event between the dispensations of Noah and Abraham” and notes that the covenant with Abraham came “immediately following mankind’s scattering.”[@donaldson-babel-1994]

## The call

“I will make of thee a great nation… and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing… and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed” ([[Gen. 12:2–3]]).

The Church’s study helps set the two stories side by side: the people of Babel sought to make a name for themselves, while God promised to make Abraham’s name great; they tried not to be scattered, while the Abrahamic covenant includes families being gathered and sealed; and through Abraham all families would be blessed.[@scripture-helps-gen-6-11] The chapter notes carry this ([Genesis 11](/genesis/11/), [Genesis 12](/genesis/12/)).

The Hebrew words are the same ones. “Families” in 12:3 is the *mishpachah* of 10:5, 20, 31, 32. “Name” (*shem*) is one word at 11:4 and 12:2, with different verbs: the builders “make” it, the Lord “makes it great.” The word is common in Genesis (113 times in 95 verses), so the match is in the pairing, not the word.[@oshb-wlc] One older commentary reads “all the families of the ground” as pointing back to the division of chapter 10, so that the blessing was to “unite the divided families.”[@keil-genesis] That is one reading.

## Spread abroad

To Jacob at Beth-el: “thou shalt spread abroad to the west, and to the east, and to the north, and to the south: and in thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed” ([[Gen. 28:14]]). “Spread abroad” here is a different Hebrew verb (*parats*) from Babel’s “scattered,” so that likeness is in the English only. What does repeat from 12:3 is the phrase “the families of the ground” (*ha-adamah*; “ground” and “earth” are both in the King James).[@oshb-wlc] See [Genesis 28](/genesis/28/).

## The gospel preached before

Paul says that “the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the heathen through faith, preached before the gospel unto Abraham, saying, In thee shall all nations be blessed” ([[Gal. 3:8]]), “that the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ” ([[Gal. 3:14]]).[@lds-scriptures]

The risen Lord quotes the promise to the Nephites: “in thy seed shall all the kindreds of the earth be blessed… The Father having raised me up unto you first, and sent me to bless you” ([[3 Ne. 20:25–26]]).[@lds-scriptures] In 1836 Elias committed “the dispensation of the gospel of Abraham, saying that in us and our seed all generations after us should be blessed,” and Moses “the keys of the gathering of Israel from the four parts of the earth” ([[D&C 110:11–12]]).[@lds-scriptures]

Set beside Genesis 10’s “tongue,” “families” and “nations,” John sees “a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues, stood before the throne, and before the Lamb” ([[Rev. 7:9]]).[@lds-scriptures] The comparison is this page’s own, and it rests on King James English: Revelation is Greek and Genesis Hebrew.
