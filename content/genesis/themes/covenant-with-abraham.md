Genesis gives the promise to Abraham, makes it a covenant, marks it, swears it and renews it, and then ends with the family owning a grave and swearing to carry a body home. One reading takes chapters 12, 15, 17 and 22 in order as promise, formal initiation, expansion and formal confirmation.[@hopkin-covenant] The land stays a promise. The New Testament names the seed.

## Promised and made

The Lord tells Abram to go “unto a land that I will shew thee,” and says, “in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed” ([[Gen. 12:1–3]]). At Shechem: “Unto thy seed will I give this land” ([[Gen. 12:7]]). In chapter 15 the seed is to be as the stars, Abram “believed in the LORD” ([[Gen. 15:5–6]]), and “the LORD made a covenant with Abram” ([[Gen. 15:18]]). The Lord’s presence passes between the divided animals, a pledge that He will do what He has promised;[@scripture-helps-gen-12-17] the notes on [Genesis 15](/genesis/15/) follow the scene.

## The sign and the name

“I will make my covenant between me and thee” ([[Gen. 17:2]]), here called “an everlasting covenant,” with the land “for an everlasting possession” ([[Gen. 17:7–8]]). Abram is renamed Abraham ([[Gen. 17:5]]), and circumcision is to be “a token of the covenant” ([[Gen. 17:11]]). The covenant is to be established “with Isaac” ([[Gen. 17:19]], [[Gen. 17:21]]).

## Sworn

Then the angel speaks from heaven: “By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD, for because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son” ([[Gen. 22:16]]), and the blessing and the seed are repeated ([[Gen. 22:17–18]]). Hebrews explains “by myself”: “because he could swear by no greater, he sware by himself” ([[Heb. 6:13]]). In Genesis the Lord says “sworn” or “sware” of Himself only here and at [[Gen. 26:3]];[@lds-scriptures] the later mentions of an oath ([[Gen. 24:7]]; [[Gen. 50:24]]) are read as looking back to this one.[@keil-genesis] See [Genesis 22](/genesis/22/).

## Renewed

The covenant is renewed with Isaac and then with Jacob.[@bd-abraham-covenant] Only the word to Isaac names the oath: “I will perform the oath which I sware unto Abraham thy father” ([[Gen. 26:3]]). To Jacob: “in thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed” ([[Gen. 28:14]]), and “the land which I gave Abraham and Isaac, to thee I will give it” ([[Gen. 35:12]]).

## A grave for a possession

When Sarah dies, Abraham asks the sons of Heth for one plot: “I am a stranger and a sojourner with you: give me a possession of a buryingplace” ([[Gen. 23:4]]). The Hebrew word for “possession” (*’achuzzah*) is the word of the promise, “an everlasting possession” ([[Gen. 17:8]]; repeated by Jacob, [[Gen. 48:4]]). Of the family’s holdings in Canaan it is used only of this grave ([[Gen. 23:9]], [[Gen. 23:20]], [[Gen. 49:30]], [[Gen. 50:13]]); its other uses are Edom’s land ([[Gen. 36:43]]) and the family’s holding in Egypt ([[Gen. 47:11]]).[@oshb-wlc] Sarah, Abraham, Isaac, Rebekah, Leah and Jacob are buried there.[@bd-machpelah] Stephen says God “gave him none inheritance in it, no, not so much as to set his foot on” ([[Acts 7:5]]), and Hebrews says the patriarchs died “not having received the promises” and “confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth” ([[Heb. 11:13]]). See [Genesis 23](/genesis/23/).

## Joseph’s bones

Jacob says, “bury me not… in Egypt,” and “Swear unto me. And he sware unto him” ([[Gen. 47:29–31]]). Joseph’s own last words are: “God will surely visit you, and bring you out of this land unto the land which he sware to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob. And Joseph took an oath of the children of Israel, saying… ye shall carry up my bones from hence” ([[Gen. 50:24–25]]).

Set side by side, the two verses answer each other: Joseph names the land God “sware” to give, and then makes Israel swear to carry him there. The Hebrew verb is the same, *shava*, the ordinary word for swearing, and it is the verb of Jacob’s burial oath too ([[Gen. 47:31]]; [[Gen. 50:5–6]]).[@oshb-wlc] The comparison is this page’s own. See [Genesis 50](/genesis/50/).

Moses takes the bones because Joseph “had straitly sworn the children of Israel” ([[Ex. 13:19]]); they are buried at Shechem ([[Josh. 24:32]]). Hebrews counts it as faith: Joseph “gave commandment concerning his bones” ([[Heb. 11:22]]).

## The seed, which is Christ

Paul reads the promise’s wording: “He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ” ([[Gal. 3:16]]). Peter quotes it: “in thy seed shall all the kindreds of the earth be blessed” ([[Acts 3:25–26]]). Hebrews pairs the oath with “strong consolation” and with “the forerunner… even Jesus” ([[Heb. 6:17–20]]). The promises to Abraham included that Christ would come through his line,[@bd-abraham-covenant] and “Jesus Christ is at the center of the Abrahamic covenant.”[@nelson-everlasting-covenant]
