The birthright was the firstborn’s inheritance: generally a land inheritance and the authority to preside.[@bd-birthright] Genesis tells of it changing hands once, when Esau sells it to Jacob. A generation later the firstborn Reuben is told he will not excel, Jacob sets Joseph’s younger son before the elder, and Chronicles says where things came to rest: the birthright with Joseph, the ruler from Judah. The scriptures also call Jesus Christ the Firstborn, and the faithful receive the firstborn’s inheritance through Him.[@bd-firstborn]

## Sold
Isaac had already been named over Ishmael, the elder, but in the words of the covenant, not of birthright ([[Gen. 17:19–21]]; see [The Covenant with Abraham](/genesis/themes/covenant-with-abraham/)); by one account Isaac was the birthright son in any case, as the first wife’s firstborn.[@ludlow-birthright]

Before the twins are born the Lord tells Rebekah, “the elder shall serve the younger” ([[Gen. 25:23]]). Jacob asks, “Sell me this day thy birthright,” and Esau sells it for bread and lentils: “thus Esau despised his birthright” ([[Gen. 25:31–34]]). This is the only transfer of the birthright Genesis narrates; Esau’s words at 27:36 look back to it.[@lds-scriptures] Hebrews calls Esau one “who for one morsel of meat sold his birthright” ([[Heb. 12:16]]). See the note on [[Gen. 25:31]].

## The blessing
Chapter 27 moves the blessing, not the birthright. Esau names the two separately and calls the sale a taking: “he took away my birthright; and, behold, now he hath taken away my blessing” ([[Gen. 27:36]]). The blessing gives Jacob lordship over his brothers: “let thy mother’s sons bow down to thee” ([[Gen. 27:29]]). See the notes on [[Gen. 27:4]] and [[Gen. 27:36]].

## Lost
Reuben was the firstborn; he “went and lay with Bilhah his father’s concubine” ([[Gen. 35:22]]). Jacob says: “Reuben, thou art my firstborn… Unstable as water, thou shalt not excel” ([[Gen. 49:3–4]]). The birthright was forfeited by that act.[@bd-reuben] See the note on [[Gen. 35:22]].

## Crossed
Jacob lays his right hand on Ephraim, the younger. Joseph objects, “Not so, my father: for this is the firstborn,” and Jacob refuses: “I know it, my son, I know it” ([[Gen. 48:18–19]]); and “he set Ephraim before Manasseh” ([[Gen. 48:20]]). Scripture does not say why Ephraim was chosen, only that Jacob knew what he did; his answer is read as showing it was the Lord’s will.[@scripture-helps-gen-42-50][@ludlow-birthright] See the note on [[Gen. 48:19]].

## Joseph’s, and Judah’s
Genesis does not use the word “birthright” in chapters 48–49.[@lds-scriptures] Chronicles does: Reuben’s “birthright was given unto the sons of Joseph… For Judah prevailed above his brethren, and of him came the chief ruler; but the birthright was Joseph’s” ([[1 Chr. 5:1–2]]). The Lord says, “Ephraim is my firstborn” ([[Jer. 31:9]]). Lordship over the brothers, which Isaac’s blessing gave Jacob, Jacob’s gives to Judah: “thy father’s children shall bow down before thee” ([[Gen. 49:8]]); the two verses are cross-referenced to each other.[@lds-scriptures] Rule and birthright now rest in different tribes; for Judah see [Judah](/genesis/themes/judah/). In the last days, the Lord says, “the richer blessing” is “upon the head of Ephraim and his fellows” ([[D&C 133:34]]).

## Reasons
Genesis gives no single reason. Of Jacob it gives a word spoken before his birth and Esau’s despising; of Reuben, his act ([[Gen. 49:4]]); of Ephraim, none. A firstborn could lose the birthright through unrighteousness,[@bd-birthright][@gs-firstborn] and Paul quotes “the elder shall serve the younger” as spoken “the children being not yet born” ([[Rom. 9:11–12]]). How the Church reads election and worthiness together is in the note on [[Gen. 25:23]].

## The Firstborn
The one who most notably holds a birthright is Jesus Christ: Firstborn of the Father in the spirit and Only Begotten in the flesh, He is the Father’s heir.[@new-era-birthright-2005] He is “the firstborn of every creature” ([[Col. 1:15]]) and “the firstborn among many brethren” ([[Rom. 8:29]]); “I was in the beginning with the Father, and am the Firstborn” ([[D&C 93:21]]). “All those who are begotten through me are partakers of the glory of the same, and are the church of the Firstborn” ([[D&C 93:22]]; compare [[Heb. 12:23]]).

One entry sets two things in a single paragraph and does not say how they relate: the faithful are made members of the Church of the Firstborn in eternity, “since they receive through Jesus Christ the inheritance of the firstborn,” and Joseph “received the inheritance as though he were the firstborn,” as did Ephraim.[@bd-firstborn] Set beside each other, they differ in one respect the words themselves show. Reuben’s birthright “was given unto the sons of Joseph” in his place ([[1 Chr. 5:1]]); the faithful are “joint-heirs with Christ” ([[Rom. 8:17]]), who remains the Firstborn. The comparison of the two is this page’s.
