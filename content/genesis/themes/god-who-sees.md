In Genesis God remembers, sees and hears, and something follows: the waters go down, a well appears, a ram is found, a child is born. From the Flood on the narrator says this of God, and God says it of himself ([[Gen. 7:1]]; [[Gen. 9:15–16]]). Hagar is the first person in Genesis to say that God sees her; Leah and Jacob say it of their own “affliction,” the word first used of hers. Each looks back on what God has done for one person. Joseph’s last words look ahead, and to a people: “God will surely visit you.” Exodus repeats his words, and in Luke the people say “God hath visited his people” when Jesus raises the dead.

## Remembered

“God remembered Noah… and God made a wind to pass over the earth, and the waters assuaged” ([[Gen. 8:1]]). “God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow” ([[Gen. 19:29]]). In both it is the narrator who says so. “Remember” here is not recalling but acting for someone, “with kindness, granting requests, protecting, delivering,” a sense under which both verses are listed.[@bdb] See [Genesis 8](/genesis/8/) and [Genesis 19](/genesis/19/).

God’s seeing is not always rescue. In chapter 1 he sees that what he has made is good; later he sees “that the wickedness of man was great” ([[Gen. 6:5]]) and comes down “to see” Babel and Sodom ([[Gen. 11:5]]; [[Gen. 18:21]]), and judgment follows.

## “Thou God seest me”

Sarah’s Egyptian handmaid Hagar[@bd-hagar] has fled. The angel of the LORD tells her, “the LORD hath heard thy affliction” ([[Gen. 16:11]]). This is the first use in Genesis of the Hebrew noun for “affliction,” and the first time God is said to hear.[@oshb-wlc] Hagar then “called the name of the LORD that spake unto her, Thou God seest me” ([[Gen. 16:13]]). This is the first time in Genesis a person says aloud that God sees.[@oshb-wlc] Others name the place where they met God; she alone in the Old Testament names God himself.[@easton-hagar] Later, sent away with her son, “God heard the voice of the lad… and God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water” ([[Gen. 21:17–19]]). See [Genesis 16](/genesis/16/) and [Genesis 21](/genesis/21/).

## “The LORD will see”

Abraham tells Isaac, “God will provide himself a lamb” ([[Gen. 22:8]]); then he names the place “Jehovah-jireh,” that is, “the LORD will see” or “provide” ([[Gen. 22:14]]).[@lds-scriptures] The Hebrew verb in verse 8 is “see.”[@oshb-wlc] See [Genesis 22](/genesis/22/); the ram is treated in [One in Another’s Place](/genesis/themes/in-anothers-place/).

## Leah, Rachel and Jacob

The narrator says, “When the LORD saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb” ([[Gen. 29:31]]). Leah then says, “the LORD hath looked upon my affliction” ([[Gen. 29:32]]) and “the LORD hath heard that I was hated” ([[Gen. 29:33]]). It is the same Hebrew noun for “affliction” as in [[Gen. 16:11]].[@oshb-wlc] See the note on [[Gen. 29:32]]. Of Rachel the narrator says, “God remembered Rachel, and God hearkened to her, and opened her womb” ([[Gen. 30:22]]). Jacob says it of himself: “God hath seen mine affliction and the labour of my hands” ([[Gen. 31:42]]), the same noun again.[@oshb-wlc] See [Genesis 29](/genesis/29/) and [Genesis 30](/genesis/30/).

## “God will surely visit you”

Dying, Joseph tells his brothers, “God will surely visit you, and bring you out of this land” ([[Gen. 50:24]]), and says it again when he takes an oath of “the children of Israel” ([[Gen. 50:25]]). The Hebrew doubles the verb (*paqod yifqod*);[@oshb-wlc] see the note on [[Gen. 50:24]].

Exodus gathers the verbs: “God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant” ([[Ex. 2:24]]); “I have surely seen the affliction of my people” ([[Ex. 3:7]]), the noun of Hagar, Leah and Jacob.[@oshb-wlc] The message to the elders, “I have surely visited you” ([[Ex. 3:16]]), doubles the verb as Joseph did (*paqod paqadti*),[@oshb-wlc] and when the people hear “that the LORD had visited the children of Israel, and that he had looked upon their affliction,” they bow and worship ([[Ex. 4:31]]). See [Genesis 50](/genesis/50/).

Several of these passages use the name the King James Version prints “the LORD”: Hagar’s ([[Gen. 16:11, 13]]), Abraham’s “Jehovah-jireh” ([[Gen. 22:14]]), Leah’s ([[Gen. 29:31–33]]), and the message to the elders ([[Ex. 3:16]]). Jehovah is the premortal Jesus Christ.[@bd-jehovah]

## “God hath visited his people”

At Nain “when the Lord saw her, he had compassion on her,” and when her son is raised the people say, “God hath visited his people” ([[Luke 7:13]], [[Luke 7:16]]); the footnote there cites [[Ex. 3:16]] and [[Luke 1:68]].[@lds-scriptures] Zacharias says, “he hath visited and redeemed his people… to remember his holy covenant” ([[Luke 1:68]], [[Luke 1:72]]).[@lds-scriptures]

Joseph’s “visit” is “the visitation of God in a gracious and merciful sense,” as in [[Ex. 3:16]], [[Ex. 4:31]] and [[Luke 1:68]].[@ryle-genesis] Setting his words beside the crowd’s at Nain ([[Luke 7:16]]) is this page’s own comparison, made across Hebrew and Greek.
