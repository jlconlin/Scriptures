In Genesis God remembers, sees and hears, and each time something follows: the waters go down, a well appears, a ram is found, a child is born. What changes is who is seen and who says so. At first only the narrator speaks of it, of one man in an ark. Then Hagar says it herself and names God for it, Leah and Jacob say it of their own affliction, and Joseph says it of the children of Israel. Exodus takes up the same words, and Luke uses them of Jesus.

## Remembered

“God remembered Noah… and God made a wind to pass over the earth, and the waters assuaged” ([[Gen. 8:1]]). “God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow” ([[Gen. 19:29]]). In both the narrator alone speaks. “Remember” here is not recalling but acting for someone: the lexicon glosses it “with kindness, granting requests, protecting, delivering,” and lists these two verses.[@bdb] See [Genesis 8](/genesis/8/) and [Genesis 19](/genesis/19/).

God’s seeing does not always rescue. “God saw that the wickedness of man was great” ([[Gen. 6:5]]); “the LORD came down to see the city and the tower” ([[Gen. 11:5]]); “I will go down now, and see whether they have done altogether according to the cry of it” ([[Gen. 18:21]]). Judgment or inquiry follows.

## “Thou God seest me”

Sarah’s Egyptian handmaid Hagar[@bd-hagar] has fled. The angel of the LORD tells her, “the LORD hath heard thy affliction” ([[Gen. 16:11]]). This is the first use in Genesis of the Hebrew noun for “affliction,” and the first time God is said to hear.[@oshb-wlc] Hagar then “called the name of the LORD that spake unto her, Thou God seest me” ([[Gen. 16:13]]). This is the first time in Genesis a person says aloud that God sees.[@oshb-wlc] She is “the only person in the Old Testament to name God.”[@easton-hagar] Later, cast out again, “God heard the voice of the lad… and God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water” ([[Gen. 21:17–19]]). See [Genesis 16](/genesis/16/) and [Genesis 21](/genesis/21/).

## “The LORD will see”

Abraham tells Isaac, “God will provide himself a lamb” ([[Gen. 22:8]]); then he names the place “Jehovah-jireh,” which the Church edition glosses “The Lord will see, or provide.”[@lds-scriptures] The Hebrew verb in verse 8 is “see.”[@oshb-wlc] See [Genesis 22](/genesis/22/); the ram is treated in [One in Another’s Place](/genesis/themes/in-anothers-place/).

## Leah and Rachel

“When the LORD saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb” ([[Gen. 29:31]]) is the narrator. Leah then says, “the LORD hath looked upon my affliction” ([[Gen. 29:32]]) and “the LORD hath heard that I was hated” ([[Gen. 29:33]]). It is the same Hebrew noun for “affliction” as in [[Gen. 16:11]].[@oshb-wlc] The Church’s study helps call this “similar to Hagar’s experience.”[@scripture-helps-gen-24-33] Of Rachel the narrator says, “God remembered Rachel, and God hearkened to her, and opened her womb” ([[Gen. 30:22]]). See [Genesis 29](/genesis/29/) and [Genesis 30](/genesis/30/).

## Jacob

Jacob says it of himself: “God hath seen mine affliction and the labour of my hands” ([[Gen. 31:42]]), the same noun again.[@oshb-wlc] Later he names “God, who answered me in the day of my distress” ([[Gen. 35:3]]).

## “God will surely visit you”

Dying, Joseph tells his brothers, “God will surely visit you, and bring you out of this land” ([[Gen. 50:24]]), and he has “the children of Israel” swear the same words ([[Gen. 50:25]]). “Visit” (*paqad*) is used of God in Genesis only at 21:1 and in these two verses; the doubled form (*paqod yifqod*) is in both.[@oshb-wlc]

Exodus has all the verbs together: “God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant… God looked upon the children of Israel, and God had respect unto them” ([[Ex. 2:24–25]]); “I have surely seen the affliction of my people” ([[Ex. 3:7]]). The Lord’s message to the elders, “I have surely visited you” ([[Ex. 3:16]]), is the same construction as Joseph’s, the verb doubled (*paqod paqadti*).[@oshb-wlc] See [Genesis 50](/genesis/50/).

## “He hath visited his people”

Jehovah of the Old Testament is the premortal Jesus Christ.[@bd-jehovah] The Topical Guide lists Gen. 22:14 under “Jesus Christ, Jehovah.”[@tg-jesus-christ-jehovah]

At Nain, when Jesus raises a widow’s son, “he had compassion on her,” and the people say, “God hath visited his people” ([[Luke 7:13]], [[Luke 7:16]]). The Church edition’s footnote on “visited” points to [[Ex. 3:16]].[@lds-scriptures] Zacharias says, “he hath visited and redeemed his people… to remember his holy covenant” ([[Luke 1:68]], [[Luke 1:72]]).[@lds-scriptures]

Set beside each other, Joseph’s “God will surely visit you” ([[Gen. 50:24]]) and the crowd’s “God hath visited his people” ([[Luke 7:16]]) share the English words “visit” and “people.” No source cited here draws that comparison; the footnote joins Luke to Exodus, and Exodus to Genesis by its Hebrew construction. Luke is Greek and Genesis Hebrew, so what joins the two is the cross-reference and the English word, not one original word.[@lds-scriptures]
