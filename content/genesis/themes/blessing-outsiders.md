The Lord tells Abram, “thou shalt be a blessing” ([[Gen. 12:2]]). Later in Genesis, people outside the family say so, or the narrator does. No source read for this page says these scenes fulfil [[Gen. 12:2–3]]; the page sets them beside the promise because the words “bless” and “for thy sake” are in them. What changes is the reach (a neighbor, a household, a house and field, a kingdom) and who says it. For the promise itself and its family line, see [the covenant with Abraham](/genesis/themes/covenant-with-abraham/).

## The promise

“I will bless thee… and thou shalt be a blessing: And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed” ([[Gen. 12:2–3]]).

## A king sees it

Abimelech says to Abraham, “God is with thee in all that thou doest” ([[Gen. 21:22]]). To Isaac he and his men say, “We saw certainly that the LORD was with thee… thou art now the blessed of the LORD” ([[Gen. 26:28–29]]). Here the outsider sees the blessing on the family; neither scene says he is blessed himself.[@lds-scriptures] See [Genesis 21](/genesis/21/) and [Genesis 26](/genesis/26/).

## For thy sake

Laban asks Jacob to stay: “I have learned by experience that the LORD hath blessed me for thy sake” ([[Gen. 30:27]]). Jacob answers that Laban had little before he came, “and the LORD hath blessed thee since my coming” ([[Gen. 30:30]]). Now the outsider says he is blessed, and why. See [Genesis 30](/genesis/30/).

## For Joseph’s sake

In Potiphar’s house, “his master saw that the LORD was with him” ([[Gen. 39:3]]), and then the narrator says it: “the LORD blessed the Egyptian’s house for Joseph’s sake; and the blessing of the LORD was upon all that he had in the house, and in the field” ([[Gen. 39:5]]).

The Hebrew behind “for thy sake” (30:27) and “for Joseph’s sake” (39:5) is one word, *biglal*. Genesis has it only three times; the third is Abram’s “that it may be well with me for thy sake” ([[Gen. 12:13]]).[@oshb-wlc] Each verse is cross-referenced to the other.[@lds-scriptures] See [Genesis 39](/genesis/39/).

## Bread for all countries

During the famine “Joseph opened all the storehouses, and sold unto the Egyptians,” and “all countries came into Egypt to Joseph for to buy corn” ([[Gen. 41:56–57]]). These verses have neither the word “bless” nor the Lord’s name, and the grain is sold.[@lds-scriptures] Later the Egyptians say, “Thou hast saved our lives” ([[Gen. 47:25]]), one Hebrew verb, the verb of Joseph’s own words at [[Gen. 45:7]] and [[Gen. 50:20]].[@oshb-wlc] See [Genesis 47](/genesis/47/).

## Jacob blesses Pharaoh

“Jacob blessed Pharaoh” ([[Gen. 47:7]]), and when he left, “Jacob blessed Pharaoh, and went out from before Pharaoh” ([[Gen. 47:10]]). The words are not recorded. The verb is read as an old man’s benediction to a man of higher rank, or as a greeting.[@cambridge-genesis] It is the last blessing spoken toward an outsider in Genesis.

## The other way

The first outsiders the family meets suffer for touching it: “the LORD plagued Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai” ([[Gen. 12:17]]), and Abimelech’s house is closed up “because of Sarah” until Abraham prays ([[Gen. 20:17–18]]).[@lds-scriptures] Genesis does not call these a curse, so the page does not set them beside the curse of [[Gen. 12:3]]; it notes that nearness to this family cuts both ways.[@oshb-wlc]

## The blessing and Christ

Abraham’s own record says what the blessing for all families is: “even with the blessings of the Gospel, which are the blessings of salvation, even of life eternal” ([[Abr. 2:11]]). Paul writes “that the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ” ([[Gal. 3:14]]); Peter says God “sent him to bless you” ([[Acts 3:26]]). Heirs of the covenant are those “chosen to responsibly carry the gospel to all the peoples of the earth.”[@bd-abraham-covenant]

Laban’s flocks, Potiphar’s house and Egypt’s bread are set beside the promise as the nearest Genesis comes to showing “thou shalt be a blessing” happening. The blessings in these scenes are flocks, a household’s prosperity and grain; the scriptures just quoted say the blessing promised to all families is the gospel of Jesus Christ.[@lds-scriptures] For what the Church’s study helps say of Joseph and the Savior, see [Brothers at Odds, and Reconciled](/genesis/themes/brothers-reconciled/).
