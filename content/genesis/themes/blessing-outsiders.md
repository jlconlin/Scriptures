The Lord tells Abram, “thou shalt be a blessing” ([[Gen. 12:2]]). Genesis does not use those words again, and does not say that any later scene is the promise coming to pass. It does say, of several people outside the line of promise, that the Lord was with the family or that He blessed them for its sake. This page sets those scenes beside the promise; the comparison is the page’s own. What changes from scene to scene is who speaks and how much is said: a king sees that the Lord is with the family; Laban says he himself has been blessed; the narrator says it of an Egyptian’s house and field; Egypt is fed, though no verse there says “bless”; and Jacob blesses Pharaoh. For the promise itself and its family line, see [the covenant with Abraham](/genesis/themes/covenant-with-abraham/).

## The promise

“I will bless thee… and thou shalt be a blessing: And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed” ([[Gen. 12:2–3]]).

## A king sees it

Abimelech says to Abraham, “God is with thee in all that thou doest” ([[Gen. 21:22]]). To Isaac, Abimelech (the same king or a later one of that name) and his men say, “We saw certainly that the LORD was with thee… thou art now the blessed of the LORD” ([[Gen. 26:28–29]]). Here the outsider sees the blessing on the family; neither scene says he is blessed himself.[@lds-scriptures] See the notes on [[Gen. 21:22]] and [[Gen. 26:28]].

## For thy sake

Laban, Jacob’s mother’s brother ([[Gen. 29:10]]) but outside the line of promise, asks Jacob to stay: “I have learned by experience that the LORD hath blessed me for thy sake” ([[Gen. 30:27]]). Jacob answers that Laban had little before he came, “and the LORD hath blessed thee since my coming” ([[Gen. 30:30]]). Now a man outside the promise says that he himself is blessed, and why. How he learned it is in the note on [[Gen. 30:27]].

## For Joseph’s sake

In Potiphar’s house, “his master saw that the LORD was with him” ([[Gen. 39:3]]), and then the narrator says it: “the LORD blessed the Egyptian’s house for Joseph’s sake; and the blessing of the LORD was upon all that he had in the house, and in the field” ([[Gen. 39:5]]).

The Hebrew behind “for thy sake” (30:27) and “for Joseph’s sake” (39:5) is one word, *biglal*. Genesis has it only three times; the third is Abram’s word to Sarai, “that it may be well with me for thy sake” ([[Gen. 12:13]]), which is no blessing on an outsider.[@oshb-wlc] The footnotes of the Church’s edition point from 30:27 to 39:5 and back.[@lds-scriptures] See the notes on [[Gen. 39:2]] and [[Gen. 39:5]].

## Bread for all countries

During the famine “all countries came into Egypt to Joseph for to buy corn” ([[Gen. 41:56–57]]). These verses have neither the word “bless” nor the Lord’s name, and the grain is sold.[@lds-scriptures] Later the Egyptians, who have sold their land and themselves for bread, say, “Thou hast saved our lives… we will be Pharaoh’s servants” ([[Gen. 47:25]]). See the notes on [[Gen. 41:54]] and [[Gen. 47:25]].

## Jacob blesses Pharaoh

“Jacob blessed Pharaoh” ([[Gen. 47:7]]), and again as he leaves ([[Gen. 47:10]]). The words are not recorded. The verb is read as an old man’s benediction to a man of higher rank, or as a greeting.[@ryle-genesis] See the note on [[Gen. 47:7]].

## The other way

Twice before any of this, a king’s house suffers on the family’s account. When Pharaoh takes Sarai, “the LORD plagued Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai” ([[Gen. 12:17]]). When Abimelech takes Sarah, the LORD closes the wombs of his house “because of Sarah,” and when Abraham prays, “God healed Abimelech, and his wife, and his maidservants” ([[Gen. 20:17–18]]).[@lds-scriptures] Neither of the two Hebrew verbs for “curse” in [[Gen. 12:3]] is used in either scene.[@oshb-wlc] See the notes on [[Gen. 12:17]] and [[Gen. 20:18]].

## The blessing and Christ

Abraham’s own record finishes the sentence: “thou shalt be a blessing unto thy seed after thee, that in their hands they shall bear this ministry and Priesthood unto all nations” ([[Abr. 2:9]]), and all families are to be blessed “even with the blessings of the Gospel, which are the blessings of salvation, even of life eternal” ([[Abr. 2:11]]). Paul writes “that the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ” ([[Gal. 3:14]]). Heirs of the covenant are those “chosen to responsibly carry the gospel to all the peoples of the earth.”[@bd-abraham-covenant] See the note on [[Gen. 12:2]].

Set beside that promise, Genesis says “blessed… for thy sake” of Laban and of Potiphar’s house; Egypt’s bread it does not call a blessing. The comparison is this page’s own, and it is a contrast as much as a likeness: these scenes give flocks, a prosperous house and grain, and the scriptures just quoted promise all families the blessings of the gospel, through Jesus Christ.[@lds-scriptures] For what the Church’s study helps say of Joseph and the Savior, see [Brothers at Odds, and Reconciled](/genesis/themes/brothers-reconciled/).
