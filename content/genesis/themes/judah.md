In Genesis Judah proposes selling a brother, then offers himself as a slave so that a brother can go home, and is then promised that his brothers will praise him. What changes is Judah. His offer to take Benjamin’s place is the step the Church’s study helps liken to the Savior, who was Judah’s descendant.[@scripture-helps-gen-42-50]

## “Let us sell him”

“What profit is it if we slay our brother, and conceal his blood? Come, and let us sell him to the Ishmeelites… for he is our brother and our flesh” ([[Gen. 37:26–27]]). The chapter notes follow the scene: [Genesis 37](/genesis/37/).

## “Discern, I pray thee”

Judah leaves his signet, bracelets and staff with Tamar as a “pledge” ([[Gen. 38:18]]). When she is accused she sends them back: “Discern, I pray thee, whose are these” ([[Gen. 38:25]]). This is the Hebrew phrase (*hakker-na*) the brothers used when they brought Jacob the coat: “know now whether it be thy son’s coat or no” ([[Gen. 37:32]]).[@oshb-wlc][@sc-knowhy-841] Judah answers, “She hath been more righteous than I” ([[Gen. 38:26]]). Through her son Pharez, “Tamar would become an ancestor of Jesus Christ.”[@scripture-helps-gen-37-41]

Why Judah changed is not stated. The Tamar story is “the only story included in the Genesis narrative to possibly explain Judah’s transformation,”[@olson-matriarchs] and that chapter “might help explain the change that is occurring in Judah.”[@sc-knowhy-841] See [Genesis 38](/genesis/38/).

## “I will be surety for him”

Jacob will not send Benjamin to Egypt. Judah says, “I will be surety for him; of my hand shalt thou require him” ([[Gen. 43:9]]). The verb for “be surety” (*arav*) means to “take on pledge, give in pledge”;[@bdb] the “pledge” of chapter 38 is *eravon*. In Genesis the verb occurs only at 43:9 and 44:32, the noun only at 38:17, 18 and 20.[@oshb-wlc] One reading: the man who left tokens of himself as a pledge now offers himself as the pledge.[@spackman-judah-tamar] The psalmist asks the same of God with this verb: “Be surety for thy servant for good” ([[Ps. 119:122]]).[@bdb] See the notes on [[Gen. 38:18]] and [[Gen. 43:9]].

## “Instead of the lad”

Benjamin is found with Joseph’s cup. Judah pleads: “let thy servant abide instead of the lad a bondman to my lord; and let the lad go up with his brethren. For how shall I go up to my father, and the lad be not with me?” ([[Gen. 44:33–34]]).

Judah would take Benjamin’s punishment so that Benjamin could return to his father. That offer can remind a reader of Jesus Christ, who took our sins upon Himself so that we can return to our Father;[@scripture-helps-gen-42-50] the passage cited with it is [[Mosiah 15:9]], where the Son is “standing betwixt them and justice… taken upon himself their iniquity and their transgressions.”[@lds-scriptures] The note on [[Gen. 44:33]] gives the source’s words.

## “Until Shiloh come”

Genesis does not say the blessing answers the offer; Jacob’s words pass over Reuben, Simeon and Levi before they reach Judah ([[Gen. 49:3–7]]). He blesses Judah: “thou art he whom thy brethren shall praise… thy father’s children shall bow down before thee” ([[Gen. 49:8]]). “Praise” is the verb of Judah’s naming ([[Gen. 29:35]]), and it comes in Genesis only in these two verses.[@oshb-wlc] The note on [[Gen. 49:8]] takes up “bow down” and Joseph’s dreams. Then: “The sceptre shall not depart from Judah… until Shiloh come” ([[Gen. 49:10]]). Shiloh is read as the Messiah, a reading “confirmed by JST Gen. 50:24,” and the name “can be read as a short form for ‘He to whom it belongs.’”[@bd-shiloh] Hebrews says “our Lord sprang out of Juda” ([[Heb. 7:14]]) and Revelation names “the Lion of the tribe of Juda” ([[Rev. 5:5]]).[@lds-scriptures] See [Genesis 49](/genesis/49/).

## Set beside

One likeness more is in the Hebrew of chapters 22 and 44; no source cited here draws it. “Instead of” is the ordinary Hebrew word for “under” and “in place of” (*tachat*, 31 times in Genesis), so the word alone proves little. It is the word for the ram Abraham offered “in the stead of his son” ([[Gen. 22:13]]).[@oshb-wlc] Set side by side: a ram in place of a son, a brother in place of a brother. See the notes on [Genesis 22](/genesis/22/) and [Genesis 44](/genesis/44/).
