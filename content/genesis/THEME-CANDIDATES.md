# Genesis: theme candidates

Topics that run across chapters, reported by the reviewers as the fifty chapters were written (2026-10-03 and 04). The author selected seven to develop on 2026-10-07; the earlier reports below remain as research leads, not additional page assignments. **Do not begin writing pages yet.** The author is selecting themes book by book and will give a separate instruction to develop them after all selections are made.

## Selected for development (author, 2026-10-07)

These are seven **distinct proposed pages**, not seven finished outlines. Research and test each arc under `STANDARDS.md` §8 before writing. The author affirmed that theme pages are an opportunity to make connections that may not have been made before; unlike chapter notes, the *connection between passages* may be the page's own. Keep facts sourced and make the comparison clear to the reader. The number is an identifier for this selection, not a writing order.

1. **The covenant carried through generations.** Trace the promise to Abraham (Gen. 12:1–3), covenant (15), sign (17), oath (22:16–18), renewal to Isaac and Jacob (26:3–5; 28:13–15; 35:9–12), and Joseph's charge concerning his bones (50:24–25). The land promised but held only as a burial place (23; 47:29–31; Heb. 11:9–16) can show the promise still awaited. The blessing in Abraham's seed points to Christ (Gal. 3:8, 16; Abr. 2:9–11). **Boundary:** this page follows the promise *within the family through time*; pages 3 and 5 address its reach beyond the family.
2. **The God who sees, hears, and remembers.** Follow divine attention that becomes action: God remembers Noah (8:1), sees and hears Hagar and Ishmael (16:11–13; 21:17–19), remembers Abraham and rescues Lot (19:29), provides for Abraham and Isaac (22:8, 13–14), sees Leah's affliction (29:31–32), and remembers Rachel (30:22). Test whether this sequence yields an arc rather than merely a shared motif. Connect Jehovah, the premortal Christ (`content/genesis/BRIEF.md`, “Names of God”), to His response to overlooked and imperiled people without making every episode mean the same thing. The earlier shortlist's “God who sees, hears, and remembers” is selected despite the earlier reviewer's concern that its endpoint needs research.
3. **From Babel's scattering to blessing all families.** Set the families and nations of Gen. 10 beside Babel's scattering in 11:1–9 and the call and promise of 12:1–3; follow “all the families of the earth” in 28:14. Gal. 3:8 calls the promise to Abraham the gospel. **Boundary:** show the world-sized problem and answer, rather than retelling the covenant's generational renewals (page 1). This was in the detailed reports under chapters 10–11, not the earlier shortlist.
4. **From hiding from God to meeting Him.** Begin with hiding and expulsion from God's presence (3:8, 23–24; compare 4:14, 16); follow Jacob at Bethel, “the gate of heaven” (28:12–17), and his declaration at Peniel that he has seen God's face (32:24–30; compare 48:15–16). Test the connection to Christ and the ladder in John 1:51. This is an opportunity for a theme page's own text-grounded comparison; do not assert that the Genesis narrator intended every later connection. **Boundary:** the page follows access to God's presence across widely separated chapters, not only Jacob's experience or a list of sacred places. Earlier reports called this too thin, but the author selected it after seeing the cross-chapter possibility.
5. **The covenant blesses people outside the family.** Trace the outward effect of Gen. 12:2–3 in Laban's words (30:27), the blessing on Potiphar's house (39:5), and Joseph's blessing of Pharaoh and preservation of Egypt (47:7–10; see also 41:54–57). The comparison to the original promise was cut from a chapter note for lack of a source; a theme page may propose it in its own voice. **Boundary:** concrete encounters with outsiders, distinct from the nations-wide promise of page 3 and the family succession of page 1. Research should check what each passage actually says of blessing before joining them.
6. **One person in another's place.** Set the ram offered “in the stead of his son” (22:13) beside Judah's offer to remain “instead of the lad” (44:33). Include **Judah's full change** from proposing Joseph's sale (37:26–27), through acknowledging Tamar (38:26) and standing surety for Benjamin (43:8–9), to his offer and the blessing on his line (49:8–12). Test the Hebrew and the Christ connection rather than claiming that the two substitutions are identical. This absorbs the earlier shortlist's separate Judah page; chapter 22 already explains Isaac's offering in detail, so link to it rather than repeat it.
7. **Brothers at odds and reconciled, with Joseph preserving life.** Cain and Abel provide a tragic opening (4:2–11); Jacob and Esau move from threatened violence to meeting and embrace (27:41–45; 32–33); Joseph's brothers sell him, are tested, and are reconciled to the man God sent ahead to preserve them (37; 42–45; 50:19–21). The climax can compare Joseph's lifesaving role with Christ, as the existing Genesis notes and *Scripture Helps* do. Do not force Cain and Abel into a reconciliation they never have. This combines earlier shortlist items **“Joseph, sent ahead to preserve life”** and **“Brothers at odds and reconciled”**; it should not become two pages. Judah's offer is treated fully in page 6 and can be linked here.

**Deliberately not selected:** “The younger chosen over the elder, and where the birthright goes” (old shortlist item 2). The author saw the repeated reversals but no interesting connection or developing Christ-centered arc. Do not turn it into a page merely because it recurs. The earlier shortlist's Judah page is folded into 6, and its Joseph and reconciliation pages are combined as 7. The author considers seven pages worthwhile if they remain distinct; research may reveal overlap, especially among 1, 3, and 5, but do not merge them without bringing that finding back to the author.

## Earlier short list (historical; superseded by the selection above)

Sorted by the coordinator from the reports below, by how many chapters reported a topic and whether something changes from its first passage to its last. The chapter numbers are the chapters that reported it. None has been researched as a theme; each would begin with the research step in `STANDARDS.md` §8.

With it: the land promised but held only as a burial place (23; Heb. 11:9–16), and the one covenant from Enoch and Noah to Abraham (JST Gen. 9; 13:14; 17:7). Reported by 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 23, 26, 27, 28, 35, 41, 46, 47, 49, 50.
2. **The younger chosen over the elder, and where the birthright goes.** Abel and Seth, Isaac over Ishmael, Jacob over Esau, Perez before Zerah, Joseph and Judah over Reuben, Ephraim before Manasseh (1 Chr. 5:1–2). Reported by 4, 10, 16, 17, 21, 25, 27, 29, 34, 35, 37, 38, 41, 48, 49.
3. **Joseph, sent ahead to preserve life.** Sold and sent (37; 45:5–8; 50:20), the pit and the prison, bread in famine, known “the second time” (Acts 7:13); *Scripture Helps* has a table of the likenesses to Christ. Reported by 37, 39, 40, 41, 42, 45, 46, 47, 48, 50.
4. **Judah, from selling a brother to standing in his place.** 37:26–27; 38:26; 43:8–9; 44:18–34; 49:8–12, and the line to Christ (Matt. 1:3). Reported by 37, 38, 39, 42, 43, 44, 46, 49.
5. **Brothers at odds and reconciled.** Cain and Abel; Jacob and Esau (27; 33); Joseph and his brothers (37; 45; 50). Reported by 4, 25, 27, 32, 33, 42, 44, 45. It may belong inside 3 and 4.
6. **The God who sees, hears, and remembers.** Hagar (16:13; 21:17), Jehovah-jireh (22:14), Leah (29:32), “God remembered” Noah, Abraham, and Rachel (8:1; 19:29; 30:22), and the barren mothers given sons. Reported by 8, 11, 16, 18, 19, 21, 22, 25, 29, 30.

Thinner, or better kept in the chapters' notes: “the LORD was with him” (26, 28, 30, 31, 39); garments in the stories of Jacob and Joseph (3, 27, 37, 38, 39); Beth-el and the way into God's presence (3, 28, 32, 35); the Flood as the Creation undone and renewed (6, 7, 8: three neighboring chapters, so the notes carry it); “be fruitful, and multiply” (1, 41, 47, 48).

**Connections only a theme page could make** (no source makes them, so the reviewers cut them from chapters; `STANDARDS.md` §8 allows a page its own connections): Abraham on the altar and Isaac on the altar (Abr. 1:15–16; Gen. 22:11); the blessing on Laban's, Potiphar's, and Pharaoh's houses beside 12:2–3 (30:27; 39:5; 47:7–10); Isaac who “discerned him not” (27:23) beside Jacob's “I know it” (48:19); chapter 37 echoed in chapter 43; grain “as the sand of the sea” (41:49) beside the promised seed (22:17).

## As the chapters reported them

- **“Be fruitful, and multiply.”** Given to the creatures and to Adam and Eve (1:22, 28), renewed to Noah (9:1, 7), and carried into the covenant line (17:6; 28:3; 35:11; 48:4; the later references untested). From chapter 1.

- **Image and likeness, from God to Adam to Seth.** 1:26–27; 5:1–3; 9:6 (untested). From chapter 1.
- **The LORD sees and provides.** 16:13–14; 21:17–19; 22:4, 8, 13–14; perhaps Peniel (32:30, untested). From chapter 22.
- **“Here am I.”** 22:1, 7, 11; to test at 27:1, 31:11, 46:2. From chapter 22.
- **The younger son chosen over the elder; Joseph as a deliverer.** From the brief; untested.
- **Abraham on the altar and Isaac on the altar.** [[Abr. 1:15–16]] (the angel of His presence frees Abraham; “Abraham, Abraham… my name is Jehovah”) beside [[Gen. 22:11]]. A connection no source makes, so it could only be a theme page's own (`STANDARDS.md` §8). From chapter 22.
