## Decided without the author

**Chapter 1**
- **Who wrote Genesis (setting):** under “newest trumps oldest,” the setting now gives the *Scripture Helps* (2025) wording (the Bible “does not identify who wrote the Pentateuch”; many scholars see several writers and editors; “Restoration scripture confirms Moses's central involvement”) with the Bible Dictionary's line on the sources Moses used. Its dating of the present form of Genesis to the exile is left out.
- **The firmament (1:6):** the solid vault stays, as how the first hearers pictured the sky; President Nelson's words say what the division accomplished. Not presented as two equal readings.
- **Joseph Smith's 7 April 1844 discourse (1:2, 1:26):** the chapter quotes “baurau… means to organize” and the “grand council” passage, and leaves out his reading of the Bible's first Hebrew word. A note is not the place to explain that reading, and no reader's question about the verse needs it.

**Chapter 22**
- **Moriah (22:2):** the note keeps both the tradition that Moriah is the temple mount (the Church's sources call it a tradition) and a BYU scholar's doubt. It is a question of place, not doctrine.
- **The angel of the LORD (22:11):** a second reading (a messenger bearing the Lord's name), which no source applies to this chapter, was cut.
- **Melvin J. Ballard's words in `christ`** are cited from the seminary manual that prints them; the original talk is to be read and cited when the chapter is next touched.

**Sources**
- **Two ancient texts readable only in user uploads on archive.org** (L. W. King's *Seven Tablets of Creation*, 1902; R. Campbell Thompson's *Epic of Gilgamish*, 1928) are not cited (`STANDARDS.md` §4 rule 8). The flood story is quoted from the British Museum's 1920 booklet and Rogers (1912); the creation epic only through an article that quotes it.
- **Cain, Ham, and Canaan:** the notes follow the Church's 2025 *Scripture Helps* and the essay “Race and the Priesthood,” and do not quote the older lines in the Bible Dictionary and Guide to the Scriptures entries “Cain” and “Ham.”

## Reader questions no source answered

- **1:** why evening comes before morning; why the second day is not called good; why the Hebrew of verse 5 says “day one.”
- **22:** why Isaac is not mentioned on the return (19). Not checked: whether the note on 22:18 mostly repeats the Gospel Library's footnotes.
- **2:** What bdellium and the onyx (shoham) stone are; the footnote calls the stone's identity uncertain, and the writer found nothing better; Where Havilah was; the lexicon gives only theories; Why Exodus 31:17 says God 'rested, and was refreshed' when Isaiah 40:28 says He is never weary; no note addresses the tension.
- **3:** Did the serpent once have legs; why 'dust shalt thou eat' (3:14)? Only outside commentary addresses it; the writer judged it a curiosity and left it out; Why fig leaves in particular (3:7)? Belnap says only 'maximum coverage'; no note was written.
- **4:** Verse 24: does Jesus' 'seventy times seven' (Matt. 18:22) answer Lamech's 'seventy and sevenfold'? The writer found no Church, BYU, or Scripture Central source that makes the connection; Verse 16: where Nod was. Only the name's meaning is answered; Verse 4 (Heb. 11:4): what Joseph Smith taught about Abel 'yet speaking.' That would need the Joseph Smith Papers discourse, which the writer didn't pursue; Verse 19: Lamech's two wives as the first plural marriage. Deferred to chapter 16 by the brief.
- **5:** Why the list has ten generations, and whether the number means anything: no source read treats it as meaningful; How the birth order of Noah's sons in Moses 8:12 fits with Gen. 11:10: no source read reconciles them; What the names Enos, Cainan, Mahalaleel, and Jared mean: not researched, since the brief rules out a note per name in lists; Two works cited in Scripture Helps endnote 4 (Holzapfel and others, Jehovah and the World of the Old Testament; The Jewish Study Bible) are not readable online, so Scripture Helps alone is cited for those two explanations of the long lifespans.
- **6:** Why would a loving God destroy everyone, and the animals too (6:7, 6:13, 6:17)? Scripture Helps answers this beside the verse with John Taylor and Neal A. Maxwell. The chapter covers part of it (God's grief in Moses 7; the chance to repent in the spirit world, in christ), but no note takes the question head-on; Why are the sons listed as Shem, Ham, Japheth (6:10) when Moses 8:12 makes Japheth the eldest? This is left out as peripheral; Two of every sort here but sevens of clean animals in 7:2: this is left for chapter 7.
- **7:** Why the animals die too (verses 21–23). This belongs to 6:7 and 6:17 in chapter 6; Whether “forty days” carries a meaning here. No source read gives one for this verse; Who Noah's wife was. The text and the sources read are silent.
- **8:** 8:21 'evil from his youth': does the verse mean people are evil from birth? No Church, BYU or Scripture Central source read applies Latter-day Saint teaching on children (Moses 6:55; D&C 29:46) to this verse, so the note gives only Keil's reading; 8:13 'the covering of the ark': what it was and how Noah removed it. Ryle says 'we are not told,' and no Latter-day Saint source takes it up (the writer found the same).
- **9:** Why the animals now "fear and dread" people (9:2): with the unsourced sentence cut, only Ryle answers it among readable sources. It is now unanswered rather than resting on a fifth Ryle note; Why Noah lived 950 years (9:29): left to chapter 5, as the brief directs.
- **10:** Why Sheba and Havilah appear under both Cush (v. 7) and Joktan (vv. 28–29): Keil calls the link obscure. The writer left it out as a curiosity; Abraham 1:21–27 (Egyptus and Ham's line in Egypt) beside Mizraim (v. 6): the writer left it out on purpose, and footnote 6c already points there.
- **11:** Why Iscah (11:29) is named: no source read explains it; What 'from the east' (11:2) means: the writer found no Church or Latter-day Saint source that takes it up; The arithmetic of 11:10 (Shem 100 'two years after the flood') set against 5:32 and 7:6: noticed by the writer and not taken up.
- **12:** How old Sarai was and how she could be 'very fair' at about 65 (only Keil raises it; the writer cut it); Who exactly the Pharaoh was, beyond the Bible Dictionary's 'probably Hyksos' and Gee's dynasty remark; Why Abraham 2:14 gives 62 and Genesis 75: no source reconciles them; What the plagues on Pharaoh's house were: Genesis does not say.
- **13:** Which way are Abram's "left" and "right" in verse 9 (Hebrew orientation to the east, so south and north)? No source read applies this to the verse; Is Lot's eastward move (v. 11) meant to echo Cain and Babel? Benson reports it only as what "some scholars" say, citing works not read.
