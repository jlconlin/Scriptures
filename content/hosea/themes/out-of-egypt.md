Egypt is named in thirteen verses of Hosea,[@oshb-wlc] and they are not of one kind: some remember the Exodus ([[Hos. 11:1]]; [[Hos. 13:4]]), two show Ephraim courting Egypt in the present ([[Hos. 7:11]]; [[Hos. 12:1]]), and some threaten a return ([[Hos. 8:13]]; [[Hos. 9:3]]). Read in the order of the story, not of the book, the verses say that the Lord called Israel out of Egypt as a son, that the son turned away, that the Lord will not give him up, and that He will bring His children home. Matthew says the first of these verses was fulfilled in the child Jesus ([[Matt. 2:15]]).

## “I loved him, and called my son”

“When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt” ([[Hos. 11:1]]). In Hosea the son is Israel and the calling is the Exodus.[@frederick-ot-in-nt-gospels] The Lord had sent Moses to say to Pharaoh, “Israel is my son, even my firstborn” ([[Ex. 4:22–23]]).[@lds-scriptures] “In love God redeemed them from Egypt.”[@bd-hosea] The Lord is nowhere called “father” in Hosea (the book's only “fathers” are Israel's ancestors, [[Hos. 9:10]]);[@oshb-wlc] the picture of a father and a son is drawn by “my son” and by what the Lord does in [[Hos. 11:1–4]].[@lds-scriptures] See the note on [[Hos. 11:1]].

## Back toward Egypt

“As they called them, so they went from them” ([[Hos. 11:2]]). Ephraim turns to Egypt and Assyria for help: “like a silly dove… they call to Egypt, they go to Assyria” ([[Hos. 7:11]]). The Lord, for His part, threatens a return to Egypt: “they shall return to Egypt” ([[Hos. 8:13]]); “Ephraim shall return to Egypt” ([[Hos. 9:3]]). The Church's footnote at [[Hos. 8:13]] gives the last of the covenant curses: “the LORD shall bring thee into Egypt again” ([[Deut. 28:68]]).[@lds-scriptures]

**Connection made by this page:** the Hebrew verb for “called” in 11:1 (*qara*) is the verb of “they call” in 7:11 (it is also in 11:2 and 11:7); set the two verses beside each other.[@oshb-wlc]

[[Hos. 11:5]] says the opposite: “He shall not return into the land of Egypt, but the Assyrian shall be his king”; see the note there. See also the note on [[Hos. 8:13]].

## “How shall I give thee up?”

The Lord “taught Ephraim also to go, taking them by their arms; but they knew not that I healed them,” and drew them “with bands of love” ([[Hos. 11:3–4]]). The picture has been read as a loving parent holding a toddler's arms to teach her to walk.[@vandyke-ogden-twelve] When the child has gone, the Lord asks, “How shall I give thee up, Ephraim? how shall I deliver thee, Israel?… for I am God, and not man” ([[Hos. 11:8–9]]). The Joseph Smith Translation of verse 8 adds “my mercies are extended to gather thee.”[@lds-scriptures] See the notes on [[Hos. 11:3]] and [[Hos. 11:8]].

## Called home

The Lord “shall roar like a lion: when he shall roar, then the children shall tremble from the west. They shall tremble as a bird out of Egypt, and as a dove out of the land of Assyria: and I will place them in their houses, saith the LORD” ([[Hos. 11:10–11]]). The verb is “roar,” not the “called” of 11:1.

**Connection made by this page:** “children” in 11:10 is the plural of the Hebrew word (*ben*) for “son” in 11:1, and the word stands in chapter 11 only in these two verses;[@oshb-wlc] and “dove” (*yonah*) is in Hosea only at 7:11, where Ephraim calls to Egypt and goes to Assyria, and at 11:11, where they come “as a bird out of Egypt, and as a dove out of the land of Assyria.”[@oshb-wlc] See the notes on [[Hos. 7:11]] and [[Hos. 11:10]].

The promise to the wife in chapter 2 also looks back to the Exodus: she will answer “as in the day when she came up out of the land of Egypt” ([[Hos. 2:15]]).[@lds-scriptures]

## The Son called out of Egypt

Joseph takes the child to Egypt, “that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Out of Egypt have I called my son” ([[Matt. 2:13–15]]).[@lds-scriptures] Israel's coming out of Egypt as a child was a “similitude” of the Lord's.[@lds-scriptures, heading to Hosea 11] Matthew's reading has been called typological: he sees Jesus retracing Israel's being called by God out of Egypt.[@griffin-matthew-portrayal][@frederick-ot-in-nt-gospels] Jesus “does not simply fulfill a prediction from Hosea”; the quotation depends on the reader knowing what Hosea's words first meant.[@rsc-combs-ahaz-sign]

The speaker of 11:1 names Himself in [[Hos. 13:4]]: “I am the LORD thy God from the land of Egypt… there is no saviour beside me.” The LORD of the Old Testament, Jehovah, is the premortal Jesus Christ.[@bd-jehovah] **Connection made by this page:** set [[Hos. 13:4]] beside [[Matt. 2:15]]: the LORD who is Israel's God “from the land of Egypt,” and the child whom Matthew shows brought out of Egypt.[@lds-scriptures] Nephi swears by the Lord God who “brought Israel up out of the land of Egypt”: “there is none other name given under heaven save it be this Jesus Christ” ([[2 Ne. 25:20]]).[@lds-scriptures]
