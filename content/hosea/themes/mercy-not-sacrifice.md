Hosea's Hebrew word *hesed* is in six verses of the book.[@oshb-wlc] Its primary meaning is “covenant loyalty.”[@sc-archive-hesed] The Lord promises it in the betrothal, finds it missing in the land, says He desired it rather than sacrifice, and at last tells the people to keep it. Jesus quoted the verse twice.[@scripture-helps-hosea]

<h2 id="one-word">One word, three English words</h2>

The King James renders *hesed* “lovingkindness” in 2:19, “mercy” in 4:1, 6:6, 10:12 and 12:6, and “goodness” in 6:4.[@oshb-wlc][@lds-scriptures] *Hesed* “has no adequate English equivalent”: it describes a covenant relationship “in which both parties are bound to be loyal and faithful to each other.”[@nelson-everlasting-covenant] The “mercy” of [[Hos. 1:6]] and [[Hos. 14:3]] is a different word, which [“I Will Betroth Thee unto Me”](/hosea/themes/betroth-thee/) follows.[@oshb-wlc]

## Promised, then missing

The betrothal is “in righteousness, and in judgment, and in lovingkindness, and in mercies… in faithfulness: and thou shalt know the LORD” ([[Hos. 2:19–20]]). Then the Lord “hath a controversy with the inhabitants of the land, because there is no truth, nor mercy, nor knowledge of God in the land” ([[Hos. 4:1]]). That controversy is a legal charge for breach of covenant, and its three charges are the terms of the betrothal: the *hesed* of 4:1 is the “lovingkindness” of 2:19, and “knowing” the Lord is in both.[@jackson-hosea-marriage] The priest is told, “My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge” ([[Hos. 4:6]]); “they have not known the LORD” ([[Hos. 5:4]]). See [Hosea 2](/hosea/2/) and [Hosea 4](/hosea/4/).

## A morning cloud

The people say, “Come, and let us return unto the LORD,” and, “Then shall we know, if we follow on to know the LORD” ([[Hos. 6:1]], [[Hos. 6:3]]). The Lord asks what He shall do, “for your goodness is as a morning cloud, and as the early dew it goeth away” ([[Hos. 6:4]]); [“I Will Heal Their Backsliding”](/hosea/themes/heal-their-backsliding/) takes up verses 1–4. Then: “For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings” ([[Hos. 6:6]]). The Hebrew behind “mercy” here is the same word as in 4:1,[@scripture-helps-hosea] and can be read “charity, or lovingkindness.”[@lds-scriptures] While sacrifices were still important, the Lord placed more value on their heartfelt devotion and compassion for others.[@scripture-helps-hosea] The note at [Hosea 6:6](/hosea/6/) takes up whether this is “not sacrifice” or “more than sacrifice.” Set beside it, Samuel's words to Saul: “to obey is better than sacrifice” ([[1 Sam. 15:22]]); and Micah's, which ask “shall I come before him with burnt offerings?” and answer, “to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God” ([[Micah 6:6–8]]).[@lds-scriptures]

<h2 id="sow-reap-keep">Sow, reap, keep</h2>

Meanwhile “Ephraim hath made many altars to sin,” and “the LORD accepteth them not” ([[Hos. 8:11]], [[Hos. 8:13]]). Then the word the Lord desired more than sacrifice returns twice.[@lds-scriptures][@oshb-wlc] In 10:12 what is sown is righteousness, and *hesed* is the harvest: “Sow to yourselves in righteousness, reap in mercy” ([[Hos. 10:12]]; see [the note](/hosea/10/)). In 12:6 it is something to keep: “turn thou to thy God: keep mercy and judgment, and wait on thy God continually” ([[Hos. 12:6]]). At the end the people's prayer says, “so will we render the calves of our lips” ([[Hos. 14:2]]): prayers offered where young bulls would have been.[@ot-manual-10] See [Hosea 14](/hosea/14/) and [Hosea 12](/hosea/12/).

## “Go ye and learn what that meaneth”

Jesus quotes Hosea 6:6 in two scenes. At a table with publicans and sinners, the Pharisees ask why He eats there; He answers, “go ye and learn what that meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice” ([[Matt. 9:10–13]]). In the grain fields on the Sabbath, the Pharisees say His disciples do “that which is not lawful”; He says, “if ye had known what this meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice, ye would not have condemned the guiltless” ([[Matt. 12:1–7]]). In each case He rebuked the Pharisees for putting observance ahead of repentance and loving others.[@scripture-helps-hosea]

The first scene challenges self-justifying obedience placed over compassion;[@lane-hostility] in the second, mercy is put above the temple and its Sabbath rules.[@lane-sabbath-synoptic] He applied the prophets' critique to other situations where keeping the law might obstruct more important duties toward God and neighbor.[@mcclellan-go-ye-learn]

Jehovah is the premortal Jesus Christ,[@bd-jehovah] so when He quoted the prophets “He was often actually quoting Himself.”[@ensign-2002-saviors-use-ot]
