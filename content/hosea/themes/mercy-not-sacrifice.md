Hosea has one word for what the Lord asks of His covenant people. It is *hesed*, and it stands in six verses of the book, where the King James Version renders it “lovingkindness,” “mercy” or “goodness.”[@oshb-wlc][@lds-scriptures] Its primary meaning is “covenant loyalty,” the proper behavior in a covenant relationship.[@sc-archive-hesed] The Lord promises it in the betrothal, finds it missing in the land, watches it vanish like the dew, says He desired it more than sacrifice, and at last tells His people to sow it and keep it.

The Church teaches the word as the heart of the covenant: President Russell M. Nelson taught that *hesed* “has no adequate English equivalent” and describes a covenant relationship “in which both parties are bound to be loyal and faithful to each other.”[@nelson-everlasting-covenant] The Lord who speaks in Hosea is Jehovah, the premortal Jesus Christ,[@bd-jehovah] and in His mortal ministry He quoted Hosea 6:6 twice to the Pharisees.

## The path

1. [[Hos. 2:19–20]]: the betrothal is made “in lovingkindness, and in mercies… and thou shalt know the LORD.”
2. [[Hos. 4:1]]: the Lord's controversy with the land, “there is no truth, nor mercy, nor knowledge of God.”
3. [[Hos. 6:4]]: the people's return does not last: “your goodness is as a morning cloud.”
4. [[Hos. 6:6]]: what the Lord wanted all along: “I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings.”
5. [[Hos. 10:12]]: mercy is the harvest of righteousness: “Sow to yourselves in righteousness, reap in mercy.”
6. [[Hos. 12:6]]: mercy is something to keep: “keep mercy and judgment, and wait on thy God continually.”
7. [[Matt. 9:13]]: at the table with publicans and sinners: “go ye and learn what that meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice” (see the note on [[Hos. 6:6]]).
8. [[Matt. 12:7]]: in the grain fields on the Sabbath: “if ye had known what this meaneth… ye would not have condemned the guiltless” (see the note on [[Hos. 6:6]]).
