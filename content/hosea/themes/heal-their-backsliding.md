The Hebrew verb *rapha*, “heal,” stands in exactly five verses of Hosea.[@oshb-wlc] Across those verses both the healer and the speaker change: Assyria cannot; the people say the Lord will; the Lord says He would have healed, and that He had; and at the end He says He will.

| Step | Passage | The words |
|---|---|---|
| The wrong physician | [[Hos. 5:13]] | “yet could he not heal you, nor cure you of your wound” |
| The people's words | [[Hos. 6:1]] | “he hath torn, and he will heal us” |
| What the Lord found | [[Hos. 7:1]] | “When I would have healed Israel, then the iniquity of Ephraim was discovered” |
| Healed without knowing it | [[Hos. 11:3]] | “they knew not that I healed them” |
| The promise | [[Hos. 14:4]] | “I will heal their backsliding, I will love them freely” |

## The wound, and where Ephraim took it

“When Ephraim saw his sickness, and Judah saw his wound, then went Ephraim to the Assyrian, and sent to king Jareb: yet could he not heal you” ([[Hos. 5:13]]). The next verse gives the reason, in the Lord's voice: “I, even I, will tear and go away; I will take away, and none shall rescue him” ([[Hos. 5:14]]). He will “return to my place, till they acknowledge their offence, and seek my face” ([[Hos. 5:15]]). The images are harsh, and they sit inside “a familiar message of prophetic warning and future assurance of hope in a God who seeks to gather his people to him.”[@hord-not-fragile] See the notes at [[Hos. 5:13]] and [[Hos. 5:14]].

## “He hath torn, and he will heal us”

Then come the people's words: “Come, and let us return unto the LORD: for he hath torn, and he will heal us; he hath smitten, and he will bind us up” ([[Hos. 6:1]]). “After two days will he revive us” ([[Hos. 6:2]]). These verses have been read as reversing the images of chapter 5: “the lion becomes a healer instead of a tearer.”[@hord-not-fragile] “Revive us” carries the alternative reading “restore us to life; i.e., resurrect us.”[@lds-scriptures] See the notes at [[Hos. 6:1]] and [[Hos. 6:2]].

In 6:4 the Lord complains: “O Ephraim, what shall I do unto thee?… your goodness is as a morning cloud, and as the early dew it goeth away” ([[Hos. 6:4]]). Readers differ on whether 6:4 answers verses 1–3; see the note there. Then: “When I would have healed Israel, then the iniquity of Ephraim was discovered” ([[Hos. 7:1]]; see the note). **Connection made by this page:** set beside the people's “he will heal us” the Lord's own “I will heal” ([[Hos. 14:4]]): the same verb, with 6:4 and 7:1 standing between them.[@lds-scriptures]

## “They knew not that I healed them”

“I taught Ephraim also to go, taking them by their arms; but they knew not that I healed them” ([[Hos. 11:3]]). **Connection made by this page:** set beside [[Hos. 5:13]], Ephraim goes to another for healing without knowing who had been healing him. The Church's footnote on “healed” gives the serpents of the wilderness: “after they were bitten he prepared a way that they might be healed” ([[1 Ne. 17:41]]; [[Alma 33:20]]).[@lds-scriptures] See the note at [[Hos. 11:3]].

In between, the book says three times that there is no return to Him: “they do not return to the LORD their God” ([[Hos. 7:10]]); “They return, but not to the most High” ([[Hos. 7:16]]); “they refused to return” ([[Hos. 11:5]]). The verb is *shuv*, to turn back or return, and the noun *meshuvah*, “backsliding,” comes from the same root with the opposite sense, turned away from God.[@spendlove-turning-whole-heart]

## “Take with you words”

“O Israel, return unto the LORD thy God” ([[Hos. 14:1]]). The prayer given to Israel renounces three helps: “Asshur shall not save us; we will not ride upon horses: neither will we say any more to the work of our hands, Ye are our gods” ([[Hos. 14:3]]). The physician who could not heal is the first one given up: 5:13 and 14:3 are cross-referenced to each other, “heal” to “save.”[@lds-scriptures] See the note at [[Hos. 14:3]].

## “I will heal their backsliding”

“I will heal their backsliding, I will love them freely: for mine anger is turned away from him” ([[Hos. 14:4]]). The object of “heal” is *meshuvah*, “turning back, apostasy,”[@bdb] the noun of “my people are bent to backsliding from me” ([[Hos. 11:7]]), its only other place in Hosea.[@oshb-wlc] This chapter “looks forward to the return of Ephraim… and its healing.”[@bowen-fruit-of-loins] Israel's restoration “shall be due to His love (2:19; 14:4).”[@bd-hosea] See the note at [[Hos. 14:4]].

Set beside it Jeremiah's “Return, ye backsliding children, and I will heal your backslidings” ([[Jer. 3:22]]), which has the same Hebrew verb and noun.[@oshb-wlc] The Church's footnote at Jeremiah, not at Hosea, gives 3 Ne. 9:13, where the risen Savior asks: “will ye not now return unto me, and repent of your sins, and be converted, that I may heal you?”[@lds-scriptures] See the note at [[Jer. 3:22]].

## Through Jesus Christ

The LORD of the Old Testament, Jehovah, is the premortal Jesus Christ.[@bd-jehovah] “There is no saviour beside me” ([[Hos. 13:4]]) is cross-referenced to 2 Ne. 25:20, where the healing of those bitten by serpents stands beside “none other name given under heaven save it be this Jesus Christ.”[@lds-scriptures] The promise of 14:4 has been pointed to as a place for “seeing the future redemption and reconciliation of God’s people to him through Jesus Christ.”[@hord-not-fragile]
