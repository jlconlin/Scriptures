Hosea's daughter is named Lo-ruhamah, “not having obtained mercy,” and his second son Lo-ammi, “not my people.” In chapter 2 the Lord betroths Israel to Himself “in mercies” and reverses both names. The last chapter's prayer returns to the verb of the daughter's name, Hebrew *racham*, “to have mercy.”[@scripture-helps-hosea][@oshb-wlc]

<h2 id="names">Named for a broken covenant</h2>

“Call her name Lo-ruhamah: for I will no more have mercy upon the house of Israel” ([[Hos. 1:6]]). “Call his name Lo-ammi: for ye are not my people, and I will not be your God” ([[Hos. 1:9]]). The names stand for compassion withdrawn and a covenant broken.[@scripture-helps-hosea] “My people” and “your God” are the covenant's own words ([[Ex. 6:7]]; [[Lev. 26:12]]).[@scripture-helps-hosea] Whether the marriage itself was literal is left open;[@scripture-helps-hosea] see [[Hos. 1:2]].

The answer comes in the next verse: “in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living God” ([[Hos. 1:10]]). Then the names lose their “not”: “Say ye unto your brethren, Ammi; and to your sisters, Ruhamah” ([[Hos. 2:1]]). See the note at [[Hos. 1:9]], and on Jezreel [[Hos. 1:4]] and [[Hos. 2:22]].

<h2 id="taken-back">What the Lord takes back</h2>

The wife says, “I will go after my lovers, that give me my bread and my water” ([[Hos. 2:5]]). The Lord answers: “she did not know that I gave her corn, and wine, and oil” ([[Hos. 2:8]]); “Therefore will I return, and take away my corn in the time thereof, and my wine” ([[Hos. 2:9]]); and “I will not have mercy upon her children” ([[Hos. 2:4]]). Then: “I will allure her, and bring her into the wilderness, and speak comfortably unto her” ([[Hos. 2:14]]). See [[Hos. 2:8]] and [[Hos. 2:14]].

<h2 id="betrothal">The betrothal</h2>

“I will betroth thee unto me for ever; yea, I will betroth thee unto me in righteousness, and in judgment, and in lovingkindness, and in mercies. I will even betroth thee unto me in faithfulness: and thou shalt know the LORD” ([[Hos. 2:19–20]]). “Lovingkindness” is *hesed*, covenant love and loyalty;[@scripture-helps-hosea] “mercies” is the plural noun *rachamim*.[@oshb-wlc] On the betrothal and what it is made “in,” see the note at [[Hos. 2:19]].

**Connection made by this page:** set beside one another, the verb *racham* in the daughter's name ([[Hos. 1:6]]), the noun *rachamim* among the five things the betrothal is made “in” ([[Hos. 2:19]]), and the verb again in “I will have mercy upon her” ([[Hos. 2:23]]) share the three letters r-ch-m.[@oshb-wlc]

**Connection made by this page:** set “she did not know” ([[Hos. 2:8]]) beside “thou shalt know the LORD” ([[Hos. 2:20]]): the same verb, *yada*.[@oshb-wlc]

<h2 id="not-yet">“Many days”</h2>

“Go yet, love a woman… according to the love of the LORD toward the children of Israel” ([[Hos. 3:1]]); “Thou shalt abide for me many days” ([[Hos. 3:3]]); “Afterward shall the children of Israel return, and seek the LORD their God, and David their king” ([[Hos. 3:5]]). On “David their king” see the note there and [One Shepherd](/ezekiel/themes/one-shepherd/).

**Connection made by this page:** set beside the promise of [[Hos. 2:19–23]], the later charges: “the spirit of whoredoms hath caused them to err” ([[Hos. 4:12]]); “they have dealt treacherously against the LORD” ([[Hos. 5:7]]); “thou hast gone a whoring from thy God” ([[Hos. 9:1]]). The later charges come after the promise, which is dated “in that day” ([[Hos. 2:18]]). In them the Lord still says “my people” ([[Hos. 4:12]]).

<h2 id="prayer">“In thee the fatherless findeth mercy”</h2>

Israel is given words to say: “Asshur shall not save us… for in thee the fatherless findeth mercy” ([[Hos. 14:2–3]]). “Findeth mercy” is the verb of Lo-ruhamah's name, in the same passive form, “be shewn compassion.”[@oshb-wlc][@bdb] The phrase has been read as a play on that name.[@bowen-fruit-of-loins] The next verse answers: “I will heal their backsliding, I will love them freely” ([[Hos. 14:4]]); [Heal Their Backsliding](/hosea/themes/heal-their-backsliding/) takes it from there.

<h2 id="husband">The husband</h2>

The LORD of the Old Testament, Jehovah, is the premortal Jesus Christ,[@bd-jehovah] and the husband of Hosea 1–2 has been taught as a figure of Jehovah, Jesus Christ.[@ot-manual-10] Paul takes the reversed names of [[Hos. 2:23]] wider:[@lds-scriptures] “I will call them my people, which were not my people; and her beloved, which was not beloved” ([[Rom. 9:25]]), after “not of the Jews only, but also of the Gentiles” ([[Rom. 9:24]]). Those who become “children of Christ” by covenant are read as the sons of the living God ([[Mosiah 5:7]]).[@scripture-helps-hosea]

Later prophets were shaped by Hosea;[@bd-hosea] see [[Jer. 3:14]] and [[Ezek. 16:60]].

**Connection made by this page:** set beside Hosea's “mercies,” “lovingkindness” and “have mercy,” Isaiah has the same Hebrew nouns and verb: “thy Maker is thine husband… with great mercies will I gather thee… with everlasting kindness will I have mercy on thee, saith the LORD thy Redeemer” ([[Isa. 54:5–8]]).[@oshb-wlc] See [Zion and Her Husband](/isaiah/themes/zion-and-her-husband/). Hosea's verse ends: “and they shall say, Thou art my God” ([[Hos. 2:23]]).
