The Lord promises to be with Moses, leads Israel in a cloud, settles on Sinai, and then asks for a sanctuary so that He may dwell among the people. After the calf He says He will not go up in their midst; Moses will not go without Him, the Lord grants it, and the book ends with His glory filling the tabernacle. John and Revelation use the verb of dwelling in a tent for the Word and for God with men.[@strongs-greek] Where the page says it sets one passage beside another, the comparison is its own.

<h2 id="with-thee">With thee</h2>

At the bush: “Certainly I will be with thee” ([[Ex. 3:12]]). On the way out, “the LORD went before them by day in a pillar of a cloud… by night in a pillar of fire,” and “He took not away the pillar” ([[Ex. 13:21–22]]). For the pillar, see the note at [[Ex. 13:21]].

<h2 id="abode">From the mountain to a tent</h2>

“The glory of the LORD abode upon mount Sinai” ([[Ex. 24:16]]). Then: “let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them” ([[Ex. 25:8]]). The Lord states the purpose of bringing Israel out: “I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God… that I may dwell among them” ([[Ex. 29:45–46]]).

“Abode” at 24:16 and “dwell” at 25:8 and 29:45–46 are one Hebrew verb, and in Exodus it occurs in just five verses: those four and [[Ex. 40:35]].[@oshb-wlc] The “tabernacle” the glory fills at 40:34–35 is, in Hebrew, the noun made from it.[@strongs-concordance] See the note at [[Ex. 25:8]].

<h2 id="withdrawn">Not in the midst of thee</h2>

After the calf the Lord still promises the land, but says, “I will not go up in the midst of thee; for thou art a stiffnecked people” ([[Ex. 33:3]]). Moses is told, “My presence shall go with thee, and I will give thee rest” ([[Ex. 33:14]]), and answers, “If thy presence go not with me, carry us not up hence” ([[Ex. 33:15]]). The Lord grants it: “I will do this thing also that thou hast spoken” ([[Ex. 33:17]]). On the mount Moses asks once more, “let my Lord, I pray thee, go among us; for it is a stiffnecked people” ([[Ex. 34:9]]). For the angel and the presence, see the note at [[Ex. 23:20]].

“In the midst of thee” at 33:3 and “among us” at 34:9 are the same Hebrew noun, and “stiffnecked people” is the same phrase in both.[@oshb-wlc] Set beside 33:3, the prayer of 34:9 uses its words: the reason given there for not going is the reason Moses gives here for asking. See the note at [[Ex. 34:9]].

<h2 id="filled">The glory filled the tabernacle</h2>

“A cloud covered the tent of the congregation, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle,” and Moses could not enter “because the cloud abode thereon” ([[Ex. 40:34–35]]). “Abode” is the verb of 24:16 again,[@oshb-wlc] and one study sets the glory filling the tent beside its dwelling on the mount.[@morales-tabernacle] The cloud was upon the tabernacle “throughout all their journeys” ([[Ex. 40:38]]). See [Exodus 40](/exodus/40/).

<h2 id="word">Dwelt among us</h2>

“The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory…)” ([[John 1:14]]). The Greek verb for “dwelt” is “to tent or encamp,” used specially for “to reside (as God did in the Tabernacle of old…).”[@strongs-greek] Revelation says of the one on the throne, “he… shall dwell among them” ([[Rev. 7:15]]), and, “the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them” ([[Rev. 21:3]]). In the city, “the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple of it” ([[Rev. 21:22]]). The Topical Guide lists Ex. 13:21 and 29:45 under the same heading as John 1:14, Rev. 7:15 and Rev. 21:3.[@tg-god-presence-of]

Just as the tabernacle, and the ark within it, represented God’s presence among His people, Jesus Christ was God’s presence among His people.[@cfm-ot-2022-thoughts-19]
