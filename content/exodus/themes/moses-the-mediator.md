At Sinai Israel asks for someone to stand between them and God. Moses does, pleading for them and offering himself. The Lord’s answer to the people’s request is to promise a prophet like him. The New Testament and modern scripture name Jesus Christ as that prophet and as the mediator of the new covenant. Where the page says it sets one passage beside another, the comparison is its own.

<h2 id="between">Someone between</h2>

The Lord tells Moses he will be “instead of God” to Aaron ([[Ex. 4:16]]), and “a god to Pharaoh” ([[Ex. 7:1]]; see the note there). At Sinai the people ask for such a go-between: “they removed, and stood afar off. And they said unto Moses, Speak thou with us… but let not God speak with us, lest we die” ([[Ex. 20:18–19]]). See the note at [[Ex. 20:19]].

<h2 id="prophet">A prophet like unto me</h2>

Moses later recalls the Lord’s answer to that request: “I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee” ([[Deut. 18:18]]). The passage itself ties the promise to the request: “According to all that thou desiredst of the LORD thy God in Horeb… Let me not hear again the voice of the LORD my God” ([[Deut. 18:16]]).

Peter applies the promise to Jesus: “A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me” ([[Acts 3:22]]).[@lds-scriptures] The risen Lord says, “I am he of whom Moses spake” ([[3 Ne. 20:23]]).[@lds-scriptures] God had told Moses, “thou art in the similitude of mine Only Begotten” ([[Moses 1:6]]).[@lds-scriptures]

<h2 id="offer">Blot me out</h2>

After the calf, “Moses besought the LORD his God” ([[Ex. 32:11]]), asking Him to “Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel” ([[Ex. 32:13]]).

The next day he says, “I will go up unto the LORD; peradventure I shall make an atonement for your sin” ([[Ex. 32:30]]), and prays, “if thou wilt forgive their sin—; and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book which thou hast written” ([[Ex. 32:32]]). The Lord’s reply in the text is “Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book” ([[Ex. 32:33]]).[@lds-scriptures] The text does not say the offer was accepted. Moses’s pleading “can remind us of Jesus Christ, who is our advocate with the Father.”[@scripture-helps-ex-19] See [Exodus 32](/exodus/32/).

<h2 id="bears">Who bears</h2>

A priest is “a mediator between his people and God,” while the people, “because of their sins and infirmities, must needs stand afar off.”[@bd-priests] Aaron carries Israel’s names “before the LORD upon his two shoulders” ([[Ex. 28:12]]) and “upon his heart” ([[Ex. 28:29]]), and he is to “bear the iniquity of the holy things” ([[Ex. 28:38]]), also given as “OR atone for.”[@lds-scriptures] The Hebrew verb is the book’s ordinary word for carrying (the staves “to bear the table,” [[Ex. 25:27]]). With sin or iniquity as its object it is also the verb behind “forgive” in Moses’s prayer ([[Ex. 32:32]]) and “bare” in [[Isa. 53:12]].[@oshb-wlc]

Set side by side, these three passages, where the verb has sin or iniquity as its object, say that the priest is to bear the iniquity of Israel’s holy things, that Moses asks the Lord to forgive the people’s sin, and that Isaiah’s servant “bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors.” See the notes at [[Ex. 28:12]] and [[Ex. 28:38]].

<h2 id="mediator">The mediator of the new covenant</h2>

The Joseph Smith Translation of [[Gal. 3:19–20]] names both: Moses “was ordained by the hand of angels to be a mediator of this first covenant,” but “there is one mediator of the new covenant, who is Christ.”[@jst-gal-3] “There is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus” ([[1 Tim. 2:5]]).[@lds-scriptures]

Hebrews sets them side by side: Moses “was faithful in all his house, as a servant,” but “Christ as a son over his own house” ([[Heb. 3:5–6]]). Christ “ever liveth to make intercession” ([[Heb. 7:25]]), and “he is the mediator of the new testament, that by means of death… they which are called might receive the promise of eternal inheritance” ([[Heb. 9:15]]).[@lds-scriptures]

In modern revelation the Advocate pleads “the sufferings and death of him who did no sin… the blood of thy Son which was shed” ([[D&C 45:4]]).[@lds-scriptures] Set beside that verse, Moses pleads the oath to “Abraham, Isaac, and Israel” ([[Ex. 32:13]]) and offers himself, and the Lord answers that the one who sinned will be blotted out ([[Ex. 32:33]]); the Advocate pleads His own blood, already shed.
