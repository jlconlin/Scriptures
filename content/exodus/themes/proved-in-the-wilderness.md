Five times in Exodus one Hebrew verb is used of a test: three times the Lord tests Israel, and twice Israel tests the Lord. In Deuteronomy Moses looks back on the manna and on Massah as tests, and the Savior, tempted in the wilderness, answers from those chapters. John and Paul then say the bread and the rock were about Christ.

## One verb, two directions

The Hebrew verb is *nasah*. It is “proved” at 15:25, “prove” at 16:4 and 20:20, with the Lord as the one who tests, and “tempt” at 17:2 and “tempted” at 17:7, with Israel as the one who tests.[@oshb-wlc] Set in order, the five verses run: the Lord proves Israel at Marah and with the manna, Israel tempts the Lord at Massah, and the Lord comes to prove Israel at Sinai.[@lds-scriptures] “Proved” at 15:25 is cross-referenced to Deut. 8:2, D&C 98:14 and Abr. 3:25.[@lds-scriptures] Psalm 95:9 has both English words, “tempted me, proved me,” but only “tempted” is *nasah*; “proved” there is another verb.[@oshb-wlc] See the notes at [Exodus 15:25](/exodus/15/), [16:4](/exodus/16/) and [17:2](/exodus/17/).

## Marah and the manna

At Marah the Lord shows Moses a tree; cast into the bitter water, it makes the water sweet ([[Ex. 15:25]]). Like the tree, Jesus Christ offers His healing power to take away bitterness.[@scripture-helps-ex-14] Then comes the bread: “Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them” ([[Ex. 16:4]]). By giving it one day at a time, the Lord was “trying to teach faith.”[@scripture-helps-ex-14] The Sabbath portion is taken up on [Sabbath and Tabernacle](/exodus/themes/sabbath-and-tabernacle/).

## Massah

With no water at Rephidim, Moses asks, “wherefore do ye tempt the LORD?” ([[Ex. 17:2]]), and the place is named “because they tempted the LORD, saying, Is the LORD among us, or not?” ([[Ex. 17:7]]). Israel was later told not to repeat it: “Ye shall not tempt the LORD your God, as ye tempted him in Massah” ([[Deut. 6:16]]), where “tempt” means to test God.[@scripture-helps-dt-6] The psalm warns, “Harden not your heart, as in the provocation, and as in the day of temptation in the wilderness” ([[Ps. 95:8]]), and Hebrews takes the warning up ([[Heb. 3:8–9]]).[@lds-scriptures] See [Exodus 17:7](/exodus/17/) and [Psalm 95](/psalms/95/).

## “God is come to prove you”

At Sinai the people ask that God not speak to them; Moses answers, “Fear not: for God is come to prove you, and that his fear may be before your faces, that ye sin not” ([[Ex. 20:20]]). Forty years on, Moses looks back: the Lord led them “to humble thee, and to prove thee, to know what was in thine heart, whether thou wouldest keep his commandments, or no” ([[Deut. 8:2]]). The manna was “that he might make thee know that man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD doth man live” ([[Deut. 8:3]]).[@scripture-helps-ex-14]

## The Savior in the wilderness

Jesus fasted “forty days and forty nights” and the tempter came ([[Matt. 4:1–3]]). He answered each temptation with “It is written”: “Man shall not live by bread alone” ([[Matt. 4:4]]), “Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God” ([[Matt. 4:7]]), and “Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve” ([[Matt. 4:10]]). The three answers come from Deuteronomy 8:3, 6:16 and 6:13.[@ot-manual-dt-1-16] The second answer is the command about Massah.[@scripture-helps-dt-6] Luke’s account of the temptations “may be intended to recall the Exodus.”[@brown-testimony-luke] The forty days are cross-referenced to Ex. 24:18, where Moses is on the mountain.[@lds-scriptures]

## The bread and the rock

The crowd cites the manna: “He gave them bread from heaven to eat.” Jesus answers, “my Father giveth you the true bread from heaven,” and “I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger” ([[John 6:31–32, 35]]). Manna was “a type and symbol” of Him.[@christofferson-daily-bread] Scripture calls Christ both “the rock” and the source of living water.[@scripture-helps-ex-14] Paul says the fathers “did all eat the same spiritual meat” and drank “of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ,” and warns, “Neither let us tempt Christ, as some of them also tempted” ([[1 Cor. 10:3–4, 9]]).[@lds-scriptures]
