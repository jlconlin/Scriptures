Exodus puts the blood of sacrifice in four places, in this order: on a door, on an altar and a people, on the priests, and on the horns of the incense altar. Set side by side, they are four things blood does in the text: the house is passed over, the people are bound to the covenant, the priests are hallowed, and atonement is made once a year. The headings below (protection, belonging, service, access) are this page’s names for them, not the text’s. The blood of the covenant and the yearly atonement are then carried to Jesus Christ.

<h2 id="door">Protection: the door</h2>

“The blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you” ([[Ex. 12:13]]). Moses tells the elders that the LORD “will not suffer the destroyer to come in unto your houses to smite you” ([[Ex. 12:23]]). The household’s obedience “was witnessed by daubing the blood of the lamb on the doorposts, symbolizing God’s protection of the firstborn from death.”[@seely-sacrifice] The passing by “represents Jesus Christ ultimately overcoming death.”[@scripture-helps-ex-7] The notes at [Exodus 12:22](/exodus/12/) and [Exodus 12:23](/exodus/12/) take up the hyssop and the destroyer.

<h2 id="altar-and-people">Belonging: the altar and the people</h2>

At Sinai Moses “took half of the blood… and half of the blood he sprinkled on the altar” ([[Ex. 24:6]]). After the people answered, “All that the LORD hath said will we do, and be obedient” ([[Ex. 24:7]]), he “sprinkled it on the people, and said, Behold the blood of the covenant” ([[Ex. 24:8]]). The blood on the altar “represented the blood of Jesus Christ”; on the people it “symbolized the blessings of the Atonement” and “the seriousness of those covenants,” showing “that both God and the people were mutually bound.”[@scripture-helps-ex-19] See the note at [Exodus 24:8](/exodus/24/).

<h2 id="priests">Service: the priests</h2>

Blood of the ram of consecration is put “upon the tip of the right ear of Aaron… and upon the thumb of their right hand, and upon the great toe of their right foot,” and blood from the altar with the anointing oil is sprinkled on Aaron, his sons and their garments: “and he shall be hallowed” ([[Ex. 29:20–21]]). Leviticus describes the rite carried out ([[Lev. 8:23–24]]).[@lds-scriptures] The splashing of blood and oil onto Aaron “ritually established the relationship between Aaron and God”; unlike the atoning rites, these rites “were not repeated.”[@belnap-skinner-sinai] The note at [Exodus 29:20](/exodus/29/) takes up the ear, thumb and toe.

<h2 id="horns">Access: the horns, once a year</h2>

“Aaron shall make an atonement upon the horns of it once in a year with the blood of the sin offering of atonements” ([[Ex. 30:10]]). Leviticus gives the reason: “it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul” ([[Lev. 17:11]]). On the Day of Atonement the high priest entered the most holy place and “sprinkled blood from the sacrifices onto the mercy seat.”[@scripture-helps-ex-35] The “once” of [[Heb. 9:7]] points back to Ex. 30:10.[@lds-scriptures] See [Exodus 30:10](/exodus/30/).

<h2 id="cup">The cup, and the way in</h2>

At the Last Supper Jesus “took the cup” and said, “this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins” ([[Matt. 26:27–28]]): “the bread and wine, rather than the animals and herbs, would become emblems of the great Lamb’s body and blood.”[@hunter-passover] Jesus “hearkened back to Exodus 24:8—‘behold the blood of the covenant.’”[@seely-sacrifice] Peter’s “sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ” ([[1 Pet. 1:2]]) points back to the same verse.[@lds-scriptures]

Hebrews retells the sprinkling in Moses’s words, “This is the blood of the testament which God hath enjoined unto you” ([[Heb. 9:19–20]]). Its high priest differs from the ancient ones: “Jesus was both our High Priest in making the offering and the very offering itself,” and He made His sacrifice “only once.”[@scripture-helps-nt-heb-7-13] Hebrews then speaks of “boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus… having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience” ([[Heb. 10:19, 22]]).
