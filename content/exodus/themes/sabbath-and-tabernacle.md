The Sabbath appears in Exodus before Sinai, in the Ten Commandments, at the end of the tabernacle’s instructions and at the start of the building. The account of the finished tabernacle then ends in words the creation account ended in. The order is the text’s; setting the passages in a sequence is this page’s own observation.

| Step | Passage | The words |
|---|---|---|
| Kept before Sinai | [[Ex. 16:29–30]] | “the LORD hath given you the sabbath… So the people rested on the seventh day” |
| Grounded in the creation | [[Ex. 20:11]] | “in six days the LORD made heaven and earth… and rested the seventh day” |
| The sign of the covenant | [[Ex. 31:13]], [[Ex. 31:17]] | “a sign between me and you”; “a sign… for ever” |
| First, when the building starts | [[Ex. 35:2]] | “Six days shall work be done, but on the seventh day there shall be to you an holy day” |
| Finished, seen, blessed | [[Ex. 39:32]], [[Ex. 39:43]], [[Ex. 40:33]] | “Thus was all the work… finished”; “and Moses blessed them”; “So Moses finished the work” |
| Lord of the Sabbath | [[Mark 2:27–28]] | “the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath” |

<h2 id="before-sinai">Before Sinai</h2>

On the sixth day the rulers report a double portion of manna, and Moses answers: “To morrow is the rest of the holy sabbath unto the LORD” ([[Ex. 16:23]]). Some go out on the seventh day and find none; “See, for that the LORD hath given you the sabbath… So the people rested on the seventh day” ([[Ex. 16:29–30]]). The noun “sabbath” does not occur in the Bible before Exodus 16.[@pike-sabbath-ot] The Sabbath “was a holy day before the giving of the law.”[@bd-sabbath] See [Exodus 16:23](/exodus/16/).

<h2 id="the-reason-given">The reason given</h2>

“Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy… For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it” ([[Ex. 20:8, 11]]). “Sabbath” means “stopping, cessation, rest (from labor).”[@lds-scriptures] The Lord’s resting “provides a pattern for us to follow.”[@scripture-helps-gen-1-2] The English “rested” translates two different Hebrew verbs: one at 20:11, another at Gen. 2:2–3 and Ex. 31:17.[@oshb-wlc] See [Exodus 20:8](/exodus/20/).

<h2 id="the-sign-that-closes-the-instructions">The sign that closes the instructions</h2>

After Bezaleel and Aholiab are named for the work ([[Ex. 31:1–6]]), the last subject before the tables of stone is the Sabbath: “it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations; that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you” ([[Ex. 31:13]]), “a perpetual covenant” ([[Ex. 31:16]]), and again the creation: “in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed” ([[Ex. 31:17]]). The Sabbath was given as a “sign” of the covenant relationship, with the Lord’s promise to make His covenant people holy.[@scripture-helps-ex-19] See [Exodus 31:13](/exodus/31/) and the theme page [That Ye May Know](/exodus/themes/that-ye-may-know/).

<h2 id="first-when-the-work-begins">First, when the work begins</h2>

Moses gathers the congregation, and his first words are the Sabbath: “Six days shall work be done, but on the seventh day there shall be to you an holy day, a sabbath of rest to the LORD” ([[Ex. 35:2]]), and “Ye shall kindle no fire throughout your habitations upon the sabbath day” ([[Ex. 35:3]]). Only then does he turn to the offering ([[Ex. 35:4–5]]). The Sabbath is the last subject of the instructions (31:12–17) and the first of the building (35:1–3), with the golden calf between.[@lds-scriptures] See [Exodus 35:3](/exodus/35/).

<h2 id="finished-seen-blessed">Finished, seen, blessed</h2>

“Thus was all the work of the tabernacle of the tent of the congregation finished: and the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses” ([[Ex. 39:32]]). “Moses did look upon all the work, and, behold, they had done it as the LORD had commanded… and Moses blessed them” ([[Ex. 39:43]]). The tabernacle is set up “on the first day of the first month” ([[Ex. 40:2]]), and “So Moses finished the work. Then a cloud covered the tent of the congregation, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle” ([[Ex. 40:33–34]]).

Set beside Genesis, the same words recur: God “saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good” ([[Gen. 1:31]]); “the heavens and the earth were finished” ([[Gen. 2:1]]); “God blessed the seventh day” ([[Gen. 2:3]]). In the Hebrew, “finished” comes from the same root (*kalah*) at Gen. 2:1–2, Ex. 39:32 and 40:33. “Work” (*melakhah*) appears at Gen. 2:2–3, Ex. 39:43 and 40:33. “Saw… and behold” and “blessed” are the same words in Gen. 1:31; 2:3 and Ex. 39:43.[@oshb-wlc] The comparison is of wording. For Ex. 40:33 “the Hebrew text strongly parallels the account of how God finished Creation,”[@bradshaw-moses-temple-text] and it appears that “the writers of Exodus deliberately invoked the Creation to describe the building of the tabernacle.”[@belnap-dwell-among] See [Exodus 39:43](/exodus/39/).

<h2 id="lord-of-the-sabbath">Lord of the Sabbath</h2>

“The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath: Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath” ([[Mark 2:27–28]]). The Joseph Smith Translation gives the reason: “For the Son of man made the Sabbath day, therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the Sabbath.”[@lds-scriptures] The Sabbath was “symbolic of the mighty works of God: the creation of the earth, the deliverance of Israel from Egypt, and the resurrection of Jesus from the dead.”[@bd-sabbath] After the Resurrection the Church observed the first day of the week to remember it.[@gs-sabbath-day]

The arc ends with the Sabbath’s Maker. “The Savior identified Himself as Lord of the Sabbath. It is His day!”[@nelson-sabbath]
