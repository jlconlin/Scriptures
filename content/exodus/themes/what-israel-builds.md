Exodus shows Israel making three things: cities for Pharaoh, a calf, and a tabernacle. One Hebrew root, “serve,” stands at the first and the last: Pharaoh forces Israel's service, the Lord claims it, and one noun from that root, *avodah*, names both the bondage and the work of the tabernacle. The calf comes between them, and the root is not used of it. Where the page says it sets one passage beside another, the comparison is its own.

<h2 id="egypt">Service with rigour</h2>

The Egyptians “set over them taskmasters to afflict them with their burdens. And they built for Pharaoh treasure cities, Pithom and Raamses” ([[Ex. 1:11]]). Then: “hard bondage, in mortar, and in brick, and in all manner of service in the field: all their service, wherein they made them serve, was with rigour” ([[Ex. 1:14]]). “Bondage,” “service” and “serve” here are one Hebrew root: the noun appears three times and the verb once.[@oshb-wlc] “Bondage” there carries the footnote “HEB labor (same word).”[@lds-scriptures] See [Exodus 1](/exodus/1/).

<h2 id="serve">That they may serve me</h2>

The Lord's demand uses the same verb: “Let my people go, that they may serve me” ([[Ex. 7:16]]).[@oshb-wlc] The claim rests on the deliverance: “I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt” ([[Ex. 20:2]]). “They are my servants whom I brought forth out of the land of Egypt” ([[Lev. 25:55]]).[@lds-scriptures] What Israel was being taught is that “we have been bought out of bondage, but we are not free from obligation.”[@lane-worship]

<h2 id="calf">The works of their own hands</h2>

When Moses delays, Aaron says, “Break off the golden earrings,” and the people “brake off the golden earrings which were in their ears” ([[Ex. 32:2–3]]); the result is “a molten calf” ([[Ex. 32:4]]). Stephen tells it this way: they “rejoiced in the works of their own hands” ([[Acts 7:41]]); three verses later he comes to “the tabernacle of witness,” made “according to the fashion that he had seen” ([[Acts 7:44]]).[@lds-scriptures] He tells the two in sequence; he does not call it a contrast.

One Hebrew word is translated “earrings” at 32:2–3 and at 35:22, and it is found in Exodus only in those three verses.[@oshb-wlc] Set side by side, the same kind of ornament is given for the calf and for the tabernacle. The text does not say the same gold was used, and draws no comparison itself. For the ornaments put off in between ([[Ex. 33:6]]), see the note at 35:22 in [Exodus 35](/exodus/35/).

<h2 id="willing">The service of the tabernacle</h2>

The offering is asked only of the willing: “whosoever is of a willing heart, let him bring it” ([[Ex. 35:5]]). They come, “every one whom his spirit made willing” ([[Ex. 35:21]]), until it is reported, “The people bring much more than enough for the service of the work.” Then “the people were restrained from bringing” ([[Ex. 36:5–6]]). See [Exodus 36](/exodus/36/).

The noun of 1:14 occurs 23 times in Exodus and names only three things: Egypt's labor (nine times, chapters 1–6), the Passover “service” ([[Ex. 12:25–26]]; [[Ex. 13:5]]), and the tabernacle (eleven times, from [[Ex. 27:19]] to [[Ex. 39:42]]), the “service” of 36:5 among them.[@oshb-wlc] The verb ranges wider: serving Egypt ([[Ex. 14:12]]), other gods ([[Ex. 20:5]]), a master ([[Ex. 21:2]]).[@oshb-wlc] In that verb, temple worship, temple service and temple work “share the same term.”[@lane-worship] Set side by side, the noun's first use is the labor Pharaoh forced ([[Ex. 1:14]]) and its last is the finished tabernacle, where the King James has “work”: “so the children of Israel made all the work” ([[Ex. 39:42]]).

<h2 id="servant">The Servant</h2>

The words for Israel's bondage come from this root, and so does the noun translated “servant” in Isaiah 53 ([[Isa. 53:11]]).[@brown-exodus-pattern] Read through that shared root, Jesus is “the expected servant” who “frees all those who will follow him from bondage.”[@brown-exodus-pattern]

Alma's people, in bondage, are told, “I will also ease the burdens which are put upon your shoulders” ([[Mosiah 24:14]]). The Church's notes in that chapter point to [[Ex. 3:12]] and to [[Matt. 11:28–30]], and the note at [[Ex. 1:11]] points back to Mosiah 24.[@lds-scriptures] Then the Savior: “Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you… For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light” ([[Matt. 11:28–30]]).

Set beside Exodus, the Savior's saying shares two words with its King James English, though Exodus is Hebrew and Matthew Greek: the “burdens” of [[Ex. 1:11]], and the “rest” of “My presence shall go with thee, and I will give thee rest” ([[Ex. 33:14]]). “Yoke” does not occur in Exodus; it is at [[Lev. 26:13]], “I have broken the bands of your yoke.”[@lds-scriptures] The Hebrew word for “burdens” in 1:11 is not the word for “service.”[@oshb-wlc] Apart from the notes named, setting Matt. 11 beside Exodus is the page's own.

Pharaoh forced Israel's service; the calf was, in Stephen's words, the work of their own hands; and the tabernacle was made from gifts brought willingly for the Lord's service.
