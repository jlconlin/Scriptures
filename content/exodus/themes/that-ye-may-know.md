Pharaoh says, “I know not the LORD.” From there to the end of Exodus, the LORD says who is to know Him and how. After a first promise to Israel (6:7), the verses turn to Egypt, to know by plague and sea, and then back to Israel, to know by rescue, bread and at last His dwelling among them. Last, Moses asks to know Him for himself, and the LORD answers that He knows Moses by name. In Hebrew the verb is one, *yada‘*, throughout.[@oshb-wlc]

## “Who is the LORD?”

Pharaoh asks, “Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice to let Israel go?” ([[Ex. 5:2]]). The LORD is Jehovah, who is the premortal Jesus Christ.[@bd-jehovah][@gs-jehovah] Before Egypt hears anything, Israel is promised: “ye shall know that I am the LORD your God, which bringeth you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians” ([[Ex. 6:7]]). Then, of Egypt: “the Egyptians shall know that I am the LORD, when I stretch forth mine hand upon Egypt” ([[Ex. 7:5]]), a promise that Pharaoh’s “I know not” will be met.[@ot-minute-ex-1] See [Exodus 5](/exodus/5/) and [Exodus 7](/exodus/7/).

## Egypt, by plague and by the sea

Egypt is to know at the river (“In this thou shalt know that I am the LORD,” [[Ex. 7:17]]), the frogs ([[Ex. 8:10]]), the flies ([[Ex. 8:22]]) and the hail ([[Ex. 9:14]], [[Ex. 9:29]]). It is to know at the firstborn (“that ye may know how that the LORD doth put a difference,” [[Ex. 11:7]]) and at the sea ([[Ex. 14:4]], [[Ex. 14:18]]). With [[Ex. 7:5]] that makes nine verses, and [[Ex. 7:5]] is cross-referenced to [[Ex. 14:4]].[@lds-scriptures] The plagues’ stated purposes are that Pharaoh and the Egyptians “shall know that I am the Lord” and that “there is none like me in all the earth.”[@scripture-helps-ex-7] They “demonstrate the Lord’s superior status as the true God.”[@sc-knowhy-631]

After [[Ex. 14:18]] no verse has Egypt as the one to know.[@oshb-wlc] The narration does not say that Pharaoh came to know. His recorded words are “I have sinned this time: the LORD is righteous” ([[Ex. 9:27]]), and the Egyptians at the sea say, “the LORD fighteth for them” ([[Ex. 14:25]]). See [Exodus 8](/exodus/8/), [Exodus 9](/exodus/9/) and [Exodus 14](/exodus/14/).

## Israel, by rescue and bread

The knowing turns to Israel: “ye shall know that the LORD hath brought you out from the land of Egypt” ([[Ex. 16:6]]), and, of the quail and the bread, “ye shall know that I am the LORD your God” ([[Ex. 16:12]]). The purpose of the signs, “that ye may know how that I am the LORD” ([[Ex. 10:2]]), “was also passed on to the children of Israel.”[@ot-minute-ex-7] At the sea Israel “believed the LORD” ([[Ex. 14:31]]); that verse has no “know.” See [Exodus 10](/exodus/10/) and [Exodus 16](/exodus/16/).

## “Now I know”

Jethro hears what the LORD did, and says, “Now I know that the LORD is greater than all gods” ([[Ex. 18:11]]). It is the only verse in which someone says he has come to know the LORD; Pharaoh’s “I know not” ([[Ex. 5:2]]) is the same verb form with a negative.[@oshb-wlc] Jethro’s words are “exactly the point that God made to the Israelites during the plagues.”[@ot-minute-ex-18] After them no verse has a foreigner as the one to know.[@oshb-wlc] See [Exodus 18](/exodus/18/).

## By His dwelling, and by His sign

“I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God. And they shall know that I am the LORD their God, that brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them” ([[Ex. 29:45–46]]). The first verse is cross-referenced to [[Ex. 6:7]].[@lds-scriptures] The words “they shall know that I am the LORD,” said three times of the Egyptians ([[Ex. 7:5]]; [[Ex. 14:4]], [[Ex. 14:18]]), are said once of Israel, here.[@oshb-wlc] The dwelling itself is on [the tabernacle page](/exodus/themes/that-i-may-dwell-among-them/).

The Sabbath is a “sign… that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you” ([[Ex. 31:13]]), a sign of the covenant relationship.[@scripture-helps-ex-19]

## “That I may know thee”

Moses says, “shew me now thy way, that I may know thee” ([[Ex. 33:13]]). The LORD answers, “I know thee by name” ([[Ex. 33:17]]). On “know” in [[Ex. 33:12]] the Church’s edition points to [[John 10:14]]: “I… know my sheep, and am known of mine.”[@lds-scriptures]

Set beside Moses’s “that I may know thee” are the Savior’s words: “this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent” ([[John 17:3]]).[@lds-scriptures] Eternal life is “to live forever as families in God’s presence,”[@gs-eternal-life] and “without God, there would be no mortal or eternal life.”[@hales-seeking-know]
