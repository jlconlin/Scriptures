# Exodus: theme candidates

Arcs that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.** Proposed by the coordinator on 2026-10-12, after all forty chapters were written, from the chapters and the reviewers' leads (those are working notes in `.cache/batch/exodus/theme-leads.md` and are not filed here). The test: an arc across several chapters that a reader would not see chapter by chapter and that changes how a passage is understood, above all how it points to Jesus Christ. A word or image that only recurs is not a candidate.

These are development briefs checked against the text and the finished chapters, not research dossiers. Whoever writes one tests it through the four steps of §8 first; research may change an arc or show there is none.

### New selection: What Israel builds: Pharaoh's cities, the calf, and the Lord's dwelling

Merged by the author on 2026-10-12 from two selections, “From forced labor to willing service” and “The calf and the tabernacle: two uses of gold,” which shared their closing passages. The title is the coordinator's working title.

- **Proposed passages:** 1:11–14; 5:6–9; 7:16; 12:35–36; 25:1–8; 32:1–5; 33:4–6; 35:20–29; 36:4–7; 40:34–38.
- **Proposed arc:** three things Israel makes. Bricks and cities for Pharaoh, under compulsion. A calf, on their own impulse and from their own gold, as an image of the one who brought them out. A dwelling for the Lord, commanded and made to His pattern, yet given so willingly that Moses has to stop the offerings. “Let my people go, that they may serve me” joins the first and the last; the gold and the earrings join the second and the last.
- **Proposed connection to Christ:** see the leads below (the Savior's yoke and rest; Stephen's comparison in Acts 7; the image God gives). The connections first proposed, Galatians 5 and Hebrews 8–9, are kept as alternatives for research to weigh.
- **Boundaries for later development:** a comparison with Paul or with Matthew is the page's own unless a source read makes it; do not equate willing offerings with every obligation under the law; do not claim the same pieces of gold were used for the calf and the sanctuary, or assign a symbolic meaning to gold itself.

## Leads toward Christ for the selected themes

Recorded at the author's word on 2026-10-12 (“Yes”), from a conversation about how each selected theme reaches Jesus Christ. **These are leads, not findings: every one is from memory and none has been read on a page.** Whoever develops a theme tests them in research (`STANDARDS.md` §8) and drops what does not hold. Recording them authorizes no development.

**Sinai: what was offered, and what Christ opens again**
- `[[Heb. 12:18–24]]`: not come to the mount that burned with fire, but to mount Sion and “to Jesus the mediator of the new covenant.” The direct answer.
- The offer of 19:6 made again in Christ: `[[1 Pet. 2:9]]`; `[[Rev. 1:6]]`.
- The veil: `[[Matt. 27:51]]`; `[[Heb. 10:19–20]]`.
- The Transfiguration as Sinai again (mountain, cloud, shining face, Moses, “hear ye him”): `[[Matt. 17:1–5]]`.
- `[[D&C 84:19–24]]`; `[[D&C 107:18–19]]`.

**“That I may dwell among them”**
- `[[John 1:14]]`: the tent and the glory together.
- “I will be with thee” (3:12) to Emmanuel (`[[Matt. 1:23]]`) and “I am with you alway” (`[[Matt. 28:20]]`).
- His body the temple: `[[John 2:19–21]]`.
- `[[Rev. 21:3, 22]]`.
- Moses's plea at 33:15 beside the sacrament's promise “that they may always have his Spirit to be with them” (`[[Moro. 4:3]]`): the page's own comparison.

**Moses the mediator, and the greater Mediator**
- `[[3 Ne. 20:23]]` on `[[Deut. 18:15]]`: “I am he of whom Moses spake.”
- The offer refused and the offer accepted: 32:32–33 beside `[[Isa. 53:12]]` and `[[D&C 45:3–5]]`. This contrast could be the page.
- Servant in the house, Son over it: `[[Heb. 3:1–6]]`.
- `[[Luke 9:31]]`: Moses and Elijah speak of His “decease”; the Greek word is believed to be *exodos* (needs a lexicon page).
- The names borne on the priest's shoulders and heart (28:12, 29) beside `[[Isa. 49:16]]`.

**Remembering: “when your children ask”**
- `[[Luke 22:19]]`, at a Passover table; `[[1 Cor. 5:7]]`.
- The sacrament prayers: `[[Moro. 4:3]]`.
- God remembers first (2:24); Zacharias says of the Savior's coming, “to remember his holy covenant” (`[[Luke 1:72]]`).
- Remembering in both directions: `[[Isa. 49:15–16]]`.

**What Israel builds: Pharaoh's cities, the calf, and the Lord's dwelling** (one page; the leads fall under its two halves)

*Whom Israel serves:*
- `[[Matt. 11:28–30]]`: labour, burden, yoke and rest, beside 1:11, 6:6 and 33:14. The best fit for the page's destination.
- `[[Luke 1:74]]`: “that we being delivered out of the hand of our enemies might serve him without fear.”
- `[[John 8:31–36]]`; `[[Rom. 6:16–22]]`; `[[Lev. 25:55]]`.
- The deliverer who serves, and gives willingly: `[[Philip. 2:7]]`; `[[Matt. 20:28]]`; `[[John 10:17–18]]`.
- Mosiah 24 as an Exodus retold (burdens eased, then deliverance).
- **To test first:** whether one Hebrew root stands behind Israel's “bondage,” “that they may serve me,” and the “service” of the tabernacle (`node scripts/occurrences.mjs exodus --hebrew <strongs>`). If it does, the arc rests on the text's own word.

*What Israel makes in order to see God:*
- `[[Acts 7:40–44]]`: Stephen sets the calf and the tabernacle side by side, “the works of their own hands” against the pattern Moses was shown. **To test first:** if this holds, the comparison is scripture's and not only the page's.
- The image men make and the image God gives: `[[Col. 1:15]]`; `[[John 1:14]]`.
- “Not made with hands”: `[[Heb. 9:11]]`.
- What gold cannot do: `[[1 Pet. 1:18–19]]`.

*For the outline:* one page under 1,000 words cannot carry all of this. The three things made are the spine; choose one destination in Christ (the yoke and rest, or the image God gives) and let the other be a sentence.

**Across the five pages** (the coordinator's observations, for the outlines):
- **Give each page a different witness,** or Hebrews will carry three of them: Hebrews 12 to Sinai, John and Revelation to the dwelling, 3 Nephi and Isaiah to the mediator, Luke 22 to remembering, Matthew 11 and Acts 7 to what Israel builds.
- **Three pages pass through the golden calf.** Each owns one thing: Sinai, the law and priesthood lost; the dwelling, the presence nearly withdrawn; the building page, the offerings and the image.
- **All five have one shape** (something partial, refused or veiled in Exodus, completed in Christ). The mediator page and the building page can break it, turning on a refusal and on a contrast.

## Proposed

The original nine candidates, in the order of the book. The selection above supersedes the coordinator's original ranking at the end.

### 1. What was offered at Sinai, and what was given

- **Passages:** 19:5–6, 10–11; 20:18–21; 24:9–11; 32:19; 33:1–3, 20; 34:1–2 (with the Joseph Smith Translation), 29–35; then `[[D&C 84:19–27]]`; `[[Ps. 95:8–11]]`; Hebrews 3–4; `[[Jacob 1:7]]`; `[[Alma 12:36–37]]`.
- **Arc:** A whole people is invited up to be a kingdom of priests and to see God. They stand afar off and ask for a go-between; seventy elders see Him and eat; forty days later there is a calf, the tables are broken, and the second tables come “not according to the first.” What remains is real but lesser: a veil, a priesthood for one family, and a law that points ahead.
- **Christ:** Hebrews 3–4 and 10:19–22 say the rest and the entrance refused at Sinai are opened by Him; D&C 84 says what the higher priesthood is for.
- **Why it earns a page:** it is the Latter-day Saint reading of the book, and the chapters each hold one piece of it (the brief split it across four notes on purpose).
- **Must not claim:** more about what was on the first tables than the JST and the Church's sources say; that the law of Moses was only a punishment (the Bible Dictionary calls it “a good law”).

### 2. “That I may dwell among them”: the presence lost and kept

- **Passages:** 3:12; 13:21–22; 15:17; 24:15–18; 25:8, 22; 29:42–46; 32:34; 33:3, 12–17; 34:9; 40:34–38; then `[[John 1:14]]`; `[[Rev. 21:3]]`.
- **Arc:** “I will be with thee” at the bush becomes a pillar that leads, a glory on the mountain, and a command to build a tent so that He can live in the camp. The calf puts all of it in doubt: the Lord says He will send an angel and not go Himself. Moses refuses to move without Him, and the book ends with the glory filling the tent. The book's last third is the answer to the question chapter 33 raises.
- **Christ:** John 1:14 uses the tabernacle's word of the Word made flesh; Revelation 21:3 ends where Exodus 29:45 points.
- **Must not claim:** that John's verb proves more than a lexicon read says; that the angel of 23:20 and the angel of 33:2 are the same or different beyond what the chapters' sources say.

### 3. The firstborn

- **Passages:** 1:16, 22; 4:22–23; 11:4–7; 12:12–13, 29; 13:1–2, 11–16; 22:29; 34:19–20; then Numbers 3:11–13; `[[Hosea 11:1]]`; `[[Matt. 2:15]]`; `[[Luke 2:22–23]]`; `[[Col. 1:15, 18]]`; `[[D&C 93:21]]`.
- **Arc:** Pharaoh kills Israel's sons. The Lord calls Israel “my son, even my firstborn” and says what refusing will cost; the last plague falls on Egypt's firstborn and passes over the houses under the lamb's blood; from then on every firstborn of Israel belongs to the Lord and must be redeemed. The Levites later stand in their place.
- **Christ:** the Firstborn who is presented at the temple under this very law and is not redeemed with a lamb but is the Lamb.
- **Must not claim:** that 12:29 is retribution for 1:22 (no source read for the chapters joins them: it would be the page's own comparison and must be worded as one); see the Church's appendix on violence before writing.

### 4. Moses the mediator

- **Passages:** 4:15–16; 7:1; 8:12, 30; 9:33; 20:18–21; 24:1–2; 28:12, 29–30, 38; 32:11–14, 30–32; 33:11–17; 34:8–9, 29–35; then `[[Deut. 18:15–19]]`; `[[Acts 3:22]]`; `[[3 Ne. 20:23]]`; `[[Moses 1:6]]`; `[[D&C 45:3–5]]`; Hebrews 3:1–6; 7:25.
- **Arc:** Moses is first God's mouth to Pharaoh and prays for Egypt; at Sinai the people ask him to stand between them and the voice; after the calf he stands between them and destruction and offers himself. Beside him the book sets the high priest, who “bears” Israel's names and its iniquity before the Lord. Two mediators, one who pleads and one who carries.
- **Christ:** “a prophet … like unto me”; the Advocate; the high priest of Hebrews. The Lord tells Moses himself that he is “in the similitude of mine Only Begotten.”
- **Must not claim:** that Moses's offer in 32:32 was accepted or that it atoned; the comparison is a likeness, as *Scripture Helps* words it.

### 5. The blood that marks a people

- **Passages:** 12:7, 13, 22–23; 24:6–8; 29:20–21; 30:10; then `[[Matt. 26:28]]`; `[[Heb. 9:18–22]]`; `[[Heb. 10:19–22]]`; `[[1 Pet. 1:2, 18–19]]`.
- **Arc:** Blood on the door marks the houses death passes over. Blood on the altar and on the people binds them to the covenant they have just accepted. Blood on the ear, thumb and toe of the priests sets them apart to serve; blood on the horns of the incense altar once a year makes atonement. From protection, to belonging, to service, to access.
- **Christ:** at the Last Supper He takes Moses's words at 24:8 (“the blood of the covenant”) for His own.
- **Must not claim:** a meaning for ear, thumb and toe that no source read gives.

### 6. Bread from heaven and water from the rock: being proved and proving God

- **Passages:** 15:22–26; 16:4, 15–31; 17:1–7; 20:20; then `[[Deut. 8:2–3, 16]]`; `[[Deut. 6:16]]`; `[[Ps. 78:17–25]]`; `[[Matt. 4:1–7]]`; `[[John 6:31–51]]`; `[[1 Cor. 10:1–6]]`; `[[1 Ne. 17:28–31]]`.
- **Arc:** In three stops the Lord “proves” Israel with want, and Israel in turn “tempts” the Lord. Deuteronomy reads the hunger as teaching. The Savior, forty days in the wilderness, is offered the same three tests and answers each from Deuteronomy's account of these chapters; then He says the bread and the rock were about Him.
- **Why it earns a page:** the temptation narrative is seldom read as the Exodus wilderness retraced, and the chapters cannot say it one at a time.
- **Must not claim:** a one-to-one match of the three temptations with three episodes beyond what Matthew's own quotations show.

### 7. “That ye may know that I am the LORD”

- **Passages:** 5:2; 6:7; 7:5, 17; 8:10, 22; 9:14, 16, 29; 10:2; 14:4, 18, 31; 16:6, 12; 18:11; 29:46; then `[[Rom. 9:17]]`; `[[John 17:3]]`.
- **Arc:** Pharaoh asks who the Lord is that he should obey Him. The plagues answer, first for Egypt, then “in all the earth”; at the sea Israel believes; Jethro, a Midianite, says “Now I know”; and the last use moves the knowing from judgment to dwelling: “they shall know that I am the LORD their God, that brought them forth … that I may dwell among them.”
- **Christ:** the LORD who makes Himself known is Jehovah; eternal life as knowing God.
- **Risk:** closest to a recurring phrase. It is a page only if the change in who knows and how (by plague, by rescue, by presence) holds up when every occurrence is listed.

### 8. The Sabbath and the making of the tabernacle

- **Passages:** 16:22–30; 20:8–11; 23:12; 31:1–5, 12–17; 35:1–3; 39:32, 43; 40:2, 17, 33–34; `[[Gen. 1:31–2:3]]`.
- **Arc:** The Sabbath is kept before it is commanded, grounded in the creation, made the sign of the covenant at the end of the tabernacle's instructions, and set first when the building starts. The making then ends as the creation ended: the work is finished, Moses sees it, and blesses it, on a new year's day.
- **Christ:** the weakest link of the nine. Hebrews 4:9–10 and the Savior as Lord of the Sabbath (`[[Mark 2:27–28]]`) are where research would start.
- **Must not claim:** the creation parallel as the text's stated meaning: one study (not a Latter-day Saint one) carries it, and the reviewers found no source applying it to 39:32, 43.

### 9. Remembering: “when your children ask”

- **Passages:** 2:24; 3:15; 10:2; 12:14, 24–27; 13:3, 8–10, 14–16; 16:32–34; 17:14; 20:2, 8; 28:12, 29; then `[[Luke 22:19]]`; `[[1 Cor. 11:24–26]]`; `[[Moro. 4:3]]`.
- **Arc:** God remembers His covenant, and then builds remembering into Israel: a feast with a script for the children's question, a jar of manna, a memorial written in a book, a commandment that opens with what He did, stones on the priest's shoulders “for a memorial.” The deliverance is meant to be handed down, not only experienced.
- **Christ:** at a Passover table He gives a new memorial, “this do in remembrance of me.”
- **Risk:** leans toward application; it stays on the site only as what the text and the sacrament prayers say.
