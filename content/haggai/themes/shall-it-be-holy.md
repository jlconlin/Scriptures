Haggai's priests rule that holiness does not spread by touch and uncleanness does, so, Haggai may be saying, the people's altar and land had not made them holy. In the book's order, the Lord's “I am with you” comes before the work, and His blessing before the harvest. Set beside the Gospels, the priests' “No” marks what a holy thing cannot do.

<h2 id="ruling">What their own things could not do</h2>

Holy flesh carried in a garment's skirt does not make bread holy: “And the priests answered and said, No” ([[Hag. 2:12]]). One unclean by a dead body does make what he touches unclean ([[Hag. 2:13]]). Haggai applies it: “So is this people… and so is every work of their hands; and that which they offer there is unclean” ([[Hag. 2:14]]). Haggai may be saying that the altar's sacrifices “could not transmit holiness to them.”[@huntington-consider-ways]

“Every work of their hands” (2:14) and “all the labours of your hands” (2:17) are the same Hebrew phrase; 1:11's “labour of the hands” is a different one.[@oshb-wlc] Set beside each other, the works called unclean in 2:14 are the works smitten in 2:17.

## Before a stone was laid

The Lord asks first for the house: “I will take pleasure in it” ([[Hag. 1:8]]). The people “did fear before the LORD” ([[Hag. 1:12]]), and His whole reply was “I am with you” ([[Hag. 1:13]]). Then “the LORD stirred up the spirit” of governor, priest and remnant, “and they came and did work” ([[Hag. 1:14]]). They were stirred “and they obeyed”;[@breitenstein-haggai-minute] the Lord gave “a willingness to carry out the work.”[@keil-haggai]

## His Spirit standing, His glory coming

When the house looked “as nothing” ([[Hag. 2:3]]), the answer was again His presence: “work: for I am with you… my spirit remaineth among you” ([[Hag. 2:4–5]]). “Remaineth” means “standing.”[@oshb-wlc] Then: “I will fill this house with glory” ([[Hag. 2:7]]), for “Jehovah Himself would personally visit the temple and fill it with His glory.”[@huntington-consider-ways]

## From this day

“The uncleanness was the reason the land was so unproductive… but when the Jews had repented and begun the work on the temple (see v. 18), the curse was to be lifted and the Lord promised His blessing (see v. 19).”[@ot-manual-30] The promise came with no seed in the barn: “from this day will I bless you” ([[Hag. 2:19]]). The blessings “perhaps would not come immediately”; “The people would have to prove themselves.”[@breitenstein-haggai-minute]

<h2 id="christ">The Holy One who touches</h2>

Hebrews sets the rite of Numbers 19 beside the Savior: “the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean, sanctifieth to the purifying of the flesh: how much more shall the blood of Christ… purge your conscience” ([[Heb. 9:13–14]]). The rules of clean and unclean were “fulfilled by the Atonement of Jesus Christ.”[@bd-clean-unclean]

Set beside the priests' answers, the Gospels show the same two kinds of contact with the opposite result. A man unclean by the dead made unclean whatever he touched; He touched the dead and the dead rose. Holy flesh in a garment's corner could not make bread holy; a woman's touch on the corner of His garment made her clean. The priests' rule is not undone; what is shown is who He is.

- He “put forth his hand, and touched” the leper, “immediately the leprosy departed” ([[Mark 1:41–42]]), and lepers “were considered to spread their uncleanness to anyone who came in contact with them.”[@nt-student-manual-11]
- He “touched the bier,” and “he that was dead sat up” ([[Luke 7:14–15]]). “His act of touching the dead brings ritual uncleanness upon Him”; yet “the stain of ritual uncleanness does not adhere to Him as it does to others.”[@brown-testimony-luke]
- A woman “touched the border of his garment: and immediately her issue of blood stanched” ([[Luke 8:44]]). The hem was “a tassel at each ‘wing’ or corner”;[@bd-hem-of-garment] the “borders” of [[Num. 15:38]] and the “skirt” of Haggai 2:12 translate the same Hebrew word.[@oshb-wlc] She “is made clean… by a touch of the holy.”[@brown-testimony-luke]

In Haggai the people fear the Lord; He is with them, stirs them to build, promises to fill the house, and blesses; in the Gospels the unclean are made clean by contact with Him. Moroni gives the condition first: “if ye shall deny yourselves of all ungodliness… then is his grace sufficient for you” ([[Moro. 10:32]]); “sanctified in Christ by the grace of God, through the shedding of the blood of Christ… that ye become holy, without spot” ([[Moro. 10:33]]).

## Where this reaches now

A holy place does not make someone holy: “attending the temple does not sanctify someone who is unworthy.”[@ot-seminary-resource-haggai] The Lord's latter-day house keeps the order of clean first, then His presence: “do not suffer any unclean thing to come into it… my glory shall rest upon it; yea, and my presence shall be there” ([[D&C 97:15–16]]). The Lord magnifies the efforts of those who consider their ways: “He will magnify our efforts as we consider our ways and make the proper adjustments to make and keep covenants with Him.”[@schmeil-consider-ways]
