# Haggai: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.** The candidates for the author are Fable's and Codex's lists (`.cache/batch/haggai/reports/themes-fable.md` and `themes-codex.md`), shown to the author numbered; only the author's choice is recorded here.

## Author's selection (2026-10-23)

**One page is chosen** (author, 2026-10-23: “Yes. let's do #1”): **what could not pass upward comes down**, Fable's first proposal. The priests' two rulings (2:11–14) say that holiness does not pass by touch and uncleanness does, so the altar and the land could not make an unclean people holy; the book's answer, given from the Lord's side before the question is asked, is His acceptance (1:8), His presence (“I am with you,” 1:13; 2:4), His Spirit stirring and standing among them (1:14; 2:5), His glory filling the house (2:7–9), and His blessing dated from the day they turned, before any harvest (2:19). To Christ: the Gospels show the priests' rule reversed in Him, the leper touched and made clean, the bier touched and the dead raised, the garment's border touched and the woman healed; the comparisons are the page's own and are worded as comparisons.

The other numbered proposals (2, “From this day,” Fable's alternative frame; 3, “I am with you”; 4, “The glory of this latter house”; 5, “Chosen thee,” the signet, which belongs with Zechariah's Branch) were not chosen. Of 3, the author asked whether a page retelling the book's story would make it easier to recognize; the answer given was that the book page's intro, the division map and the chapters' threads carry the book's story, and a theme page is for an arc a reader would not see reading straight through.
