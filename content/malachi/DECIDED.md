# Malachi: decided while writing

What reviewers and the coordinator settled chapter by chapter, by the standard and the book's brief, without the author. Kept out of `BRIEF.md` so the brief stays brief (author, 2026-10-11: “We don't have to record every decision of every verse”). Nothing is added here any more. Search it for a chapter when that chapter is edited; nobody needs to read it through.

Settled by the reviewers on 2026-10-04, by the standard and this brief, without the author; any of them can be overturned.

**Chapter 1**

- 1:8, Witness of Christ note "The unblemished offering and the Lamb": cut. It repeated Scripture Helps, its endnote scriptures and footnote 10a, and its only addition was the writer's own inference; its sourced content is merged into the closing Christ section.
- 1:10 "for nought": the note now gives the King James sense first (the priests do nothing unpaid, with the Student Manual's sentence), then the lexicon's listing of this verse under "in vain" and the Updated NRSV's wish. The first version followed only the outside reading and was silent on the manual. The plain-words section follows the King James.
- 1:11 present or future: the note now gives the King James and the Church's chapter heading ("will be great among the Gentiles") as the reading, with the other translations as the variation. The paragraph that only listed the Church's footnotes was cut, the kind changed from Related Scriptures to Language, and the "spiritual surrender" reading of the pure offering was cut because it comes from a sectarian aside in Keil.
- 1:6 and 1:14, fear and dreadful: the shared Hebrew root stays as an observation on the text, said once in the thread and once in the note on verse 6. The claim that verse 14 "answers" verse 6 was cut as the chapter's own reading. The note is retitled "Fear in verse 6, dreadful in verse 14" and now gives the lexicon's "reverence" for the word.
- 1:3 hated Esau: the Scripture Central sentence stays, without announcing the source and worded to what its footnote says (the "love" of these verses can be read as alliance language). Deuteronomy 21 stays as the same Hebrew word, with a pointer to the note on Gen. 29:31. Ezekiel 25:12 is now tied to the Church's footnote on "waste" (Ezek. 25:13). The bare Romans 9:13 line moved to the setting.
- 1:8 governor: "one commentary takes the allusion to be to the Persian governor" was not what the page says. The note now says the governor is not identified and "might also apply to Nehemiah", and that Malachi's words may be about gifts and not the governor's levy. The setting says only that the people had a governor (verse 8).
- 1:9: the challenge reading is kept as the better one, with a sentence that the verse has also been read as an earnest call to repentance.
- 1:7 polluted bread: "the priests are accused of the very thing their law warned them against" was cut. Leviticus 21:6 and Malachi 1:12 are set side by side with the shared verb, and the table as altar now rests on the Church's footnote to Ezekiel 41:22 as well as Keil.
- 1:14: Leviticus 22:18-19 requires a male for a vow paid as a burnt offering, not for every vow, and the note now says so. The opening "the problem is not poverty" was replaced by what the verse says.
- 1:2 the Wherein question: "six replies of the same shape" became six that begin with the same word, with the two differently worded replies (2:14, 3:13) mentioned. The second paragraph, which silently rested on Keil, was trimmed to a pointer.
- 1:1 name: "nothing in the book's message depends on it" was cut. The note says the title is a question, not a finding, and that Malachi is treated as the prophet's name, as the Church's study helps describe him.
- 1:1 burden: "from there it came to mean" was reworded, because the lexicon lists the "oracle" word as a separate entry and does not derive it from "load".
- 1:13 torn: the closing paragraph on "weariness" was cut (no source; it also called the priests' words the people's).
- Setting and thread: the unsourced "a tired house" sentence now rests on Scripture Helps and verses 8 and 13; "what the priests will not give, others do" and "the priests owe the most" were cut; the love-as-ground-of-the-charge sentence now quotes Scripture Helps.
- Section paraphrases: "jackals" and "shattered" now have ledger rows; verse 5 is "from the border" as the King James has it; verse 9 is a command, not "they ask God".
- Closing sections: D&C 59:8 was dropped from the Christ section (no source joins it to this chapter; it stays as a pointer under Explore), and the Explore line on Leviticus 21 was corrected.
- Ledger: 16 stale Keil rows under notes that no longer cite him were removed, along with the jussive rows for 1:10; rows were added for every new quotation.

**Chapter 2**

- Setting: the 430/432 B.C. date argument was removed and replaced by a pointer to Mal. 1:1, since the brief says the date is argued once, in chapter 1. `when` keeps the Bible Dictionary's date and now has its own ledger rows.
- Setting and notes 2:8, 2:11: the link to Ezra and Nehemiah now rests on Scripture Helps (Malachi "may have been a contemporary of Ezra and Nehemiah") instead of an uncited Keil cross-reference.
- Thread: "neighbor's with neighbor" as the second covenant contradicted note 2:10; it now names the three covenants in the verses' own words (Levi, the fathers, the wife).
- 2:17, thread and christ: the claim that Mal. 3:1-5 answers "Where is the God of judgment?" had no source. Keil on 3:1 says it outright and is now cited with rows. No Church or BYU source read makes the link.
- 2:2: the reading "blessings the priests pronounced" was Keil's, uncited; now cited. The lexicon's other reading ("I will curse your prosperity") is added beside it, and the note is retitled "The blessings that are cursed." The chapter's own closing sentence on the covenant curses was cut.
- 2:3: "either way the punishment reaches the priests through their work" was cut as the chapter's own. The festival-offerings gloss and the link to the blemished offerings of 1:8 now have Keil and NIV rows.
- 2:4: Keil was replaced by the Bible Dictionary ("Levites") and the NIV's cross-references. Keil's alternative reading (the punishment decree replaces the covenant) was cut: it was one commentator against every version cited, and "the verse does not settle it" was the writer's own verdict.
- 2:10: the Student Manual's "We are all children of the same God" now leads, unannounced; Keil's argument against Adam and Abraham follows as support.
- 2:11: the Bible Dictionary's "marriage outside the covenant" and the manual's literal reading lead; the manual's figurative reading follows as "also read." "Two readings are open... the verse does not choose" was cut, following the brief.
- 2:12: the manual's reading leads; the Hebrew difficulty and the versions follow. "Witness" for the proposed Hebrew word now has a lexicon row.
- 2:13: the Jacob 2:31-35 paragraph was cut as a connection no source makes (the Church's footnotes pair only Jacob 2:30 with Mal. 2:15). Keil was dropped here because the manual gives the same reading of the wives' tears.
- 2:14: the transliteration was corrected to chabereth, as the lexicon gives it. "Marriage is a covenant here" now rests on the lexicon's "alliance of marriage." The Bible Dictionary line is used for what it says (wives within the covenant), not as proof that marriage is a covenant.
- 2:15: "the ending is clear in all of them" was narrowed to the versions made from the Hebrew, since the Septuagint has "a seed" and "forsake not." The advice to the reader became a statement of certainty.
- 2:16: retitled "The Lord hates putting away" and reordered so the King James reading leads and the Septuagint, NIV and ESV are reported as differences, per the brief. "The readings agree it is called violence" was cut as false for the Septuagint; the ESV note is now "marks as probable," not "follows the Septuagint."
- 2:16: the Student Manual's "the Lord ... hateth putting away" was not added, because it would be a fifth manual note of 13; the King James text carries the Church's reading.
- The Gospel Topics "solemn covenants" quotation was in both 2:14 and 2:16; it stays in 2:14 only.
- Explore: the fourth item repeated note 2:17; it now points to the complaint's return at Mal. 3:14, which is the Church's own footnote link from 2:17.
- Left as written: "this chapter is not among them" (3 Ne. 24-25), a sound inference from the Bible Dictionary; the thread's overlap with the chapter heading (it adds the Hebrew counts); note 2:3's opening "rebuke," which the note goes beyond; the NRSVUE "he hates" wording in 2:16, which is accurate.
- Ledger rows added for `when`, section 1 plain ("rebuke") and section 5 plain ("obscure").

**Chapter 3**

- 3:16 book of remembrance (A1): the quotation "echoed the language of Malachi" was given to Moses 6; the source says it of the 1832 revelation, so the note now quotes D&C 85:9 and adds the source's statement that the Prophet's "book of life" (D&C 128:7) corresponds to Malachi's book.
- 3:3 offering (A2, C2): Keil's "moral character of the offerers" was the writer's inference toward the D&C 128 reading; replaced with Joseph Smith's own words from the Joseph Smith Papers page (he applies Mal. 3:3-4 to sacrifice and ties "in righteousness" to priesthood authority). The Scripture Helps line on animal sacrifice from 1:7-8 was cut as belonging to another verse.
- 3:1 messenger (A9, D1): cut Keil's identification of the messenger with the Elijah of 4:5 (he means John the Baptist, against the Church's reading of 4:5) and the Luke 1:17 aside; the Isaiah 40:3 link now rests on Mark 1:2-3, and Keil is out of this note.
- 3:1 explore: "The forerunner and Elijah" reworded so it does not imply the two are one figure; the Levi and book-of-remembrance bullets were cut as repeats of the notes.
- Hebrew word repetitions no source comments on (3:2 soap and Jer. 2:22; 3:9-11 and Deut. 28:20, 38; 3:10/3:15 prove/tempt; 3:12/3:15 blessed/happy; 3:13/3:16 spoke; "serve" in 3:14-18; "delight" in 2:17/3:1): kept as observations of the text with lexicon rows, following Jeremiah's convention that shared words in the texts stay; every interpretation built on them was cut ("the Lord is the soap," "the curse reversed," "the people use the Lord's own word against Him").
- Cut as connections no source makes: Deut. 28:12 beside the windows of heaven (3:10), Zech. 1:3 beside "Return unto me" (3:7), Moro. 8:18 and the verse-7 contrast (3:6), Mal. 2:14 as a courtroom image (3:5), D&C 110:5 beside the refining of verse 3 (christ), and the 1:10/3:12 occurrences of "delight" (a word-chain).
- 3:5 charges (B2, D5): the pairings with the law are Keil's, so Keil rows were added; the unsourced courtroom paragraph was replaced by his reading of "swift" as answering the complaint of delay in 2:17, labelled one reading.
- 3:6 and 3:14 (A8): Keil's readings are now labelled "one reading" rather than stated as the verse's meaning; no Church, BYU, or Scripture Central source was found for 3:6 (Student Manual, Scripture Helps, and the Van Dyke-Ogden article checked), so Keil stays as that note's only commentary.
- 3:11 devourer: the Student Manual's "may refer to Satan as well" was cut; the brief follows Scripture Helps (pests), and the newest source does not repeat it. The chapter no longer cites ot-manual-34. A lexicon row now supports "the eater" (A6). Note retitled "The devourer and the curse."
- 3:10 storehouse (A4): "That is the setting" removed; the Nehemiah 13 link is now carried by the Bible chronology row, and "food for those who served" by Scripture Helps on the tithe supporting priests and Levites. Keil was not added here, to keep him at six notes.
- 3:10 windows (A5): "same Hebrew phrase" corrected to the same Hebrew word; Elder Andersen and Elder Bednar are now named as the speakers of their quotations.
- 3:17 jewels (A7): "always of people of Israel" limited to the lexicon's first sense; the Scripture Helps sentence restored to its own "suggests ... one purpose."
- 3:18: the claim that the verse answers the complaints now rests on Keil ("will learn that it is always profitable to serve God"), as one reading; the KnoWhy sentence was cut here and in 3:5 because the first note on 3:1 already says it.
- 3:2 refiner (A3): "a Church article draws the lesson" removed; the Ensign sentence is kept as the Church magazine's reading of the image, not as advice to the reader.
- Announced sources reworded at 3:1, 3:3, 3:8, and explore; "the Bible Dictionary dates ... Scripture Helps calls the timing uncertain" in when and setting is the brief's own form and stays.
- C1 left as it is: Keil's denial that the covenant messenger mediates a new covenant is not cited in the chapter, and the lexicon's "advent of the Lord for judgment" does not conflict with Scripture Helps. Millet's "restorer" is now worded as one way to read the title, as he words it.
- C3 left as it is: Schade and Bowen's gloss of segullah as "sealed possession" is not used; the lexicon, Scripture Helps, and Elder Nelson agree on "valued property, treasure."
- 3:3 "Who the sons of Levi are" kept as a separate note though it starts from Scripture Helps: it goes further by quoting D&C 84:31-34 on who becomes the sons of Moses and of Aaron, which the footnote only points to. "Just given" corrected (the rebuke is in 1:6-2:9) and "widens the picture" removed.

**Chapter 4**

- when and setting: the date is now attributed ("The Bible Dictionary dates the prophecy to about 430 B.C.; the exact timing is uncertain"), as the brief prescribes; a row added for Moroni's visit in 1823.

- 4:1 "Oven and stubble": kept but rebuilt so it goes beyond Scripture Helps: why an oven (Keil), Scripture Helps paraphrased once, D&C 133:64, and Moroni's wording of verse 1. Cut "Nephi puts them in his own words" (1 Ne. 22:15; Nephi is the earlier writer) and "the sense is the same in both" (finding 3).
- 4:1 "Roots and branches": cut the "stock, family" gloss from Isa. 11:1 and the writer's "nothing from which a family could grow again"; the lexicon lists this verse under "of person" only. Scripture Helps is paraphrased, with Christofferson and Cook quoted from their own pages (finding 4).
- 4:2 "The Sun of righteousness": "Nephi reads Malachi's image" replaced with the KnoWhy's own statement that the two "may have been independently drawing upon a common earlier tradition"; cut "Malachi's hearers may therefore have known the picture"; the Isa. 30:26 and D&C 88:7 links now carry rows from the KnoWhy that makes them. - 4:2 "Calves let out of the stall": cut the paragraph that only repeated footnotes 2a and 2c (1 Ne. 22:24; "calves in the stall") and the clause "which fits a sun that has risen with healing" (finding 6).
- 4:4 note retitled "The book's last instruction": cut Elijah at Horeb (KnoWhy #858 never mentions Malachi), the Transfiguration and Kirtland "pairing" (which also confused Elias with Elijah), and the 3 Nephi 15 paragraph; all were the chapter's own connections. Replaced with Keil's point that the command answers the rebuke of 3:7 and looks to the ban of verse 6; the KnoWhy #591 paragraph now quotes it directly and drops "hinge" and "one account" (finding 7). The explore item on Horeb and sc-knowhy-858 are removed.
- 4:5 "Moroni's wording": opening rewritten ("each shows something the others do not" was the writer's); D&C 110:14 is described as keeping Malachi's "sent", not as a third form of verse 5; the KnoWhy's suggestion is quoted and still labelled an inference (finding 8).
- 4:5 note retitled "Why Elijah was the one sent": it now leads with Joseph Smith's "Why send Elijah?" and trims what repeated Scripture Helps. The quotation is cited to Teachings of Presidents of the Church: Joseph Smith, chapter 26, not through Elder Christofferson's talk (finding 9; STANDARDS section 4 rule 6).
- 4:5 "Elijah, Elias, and John the Baptist": the KnoWhy phrase "himself a type of Elijah" replaced with John 1:21 ("Art thou Elias?... I am not"), which is the Church's footnote and the direct answer; a Bible Dictionary row added for Elias as the Greek form of Elijah. Keil's reading that John alone fulfils the verse stays out (finding 10).
- 4:5 "What great and dreadful means": "often taken" changed to "Many Latter-day Saints take", as the article says; "the verb of you that fear" changed to "forms of one Hebrew word" because the Hebrew text tags verse 2's word as an adjective; explore line no longer claims the article covers "every use of the phrase" (finding 11).
- 4:6 "Turn, plant, or bind": cut the closing synthesis ("each wording stresses a different side of one work") and the repeated "bringing back between the generations"; Joseph Smith's two statements are given in order with no comment on whether he was translating, as the brief directs (finding 12).
- 4:6 "Who are the fathers?": cut the third paragraph; Elder Christofferson's words do not "join the two" readings and are what Scripture Helps prints beside the verse (finding 13).
- 4:6 "A curse that is a ban": cut the Masora paragraph (synagogue practice, reception history from a Keil aside) and the 2019 Christofferson quotation that Scripture Helps prints; kept his 2023 statement, which joins the curse to verse 1 and is not in Scripture Helps (finding 14).
- christ: "The sealing power also depends on Him" replaced with what Elder Christofferson said; "He said of the law of verse 4" changed to "Of the law given to Moses, which verse 4 tells Israel to remember, He said" (finding 15).
- Lexicon named in running text ("BDB is unsure", "BDB glosses") reworded so the statements stand on their own; check-content now reports no announced source.
