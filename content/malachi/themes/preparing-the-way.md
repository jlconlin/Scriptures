Malachi promises two sendings. In chapter 3 the Lord says, “Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me” ([[Mal. 3:1]]). At the end of the book He says, “Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD” ([[Mal. 4:5]]). In English both sentences begin the same way, and each sends someone ahead of a coming of the Lord.

Scripture says who was sent, and when. The Savior said the first messenger was John the Baptist. Elijah came himself, to the Kirtland Temple on April 3, 1836, after the Lord had appeared there [@lds-scriptures]. Joseph Smith put the two sendings in order: the forerunner first, Elijah after, and the Messiah last [@rsc-words-js-1844-03-10]. Both sendings go before a coming of Jesus Christ: Elijah's errand at Kirtland “was in preparation for the coming of the Lord” [@bd-elijah].

## “My messenger”

“Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me” ([[Mal. 3:1]]).

The messenger is not the priest, although Malachi has just called the priest “the messenger of the LORD of hosts” ([[Mal. 2:7]]). The words “behold I send” point to someone the Lord has yet to send, and it was because the priests had not done their duty as God's “ordinary ambassadors” that He “was about to send an extraordinary messenger” [@keil-malachi]. The priest's calling is in the note “The priest as the Lord's messenger” at [Malachi 2:7](/malachi/2/).

Who the messenger was, the Savior said Himself. Speaking of John the Baptist, He told the crowds, “For this is he, of whom it is written, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face, which shall prepare thy way before thee” ([[Matt. 11:10]]). Nephi had seen the same man in vision: “I also beheld the prophet who should prepare the way before him. And the Lamb of God went forth and was baptized of him” ([[1 Ne. 11:27]]). In the Joseph Smith Translation, Jesus quotes Malachi's words as Malachi wrote them: “Elias has come already, concerning whom it is written, Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me,” and then, “this is Elias, whom I send to prepare the way before me” (JST Matt. 17:11, 13) [@lds-scriptures]. Matthew and the Joseph Smith Translation apply Malachi's own words to John, and Nephi's vision shows the same man preparing the way.

The words rest on Isaiah's voice in the wilderness, “Prepare ye the way of the LORD” ([[Isa. 40:3]]) [@keil-malachi]; see [The New Exodus and the Highway](/isaiah/themes/new-exodus/#prepare-the-way). What a latter-day prophet taught about a forerunner in our own dispensation is in the note “The messenger who prepares the way” at [Malachi 3:1](/malachi/3/).

## The Lord who comes to His temple

The verse goes on: “and the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, whom ye delight in: behold, he shall come, saith the LORD of hosts” ([[Mal. 3:1]]).

The one the messenger goes before is the Lord Himself. “The Messenger of the covenant” is among the names and titles of Jesus Christ in the Old Testament [@bd-christ-names], and He is not “my messenger” at the start of the verse but a figure “mentioned afterwards” [@keil-malachi]. What the Hebrew has where the King James reads “even” is in the note “The Lord who comes to His temple” at [Malachi 3:1](/malachi/3/).

The revelations of the Restoration take Malachi's words into the Lord's own voice. On December 9, 1830, He said, “I am Jesus Christ, the Son of God; wherefore, gird up your loins and I will suddenly come to my temple” ([[D&C 36:8]]) [@lds-scriptures]. On November 3, 1831, a revelation spoke of “the Lord who shall suddenly come to his temple; the Lord who shall come down upon the world with a curse to judgment” ([[D&C 133:2]]) [@lds-scriptures]. On April 3, 1836, He appeared to Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery in the Kirtland Temple and said, “For behold, I have accepted this house, and my name shall be here” ([[D&C 110:7]]) [@lds-scriptures]. Given after the first advent, these revelations speak of the coming to the temple as still ahead, “I will suddenly come,” and the second joins it to His coming in judgment. A reading that takes the prophecy as fulfilled once differs, and finds it in the first advent: “so did the Lord come to His temple in the appearing of Jesus Christ” [@keil-malachi].

What He comes to do is on two other pages: the answer to “Where is the God of judgment?” and the question who may abide His coming ([“Where Is the God of Judgment?”](/malachi/themes/god-of-judgment/)), and the refining of the sons of Levi ([From Polluted Offerings to an Offering in Righteousness](/malachi/themes/purified-offering/)).

## Elijah, before the day

The book ends with the second sending: “Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD: And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse” ([[Mal. 4:5–6]]). Moroni gave Joseph Smith these verses in other words: “I will reveal unto you the Priesthood, by the hand of Elijah the prophet” ([[JS—H 1:38]]; [[D&C 2:1]]). The note “Moroni's wording” at [Malachi 4:5](/malachi/4/) has the rest.

John stands close to this promise too. Before his birth the angel told his father that he would “go before him in the spirit and power of Elias, to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children” ([[Luke 1:17]]), the words of Malachi 4:6. Yet when priests and Levites asked John, “Art thou Elias?”, he answered, “I am not” ([[John 1:21]]). The Joseph Smith Translation gives his answer in full: “I am not that Elias who was to restore all things” (JST John 1:22), and the chapter's heading in that translation names Jesus Christ as “the Elias who restores all things” [@lds-scriptures]. Coming down from the mountain where He was transfigured, Jesus spoke in the same double way: “Elias truly shall first come, and restore all things,” and “Elias is come already, and they knew him not” ([[Matt. 17:11–12]]). The Joseph Smith Translation separates the two: the Elias who has come already is John, and the disciples understood that Jesus spoke “also of another who should come and restore all things” (JST Matt. 17:14) [@lds-scriptures].

The promise of Elijah, then, was not used up in John. John “most certainly served as an Elias—in partial, but not complete, fulfillment of Malachi's prophecy,” and the prophecy has “a double sense”: one applicable “to the Baptist, and which has been fulfilled in him,” and the other “to Elijah himself, and which remains yet to be fulfilled” [@alford-elijah]. The note “Elijah, Elias, and John the Baptist” at [Malachi 4:5](/malachi/4/) sets out the names.

## A preparer and a restorer

Restoration sources speak of two Eliases, and name them John and Jesus. “Anciently two Eliases were spoken of, one as a preparer and the other a restorer. John was sent to prepare the way for Jesus, Jesus Himself being the Restorer” [@bd-elias]. In the Joseph Smith Translation of Matthew 17, Jesus “teaches of two Eliases—one to prepare and another to restore” [@lds-scriptures]. A limit stands beside these statements: “the fragmentary information in our current Bibles is not sufficient to give an adequate understanding of what was involved in use of the term” [@bd-elias].

In a sermon of March 10, 1844, as a hearer wrote it down, Joseph Smith said that “the spirit of Elias is first Elijah second, & Masiah last. Elias is a fore runner to prepare the way, & the spirit & power of Elijah is to come after,” and that when all is ready, “then Mesiah comes to his Temple which is last of all” [@rsc-words-js-1844-03-10]. In that order the forerunner and Elijah are two, and both go before the Messiah.

Read without the latter-day revelations, Malachi can be taken another way. The messenger of 3:1 is then “the same who is called the prophet Elijah in Malachi 4:5,” a prophet who “can only be a prophet with the spirit or power of Elijah the Tishbite,” and who “was sent in the person of John the Baptist” [@keil-malachi]. On that reading a fulfilment still to come has “no tenable foundation” in the prophecy [@keil-malachi]. Latter-day scripture keeps John and Elijah apart, and it records Elijah's coming in person.

## At Kirtland

In the Kirtland Temple the Lord appeared first: “We saw the Lord standing upon the breastwork of the pulpit, before us” ([[D&C 110:2]]) [@lds-scriptures]. “Following the appearance of Jesus Christ, Moses, Elias, and Elijah also came” [@gong-hosanna-hallelujah]. Elijah “stood before us, and said: Behold, the time has fully come, which was spoken of by the mouth of Malachi—testifying that he [Elijah] should be sent, before the great and dreadful day of the Lord come” ([[D&C 110:13–14]]). He committed keys to Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery ([[D&C 110:16]]), and “all of this was in preparation for the coming of the Lord, as spoken of in Mal. 4:5–6” [@bd-elijah]. What those keys do, and why Elijah held them, is in the notes “Why Elijah was the one sent” at [Malachi 4:5](/malachi/4/) and “‘Turn,’ ‘plant,’ or ‘bind’” at [Malachi 4:6](/malachi/4/).

The Lord uses Malachi's words of more than a man. He says of the gospel covenant itself, “even so I have sent mine everlasting covenant into the world … to be a messenger before my face to prepare the way before me” ([[D&C 45:9]]). And Elijah's last words at Kirtland tie the keys he committed to the day Malachi named: “Therefore, the keys of this dispensation are committed into your hands; and by this ye may know that the great and dreadful day of the Lord is near, even at the doors” ([[D&C 110:16]]).

Those keys are in use now. President Russell M. Nelson taught that the keys given at Kirtland authorize the President of the Church “to place a ratifying seal on priesthood ordinances and covenants, and to seal families eternally” [@nelson-priesthood-keys-2024]. So what Elijah was sent to restore reaches families now, whenever they are sealed by that authority.
