Malachi’s people say two things about the Lord that the book does not let stand. At the end of chapter 2 they ask, “Where is the God of judgment?” ([[Mal. 2:17]]). In chapter 3 they say, “It is vain to serve God” ([[Mal. 3:14]]). The Lord’s reply to both is that He will come: suddenly, to His temple and to judgment, and on a day that burns the proud and heals those who fear His name. The reply runs across three chapters, from 2:17 to 4:2, and so is gathered here.

The Book of Mormon names the one who rises on that day the Son of Righteousness. The answer to the people’s question is Jesus Christ, and the question left standing is the one He turns back on those who asked: “who may abide the day of his coming?” ([[Mal. 3:2]]).

## The question

The first complaint closes chapter 2. “Ye have wearied the LORD with your words. Yet ye say, Wherein have we wearied him? When ye say, Every one that doeth evil is good in the sight of the LORD, and he delighteth in them; or, Where is the God of judgment?” ([[Mal. 2:17]]). It is one of the book’s “Wherein” exchanges, listed in the note at [Malachi 1:2](/malachi/1/).

On one reading, what the people want is judgment on someone else: by those who do evil “the murmurers mean, not notorious sinners in their midst, but the heathen who enjoyed undisturbed prosperity,” and the question amounts to “why does not God punish the ungodly heathen?”[@keil-malachi]

## “The Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come”

The answer comes in the next verse. “Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me: and the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, whom ye delight in: behold, he shall come, saith the LORD of hosts” ([[Mal. 3:1]]). The people asked where He was; this is “the Lord, whom ye seek.”

“To the question, Where is or remains the God of judgment? the Lord Himself replies that He will suddenly come to His temple.”[@keil-malachi] The messenger of the covenant is among the Old Testament names of Jesus Christ.[@bd-christ-names] Who He is, and who prepares His way, is the subject of [Sent Before the Day](/malachi/themes/preparing-the-way/).

Then the answer turns on the askers. “But who may abide the day of his coming? and who shall stand when he appeareth? for he is like a refiner’s fire, and like fullers’ soap” ([[Mal. 3:2]]). The people asked for judgment and are asked in return whether they can bear it. On the reading that took the evildoers of 2:17 to be the heathen, this is where the judgment changes direction: “With the coming of the Lord the judgment will also begin; not the judgment upon the heathen, however, for which the ungodly nation was longing, but the judgment upon the godless members of the covenant nation.”[@keil-malachi] The refiner of verses 2–4 is the subject of [From Polluted Offerings to an Offering in Righteousness](/malachi/themes/purified-offering/).

Verse 5 takes up the word of the people’s question. “And I will come near to you to judgment; and I will be a swift witness against the sorcerers, and against the adulterers, and against false swearers… and fear not me, saith the LORD of hosts” ([[Mal. 3:5]]). The God of judgment comes near “to you.” This judgment “is threatened against those who wanted the judgment of God to come, according to Malachi 2:17,” and “swift” answers their murmuring: “the Lord will come with unexpected rapidity, in contrast with the murmuring of the people at the delay of judgment.”[@keil-malachi]

## “It is vain to serve God”

The complaint comes back later in chapter 3. “Ye have said, It is vain to serve God: and what profit is it that we have kept his ordinance, and that we have walked mournfully before the LORD of hosts? And now we call the proud happy; yea, they that work wickedness are set up; yea, they that tempt God are even delivered” ([[Mal. 3:14–15]]). It is the grievance of 2:17 in new words, and it was not everyone’s: the feelings of 2:17 and 3:13 “were not cherished by the whole nation without exception, but only by the great mass of the people.”[@keil-malachi]

The Lord’s reply follows. “Then they that feared the LORD spake often one to another: and the LORD hearkened, and heard it, and a book of remembrance was written before him for them that feared the LORD, and that thought upon his name. And they shall be mine, saith the LORD of hosts, in that day when I make up my jewels; and I will spare them, as a man spareth his own son that serveth him. Then shall ye return, and discern between the righteous and the wicked, between him that serveth God and him that serveth him not” ([[Mal. 3:16–18]]).[@lds-scriptures]

The people called service vain while they saw the proud happy and the wicked delivered. The Lord answers that He has heard those who fear Him, that a record of them is written (see the note at [Malachi 3:16](/malachi/3/)), and that a day is coming when the difference between the one who serves and the one who does not will be seen: He shows them “that their murmuring against Him is unjust, and that the coming day of judgment will bring to light the distinction between the wicked and those who fear God.”[@keil-malachi]

## The day

Chapter 4 names the day. “For, behold, the day cometh, that shall burn as an oven; and all the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly, shall be stubble: and the day that cometh shall burn them up, saith the LORD of hosts, that it shall leave them neither root nor branch” ([[Mal. 4:1]]).[@lds-scriptures]

The words of the complaint come back. The people had called “the proud happy” and said “they that work wickedness are set up”; now “all the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly, shall be stubble.” The two expressions “point back to Malachi 3:15. Those who are called blessed by the murmuring nation will be consumed by the fire, as stubble is burned up, and indeed all who do wickedness, and therefore the murmurers themselves.”[@keil-malachi] The note at [Malachi 4:1](/malachi/4/) gives the Hebrew. In latter-day scripture the day is still ahead: a revelation quotes the verse as “that which was written by the prophet Malachi” ([[D&C 133:64]]), and another says, “tomorrow all the proud and they that do wickedly shall be as stubble” ([[D&C 64:24]]).[@lds-scriptures]

The next verse is for the others. “But unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness arise with healing in his wings; and ye shall go forth, and grow up as calves of the stall” ([[Mal. 4:2]]).[@lds-scriptures] “You that fear my name” are the ones of 3:16, “them that feared the LORD.” The day that burns the proud heals them.

The Book of Mormon names who rises. When the Savior gave the Nephites this verse, it read, “unto you that fear my name, shall the Son of Righteousness arise with healing in his wings” ([[3 Ne. 25:2]]).[@lds-scriptures] Nephi had used the title of the Savior’s coming to his people: “the Son of Righteousness shall appear unto them; and he shall heal them” ([[2 Ne. 26:9]]).[@lds-scriptures] Malachi 4:2 stands among the Old Testament names of Christ, with “the Lord our Righteousness.”[@bd-christ-names] Read in its own context alone, the verse has been taken otherwise: the early Christian writers “understand Christ,” but “the context does not sustain the personal view, but simply the idea that righteousness itself is regarded as a sun.”[@keil-malachi] The winged sun is explained in the note at [Malachi 4:2](/malachi/4/).

So the God of judgment the people asked for is the one who rises with healing for those who fear His name, and the Book of Mormon names Him the Son of Righteousness, Jesus Christ.

## Who may abide

He spoke these chapters again. The risen Lord “commanded them that they should write the words which the Father had given unto Malachi, which he should tell unto them. And it came to pass that after they were written he expounded them” ([[3 Ne. 24:1]]).[@lds-scriptures] Malachi 3–4, given that way as 3 Nephi 24–25, “provides answers to this question,” the question of who may abide the day.[@sc-knowhy-218]

The question can be heard two ways. On one reading it expects the answer “no one endures it,” for “the prophet is speaking to the ungodly.”[@keil-malachi] On another, verses 2–3 are a promise: “The Lord here promises faithful Israelites, including the sons of Levi, that they ‘may abide the day of his coming’ if they successfully endure refining and purifying.”[@pike-great-dreadful-day] Latter-day scripture uses the same words to say who does abide. “He that is not purified shall not abide the day” ([[D&C 38:8]]). Those who “have taken the Holy Spirit for their guide, and have not been deceived… shall abide the day” ([[D&C 45:57]]).[@lds-scriptures] The revelation that quotes Malachi 3:2–3 in [D&C 128:24](/malachi/themes/purified-offering/) answers it with an offering in righteousness.

“Today, as well as in the days of Malachi, a book of remembrance is written before the Lord for those that fear him and think upon his name.”[@romney-trust-1979] The command for the time before the day is “stand ye in holy places, and be not moved, until the day of the Lord come” ([[D&C 87:8]]). And the promise of Malachi 3:17 is given again to the Saints: “Yet I will own them, and they shall be mine in that day when I shall come to make up my jewels” ([[D&C 101:3]]).[@lds-scriptures]
