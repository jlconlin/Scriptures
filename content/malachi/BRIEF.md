# Malachi brief

Book-level decisions for Malachi. The site-wide standard is in `STANDARDS.md`; this file records what is particular to Malachi. Writers, checkers, and reviewers read both before starting a chapter. Genesis's brief (`content/genesis/BRIEF.md`) is the model for the form, and the author's rules recorded there carry over to every book.

**Status (2026-10-04):** drafted by a Sonnet research agent from the sources named below, each read in the session with the research scripts, and approved by the coordinator the same day. The author has not read it and chose the book as a test of the new arrangement. The book is in preview (`status: preview` in `book.yaml`). Where a section says “Proposed,” that is the approach the chapters follow; it is a decision of the coordinator's, not a fact from a source, and the author can overturn it.

Related files: `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` at the top of the repository (what waits for the author, for every book). There is no `CHURCH-STATEMENTS.md` for this book; the Church's statements on the contested questions are quoted below, with their pages.

## What the author has decided

- **Malachi is the next book, as a short test of the new way of working** (AUTHORING.md, “Plan for the next book”): a Sonnet writer, then the ledger script, then a Sonnet checker, then an Opus reviewer. The author gave no focus questions for this book, so writers work from the questions a careful reader would ask (`STANDARDS.md` §3).
- **Don't replicate the Gospel Library** (author, 2026-10-03; `STANDARDS.md` §1, and Genesis's brief). The footnotes, the chapter headings, and *Scripture Helps* are beside the verse already. A note that only says what *Scripture Helps* says on the verse is not worth writing; it goes further or is left out.
- **Newest trumps oldest** (author, 2026-10-03; Genesis's brief, “What the author has decided”). Where the Church's sources differ, a chapter follows the newest and does not repeat the older wording. For Malachi the newest is *Scripture Helps: Old Testament* (2025); the Student Manual chapter is from 1982 and the Bible Dictionary is “not intended as an official statement of Church doctrine” (Genesis's brief).
- **Follow *Scripture Helps* to what it cites** (author, 2026-10-03; `STANDARDS.md` §9, last bullet). Its Malachi chapter has 68 endnotes; “Sources for Malachi” below says which of them can be read online.
- **Decide what the standard decides** (author, 2026-10-03: “Do not save for me what you can decide on your own”; `STANDARDS.md` §4 rule 14). Writers and reviewers settle by the standard and this brief; `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` is for what is truly the author's.
- **The Student Manual limits apply from the first chapter** (`STANDARDS.md` §1): about a third of a chapter's notes at most, and a note that cites it also rests on something from beyond the Church's own publications.
- **Chapters follow the new structure and checks** in `STANDARDS.md` §5–§11 (ledger, `check-ledger`, `check-content`).

## Divisions

The Bible Dictionary (“Malachi”) divides the book in two: “The first part (Mal. 1:1–2:9) is addressed to the priesthood… The second part (2:10–4:6) is addressed to the people.” That split falls inside chapter 2, so it cannot be used as whole chapters. The divisions in `book.yaml` follow the Church's chapter headings instead. Proposed: keep them; they are only the map on the book page.

| Chapters | Division (key) | Church chapter heading |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | The Lord's Love and the Priests' Failure (`priests`) | 1: “The Jews despise the Lord by offering polluted bread upon the altar and by sacrificing animals with blemishes—The Lord's name will be great among the Gentiles.” 2: “The priests are reproved for not keeping their covenants and not teaching the people—The Jews are condemned for dealing treacherously with one another and with their wives.” |
| 3 | The Messenger, the Tithe, and the Book of Remembrance (`messenger`) | “The Lord's messenger will prepare the way for the Second Coming—The Lord will sit in judgment—The people of Israel are commanded to pay tithes and offerings—They keep a book of remembrance.” |
| 4 | The Day That Burns and the Return of Elijah (`elijah`) | “At the Second Coming, the proud and wicked will be burned as stubble—Elijah will return before that great and dreadful day.” |

The keys are not used by Isaiah, Jeremiah, or Genesis. The colors are new ones, chosen by eye (`#4a4fa8`, `#b83a7a`, `#c9a227`). The Guide to the Scriptures names four themes (“the sins of Israel,” “the judgments to come,” “the promises for obedience,” “prophecies pertaining to Israel”); they cut across chapters and are not used as divisions. The Student Manual's sections (34-2 to 34-17) run in verse order and match the chapters.

The chapter titles in `book.yaml` are descriptive and were checked against the headings above.

## Book-wide conventions

- **Who Malachi was and when, said once, in chapter 1's setting, from the Church's sources.** *Scripture Helps* (2025): “Malachi was a prophet who preached to the Jews in Jerusalem after they returned from Babylonian exile. The exact timing of Malachi's ministry is uncertain, but his book is generally dated between 500 and 350 BC.” The Bible Dictionary: “The prophecy of Malachi was spoken about 430 B.C.” The Bible Chronology (“Chronology of the Old Testament”) lists, at 432, “Nehemiah's second mission to Jerusalem. Prophecy of Malachi.” Proposed: give no year of the writer's own; if a year is wanted, say “the Bible Dictionary dates the prophecy to about 430 B.C.” and say *Scripture Helps* calls the timing uncertain. Later chapters don't argue the date.
- **The Hebrew chapter numbering.** The Hebrew text of Malachi (the Westminster Leningrad Codex) has three chapters. Chapters 1 and 2 agree with the King James Version's; Hebrew chapter 3 has 24 verses, and its verses 19–24 are the King James Version's 4:1–6 (checked with `scripts/hebrew.mjs malachi 3:19`, `3:23`, `3:24`). A note on chapter 4 that cites the Hebrew says so once: “Hebrew 3:23.” Two tools do not follow this: `hebrew.mjs malachi 4:5` stops with “Mal 4:5 is not in the file (it has 0 verses in chapter 4)” and exit code 1, so ask for `3:19`–`3:24`; and Bible Hub's interlinear (`biblehub.com/interlinear/malachi/4-5.htm`) uses the King James numbers, so `…/3-23.htm` does not load. Keil's chapter 4 page uses the King James numbers, but his introduction on chapter 1 gives “Malachi 3:22” for 4:4. Brenton's Septuagint has four chapters, numbered as the King James Version.
- **The name Malachi.** *Scripture Helps*: “The name Malachi means ‘my messenger’ in Hebrew.” In the Hebrew the name (1:1, H4401) is spelled like “my messenger” in 3:1 (H4397 with the ending “my”; `hebrew.mjs malachi 1:1`, `3:1`, and `--word H4397`). See “Contested and sensitive questions.”
- **Names of God.** The Bible Dictionary (“God,” “Jehovah”), as in Genesis's brief: the one “usually identified in the Old Testament as Lord (in small capitals), is the Son, known as Jesus Christ.” The book's speaker is “the LORD of hosts” (24 times in the King James text). Said once where it first matters, not in every chapter. Two verses print “Lord” and not “LORD” in the Church edition: 1:14 (“unto the Lord a corrupt thing”) and 3:1 (“the Lord, whom ye seek”); in 3:1 the Hebrew is *ha-adon* (H113, “the Lord”) and not the divine name. A phrase from either verse must be copied from `data/kjv/malachi.json`.
- **Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Genesis are on the site.** `[[Isa. 56:7]]`, `[[Jer. 31:31–34]]`, `[[Gen. 25:23]]` and the like link to the chapter. Link rather than re-explain. Likely links: Jacob and Esau (Genesis 25–33), the covenant with Levi, the new covenant (Jeremiah 31).
- **Book of Mormon comparison panel: none.** Isaiah has `data/bom/isaiah-parallels.json`; Malachi does not, and none is proposed (the author's rule against a side-by-side panel, Genesis's brief). A hand-written `bom` note must sit inside that panel and the build warns if the verse has none (`STANDARDS.md` §7), so use the `scripture` kind for 3 Nephi. Chapters 3 and 4 use `parallels` with `primary: true` for [[3 Ne. 24]] and [[3 Ne. 25]], which puts “Also in 3 Nephi 24” in the chapter's header.
- **Graphic passages.** Mal. 2:3 (“spread dung upon your faces”) is described, not quoted at length.
- **Theme candidates.** When a chapter develops a topic that runs across the book, the writer reports it; the coordinator adds it to `THEME-CANDIDATES.md`. With four chapters, none is expected: the messenger who prepares the way (3:1, with Isaiah 40 and Jeremiah on the site) is the likeliest, and a topic in one chapter belongs in that chapter's notes (`STANDARDS.md` §8).

## Contested and sensitive questions

One approach for each, from the Church's own statements, so that four chapters say the same thing. Each quotation was copied from the page named, read with `source.mjs` on 2026-10-04. Where the Church's sources differ the newest is followed (author, 2026-10-03).

- **Who Malachi was, and whether the name is a title.** *Scripture Helps* (2025), “What is the book of Malachi?” (`…/scripture-helps-old-testament/49-malachi`): “The name Malachi means ‘my messenger’ in Hebrew.” Its endnote 3: “Because the concept of a messenger is a prominent theme in Malachi 3:1, some scholars have wondered whether Malachi is a title rather than the personal name of the prophet.” The Bible Dictionary (“Malachi”) begins “My messenger.” and goes on to describe the prophet and his date. The Student Manual (34-2, 1982): “little is known of the life of Malachi”; he “is believed to have been a contemporary of Nehemiah”. Keil on 1:1 (Bible Hub): “it is a disputed point” whether the word is a person's name or an ideal name; he gives the Septuagint reading and finds the arguments for a title “not conclusive.” Brenton's Septuagint 1:1: “The burden of the word of the Lord to Israel by the hand of his messenger.” Proposed: the chapters treat Malachi as the prophet's name, as the Church's reference works do. One note on 1:1 says the name means “my messenger,” that it is spelled like the word in 3:1, that the Septuagint reads “his messenger,” and that scholars ask whether it is a title; it states each from its source and does not decide. Newest wins on the date: *Scripture Helps* says uncertain, and the Bible Dictionary's “about 430 B.C.” is given only as attributed. Whether to say “Ezra and Nehemiah's time” is for the setting: *Scripture Helps* says “Malachi may have been a contemporary of Ezra and Nehemiah,” and Keil argues he followed Nehemiah's first visit.
- **“I loved Jacob, and I hated Esau” (1:2–3).** *Scripture Helps*, 1:2–5: “In the Bible, the word translated as hate can sometimes mean ‘not to choose’ rather than being an expression of contempt. That appears to be the case in this passage.” It cites [[Gen. 29:31]] and [[Rom. 9:13]]. The Student Manual (34-3): “The word hated in Hebrew means to be loved less than someone else, not to be disliked with bitter hostility (compare Genesis 29:31).” Elder David A. Bednar, quoted there: “To be or to become chosen is not an exclusive status conferred upon us. Rather, you and I ultimately can choose to be chosen through the righteous exercise of our moral agency.” Proposed: the note follows *Scripture Helps*, shows the Hebrew verb (H8130; `hebrew.mjs malachi --word H8130`) beside Gen. 29:31, and says the Lord “chose” Jacob and did not choose Esau; it states that 1:3–4 is judgment on Edom as a nation, as the text says. It does not say God hates a person. The manual's “Esau (Edom) symbolized the world” is not repeated unless a second source is read.
- **The priests' offerings and “the sons of Levi” (1:6–14; 3:3; D&C 13; 128:24).** *Scripture Helps*, 3:3: “The term ‘sons of Levi’ refers to ancient holders of the Levitical priesthood.” It adds: “In the Doctrine and Covenants, this promised ‘offering unto the Lord’ is associated with temple and family history work. Additionally, the Prophet Joseph Smith taught that animal sacrifice would be restored in some form near the time of the Savior's Second Coming and that it would be performed in righteousness by sons of Levi.” [[D&C 128:24]]: “let us, therefore, as a church and a people, and as Latter-day Saints, offer unto the Lord an offering in righteousness; and let us present in his holy temple, when it is finished, a book containing the records of our dead.” The Student Manual (34-10) prints Joseph Smith: “It is not to be understood that the law of Moses will be established again with all its rites and variety of ceremonies… but those things which existed prior to Moses' day, namely, sacrifice, will be continued.” On 1:7–8, *Scripture Helps* says “Animal sacrifice symbolized the atoning sacrifice of Jesus Christ.” Proposed: say both meanings of “an offering in righteousness,” each with its source, and say the Church's sources do not choose between them or give a date; do not describe how animal sacrifice would be done. Quote Joseph Smith from a source read in full: the Student Manual prints it; the Joseph Smith Papers page *Scripture Helps* cites (History, 1838–1856, volume C-1, page 18 [addenda], `…/history-1838-1856-volume-c-1-2-november-1838-31-july-1842/553`) also loads. The older manual's remarks on what the priests were like (34-4: “not one of them would kindle a fire on the hearth of the altar unless he were paid for it”) go further than Mal. 1:10 and are used only if a second source supports them.
- **Divorce and “the wife of thy youth” (2:14–16).** The Gospel Topics entry “Divorce” (`…/study/manual/gospel-topics/divorce`): “When men and women marry, they make solemn covenants with each other and with God. Every effort should be made to keep these covenants and preserve marriage. When divorce occurs, individuals have the obligation to forgive, lift, and help rather than to condemn.” The Bible Dictionary (“Divorce”): “Permitted under some circumstances because of the hardness of the people's hearts, but as explained by Jesus, ‘from the beginning it was not so’ (Matt. 19:3–12).” The Student Manual (34-7) reads 2:14–16 as men turning from their wives; it adds that they were “seeking the companionship of younger women,” which the verses do not say. *Scripture Helps* has no section on these verses. Keil translates 2:16 “For I hate divorce, saith Jehovah” and says “God hates putting away.” Brenton's Septuagint 2:16 puts the hatred in the husband: “But if thou shouldest hate thy wife and put her away, saith the Lord God of Israel, then ungodliness shall cover thy thoughts.” Proposed: the note says what the verses say (the Lord is “witness between thee and the wife of thy youth”; she is “the wife of thy covenant”), gives the King James and Keil's reading with the Septuagint's difference as a difference, and gives the Church's statement in one sentence with a link to the entry. No note advises a reader about a divorce, and none repeats the manual's “younger women.” The Hebrew of 2:16 (`hebrew.mjs malachi 2:16`: *kî śānēʾ šallaḥ…*) is for a note only if a source read explains its grammar.
- **Tithing and “the windows of heaven” (3:8–12).** *Scripture Helps*, 3:8: “In our day, ‘tithing is the donation of one-tenth of one's income to God's Church… All members who have income should pay tithing.’” Elder Neil L. Andersen (conference, Oct. 2023): “The windows of heaven open in many ways. Some are temporal, but many are spiritual. Some are subtle and easy to overlook.” The Bible Dictionary (“Tithe”): “A clear exposition of the tithe is given in Mal. 3:8–18, in which it is shown that blessings from the payment of tithing are both temporal and spiritual, and failure to pay an honest tithe is a form of robbery.” The General Handbook 34.3.1: “Tithing is the donation of one-tenth of one's income to God's Church.” Proposed: the notes say what the verses meant in Malachi's setting (the storehouse, “meat in mine house,” the devourer as pests, per *Scripture Helps*) and what the Church teaches now, in its words. They promise no particular return and give no personal application (`STANDARDS.md` §1: that is *Come, Follow Me*'s). The Student Manual's quotation of President Kimball (34-13) is about a different verse (3:13–15) and is not needed.
- **Elijah, “the hearts of the fathers,” and Moroni's wording (4:5–6).** *Scripture Helps*, 4:5–6: “Malachi's prophecy that Elijah would return to the earth before the Second Coming was fulfilled in our dispensation”; on 4:6, “The angel Moroni stated this line differently to Joseph Smith. He said that if Elijah did not come, ‘the whole earth would be utterly wasted’ at the Second Coming of Jesus Christ.” On “the fathers”: “Modern prophets have spoken of ‘the fathers’ in two ways. President Joseph Fielding Smith taught it can refer to our ancestors. President Russell M. Nelson taught it can also refer to the ancient patriarchs Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.” The Student Manual (34-17) prints Joseph Smith: “Now, the word turn here should be translated bind, or seal.” Proposed: say that Moroni's wording is a variation the Church itself records ([[JS—H 1:36]]) and that [[D&C 110:14–16]] gives a third; do not call any wording wrong; let a note on 4:6 show the Hebrew verb (*hešîb*, H7725, in Hebrew 3:24) beside Joseph Smith's statement, without saying whether he was translating; give both readings of “the fathers.” Read the talks *Scripture Helps* cites (Christofferson 2023; Nelson 2024; Eyring 2017; Cook; Bednar 2011), not only its quotations of them. “Elijah” here is the Hebrew *Eliyah* (Hebrew 3:23, H452); the Septuagint reads “Elias the Thesbite” and “the great and glorious day” (Brenton 4:5), which a note may report as the Septuagint's wording.
- **Who is “my messenger,” and who is “the messenger of the covenant” (3:1).** *Scripture Helps*: “The Savior declared that John the Baptist fulfilled this prophecy… In our dispensation, the Prophet Joseph Smith was sent as a messenger to prepare the way for the Savior's Second Coming.” “‘Messenger of the covenant’ is a title of Jesus Christ… Malachi's prophecy that the messenger of the covenant would ‘suddenly come to his temple’ likely has multiple fulfillments.” Proposed: give the three readings as *Scripture Helps* does, each with its scripture ([[Matt. 11:10]]; [[D&C 110:1–10]]; [[D&C 133:2–3]]), and say “likely has multiple fulfillments.” The Student Manual's added detail (34-8, Elder McConkie: a coming “to others of his temples, more particularly that which will be erected in Jackson County, Missouri”) is not in *Scripture Helps* and is left out; it does not conflict.
- **“Neither root nor branch” (4:1).** *Scripture Helps*: “Roots and branches can refer to our ancestors and descendants. To be left without either is to be cut off from the blessing of an eternal family,” with Elder Christofferson's “neither ancestry nor posterity.” The plain sense is total destruction of the wicked (“stubble”). Proposed: give the plain sense first and the Church's reading as the Church's, with its source; the older manual (34-16) says the same.
- **“Sun” and “Son” of righteousness (4:2; 3 Ne. 25:2).** The King James text of Malachi has “Sun,” the Book of Mormon has “Son” (compared above); the Church footnote at 4:2 points to 3 Ne. 25:2. Scripture Central's KnoWhy #653 explains the winged-sun image and does not discuss the difference; it also prints “the Sun of Righteousness shall appear unto them” as a quotation of [[2 Ne. 26:9]], where the Church edition reads “Son of Righteousness.” A writer copies the scripture from the scripture, not from the KnoWhy. Proposed: a note may show the difference between Malachi and 3 Nephi; any claim about the reason for it needs a source read.
- **Marriage “to the daughter of a strange god” (2:11).** The Church footnote reads “Marriage, Interfaith.” The Student Manual (34-7) reads it as Judah forming “a temporal or spiritual alliance” with a nation that did not honor Jehovah, and also as “marrying women who worship idols.” Keil connects it to [[Neh. 13:23]]. Proposed: describe the verse as the book does, a breach of the covenant, with the manual's two readings as readings; do not apply it to marriage today.

## The text

`data/kjv/malachi.json` was imported on 2026-10-04 (4 chapters, 55 verses) and compared verse by verse with the Gospel Library edition by `scripts/check-kjv.mjs malachi`. It found two differences, both a “Lord” (adonai) where the source file has LORD: 1:14 and 3:1. They are corrected by two entries in `LDS_SPELLINGS` in `scripts/fetch-kjv.mjs` (under a `// Malachi` comment, each anchored to its own phrase, so no other book changes), and the two now agree in every verse. `check-kjv` still reports 0 for Genesis, Jeremiah, and Isaiah.

## Checks while the book is in preview

The plain build leaves a preview book out, and several agents build at once, so use these forms (replace `NN` with the chapter number):

```sh
node scripts/fix-yaml.mjs content/malachi/chapters/NN.yaml
PREVIEW=1 BUILD_OUT=.cache/batch/malachi/dist-NN node scripts/build.mjs
node scripts/check-quotes.mjs malachi <n>
node scripts/check-content.mjs malachi <n>
node scripts/check-ledger.mjs malachi <n>
```

The working folder for the batch is `.cache/batch/malachi/` (not committed): `proposed/NN.yaml`, `reports/NN-writer.md`, `reports/NN-check.md`. The research files already there are the comparison scripts (`compare.mjs`, `compare2.mjs`) and their output, and the saved output of `footnotes.mjs` for each chapter (`fn1.txt` to `fn4.txt`), which writers may read instead of running it again.
