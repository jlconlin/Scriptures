## What a chapter is for here

A reader of Joseph Smith's own account asks: *Where did the Smiths live, and who were they? What was the religious excitement, and when? Why did a fourteen-year-old go to the woods to pray, and what did he see? Why did he tell it this way in 1838 and differently in 1832? Who was Moroni, and why was he once called Nephi? What did Moroni quote, and why those passages? Where is Cumorah? Why did Joseph Smith work for Josiah Stowell, and what was money-digging? What were the Urim and Thummim, and what of the seer stone? What happened with Charles Anthon? Who was Oliver Cowdery? How was the priesthood restored, and when?* The chapter answers these from the text first, then from the documents (the Joseph Smith Papers) and the Church's history pages, then from the historians.

**Two settings.** Every passage has the time it tells of (1805–1829, Vermont, Palmyra and Manchester, Harmony) and the time it was written (1838–39, after the troubles in Kirtland and Missouri, “owing to the many reports … in circulation by evil-disposed and designing persons,” 1:1). The `setting` gives both; a note says why the later vantage matters only where a source does (the Church's *Multiple Accounts of the First Vision*, paragraph 20: in 1838 Joseph Smith “reflected on the vision's meaning not only to him but to the Church and the world”).

**Testimony, not argument.** The text is Joseph Smith's testimony. The chapter explains it and reports what the documents show; it does not argue that the events happened, and it does not report critics' arguments except where a Church page or a Latter-day Saint historian takes one up and a reader would ask the question anyway (the revival's date, the differing accounts, money-digging). Then it gives the historian's answer as the historian's, in his or her words.

**The Witness of Christ.** The book's center is the Father's words, “This is My Beloved Son. Hear Him!” (1:17). The `christ` section rests on that, on the Son's answer, and on John the Baptist's ordination “in the name of Messiah” (1:69); the application is the one Joseph Smith drew: ask of God (1:11–13, James 1:5).

## How the chapter is written

**Three writers, one after another, one file.** Each is a `chapter-writer` given a range; each reads the whole text and the file as it stands, writes the sections and notes for its range and its rows in the one ledger, and touches nothing outside its range except to fix a clash it would create. The ranges:

1. **Part 1, verses 1–26** (why the history; the family; the excitement; James 1:5; the grove; the vision; the preacher; persecution; “I had actually seen a light”). Part 1 also writes `title`, `tagline`, `when`, `setting` and `thread`, for the whole chapter, from this brief, and leaves `christ`, `explore` and `sources` for part 3.
2. **Part 2, verses 27–54** (three years of follies; Moroni's three visits and the fourth in the field; the quotations; Cumorah; the four years). It may revise the `thread` once it sees what the middle carries.
3. **Part 3, verses 55–75, with Oliver Cowdery's account** (Alvin's death; Stowell and Harmony; Emma; the plates obtained; Martin Harris and Anthon; Oliver Cowdery; the priesthood; baptism; persecution). It writes `christ`, `explore` and `sources`, and reads the whole for one voice.

Then one checker, one Codex reading and one reviewer, for the whole chapter, as for any chapter (`PROCESS.md`). Each writer reports what it left for the next. A note on Oliver Cowdery's account hangs on verse 75's last words or on the verse his account bears on (68–73); the paragraphs after verse 75 have no number and cannot carry a note.

**Length.** A dense chapter: Genesis 24 (67 verses) is the nearest in size. About as many notes as a reader has questions; the First Vision and Moroni's visits carry the most.

## Book-wide conventions

**What in this brief is a lead and not a fact.** Words in quotation marks below were copied from the page named. The rest is the coordinator's direction of where to look. None of it goes into the chapter until the writer has read it on a page and given it a ledger row (`STANDARDS.md` §4).

- **What the book is, said once in the `setting`.** The Introduction to the Pearl of Great Price, paragraph 8: “Excerpts from Joseph Smith's official testimony and history, which he and his scribes prepared in 1838–39 and which was published serially in the Times and Seasons in Nauvoo, Illinois, beginning on March 15, 1842.” Paragraph 2: canonized “on October 10, 1880.” The Joseph Smith Papers have the manuscripts: History, circa June 1839–circa 1841 [Draft 2] (the earliest surviving manuscript of this text, in James Mulholland's hand) and History, 1838–1856, volume A-1. `when`: “Written 1838–39; the events of 1805–1829.”
- **The other First Vision accounts.** Four firsthand accounts (1832, 1835, 1838, 1842), listed with their pages on the Joseph Smith Papers' “Accounts of the First Vision.” The Church's newest statement is *Topics and Questions*, “Multiple Accounts of the First Vision” (2023): “The various accounts tell a consistent story, though naturally they differ in emphasis and detail” (paragraph 6). **Proposed:** one Context note at 1:14–17 (where a reader first wonders) says there are four accounts, what each adds (the 1832 account's forgiveness of sins; the 1835 pillar of fire and many angels; the 1842 letter's “two glorious personages who exactly resembled each other”), and that the Church publishes all of them, in the Church's words. The Gospel Topics essay of 2016 is the fuller history, used where the 2023 page is silent. No side-by-side table.
- **Joseph's age.** “In my fifteenth year” (1:7) and “early in the spring of eighteen hundred and twenty” (1:14): born 23 December 1805 (1:3), he was fourteen. The 1832 account's “16th year” is, in the Church's words, “most likely the result of a scribal mistake” (*Multiple Accounts*, paragraph 8). Say this only if a note on age is needed; the text is consistent.
- **Places and people.** Church History Topics are readable for most (`…/study/history/topics/<slug>`): `palmyra-and-manchester`, `sacred-grove-and-smith-family-farm`, `joseph-sr-and-lucy-mack-smith-family` (with a section on Alvin), `emma-hale-smith`, `oliver-cowdery`, `angel-moroni`, `gold-plates`, `martin-harriss-consultations-with-scholars`, `treasure-seeking`, `seer-stones`, `joseph-smiths-1826-trial`, `awakenings-and-revivals`, `christian-churches-in-joseph-smiths-day`, `restoration-of-the-aaronic-priesthood`, `restoration-of-the-melchizedek-priesthood`. There is no topic for Harmony, Cumorah, Josiah Stowell or Alvin by name. *Saints*, volume 1, chapters 1–7, at `…/study/history/saints-v1/<slug>` (`01-ask-in-faith` to `07-fellow-servants`; the `…/history/saints/…` path does not load), are the Church's narrative; cite them for a fact, and follow their notes to the documents.
- **Persecution.** Say what the text says and what a source read adds; name no person or church beyond the text, and say nothing against a denomination today (`STANDARDS.md` §3). The Methodist preacher of 1:21 is not named by the text; a historian's identification comes in only as that historian's.
- **The site's other books.** Moroni's quotations: `[[Mal. 3:1]]` and the Malachi 4 notes (Malachi's brief has a table of the wording; link, don't copy); `[[Joel 2:28]]` (“Peter's day and Moroni's”); `[[Isa. 11:1]]`; `[[Deut. 18:15]]` for the prophet like unto Moses (Acts 3 is not on the site). Isaiah 29:11–12, the sealed book, is on the site: link it at 1:65. Run `node scripts/occurrences.mjs all "JS—H" --site` before writing, and don't say again what a note elsewhere says.
- **Theme candidates.** Report what runs across the book (asking of God and the answer; being prepared; the testimony that persecution could not change; light). The coordinator adds them to `THEME-CANDIDATES.md` after the author chooses.

## Contested and sensitive questions

One approach for each. The writer reads the source again before writing the note and quotes from the page.

- **The revival's date (1:5–8, 14).** The verse puts the excitement “in the second year after our removal to Manchester”; 1:14 dates the vision to spring 1820. *Multiple Accounts* (paragraphs 26–27): “There was a three-day Methodist camp meeting in Palmyra in the late spring of 1820, and extensive revivals occurred several years later in 1824.” The 2016 essay (paragraph 14): “Documentary evidence, however, supports Joseph Smith's statements about the revivals.” Steven C. Harper (RSC, *Exploring the First Vision*, “Evaluating Three Arguments,” paragraphs 24–30) and Backman (BYU Studies 9:3) are the historians. **Proposed:** one note says the region was in revival (the Church History Topic “Awakenings and Revivals”), what the Church's page says of 1820, and, as Harper's reading, that the text dates the excitement to about 1819 and the vision to 1820. It names the critic only if Harper's argument needs it.
- **“All their creeds were an abomination” (1:19).** No Church page discusses the line. The nearest: *Topics and Questions*, “Joseph Smith,” paragraph 3, the Savior “told him to join none of the churches then in existence because they were teaching incorrect doctrines.” John W. Welch (RSC, “‘All Their Creeds Were an Abomination’”) and KnoWhy #773 explain “creeds” as the formal statements of the councils, not the believers: Welch, “Latter-day Saints should not condemn all creeds equally.” J. B. Haws (RSC, *Religious Educator* 14:2) reconciles 1:10 with 1:18–19. **Proposed:** the note quotes the verse, says what a creed was, gives Welch's reading as his, and notes that the same verse goes on to Isaiah 29:13 (“they draw near to me with their lips”), which says where the fault lay. Nothing that disparages a church today.
- **Moroni or Nephi (1:33).** The Church History Topic “Angel Moroni,” paragraphs 5–7: Joseph Smith named the angel Moroni in 1835 in the Doctrine and Covenants; the earliest manuscript of this text calls him “Nephi,” a reading that “likely originated with Joseph Smith's clerk James Mulholland”; Church historians under Brigham Young corrected it; “No evidence indicates that Joseph Smith ever called the angel ‘Nephi.’” The Joseph Smith Papers show “Nephi” in Draft 2 (image 5) and in the *Times and Seasons* of 15 April 1842 (page 753), and “*Moroni” inserted in A-1 (image 5). **Proposed:** one Context note at 1:33, from the Church History Topic and the manuscripts.
- **Money-digging (1:56).** The text itself says Joseph Smith worked for Josiah Stowell, who “had heard something of a silver mine,” and that this gave rise to “the very prevalent story of my having been a money-digger.” The Church History Topic “Treasure Seeking”: “Rather than deny the charge, Joseph acknowledged in his official history that Josiah Stowell had hired him in 1825” (paragraph 3), and that he was later commanded to have nothing further to do with treasure seekers (paragraph 5). The 1826 hearing: Church History Topic “Joseph Smith's 1826 Trial” (“The outcome of the hearing remains a puzzle,” paragraph 6). Richard L. Bushman (RSC, *A Reason for Faith*, “Joseph Smith and Money Digging”) is the historian. **Proposed:** say it plainly, as the Church's pages do; give the trial in a sentence; note that the Church pages date the hiring 1825 (the text) and the hearing 1826 without reconciling them further.
- **The Urim and Thummim and the seer stone (1:35, 42, 52, 59, 62).** The text speaks only of the Urim and Thummim, “two stones in silver bows … fastened to a breastplate” (1:35). The Church's newest statement is *Topics and Questions*, “Book of Mormon Translation” (2025): the interpreters were “a pair of clear stones set in metal rims and bound together by a metal bow” (paragraph 14); Joseph Smith also used “a small brown stone” (paragraph 15); “These objects could apparently be used interchangeably” (paragraph 16); “The early Saints sometimes referred to both … as Urim and Thummim” (paragraph 17). **Proposed:** one note at 1:35 describes the instrument in the text's words and the Church's; a note at 1:62 (“by means of the Urim and Thummim I translated some of them”) says, in the Church's words, that the seer stone was also used in the translation. No theory of how the instruments worked beyond the Church's page; the Old Testament Urim and Thummim are linked (`[[Ex. 28:30]]`), not explained again.
- **Charles Anthon (1:63–65).** Church History Topic “Martin Harris's Consultations with Scholars”: “According to Harris, Charles Anthon examined the characters and prepared a signed statement … In later statements, Anthon denied having authenticated the characters” (paragraph 8); the accounts leave “some questions about the trip unanswered” (paragraph 3). “Dr. Mitchell” is Samuel L. Mitchill (paragraph 7). Richard E. Bennett (RSC) and Stanley B. Kimball (BYU Studies 10:3) are the historians. **Proposed:** report both accounts as the Church's page does, without deciding between them, and link Isaiah 29:11–12 for “I cannot read a sealed book.”
- **The priesthood (1:68–73; Cowdery's account).** The Aaronic Priesthood: 15 May 1829 (the text, 1:72). The Melchizedek: the Church History Topic “Restoration of the Melchizedek Priesthood,” paragraph 6: “It is impossible to precisely date this heavenly manifestation from existing sources.” *Topics and Questions*, “Restoration of the Priesthood” (2023), paragraph 6: “Sometime after John the Baptist's appearance.” **Proposed:** give no date for Peter, James and John; say that 1:72 promises it and that D&C 27:12 and 128:20 tell of it. Michael Hubbard MacKay (BYU Studies 60:1, “Event or Process?”) is the historian for how the restoration unfolded, as his reading.
- **Why early accounts begin with Moroni.** *Multiple Accounts*, paragraph 30: Lucy and William Smith and Oliver Cowdery “left accounts that began with the angel Moroni's visit in 1823.” Bring it in only if a note on 1:21–26 or 1:27 needs it.
- **Oliver Cowdery's letters.** KnoWhy #453: his letters to W. W. Phelps in the *Messenger and Advocate* (1834–35) “must be used cautiously in reconstructing the early history.” The account printed after 1:75 is from Letter I (October 1834; the edition gives “pp. 14–16”). A note may quote it, as Cowdery's witness.

## The text

`data/kjv/joseph-smith-history.json` (1 chapter, 75 verses) was imported on 2026-11-17 by `scripts/fetch-gl.mjs joseph-smith-history pgp/js-h 1 "Joseph Smith—History"`; `scripts/check-kjv.mjs joseph-smith-history` reports no difference from the Gospel Library apart from the readings below.

**Compared with the 1921 edition** (`.cache/pgp/jsh-1921.txt`; `scripts/compare-edition.mjs`, every difference read in the scan). The site prints the 1921 wording (`data/kjv/joseph-smith-history.edition.json`) in two verses:

- **1:18:** 1921 “which of all the sects was right—and which I should join” (today “which of all the sects was right (for at this time it had never entered into my heart that all were wrong)—and which I should join”). The clause is in both manuscripts (Draft 2 and A-1, image 3: “(for at this time it had never entered into my heart that all were wrong)”), and today's edition restores it. **Proposed:** a Context note at 1:18 gives the clause and where it is, citing the Joseph Smith Papers, because it answers a question a reader of 1:10 (“are they all wrong together?”) will ask.
- **1:66:** 1921 “the circumstance of my having received the plates” (today “circumstances”). No note.

**Oliver Cowdery's account** (printed after 1:75; seven paragraphs in `data/kjv/joseph-smith-history.extra.json`, which the page prints without numbers). It was in the 1902 edition, as a footnote at what is now 1:71, and is not in the 1921. Its words are Cowdery's of 1834. The site prints it from the 1902 printing (`.cache/pgp/cowdery-1902.txt`, compared word by word): two readings differ from today's, “as the prophet said would be” (today “said it would be”) and “it was easy to be seen, that amid” (today “it was as easy to be seen that amid”), listed in the edition file with `ref: "1:75+"`. When it came back into the text is not established by any page read: the Introduction says only that “some changes have been made to bring the text into conformity with earlier documents.” Don't date it.

Copy every quoted phrase from `data/kjv/joseph-smith-history.json` or the extra file.
