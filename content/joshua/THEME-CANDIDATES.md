# Joshua: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.** The candidates came from Fable's and Codex's lists (`.cache/batch/joshua/reports/themes-fable.md` and `themes-codex.md`), shown to the author as one combined list (`AUTHORING.md`, “Theme candidates at the end of a book”); only the author's choice is recorded here.

## Author's selection (2026-10-24)

**Three pages are chosen** (author, 2026-10-24: “Write theme pages 1, 2 and 3 for Joshua”):

- **J1. “There remaineth therefore a rest”: rest given, rest declared, rest still ahead** (Fable's second, with Codex's first; both lists). The eastern tribes told “The LORD your God hath given you rest” before the Jordan is crossed, and charged to fight “Until the LORD have given your brethren rest” (1:13, 15); the land resting from war (11:23; 14:15) beside “very much land” remaining (13:1); rest declared, “the LORD gave them rest round about” (21:43–45), and the soldiers sent home (22:4); the farewell that opens on rest (23:1) and closes on snares and thorns (23:13–16). To Christ: Heb. 3:7–4:11 (“if Jesus had given them rest … There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God,” 4:8–9), with Ps. 95:11 (on the site: link, don't repeat); Alma 12:34; Alma 13:12–13, 29; D&C 84:24 (the footnote at 1:13). Applies: Matt. 11:28–29; Moro. 7:3. The Septuagint's verb for “give rest” beside Heb. 4:8 only if a source read confirms it.
- **J2. “Ye have no part in the LORD”: the inheritance that is the LORD Himself** (Fable's third, with Codex's “out of their inheritance”). “Part” (*ḥēleq*) as a share of ground in 13–19; the Levites with no part, “the priesthood of the LORD is their inheritance” (13:14, 33; 14:4; 18:7; Num. 18:20; Deut. 18:1–2); the tribes giving the Levites cities out of their own inheritance (21:1–3) and the west offering the east a place in its land (22:19); the eastern tribes' fear, “ye have no part in the LORD” (22:25, 27), and the altar of witness never to be used (22:26–29, 34). To Christ: Ps. 16:5 (on the site: agree) with Acts 2:25–31; Rom. 8:17; Heb. 9:15; D&C 84:33–38. Applies: Ps. 73:26; Lam. 3:24; Mosiah 4:19–26. The altar beside Heb. 10:12–14 only as the page's own comparison, not as what the tribes meant.
- **J3. “She dwelleth in Israel”: from being spared to belonging** (Codex's second; Fable set the topic aside as already in the notes). Rahab's confession (2:9–14) and her house spared and dwelling “in Israel even unto this day” (6:22–25); Achan of Judah, the insider who takes the devoted thing (7:1, 11–12); the stranger at Ebal hearing the law (8:33–35); Gibeon's league kept and defended (9:15–27; 10:6–14; 11:19–20); refuge “for the stranger that sojourneth among them” (20:9). To Christ: Matt. 1:5; Heb. 11:31; 3 Ne. 9:17. Applies: inherited identity does not replace faithfulness, and no one's origin puts them beyond His reach. **It must add the movement from spared to belonging to protected**, not repeat the comparison of Rahab, Gibeon and Achan already made at 6:21 and 7; Gibeon's servitude is said plainly; Caleb's ancestry is left out.

Left out of the combined list: Shechem from the first promise to John 4 (Fable's first; judged mostly a sequence of places, and Genesis 33 already makes the John 4 link), the land given and received (Codex's third; its parts are in J1 and the chapters), witness stones and the next generation (Exodus has *When Your Children Ask*), and hearts that melt and incline (Fable's fourth; not one developing arc).
