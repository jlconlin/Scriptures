The word the King James Version renders “part” in Joshua, *ḥēleq*, stands nine times in the book, and seven of them are ground: Caleb’s “part among the children of Judah” (15:13), Simeon’s share inside Judah because “the part of the children of Judah was too much for them” (19:9), the “seven parts” the surveyors describe in a book (18:5, 6, 9), and the Levites, to whom “they gave no part … in the land” (14:4) and who “have no part among you; for the priesthood of the LORD is their inheritance” (18:7).[@oshb-wlc] The last two are different. In chapter 22 the tribes that settled east of the Jordan explain the altar they built there: they feared that “your children might speak unto our children, saying, What have ye to do with the LORD God of Israel? … ye have no part in the LORD” (22:24–25), and they built the altar “that your children may not say to our children in time to come, Ye have no part in the LORD” (22:27). The lexicon files these two under a different sense from the other seven: not a “portion, tract, of land” but a “share or interest in,” a “right (privilege) in YHWH,” the LORD’s name.[@bdb]

So the book uses one word for two things, a share of the land and a share in the LORD, and it gives the two to different tribes. Levi has no part in the land, and the text says three times what it has instead. The eastern tribes have their part in the land, chosen by themselves before the Jordan was crossed, and they fear having no part in the LORD. Joshua itself sets Levi beside the eastern tribes four times, in 13:8–14, 13:32–33, 14:3 and in the one verse 18:7, and the Church’s footnote at 18:7 leads to the eastern tribes’ dismissal in 22:1.[@lds-scriptures] Carrying that pairing on to the eastern tribes’ fear in 22:25 and 27 is this page’s; no commentary I read makes it, and the Church’s edition has no footnote at either verse. What the pairing shows is where Joshua locates a part in the LORD. It is not in a tribe’s own ground. The tribe without ground has it by the LORD’s word, and the tribes with ground secure it by an altar that points to the one altar where the LORD is served.

## “Seven parts”

In the allotment chapters a part is measured ground. Joshua sends men to “describe the land into seven parts,” and they “described it by cities into seven parts in a book” (18:5–9). Caleb receives Hebron as “a part among the children of Judah, according to the commandment of the LORD” (15:13). Simeon’s inheritance falls “within the inheritance” of Judah because Judah’s part was too much (19:9). The word for inheritance, *naḥălâ*, is the book’s refrain, fifty times in forty-three verses, and it closes almost every tribe’s section: “This is the inheritance of the children of …”[@oshb-wlc]

## “The LORD God of Israel was their inheritance”

The lists stop three times for Levi, and each time say what the tribe has in place of ground. “Only unto the tribe of Levi he gave none inheritance; the sacrifices of the LORD God of Israel made by fire are their inheritance, as he said unto them” (13:14). “But unto the tribe of Levi Moses gave not any inheritance: the LORD God of Israel was their inheritance, as he said unto them” (13:33). “But the Levites have no part among you; for the priesthood of the LORD is their inheritance: and Gad, and Reuben, and half the tribe of Manasseh, have received their inheritance beyond Jordan on the east, which Moses the servant of the LORD gave them” (18:7). Each time the book speaks of Levi’s lot it speaks of the eastern tribes’ in the same breath, in 13:8–14, 13:32–33, 14:3 and here.[@lds-scriptures] The Cambridge commentary notices the change of wording across the three: “the sacrifices of Jehovah,” then “Jehovah, God of Israel,” then “the priesthood of Jehovah.”[@cambridge-joshua] “As he said unto them” looks back to the word to Aaron, which the Church’s footnote at 13:14 gives: “Thou shalt have no inheritance in their land, neither shalt thou have any part among them: I am thy part and thine inheritance among the children of Israel” (Num. 18:20).[@lds-scriptures] Keil reads the first two as one: the offerings made by fire, “the firings of the LORD,” are Levi’s inheritance, “so that the God of Israel himself is called the inheritance of Levi in Joshua 13:33 as in Numbers 18:20.”[@keil-joshua] The Student Manual says of the word in Numbers that the Levites “were not given land as the other tribes were because their inheritance was the priesthood instead.”[@ot-manual-numbers-13-36] What they had in fact, the Bible Dictionary lists: no inheritance in Canaan, “but they had the tithe,” “48 cities,” and “a claim on the alms of the people.”[@bd-levites]

The phrase “no part” in 18:7 is Deuteronomy’s phrase for the Levite, said there always with “inheritance” beside it: “he hath no part nor inheritance with you” (Deut. 12:12; 14:27, 29), “Levi hath no part nor inheritance with his brethren; the LORD is his inheritance” (Deut. 10:9; so too 18:1).[@lds-scriptures][@oshb-wlc] In Joshua the phrase is used of Levi in 14:4 and 18:7, and then, in a form with no “inheritance” beside it, of someone else.

## “Out of their inheritance”

The tribes with ground gave from it. The Levite heads came to Eleazar the priest, to Joshua and to the heads of the tribes at Shiloh (21:1), and “the children of Israel gave unto the Levites out of their inheritance, at the commandment of the LORD, these cities and their suburbs” (21:3). The *Old Testament Minute* reads the order of the verse, the priest named first, as the point: the heads came “not to Joshua, the military commander, but rather to Eleazar, the priest,” and the Levites were “assigned not their own territory but rather certain cities within the territories given to the other tribes.”[@ot-minute-josh-21] Some of the forty-eight were east of the Jordan, in Reuben, Gad and Manasseh (21:27, 36–39).[@lds-scriptures] The tribe with no part in the land lived in cities within the other tribes’ territories, on both sides of the river.

## “Ye have no part in the LORD”

The eastern tribes had chosen their ground. In Numbers they asked Moses, “let this land be given unto thy servants for a possession, and bring us not over Jordan,” and Moses answered, “wherefore discourage ye the heart of the children of Israel from going over into the land which the LORD hath given them?” (Num. 32:5, 7); they said, “we will not inherit with them on yonder side Jordan, or forward; because our inheritance is fallen to us on this side Jordan eastward” (Num. 32:19).[@lds-scriptures] The Pulpit commentary: “For their own advantage these tribes had chosen to remain in the trans-Jordanic territory conquered by Moses.”[@pulpit-joshua] Joshua dismisses them with a blessing (22:1–6), and then they build “a great altar to see to” by the Jordan (22:10), and Israel gathers at Shiloh for war (22:12).

Their defense is that they feared for their children. “In time to come your children might speak unto our children, saying, What have ye to do with the LORD God of Israel? For the LORD hath made Jordan a border between us and you … ye have no part in the LORD: so shall your children make our children cease from fearing the LORD” (22:24–25). The Hebrew of the first question is “what to you and to the LORD,” and Keil glosses it, “He does not concern you; He is our God.”[@oshb-wlc][@keil-joshua] Keil finds the anxiety reasonable: “inasmuch as in all the promises and laws Canaan alone … is always mentioned as the land which Jehovah would give to His people for their inheritance, it was quite a possible thing that at some future time the false conclusion might be drawn from this, that only the tribes who dwelt in Canaan proper were the true people of Jehovah.”[@keil-joshua] The Pulpit commentary, following Keil, puts it: “It was quite possible that in future ages they might be regarded as outside the blessings and privileges of the Mosaic covenant.”[@pulpit-joshua] The fear is never spoken as a verdict. No one in Joshua says “ye have no part in the LORD” to anyone; the eastern tribes report what might be said, and build so that it will not be.

Here the two senses of the word meet. Levi has no part in the land and has the LORD; the eastern tribes have their part in the land and fear losing the LORD. Both passages use the same two words, “no part,” *ʾên ḥēleq*, though the Hebrew word order differs. The endings differ too: “among you” for Levi, “in the LORD” for the fear. And the fear drops the word “inheritance,” which stands beside “part” in 18:7 and in every Levite verse of Deuteronomy.[@oshb-wlc] The lexicon’s two senses are the page’s warrant for reading them together, and the reading is this page’s. What the eastern tribes feared losing was not ground. It was the thing the tribe without ground was given.

## “Behold the pattern of the altar of the LORD”

The delegation had offered the eastern tribes land among them: “if the land of your possession be unclean, then pass ye over unto the land of the possession of the LORD, wherein the LORD’s tabernacle dwelleth, and take possession among us” (22:19); the note on [[Josh. 22:19]] weighs the offer. The eastern tribes do not take it, and Phinehas in the end accepts them in their own land (22:31, 33). Keil calls the inference they feared, that only the tribes in Canaan proper were the LORD’s people, “the false conclusion.”[@keil-joshua] Their remedy is an altar not used for sacrifice. “Let us now prepare to build us an altar, not for burnt offering, nor for sacrifice: But that it may be a witness between us, and you … that we might do the service of the LORD before him with our burnt offerings, and with our sacrifices” (22:26–27). The service is to be done “before him,” and Keil reads the phrase as “before the tabernacle in which Jehovah was enthroned,” across the river.[@keil-joshua] If the words are spoken, the answer is to be, “Behold the pattern of the altar of the LORD, which our fathers made, not for burnt offerings, nor for sacrifices; but it is a witness between us and you” (22:28). The Cambridge commentary reads the altar as “a monument to future ages of the connection between the tribes divided by the river,” to be pointed to if Israel should ever “declare that the people beyond the river ‘had no part in the Lord.’”[@cambridge-joshua] A copy of the one altar, built not for sacrifice, says that the copy is not the thing. Keil: they built it “according to the pattern of the altar before the tabernacle … as a witness that they worshipped one and the same God with the tribes on this side.”[@keil-joshua] The part in the LORD is at the one altar, and the eastern tribes claim it by pointing there. The note on [[Josh. 22:28]] follows the copy.

Phinehas, the priest, answers for the delegation: “This day we perceive that the LORD is among us, because ye have not committed this trespass against the LORD” (22:31). The Church’s footnote on “among” leads to the promise of the law, “I will walk among you, and will be your God” (Lev. 26:12).[@lds-scriptures] The altar is then named by what it is for: “a witness between us that the LORD is God” (22:34). The note on [[Josh. 22:34]] explains the name. The Student Manual reads the chapter as “the critical balance between true worship and apostate idolatry,” and the *Old Testament Minute* reads the altar as a memorial that Phinehas “determined … was legitimate.”[@ot-manual-josh][@ot-minute-josh-22]

## “The LORD is the portion of mine inheritance”

The LORD who is Levi’s inheritance is Jehovah, “the premortal Jesus Christ.”[@bd-jehovah] The psalm that uses Levi’s word of a single worshipper, “The LORD is the portion of mine inheritance and of my cup” (Ps. 16:5), is headed in the Church’s edition “A messianic psalm of David.” The Church’s footnote at Deuteronomy 18:2, where the Levite’s inheritance is the LORD, leads to this verse.[@oshb-wlc][@lds-scriptures] The lexicon puts the psalm’s use beside the Levite’s and calls it the same word “in spiritual sense.”[@bdb] The theme page [My Portion for Ever](/psalms/themes/my-portion-for-ever/) follows that word through the Psalms.

Joshua’s own word for what Levi has is “the priesthood of the LORD” (18:7), and the Church’s footnote on “Levites” in that verse points to the Topical Guide’s “Priesthood, Aaronic.”[@lds-scriptures] In the Restoration the Lord says that receiving the priesthood is receiving Him: “all they who receive this priesthood receive me, saith the Lord; … And he that receiveth me receiveth my Father; And he that receiveth my Father receiveth my Father’s kingdom; therefore all that my Father hath shall be given unto him” (D&C 84:35–38), spoken of those who become “the sons of Moses and of Aaron” (84:34).[@lds-scriptures] Setting that revelation beside Joshua 18:7 is this page’s; the Church’s footnotes at the revelation do not go to Joshua. Read together, the two name the same thing in place of ground: Joshua calls Levi’s inheritance “the priesthood of the LORD,” and the revelation says that those who receive the priesthood “receive me, saith the Lord.” The page does not say the Levites held the promise of “all that my Father hath”; the revelation speaks of those who obtain “these two priesthoods” and magnify their calling (84:33). The Student Manual on the revelation adds that “the blessings of the oath and covenant of the priesthood are not just for priesthood holders.”[@dc-student-manual-2017-84]

Two statements follow for a disciple. The eastern tribes feared that a border, not a sin, would be taken as the loss of their part in the LORD, and their answer, which Phinehas accepted, put their part in the LORD in “the service of the LORD before him” (22:27), done at the one altar though they lived across the river; Keil calls the inference they feared “the false conclusion.”[@keil-joshua] And the tribe with the least ground had the Lord Himself as its inheritance, the inheritance the psalmist names and the one the revelation ties to receiving the priesthood; President Russell M. Nelson, quoted in the Student Manual on “all that my Father hath,” said that “no earthly reward—no other success—could compensate for the bounties the Lord will bestow upon those who love Him, keep His commandments … and endure to the end.”[@dc-student-manual-2017-84]
