Joshua says several times that the LORD gave Israel rest, and each time it sets beside the rest what remained or what could be lost: land still to be possessed, nations still in it, and a warning that the good land could be forfeited. Hebrews takes up the same word. “Jesus” in [[Heb. 4:8]] is Joshua ([[Acts 7:45]]), and the argument is that the rest Joshua gave was not the last: “There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God” ([[Heb. 4:9]]).

The Church teaches that the Lord “has promised such rest to His faithful followers during this life,” and “has also prepared a place of rest for them in the next life.”[@gs-rest] Modern revelation names the rest Israel lost in the wilderness “the fulness of his glory” ([[D&C 84:24]]), and Alma says who enters it: “whosoever repenteth, and hardeneth not his heart, he shall have claim on mercy through mine Only Begotten Son” ([[Alma 12:34]]).

## The path

1. [[Josh. 1:13]]: before the Jordan, Moses' word recalled, “The LORD your God hath given you rest, and hath given you this land” (see the note on [[Josh. 1:15]]).
2. [[Josh. 1:15]]: the rest comes with a term, “Until the LORD have given your brethren rest, as he hath given you.”
3. [[Josh. 11:23]]: the wars close, “And the land rested from war” (see the note on [[Josh. 13:1]]).
4. [[Josh. 13:1]]: and yet “there remaineth yet very much land to be possessed.”
5. [[Josh. 21:43–45]]: the promise kept, “the LORD gave them rest round about… all came to pass.”
6. [[Josh. 22:3–4]]: the eastern tribes sent home in the words of 1:15, “hath given rest unto your brethren, as he promised them.”
7. [[Josh. 23:1]], [[Josh. 23:4]]: the farewell opens on the rest given “from all their enemies round about,” and within four verses names “these nations that remain” (see the note on [[Josh. 23:13]]).
8. [[Josh. 23:13–16]]: how the rest could be lost, “until ye perish from off this good land.”
9. [[Heb. 4:8–9]]: “if Jesus had given them rest, then would he not afterward have spoken of another day.”
