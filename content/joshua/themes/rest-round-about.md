The book of Joshua says several times that the LORD gave Israel rest, and sets beside it what remained or what could be lost. Hebrews, where “Jesus” in 4:8 is Joshua, reasons that the rest Joshua gave was not the last, and Alma says who enters the rest that remains and through whom.

## Rest before the Jordan

Joshua recalls Moses’ word to Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh: “The LORD your God hath given you rest” ([[Josh. 1:13]]). That rest comes with a term: they cross armed “until he hath driven out his enemies” ([[Num. 32:21]]),[@lds-scriptures] “Until the LORD have given your brethren rest, as he hath given you” ([[Josh. 1:15]]). “Given rest” is the Hebrew verb “rest” in the form meaning “cause to rest,” with the LORD as the one who gives it; it is in five verses of Joshua (1:13, 1:15, 21:44, 22:4, 23:1).[@oshb-wlc] In modern revelation the rest Israel lost in the wilderness is “the fulness of his glory” ([[D&C 84:24]]);[@lds-scriptures] chapter 1 has [the note](/joshua/1/).

Set beside this, Moroni speaks of a rest entered “from this time henceforth until ye shall rest with him in heaven” ([[Moro. 7:3]]): in both, a rest already given is not yet the last rest.

## The land rested, and very much land remained

“The land rested from war” ([[Josh. 11:23]]; again [[Josh. 14:15]]) uses a different verb, “was quiet,” found in Joshua only at these two verses.[@oshb-wlc] Then the LORD tells the aged Joshua: “there remaineth yet very much land to be possessed” ([[Josh. 13:1]]), to be divided by lot ([[Josh. 13:6]]), and some cities are not taken ([[Josh. 15:63]]; [[Josh. 16:10]]; [[Josh. 17:12–13]]). The book does not explain how the two statements fit together; possible readings are at [Joshua 13:1](/joshua/13/).

## Rest round about

“The LORD gave them rest round about, according to all that he sware unto their fathers… There failed not ought of any good thing which the LORD had spoken unto the house of Israel; all came to pass” ([[Josh. 21:44–45]]; compare [[Deut. 3:20]]; [[1 Kgs. 8:56]]).[@lds-scriptures] Then Joshua sends the eastern tribes home in the words of 1:15: “hath given rest unto your brethren, as he promised them” ([[Josh. 22:4]]; see [Joshua 22:3](/joshua/22/)).

## The farewell

The farewell opens “a long time after that the LORD had given rest unto Israel from all their enemies round about” ([[Josh. 23:1]]). Within four verses: “these nations that remain” ([[Josh. 23:4]]). If Israel cleaves to them, “they shall be snares and traps unto you, and scourges in your sides, and thorns in your eyes, until ye perish from off this good land” ([[Josh. 23:13]]); every good word has come, and the evil will come as surely if they transgress ([[Josh. 23:14–16]]).

Three times the rest is followed at once by what remains or by a condition ([[Josh. 1:13]] by 1:15; [[Josh. 11:23]] by 13:1; [[Josh. 23:1]] by 23:4, 13), and the full rest of 21:44–45 is turned into a warning when Joshua repeats it in 23:14–16. Set beside that pattern, Hebrews’ sentence reads as the same pattern carried to its end; that is this page’s comparison, not Hebrews’ argument.

<h2 id="christ">If Jesus had given them rest</h2>

“For if Jesus had given them rest, then would he not afterward have spoken of another day. There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God” ([[Heb. 4:8–9]]). “Jesus” is Joshua ([[Acts 7:45]]).[@lds-scriptures] Hebrews reasons from the Psalm’s later “To day,” spoken “in David” ([[Heb. 4:7]]).[@lds-scriptures] The rest Israel failed to enter “was symbolically represented by the land of Canaan,”[@nt-manual-hebrews-1-6] and Joshua “can remind us of Jesus Christ leading all the faithful into the ultimate land of promise, the celestial kingdom”;[@scripture-helps-josh] [Psalm 95:11](/psalms/95/) and [what was offered at Sinai](/exodus/themes/offered-at-sinai/#rest) take up the oath that Israel would not enter the rest.

Joshua’s last word on the rest is how it could be lost; Hebrews’ first word on the rest that remains is the same fear: “any of you should seem to come short of it” ([[Heb. 4:1]]).

Alma says who enters: “whosoever repenteth, and hardeneth not his heart, he shall have claim on mercy through mine Only Begotten Son, unto a remission of his sins; and these shall enter into my rest” ([[Alma 12:34]]; see [[D&C 84:24]]).[@lds-scriptures] “Many, exceedingly great many… were made pure and entered into the rest of the Lord their God” ([[Alma 13:12]]), and the ordinances were given that people “might look forward to him for a remission of their sins, that they might enter into the rest of the Lord” ([[Alma 13:16]]). To enter the Lord’s rest is to be “won over to Him, so that I can feel at rest in Christ.”[@jfs-teachings-48]

## “I will give you rest”

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest” ([[Matt. 11:28]]): the rest is offered now and is His to give. “The Lord has promised such rest to His faithful followers during this life. He has also prepared a place of rest for them in the next life.”[@gs-rest] Joshua’s charge was to “cleave unto the LORD your God” ([[Josh. 23:8]]); the rest from “the intensity, uncertainty, and anguish of this world” is found “by overcoming the world through your covenants with God.”[@nelson-overcome-world]
