At Jericho Rahab's household is spared and set outside Israel's camp; two verses later the narrator says Rahab is “in the midst of Israel.” The book then follows what Israel gives the outsider in its midst: the law read in the stranger's hearing, an oath kept to Gibeon, a refuge provided for the stranger. One man of Judah is carried out of that same midst.

## Outside the camp, then in the midst

Rahab asked an oath “by the LORD” to save her father's house alive ([[Josh. 2:12–13]]). The spies “brought out all her kindred, and left them without the camp of Israel” ([[Josh. 6:23]]), a phrase found only here in Joshua.[@lds-scriptures] Two verses on: “she dwelleth in Israel even unto this day” ([[Josh. 6:25]]). The Hebrew behind “in Israel” is *bĕqereb yiśrāʾēl*, “in the midst of Israel.”[@oshb-wlc] The King James Version translates it “merely ‘in’ Israel”; Rahab's place in Israel is “a far cry from her marginal existence in Jericho.”[@fisher-rahab] “Rahab and her family became part of the Israelite nation.”[@scripture-helps-josh]

The account does not say how the distance was crossed (see [chapter 6](/joshua/6/)).

## What the midst cannot hold

“There is an accursed thing in the midst of thee, O Israel … until ye take away the accursed thing from among you” ([[Josh. 7:13]]); the noun behind “midst” is the one behind Rahab's “in Israel” at 6:25.[@oshb-wlc] Achan, “of the tribe of Judah” ([[Josh. 7:1]]),[@bd-achan] is taken out to the valley of Achor ([[Josh. 7:24]]). Set side by side, Rahab comes in from outside the camp and Achan goes out to Achor (see [chapter 7](/joshua/7/)).

## The stranger at the ark

At Ebal the stranger stands with “he that was born among them” ([[Josh. 8:33]]), and Joshua reads the law “before all the congregation of Israel, with the women, and the little ones, and the strangers that were conversant among them” ([[Josh. 8:35]]). The word *gēr*, “stranger,” is in Joshua only at 8:33, 8:35 and 20:9;[@oshb-wlc] the text does not call Rahab one.

The stranger's standing is described two ways: he “did not share full membership in the covenant community,” though given “equal treatment”;[@seely-cry-widow] and “the foreigner could, through covenant, become part of Israel.”[@muhlestein-ruth] Whatever his standing, the stranger heard every word the native-born heard (see [chapter 8](/joshua/8/)).

## Let them live, and go up to save them

Joshua “made a league with” the Gibeonites, “to let them live” ([[Josh. 9:15]]). The oath is kept, sworn “by the LORD God of Israel” ([[Josh. 9:19]]), and Joshua makes them “bondmen, and hewers of wood and drawers of water for the house of my God” ([[Josh. 9:23]]): “the people of Gibeon became the perpetual slaves of Israel.”[@ot-manual-josh] Yet “the Gibeonites were not of the children of Israel” ([[2 Sam. 21:2]]).[@lds-scriptures]

Then they send, “come up to us quickly, and save us, and help us” ([[Josh. 10:6]]). “Faithful to the treaty, Joshua led the armies of Israel,”[@brandt-conquest] and “the LORD fought for Israel” ([[Josh. 10:14]]).

The verb “let live” (*ḥāyâ*) is the same in Rahab's “save alive” and “shall live” ([[Josh. 2:13]]; [[Josh. 6:17]]; [[Josh. 6:25]]) and in Gibeon's “let them live” ([[Josh. 9:15]]; [[Josh. 9:20]]; [[Josh. 9:21]]); both oaths were sworn by the LORD ([[Josh. 2:12]]; [[Josh. 9:19]]).[@oshb-wlc] Of the eight verses in Joshua with this verb, six are about Rahab and Gibeon; Gibeon's league is the second time the book lets a people live by an oath sworn by the LORD ([chapter 9](/joshua/9/); [chapter 10](/joshua/10/)).

## A refuge for the stranger

The cities of refuge are “for all the children of Israel, and for the stranger that sojourneth among them” ([[Josh. 20:9]]; compare [[Num. 35:15]]).[@lds-scriptures] They served “aliens who might not have any other judicial recourse.”[@ot-minute-josh-20] At Jericho and at Gibeon the text explains why the outsider is there; here the law provides for him ([chapter 20](/joshua/20/)).

<h2 id="christ">No more strangers</h2>

Rahab “perished not with them that believed not, when she had received the spies with peace” ([[Heb. 11:31]]), and “she is mentioned in Matthew as an ancestor of Jesus Christ” (see [[Matt. 1:5]]).[@scripture-helps-josh] The Lord says, “as many as have received me, to them have I given to become the sons of God … for behold, by me redemption cometh” ([[3 Ne. 9:17]]).[@lds-scriptures] A person “of non-Israelite lineage becomes a member of the house of Israel through faith in Jesus Christ” with baptism and the Holy Ghost.[@bd-adoption]

Rahab was brought from outside the camp into the midst by an oath Israel kept; the stranger at Ebal heard the covenant but, on one reading, did not share full membership in it; Gibeon lived as servants. Set beside them Paul's words: “strangers from the covenants of promise … far off … made nigh by the blood of Christ … no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God” ([[Eph. 2:12–19]]).[@lds-scriptures]

## Gathered into his family

The promised blessings go to “the literal seed of Abraham and those who are gathered into his family by adoption,” “predicated upon acceptance of the Lord and obedience to his commandments.”[@nelson-1995-children-of-the-covenant] Being brought in carries an obligation not to oppress the stranger: “Thou shalt neither vex a stranger, nor oppress him: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt” ([[Ex. 22:21]]).[@muhlestein-ruth]
