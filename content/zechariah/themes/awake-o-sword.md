A flock without a shepherd prices the one sent to it, and the covenant is broken. What changes is who the sword falls on: first a worthless shepherd, then the Lord's own. After that stroke the Lord again names them His people. Jesus quoted 13:7 on the night of His arrest, and the covenant blood of 9:11 stands beside the cup He gave that night.

## A flock with no shepherd

They are “the flock of his people” ([[Zech. 9:16]]); they “went their way as a flock… because there was no shepherd” ([[Zech. 10:2]]), and “the LORD of hosts hath visited his flock” ([[Zech. 10:3]]). “Jesus Christ is the true shepherd of our souls.”[@ot-manual-33] See Ezekiel's [One Shepherd](/ezekiel/themes/one-shepherd/).

## The shepherd they would not have

The prophet is told, “Feed the flock of the slaughter” ([[Zech. 11:4]]), and takes two staffs. He breaks the first “that I might break my covenant” ([[Zech. 11:10]]); the staffs “represent the broken covenant between God and His people—and the breakdown of national unity.”[@scripture-helps-hag-1-2] The wage is “thirty pieces of silver,” thrown “to the potter in the house of the LORD” ([[Zech. 11:12–13]]), “the price of a slave.”[@scripture-helps-hag-1-2] They “rejected Jehovah as their shepherd and protector.”[@seminary-ot-teacher-157] The flock put a price on the shepherd it refused. See [chapter 11](/zechariah/11/).

## Two swords

“Sword” is in Zechariah three times: 9:13, 11:17 and 13:7.[@oshb-wlc] The last two fall on shepherds. “Woe to the idol shepherd that leaveth the flock! the sword shall be upon his arm” ([[Zech. 11:17]]); the acts of that shepherd “are the Lord's dread judgment on a people which rejected the true shepherd.”[@ot-manual-33]

Then: “Awake, O sword, against my shepherd, and against the man that is my fellow, saith the LORD of hosts: smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered: and I will turn mine hand upon the little ones” ([[Zech. 13:7]]). One reading holds that this shepherd is not “the foolish shepherd mentioned in Zechariah 11:15–17” but “the Messiah,” the smiting taking place “with or according to the will of God.”[@keil-zechariah] “The Shepherd of Israel is Jesus Christ.”[@ot-manual-33]

Set side by side, the first sword falls on the shepherd who was himself the Lord's judgment on a people that rejected the true shepherd; the second falls, at the Lord's own word, on the Shepherd betrayed for thirty pieces, who “would be smitten and His followers would be scattered.”[@seminary-ot-teacher-157] See [13:7](/zechariah/13/).

## The night He quoted it

At the table Jesus gave the cup: “this is my blood of the new testament” ([[Matt. 26:28]]). Leaving for the Mount of Olives He said, “it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered abroad. But after I am risen again, I will go before you into Galilee” ([[Matt. 26:31–32]]).[@lds-scriptures] He used the prophecy “to anticipate how the disciples would flee.”[@huntsman-peters-denial] Galilee is “a practical exposition of the bringing back of the hand over the small ones.”[@ot-manual-33]

<h2 id="whose-blood">Whose blood</h2>

“By the blood of thy covenant I have sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit wherein is no water” ([[Zech. 9:11]]). In one reading, “the covenant blood belonged to all Israel of the twelve tribes (Exodus 24:8).”[@keil-zechariah] Elder McConkie read it as “the gospel covenant, which is efficacious because of the shedding of the blood of Christ,”[@ot-manual-33] and the prisoners are read as the dead.[@seminary-ot-teacher-157] See [9:11](/zechariah/9/).

The cup uses the words of Exodus: “Behold the blood of the covenant” ([[Ex. 24:8]]); in saying “my blood of the new testament,” Jesus “alluded to” that statement.[@nt-student-manual-9] Set beside each other, the covenant blood of 9:11, “efficacious because of the shedding of the blood of Christ,” and the cup's “my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many” ([[Matt. 26:28]]) both join blood and covenant, and the cup was given on the night the Shepherd was struck. See [Four Rites of Blood](/exodus/themes/blood-of-the-covenant/).

## It is my people

The third is brought “through the fire”: “I will say, It is my people: and they shall say, The LORD is my God” ([[Zech. 13:8–9]]). 13:8–9 is read with the last battle, when “only one-third of the nation of Israel will survive.”[@ot-manual-kings-i]

In 11:10 the staff is broken “that I might break my covenant.” It is read as the covenant between God and His people;[@scripture-helps-hag-1-2][@seminary-ot-teacher-157] others, from the Hebrew's plural “all the peoples,”[@oshb-wlc] read a covenant made with the nations.[@keil-zechariah] In 13:9 the Lord says “my people,” and the word “covenant” is not there.[@oshb-wlc] “People” here points to Jer. 30:22, “ye shall be my people, and I will be your God,” and Hosea 2:23.[@lds-scriptures] Set beside each other, the covenant relationship broken in 11:10 stands before the stroke, and the “my people … my God” of 13:9 after it. See [13:9](/zechariah/13/).

## The Shepherd struck for the flock

The Shepherd was refused and sold “by His own people,”[@ot-manual-33] and struck at the Lord's will. “I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep” ([[John 10:11]]).

What the Shepherd did for the scattered sheep reaches a disciple now. Those who fled were to be met in Galilee ([[Matt. 26:32]]). Jesus knew Peter better than Peter knew himself, and He knows each disciple the same way.[@nt-student-manual-9] “My sheep hear my voice, and I know them… and they shall never perish” ([[John 10:27–28]]). The covenant blood reaches those who cannot reach it themselves: the prisoners of 9:11 are read as the dead.[@seminary-ot-teacher-157]

Hebrews has shepherd and covenant blood in one verse: God “brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant” ([[Heb. 13:20]]).
