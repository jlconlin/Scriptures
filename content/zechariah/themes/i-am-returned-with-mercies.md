“Turn ye unto me, saith the LORD of hosts, and I will turn unto you” ([[Zech. 1:3]]). The Hebrew verb *šûb* is in Zechariah 18 times, and in eight of them the Lord is the one who turns.[@oshb-wlc] In the book's order they run from a promise (1:3) to “I am returned” (1:16), to a bringing home (10:6), to the last clause of 13:7. The people's turning is asked for throughout; His is given before theirs is complete.

| Step | Passage | The words |
|---|---|---|
| Turn, and I will turn | [[Zech. 1:3]] | “Turn ye unto me… and I will turn unto you” |
| The fathers would not | [[Zech. 1:4]] | “but they did not hear” |
| Returned with mercies | [[Zech. 1:16]] | “I am returned to Jerusalem with mercies” |
| Returned unto Zion | [[Zech. 8:3]] | “I am returned unto Zion” |
| Bring them again | [[Zech. 10:6]] | “I will bring them again… for I have mercy upon them” |
| The hand brought back | [[Zech. 13:7]] | “I will turn mine hand upon the little ones” |
| King over all the earth | [[Zech. 14:9]] | “the LORD shall be king over all the earth” |

## One verb, two sides

The one verb stands in both halves of [[Zech. 1:3]], as command and as promise.[@oshb-wlc] The fathers were told, “Turn ye now from your evil ways… but they did not hear” ([[Zech. 1:4]]). Chapter 7 gives the refusal: “they refused to hearken, and pulled away the shoulder, and stopped their ears,” and so “as he cried, and they would not hear; so they cried, and I would not hear” ([[Zech. 7:11–13]]). The fathers “returned and said” only after His words had taken hold of them ([[Zech. 1:6]]).

The returned exiles are asked to turn before that. Their return “was not a perfect one”; “So had Jehovah also turned to the people again,” and “the more earnestly… the people turned to Him, the more… would He bestow upon them His grace.”[@keil-zechariah] See [chapter 1](/zechariah/1/).

## Returned with mercies

The call to turn came in “the eighth month” ([[Zech. 1:1]]); in the vision of “the eleventh month” ([[Zech. 1:7]]) the angel of the LORD asks, “how long wilt thou not have mercy on Jerusalem?” ([[Zech. 1:12]]), and the Lord answers, “I am returned to Jerusalem with mercies” ([[Zech. 1:16]]): the verb of 1:3, now spoken as done.[@oshb-wlc] He says it again: “I am returned unto Zion” ([[Zech. 8:3]]).

Set side by side, the mercy asked for in 1:12 is what the Lord says He has returned with in 1:16, and it is the reason He gives in 10:6, “for I have mercy upon them.” The Lord's mercy is named in these three verses; the only other “mercy” in the book is the people's duty to each other ([[Zech. 7:9]]).[@oshb-wlc]

## I will bring them again

From chapter 9 the Lord's verb is the causative form each time, the form that makes another turn back: “I will render double” ([[Zech. 9:12]]), “I will bring them again” ([[Zech. 10:6]]; [[Zech. 10:10]]).[@oshb-wlc] Chapter 9 has both sides again: “Turn you to the strong hold, ye prisoners of hope.” Of Judah and Joseph: “I will bring them again to place them; for I have mercy upon them: and they shall be as though I had not cast them off” ([[Zech. 10:6]]). The sown people “shall remember me in far countries… and turn again” ([[Zech. 10:9]]). See [chapter 10](/zechariah/10/).

## His hand brought back

“Awake, O sword, against my shepherd… smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered: and I will turn mine hand upon the little ones” ([[Zech. 13:7]]). The last clause is the causative; set side by side, it is the form of “bring them again” at 10:6 and 10:10, not of 1:3.[@oshb-wlc] The bringing back of the hand “is to be understood here in a good sense”: “the Lord soon brought back His hand over the disciples.”[@ot-manual-33] And “Zechariah 13:9 shows how the bringing back of the hand upon the small ones will be realized in the remnant of the nation.”[@keil-zechariah] There the third part is refined: “they shall call on my name, and I will hear them: I will say, It is my people: and they shall say, The LORD is my God” ([[Zech. 13:9]]). The sword's verse has its own page: [“Awake, O Sword, against My Shepherd”](/zechariah/themes/awake-o-sword/).

<h2 id="christ">Before They Had Finished Turning</h2>

“Jesus Christ and Jehovah… are, in fact, the same person,”[@olmstead-saviors-use] so the Lord who promises “I will turn unto you” ([[Zech. 1:3]]) is “returned” ([[Zech. 1:16]]) and says “I will bring them again” ([[Zech. 10:6]]) is Jesus Christ. “The Shepherd of Israel is Jesus Christ.”[@ot-manual-33] On the night of His arrest He said, “it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered abroad,” and went on, “after I am risen again, I will go before you into Galilee” ([[Matt. 26:31–32]]). Of 13:7's last clause: it “began to be fulfilled in the gathering together of the disciples by the risen Saviour.”[@ot-manual-33] Of 14:9, “Jesus Christ will reign as ‘king over all the earth.’”[@scripture-helps-hag-1-2]

In this book His turning is given before the exiles' own is finished.[@keil-zechariah] To a scattered people He promises that they shall be “as though I had not cast them off.”

## Where this reaches now

The Lord who promised the exiles “I will turn unto you” gives the same two-sided promise to the Saints: “Draw near unto me and I will draw near unto you; seek me diligently and ye shall find me” ([[D&C 88:63]]), the verse the Church's footnote gives at 1:3.[@lds-scriptures] Of Saints who, like the fathers of 1:4 and 7:11–13, were slow to hearken: “They were slow to hearken unto the voice of the Lord their God… I will not utterly cast them off; and in the day of wrath I will remember mercy” ([[D&C 101:7–9]]). Set beside 10:6, “as though I had not cast them off,” the words are the Lord's in both places.
