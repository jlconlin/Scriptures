Zechariah sees a high priest in filthy garments, and the book ends with “HOLINESS UNTO THE LORD” on the horses' bells and every pot. Between them, what the Lord removes widens from one man's iniquity to the land's, and then to the city's sin and uncleanness.

## One man, and he does nothing

Joshua stands “clothed with filthy garments” ([[Zech. 3:3]]). The order is “Take away the filthy garments from him,” and to him, “I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee” ([[Zech. 3:4]]). No word of Joshua's is given. The filthy garment “represents sin.”[@ot-manual-33] Zechariah asks, “Let them set a fair mitre upon his head” ([[Zech. 3:5]]). The clean turban is read as the base for the golden plate of [[Ex. 28:36–38]].[@keil-zechariah] Then: “If thou wilt walk in my ways, and if thou wilt keep my charge” ([[Zech. 3:7]]). The cleansing is done to him; the walking is asked of him.

## The land, in one day; wickedness carried out

“I will remove the iniquity of that land in one day” ([[Zech. 3:9]]). The Branch is read as the one who will do it,[@bd-zechariah][@scripture-helps-hag-1-2] and the day as the Second Coming.[@lds-scriptures]

Chapter 5 shows removal as judgment: the curse “shall consume it with the timber thereof and the stones thereof” ([[Zech. 5:4]]); “This is wickedness” ([[Zech. 5:8]]), carried off, “the removing of the sin of the people to Shinar.”[@bd-zechariah]

## A fountain for the city

The Lord pours out the spirit of grace, and “they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him” ([[Zech. 12:10]]). John quotes it of the cross ([[John 19:37]]). The Lord says, “then shall they weep because of their iniquities” ([[D&C 45:53]]). Then “a fountain opened to the house of David and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem for sin and for uncleanness” ([[Zech. 13:1]]). The outpoured spirit and the fountain are read as one movement,[@bd-zechariah] and the fountain is linked to baptism.[@lds-scriptures]

“I will cause the prophets and the unclean spirit to pass out of the land” ([[Zech. 13:2]]). The verb is the one of [[Zech. 3:4]], “caused to pass”: the Lord removing sin in one verse and uncleanness in the other.[@oshb-wlc]

Not all the land comes through: “two parts therein shall be cut off and die; but the third shall be left therein,” and “refine them as silver is refined” ([[Zech. 13:8–9]]).

## Every pot

“In that day shall there be upon the bells of the horses, HOLINESS UNTO THE LORD; and the pots in the LORD's house shall be like the bowls before the altar. Yea, every pot in Jerusalem and in Judah shall be holiness unto the LORD of hosts” ([[Zech. 14:20–21]]). They are the words of the high priest's plate, the same two Hebrew words ([[Ex. 28:36]]).[@oshb-wlc] Set beside chapter 3, where the clean turban is read as the base for the plate that bore these words,[@keil-zechariah] the scenes show the words first on one priest and last on horses' harness and cooking pots.

The line between holy and common is read as ending only “when the sin and moral defilement… have been entirely removed and wiped away.”[@keil-zechariah] Not everyone: the nations that will not go up have “no rain” ([[Zech. 14:17]]), and there is “no more the Canaanite” in the house, read as “the wicked.”[@ot-manual-33]

<h2 id="christ">For the whole city</h2>

Joshua “was a type of Jesus Christ, the great High Priest.”[@ot-manual-33] The pierced one is Jesus: “I am Jesus that was crucified” ([[D&C 45:52]]).

Haggai's priests answered “No”: holy flesh in a garment does not make bread holy, while one unclean by the dead makes what he touches unclean ([[Hag. 2:12–13]]). Zechariah's “uncleanness” in [[Zech. 13:1]] is the Hebrew word of the “water of separation” of [[Num. 19:9]],[@oshb-wlc] the water without which one who touched the dead “shall be unclean” ([[Num. 19:13]]), the uncleanness of [[Hag. 2:13]]. Set side by side, the holy pots come after the fountain, not instead of it: holiness is not caught from holy things, and the Lord makes a people holy by taking their sin away. See [“Shall It Be Holy?”](/haggai/themes/shall-it-be-holy/).

## Where this reaches now

“If we repent, Jesus Christ can take away our sins. We are forgiven and can become more holy,”[@liahona-2022-12-holy] and the condition of [[Zech. 3:7]] stands. The inscription reaches the common things of a day: “We seek it even in the details of daily living: our speech, our dress, our thoughts.”[@christofferson-living-bread] It “must be engraved not just on a temple wall, or even on ‘the bells of the horses’ and ‘every pot in Jerusalem’… They must also be engraved on every heart.”[@cfm-ot-2026-50]
