# Deuteronomy: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.**

## Author's selection

None yet. The list is proposed at the end of the book (`propose-themes` by Fable, then Codex's review; `AUTHORING.md`, “Theme candidates at the end of a book”), and only the author's choice is recorded here. Fable's and Codex's lists of 2026-10-16 are in `.cache/batch/deuteronomy/reports/themes-fable.md` and `themes-codex.md`; shown to the author as nine proposals, not yet judged.

**The author's reading of proposal 4, “Thou shalt not go over this Jordan”** (2026-10-16, not yet a selection): the arc is the righteous prayer refused. “There are many times in life when we want something … and are righteous enough to reasonably expect to receive it. However, in God's wisdom, He is not going to give it to us… This is similar to Moses' experience.” So, if chosen, the page is about the prayer: Moses heard for Israel (9:19; 10:10) and refused for himself (3:23–26), the reason the text gives (32:51; Num. 20:12; “for your sakes,” 1:37), shown the land (34:1–4), and standing in it with the Savior (Matt. 17:3). Its witness of Christ is Gethsemane, “nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt” (Matt. 26:39), and Heb. 5:7–8; beside it 2 Cor. 12:9 and D&C 122:7. It applies the arc to anyone who righteously asks for what the Lord does not give (`STANDARDS.md` §8, “It applies the arc to the reader”). Psalms' *The Savior Enters the Unanswered Prayer* is linked, not repeated.

## Leads seen while drafting the brief (2026-10-15)

Working leads for the proposers, not candidates; each must still pass §8 (“The insight is about Christ's work”).

- **Remember, lest thou forget.** “Remember” and “forget” run through the speeches (4:9; 6:12; 8:2, 11–18; 9:7; 15:15; 16:3, 12; 24:18, 22; 32:7, 18), and memory is tied to redemption: “thou wast a bondman … and the LORD thy God redeemed thee” (15:15). The arc runs from a people told to remember what they saw, to a song written so that a later people who forget will still have a witness (31:19–21), toward the sacrament's “always remember him.” Count the words with `occurrences.mjs --stem` first.
- **The heart circumcised.** From the command “Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart” (10:16) to the promise “the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart … to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart” (30:6): what Israel is told to do, the Lord promises to do for them after the scattering. Jeremiah 4:4 and 31:33 on the site, Romans 2:29. A change of nature as the Lord's gift.
- **A prophet like unto me.** Moses as mediator at Horeb (5:5, 23–27), the people's request that brings the promise (18:16–18), Moses barred from the land for the people's sake (1:37; 3:26; 4:21), and the closing word that none arose like him (34:10), set beside the One who fulfils it (Acts 3:22–23; 3 Ne. 20:23). Exodus's theme page *Moses the Mediator* already has a section on 18:15: a Deuteronomy page would have to add the arc within this book, or it is not written.
- **The place the LORD shall choose.** One sanctuary “to put his name there” (12:5, 11, 21; 14:23; 16:2, 6, 11; 26:2), where the feasts and the firstfruits are brought, and where the rejoicing is shared with the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless and the widow (16:11, 14). Where it reaches Christ is not yet clear; it may be the volume's subject rather than the book's.
- **Choose life.** The two ways set before Israel at Ebal and Gerizim (11:26–29; 27–28) and on the plains of Moab (30:15–20), in which life is a person to cleave to (“he is thy life,” 30:20), beside Lehi's “free to choose liberty and eternal life” (2 Ne. 2:27) and the Savior's “I am … the life.”
