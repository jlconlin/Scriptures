# Deuteronomy: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.**

## Author's selection (2026-10-16)

From nine proposals merged from Fable's and Codex's lists of 2026-10-16 (`.cache/batch/deuteronomy/reports/themes-fable.md`, `themes-codex.md`), the author chose five, in this order (author: “1,3,2,5,4”). Each also applies its arc to the reader (`STANDARDS.md` §8, “It applies the arc to the reader”); the application lines below are the coordinator's sketches for the outline, each resting on scripture a page can cite.

1. **“For he is thy life”** (`for-he-is-thy-life`; Fable 1, with Codex's “Choose life” and “circumcised heart”). The heart as wish (5:29), command (10:16), not yet given (29:4), and the LORD's gift (30:6), whose purpose clause is the noun “for thy life,” the word of 30:20 “he is thy life”; 4:4 and 30:20 share *cleave* and *life*. Christ: Jer. 31:33; Ezek. 36:26; Mosiah 5:2; the page's own, marked: Col. 2:11 and John 14:6. Applies: the love the first commandment asks is received as well as given; Moro. 7:48. Ezekiel's *A New Heart and a New Spirit* is linked, not repeated.
2. **“He turned the curse into a blessing”** (`curse-into-blessing`; Fable 3). The curse offered (11:26), placed on Ebal (11:29), spoken with twelve Amens (27:15–26), overtaking (28:15, 45), turned onto the enemies (30:1, 7), and a last chapter all blessing (33); 23:5 as the hinge, “because the LORD thy God loved thee,” sharing the noun *qelalah* with 21:23. Christ: the Church's footnote at 21:23 to Gal. 3:13–14; the page's own, marked: 23:5 beside Gal. 3:13 and John 3:16. Applies: D&C 90:24 (the footnote at 23:5). Must not say 23:5 predicts the cross.
3. **“Speak not thou in thine heart”** (`speak-not-in-thine-heart`; Fable 2, with Codex's “Not by thy righteousness”). The five things Israel says in its heart (7:17; 8:17; 9:4; 18:21; 29:19), each answered from outside it; 9:4–6 and 6:25 set side by side, not reconciled; 30:14. Christ: Rom. 10:4–8 on 30:12–14; the page's own, marked: Rom. 10:6 opens with the words of 9:4 (the same Greek clause as the Septuagint's); 2 Ne. 2:5; 25:23. Applies: Alma 38:11. No argument about faith and works beyond the verses.
4. **“Hear ye him”** (`hear-ye-him`; Fable 5, with Codex's “A prophet like unto me”). The voice at Horeb (4:12; 5:24–27), the request and the promise (18:15–18), none like Moses (34:10). Christ: the Church's footnotes at Matt. 17:6 (to Deut. 5:5) and John 12:50 (to Deut. 18:18); Acts 3:22–23; 3 Ne. 11:3–8; 20:23. Applies: President Nelson, “Hear Him” (April 2020), if read and it says so. Exodus's *Moses the Mediator* owns the office and the intercession: linked, not repeated.
5. **The righteous prayer refused** (`let-it-suffice-thee`; Fable 4, as the author reads it). The author (2026-10-16): “There are many times in life when we want something … and are righteous enough to reasonably expect to receive it. However, in God's wisdom, He is not going to give it to us… This is similar to Moses' experience.” Moses heard for Israel (9:19; 10:10) and refused for himself (3:23–26), with the reason the text gives (32:51; Num. 20:12; “for your sakes,” 1:37), shown the land (34:1–4), and standing in it with the Savior (Matt. 17:3). Christ: Gethsemane, “nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt” (Matt. 26:39); Heb. 5:7–8. Applies: 2 Cor. 12:9 and D&C 122:7. Leaves the voice on the Mount to page 4; Psalms' *The Savior Enters the Unanswered Prayer* is linked, not repeated.

## Leads seen while drafting the brief (2026-10-15)

Working leads for the proposers, not candidates; each must still pass §8 (“The insight is about Christ's work”).

- **Remember, lest thou forget.** “Remember” and “forget” run through the speeches (4:9; 6:12; 8:2, 11–18; 9:7; 15:15; 16:3, 12; 24:18, 22; 32:7, 18), and memory is tied to redemption: “thou wast a bondman … and the LORD thy God redeemed thee” (15:15). The arc runs from a people told to remember what they saw, to a song written so that a later people who forget will still have a witness (31:19–21), toward the sacrament's “always remember him.” Count the words with `occurrences.mjs --stem` first.
- **The heart circumcised.** From the command “Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart” (10:16) to the promise “the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart … to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart” (30:6): what Israel is told to do, the Lord promises to do for them after the scattering. Jeremiah 4:4 and 31:33 on the site, Romans 2:29. A change of nature as the Lord's gift.
- **A prophet like unto me.** Moses as mediator at Horeb (5:5, 23–27), the people's request that brings the promise (18:16–18), Moses barred from the land for the people's sake (1:37; 3:26; 4:21), and the closing word that none arose like him (34:10), set beside the One who fulfils it (Acts 3:22–23; 3 Ne. 20:23). Exodus's theme page *Moses the Mediator* already has a section on 18:15: a Deuteronomy page would have to add the arc within this book, or it is not written.
- **The place the LORD shall choose.** One sanctuary “to put his name there” (12:5, 11, 21; 14:23; 16:2, 6, 11; 26:2), where the feasts and the firstfruits are brought, and where the rejoicing is shared with the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless and the widow (16:11, 14). Where it reaches Christ is not yet clear; it may be the volume's subject rather than the book's.
- **Choose life.** The two ways set before Israel at Ebal and Gerizim (11:26–29; 27–28) and on the plains of Moab (30:15–20), in which life is a person to cleave to (“he is thy life,” 30:20), beside Lehi's “free to choose liberty and eternal life” (2 Ne. 2:27) and the Savior's “I am … the life.”
