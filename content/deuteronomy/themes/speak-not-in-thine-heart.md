Four times, at 7:17, 8:17, 9:4 and 18:21, Moses gives Israel a sentence it might say in its heart, each with one Hebrew clause, “say in thine heart.”[@oshb-wlc] Each time the answer comes from outside the heart, and in the Hebrew only at 9:4 is the saying forbidden outright. Reading the four as one sequence is this page's own, as is each comparison below made without a citation; the step to Christ is Paul's ([[Rom. 10:6–8]]).

| My power got this | [[Deut. 8:17–18]] | “My power… hath gotten me this wealth… thou shalt remember the LORD thy God” |

<h2 id="if-thou-say">If thou shalt say in thine heart</h2>

“If thou shalt say in thine heart, These nations are more than I” ([[Deut. 7:17]]); the answer is a memory: “shalt well remember what the LORD thy God did unto Pharaoh” ([[Deut. 7:18]]). Then: “thou say in thine heart, My power and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth” ([[Deut. 8:17]]); the answer: “thou shalt remember the LORD thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power” ([[Deut. 8:18]]). Set beside each other, the two share the clause and the same answer, “remember” (*zakar*),[@oshb-wlc] and what the heart speaks of has moved from the enemy to itself.

The last of the four is a question, “How shall we know the word which the LORD hath not spoken?” ([[Deut. 18:21]]), and the answer is what comes to pass: “if the thing follow not, nor come to pass” ([[Deut. 18:22]]).

<h2 id="speak-not">Speak not thou in thine heart</h2>

“Speak not thou in thine heart… saying, For my righteousness the LORD hath brought me in to possess this land: but for the wickedness of these nations the LORD doth drive them out” ([[Deut. 9:4]]). Here the Hebrew is the negative command *ʾal tōʾmar* (“Speak not thou”); the other three are “if thou say” and “and thou say.”[@oshb-wlc] Verses 5–6 deny it twice more: “Not for thy righteousness.” The noun “righteousness” (*ṣedaqah*) stands six times in the book, three of them at 9:4–6, each with a possessive and each denied.[@oshb-wlc] Across 7:17, 8:17 and 9:4 the heart's sentence moves from the enemy to Israel's hand to Israel's own righteousness, and the one Moses forbids outright in the Hebrew is the last. See [[Deut. 9:1–6]].

It also stands at [[Deut. 6:25]]: “it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do all these commandments.”[@oshb-wlc]

<h2 id="in-thy-heart">In thy mouth, and in thy heart</h2>

At the end the shape returns, without the clause. “It is not in heaven, that thou shouldest say, Who shall go up for us to heaven… Neither is it beyond the sea, that thou shouldest say, Who shall go over the sea for us” ([[Deut. 30:12–13]]). The answer is not “remember” but “the word is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it” ([[Deut. 30:14]]). Set beside the four, this has the same shape, something Israel might say and an answer. See [[Deut. 30:12]].

Just before, a different form: a man who hears the curse “bless himself in his heart, saying, I shall have peace, though I walk in the imagination of mine heart,” and “The LORD will not spare him” ([[Deut. 29:19–20]]). It says “bless himself… saying,” in the third person, not the clause of the four.[@oshb-wlc] See [[Deut. 29:19]].

<h2 id="christ">The righteousness of faith speaks</h2>

“Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth” ([[Rom. 10:4]]). Then: “the righteousness which is of faith speaketh on this wise, Say not in thine heart, Who shall ascend into heaven? (that is, to bring Christ down from above:)… The word is nigh thee, even in thy mouth, and in thy heart” ([[Rom. 10:6–8]]).

Paul's opening words are not the Septuagint's wording at 30:12 (“λέγων Τίς ἀναβήσεται,” “saying, Who shall go up”). They are, letter for letter, its clause at 8:17 and at 9:4, “μὴ εἴπῃς ἐν τῇ καρδίᾳ σου”; the Greek forbids both, where the Hebrew forbids only 9:4.[@swete-lxx-deut; @greek-nt] Rom. 10:6–8 is listed with “Deuteronomy 9:4; 30:12–14.”[@ludlow-paul-ot] Set beside each other: Moses forbade saying in the heart that righteousness had brought Israel in; the righteousness of faith forbids, in the same words, saying that Christ must be brought down or up.

Lehi: “by the law no flesh is justified… redemption cometh in and through the Holy Messiah; for he is full of grace and truth” ([[2 Ne. 2:5–6]]). The Church joins [[Rom. 10:4]] and [[2 Ne. 2:7]] by footnote in both directions.[@lds-scriptures]

<h2 id="now">The same sentence now</h2>

Alma tells his son, “there is no other way or means whereby man can be saved, only in and through Christ” ([[Alma 38:9]]), and two verses later, “see that ye do not boast in your own wisdom, nor of your much strength” ([[Alma 38:11]]).

Remembering is the answer Moses gave twice. Elder Renlund: “By reflecting on Their compassion, we become more humble, prayerful, and steadfast.”[@renlund-goodness-greatness] Through faith in the Atonement people “receive strength and assistance to do good works that they otherwise would not be able to maintain if left to their own means,” yet “grace cannot suffice without total effort on the part of the recipient.”[@bd-grace] A disciple's good works are received as well as done, so the sentence of 9:4 has no more place in a disciple's heart than in Israel's.
