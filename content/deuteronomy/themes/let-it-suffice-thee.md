When Moses prayed for Israel, “the LORD hearkened unto me at that time also” ([[Deut. 9:19]]; [[Deut. 10:10]]). When he prayed for himself, “let me go over, and see the good land” ([[Deut. 3:25]]), the LORD “would not hear me” ([[Deut. 3:26]]). “Hearkened” and “hear” are one Hebrew verb, affirmed when Moses prays for Israel and negated when he prays for himself.[@oshb-wlc] This page reads these passages as one sequence, ending with Moses on the mount with the Savior ([[Matt. 17:3]]), and sets it beside Gethsemane ([[Matt. 26:39]]).

## Heard for Israel

After the calf Moses “fell down before the LORD” forty days and nights ([[Deut. 9:18]]) and said, “O Lord GOD, destroy not thy people” ([[Deut. 9:26]]). Both times, “the LORD hearkened unto me at that time also.” For his office as mediator, see [Moses the Mediator](/exodus/themes/moses-the-mediator/#offer).

## Refused, with a reason

Moses “besought the LORD” ([[Deut. 3:23]]): “let me go over.” The answer: “the LORD was wroth with me for your sakes, and would not hear me: and the LORD said unto me, Let it suffice thee; speak no more unto me of this matter” ([[Deut. 3:26]]). He asked “let me go over”; the answer is “thou shalt not go over” ([[Deut. 3:27]]; [[Deut. 34:4]]).

“Let it suffice thee” is the Hebrew of “long enough” at [[Deut. 1:6]] and [[Deut. 2:3]], said there to Israel in the plural and here to Moses alone.[@oshb-wlc] Set beside those verses, the word that sent Israel forward now stops Moses where he is.

The text gives the reason: “for your sakes” ([[Deut. 1:37]]; [[Deut. 4:21]]), and at the end, “because ye sanctified me not in the midst of the children of Israel” ([[Deut. 32:51]]; compare [[Num. 20:12]]). The note “Why Moses could not cross,” at [[Deut. 3:26]], sets them side by side.

## Given to see, and on the mount

The refusal is followed at once by a command: “lift up thine eyes… and behold it with thine eyes” ([[Deut. 3:27]]). At the end “the LORD shewed him all the land” ([[Deut. 34:1]]), and Moses died “according to the word of the LORD” ([[Deut. 34:5]]).

His “actions did not disqualify him from all future responsibilities and blessings.”[@scripture-helps-num] “Although Moses never set foot in the ‘land flowing with milk and honey’… he did enter the promised land that God has prepared for all those who follow Him.”[@cfm-ot-2026-20] Matthew: “there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him” ([[Matt. 17:3]]).

Moses “committed unto us the keys of the gathering of Israel” ([[D&C 110:11]]). Set beside “ye shall not bring this congregation into the land” ([[Num. 20:12]]), the man who was not to bring Israel in held the keys of their gathering.

## The cup not removed

The Savior prayed, “if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt” ([[Matt. 26:39]]); and an angel appeared, “strengthening him” ([[Luke 22:43]]). The Father’s answer “was to deny the plea of his Only Begotten Son. The Atonement had to be worked out by that lamb without blemish. But though the Son’s request was denied, his prayer was answered.”[@oaks-1994-faith] Hebrews: He “was heard in that he feared” ([[Heb. 5:7]]).

Set beside each other: of Moses, the LORD “would not hear” him; of the Son, He “was heard,” the King James word in both. The one the LORD would not hear was shown the land and stood on the mount with the Savior; the One the Father heard drank the cup. Moses did not go over, and the cup did not pass; in both something was given in its place.

The Savior, who says in [[3 Ne. 15:5]], “I am he that gave the law,” prayed “the third time, saying the same words” ([[Matt. 26:44]]). Set beside 3:26, He who told Moses to stop asking ended with “thy will be done” ([[Matt. 26:42]]). This compares two scenes; it does not say what the LORD intended at 3:26. For God’s silence, see [The Savior Enters the Unanswered Prayer](/psalms/themes/the-unanswered-prayer/).

## Sufficient for thee

Paul “besought the Lord thrice” (the King James word of [[Deut. 3:23]]) and was told, “My grace is sufficient for thee” ([[2 Cor. 12:8–9]]). The Lord “declined to heal him”; “sometimes we are ‘healed’ by being given strength or understanding or patience to bear the burdens placed upon us.”[@oaks-2006-heals] And “sometimes our righteous desires are not granted in this life,” though God “answers our prayers perfectly, according to His infinite wisdom.”[@hales-2019-answers]

A disciple who asks for a good thing and is refused stands where Moses and Paul stood. “An important element of all of our prayers might well be to follow the pattern of that prayer in Gethsemane.”[@faust-2002-lifeline] The Lord’s word to Joseph Smith, a prisoner in the jail at Liberty,[@lds-scriptures] was that “all these things shall give thee experience, and shall be for thy good” ([[D&C 122:7]]).
