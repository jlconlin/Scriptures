Deuteronomy tells of Horeb as a day Israel heard God and did not see Him. The people asked to hear Moses instead, the LORD approved and promised a prophet with His words in his mouth, and the book closes with that promise open ([[Deut. 34:10]]). The Church's footnotes carry the scene to the disciples “sore afraid” at the voice from the cloud ([[Matt. 17:6]]) and to “even as the Father said unto me, so I speak” ([[John 12:50]]).[@lds-scriptures] “Hear ye him” is the Father's phrase, not Deuteronomy's; reading these passages as one sequence is this page's. Moses standing between is followed in [Moses the Mediator](/exodus/themes/moses-the-mediator/); this page follows the voice.

<h2 id="voice">A voice, and no form</h2>

“The LORD spake unto you out of the midst of the fire: ye heard the voice of the words, but saw no similitude; only ye heard a voice” ([[Deut. 4:12]]). Moses asks whether any people ever heard “the voice of God speaking out of the midst of the fire, as thou hast heard, and live” ([[Deut. 4:33]]); the Church's footnote at that verse points to [[3 Ne. 11:3]].[@lds-scriptures]

<h2 id="request">Speak thou unto us</h2>

The people say the wonder themselves: “God doth talk with man, and he liveth” ([[Deut. 5:24]]). Next: “if we hear the voice of the LORD our God any more, then we shall die” ([[Deut. 5:25]]). What they ask for is a man: “Go thou near, and hear… and speak thou unto us… and we will hear it, and do it” ([[Deut. 5:27]]). The LORD answers, “They have well said all that they have spoken” ([[Deut. 5:28]]). The footnote at 5:27 is [[D&C 1:38]].[@lds-scriptures] The book does not say how the approval fits the people's drawing back; see [Deuteronomy 5](/deuteronomy/5/).

<h2 id="promise">My words in his mouth</h2>

Moses gives Horeb as the reason for the promise ([[Deut. 18:16]]). The LORD says, “I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee, and will put my words in his mouth; and he shall speak unto them all that I shall command him. And it shall come to pass, that whosoever will not hearken unto my words which he shall speak in my name, I will require it of him” ([[Deut. 18:18–19]]). Set beside chapter 5, the man's voice the people asked for and the voice of God they drew back from are one for the hearer.

“We will hear it” ([[Deut. 5:27]]), “unto him ye shall hearken” ([[Deut. 18:15]]) and “will not hearken” ([[Deut. 18:19]]) are one Hebrew verb, *šamaʿ*, the book's everyday word for hearing, so the point rests on the scene and not the word.[@oshb-wlc] “After His Resurrection, Jesus Christ declared that He was the fulfillment of Moses's prophecy.”[@scripture-helps-deut] See [Deuteronomy 18](/deuteronomy/18/).

<h2 id="none-like-moses">None like Moses</h2>

“And there arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face” ([[Deut. 34:10]]). The footnote at “prophet” is [[Deut. 18:15]].[@lds-scriptures] The book that promised a prophet like Moses ends by saying none has arisen.

<h2 id="hear-ye-him">Hear ye him</h2>

On the mount, “a voice out of the cloud… said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him. And when the disciples heard it, they fell on their face, and were sore afraid” ([[Matt. 17:5–6]]). The Church's footnote at “afraid” is [[Deut. 5:5]].[@lds-scriptures] Set beside [[Deut. 5:22]], “out of the midst of the fire, of the cloud, and of the thick darkness,” the two scenes match: a voice from a cloud on a mountain ([[Matt. 17:1]]), hearers afraid, Moses present ([[Matt. 17:3]]). The LORD who spoke at Horeb is Jehovah, the premortal Christ (see the Witness of Christ notes at [Deuteronomy 4](/deuteronomy/4/) and [Deuteronomy 5](/deuteronomy/5/)). At Horeb the voice “added no more”; on the mount the Father's voice names the Son as the one to hear.

Jesus says, “whatsoever I speak therefore, even as the Father said unto me, so I speak” ([[John 12:50]]); the footnote at “speak” is [[Deut. 18:18]].[@lds-scriptures] Set beside the request of [[Deut. 5:27]], God's words in the mouth of one of their brethren is how the Son describes His own speaking. At Bountiful the voice was “not a harsh voice, neither was it a loud voice,” and then “they saw a Man descending out of heaven” ([[3 Ne. 11:3–8]]); the footnote at “voice” is [[Deut. 4:33]].[@lds-scriptures]

<h2 id="now">Hearing Him now</h2>

The Lord says of His church, “whether by mine own voice or by the voice of my servants, it is the same” ([[D&C 1:38]]), and gives the other side: “they who will not hear the voice of the Lord, neither the voice of his servants… shall be cut off from among the people” ([[D&C 1:14]]).[@lds-scriptures] To hearken is “to hear and obey the voice or teachings of the Lord.”[@gs-hearken] “To hearken means to ‘hear Him’—to hear what the Savior says and then to heed His counsel.”[@nelson-hear-him] Set beside the people's “hear it, and do it,” it is the same pairing, hearing and then doing. And “when we are surrounded by uncertainty and fear, what will help us the very most is to hear His Son”; “we hear Him as we heed the words of prophets, seers, and revelators.”[@nelson-hear-him] God's words in His servants' mouths is still the arrangement.
