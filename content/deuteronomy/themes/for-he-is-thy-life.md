At Horeb the LORD wishes for a heart Israel does not have, “O that there were such an heart in them” ([[Deut. 5:29]]); in Moab Moses promises a heart the LORD gives: “the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart… to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart” ([[Deut. 30:6]]). “Circumcise” stands in the book only at 10:16, the command, and 30:6, the promise.[@oshb-wlc] Reading 5:29, 10:16, 29:4 and 30:6 as one sequence is this page's. What the gift is “for” is a word the King James hides, “thy life,” and fourteen verses later that word is the LORD: “for he is thy life” ([[Deut. 30:20]]).[@oshb-wlc]

|---|---|---|
| Wished for | [[Deut. 5:29]] | “O that there were such an heart in them” |
| Commanded | [[Deut. 6:5]]; [[Deut. 10:16]] | “love the LORD thy God with all thine heart”; “Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart” |
| Not given | [[Deut. 29:4]] | “the LORD hath not given you an heart to perceive… unto this day” |
| Given, for thy life | [[Deut. 30:6]] | “the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart… that thou mayest live” |

<h2 id="wished">Wished for</h2>

The LORD speaks here, answering the people's request at Horeb ([[Deut. 5:28]]): “O that there were such an heart in them, that they would fear me, and keep all my commandments always, that it might be well with them, and with their children for ever!” ([[Deut. 5:29]]). “O that” is *mi yitten*, literally “who will give,” Hebrew's way of voicing a wish.[@oshb-wlc]

<h2 id="commanded">Commanded</h2>

“Thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart” ([[Deut. 6:5]]) is “the central and greatest commandment,” which Moses reiterates at 30:6, 10, 16 and 20.[@muhlestein-love-neighbor] Then the command to Israel: “Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart, and be no more stiffnecked” ([[Deut. 10:16]]), an imperative with Israel as its subject.[@oshb-wlc]

<h2 id="not-given">Not given</h2>

In Moab, forty years on: “Yet the LORD hath not given you an heart to perceive, and eyes to see, and ears to hear, unto this day” ([[Deut. 29:4]]). The noun here is *lev*, where 5:29 and 30:6 have *levav*, so what joins these steps is the verb “give,” not the noun.[@oshb-wlc] Set beside 5:29, the heart wished for at Horeb has not been given “unto this day.”

<h2 id="given">Given</h2>

“And the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart, and the heart of thy seed, to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, that thou mayest live” ([[Deut. 30:6]]). Moses has said what comes first: “shalt return unto the LORD thy God, and shalt obey his voice” ([[Deut. 30:2]]). Then the promise. The verb is the one of 10:16, now with the LORD as subject, and the love of 6:5 is now the purpose of His act. A circumcised heart means “a change in their nature,” and this verse is tied to 10:16, [[Jer. 4:4]] and [[Jer. 31:33]].[@scripture-helps-deut] Elsewhere “that ye may live” (for example 4:1; 5:33; 8:1; 16:20; 30:16, 19) renders a verb; here it renders a noun, *lemaʿan ḥayyekha*, “for thy life.”[@oshb-wlc]

<h2 id="thy-life">He is thy life</h2>

“That thou mayest love the LORD thy God, and that thou mayest obey his voice, and that thou mayest cleave unto him: for he is thy life, and the length of thy days” ([[Deut. 30:20]]). “Thy life” is the noun of 30:6, now with “he” as subject.[@oshb-wlc] The same noun stands at 4:9, 6:2, 16:3 and 28:66 and, in the plural, at 32:47 (“it is your life”); only 30:20 says the life is a person, “he.”[@oshb-wlc] With “life” here go “in him we live, and move, and have our being” ([[Acts 17:28]]) and “the light which is in all things, which giveth life to all things” ([[D&C 88:13]]).[@lds-scriptures]

Moses had proved “that ye may live” ([[Deut. 4:1]]) from Baal-peor: “ye that did cleave unto the LORD your God are alive every one of you this day” ([[Deut. 4:4]]). “Cleave” there and at 30:20 is one root, and “alive” is the same Hebrew word as “thy life.”[@oshb-wlc] Side by side, 4:4 has cleaving and being alive as act and outcome; 30:20 has cleaving and “he is thy life.” The land stays (“that thou mayest dwell in the land”); the person is added.

<h2 id="christ">The heart He changes, the life He is</h2>

The LORD of these verses is Jehovah, the premortal Christ ([Deuteronomy 30](/deuteronomy/30/); [[Ex. 3:14]]). Being born again is “to have the Spirit of the Lord cause a mighty change in a person’s heart.”[@gs-born-again] Benjamin's people say the Spirit “has wrought a mighty change in us, or in our hearts” ([[Mosiah 5:2]]), “through faith on his name” ([[Mosiah 5:7]]).[@lds-scriptures] Ezekiel gives the same promise, “A new heart also will I give you” ([[Ezek. 36:26]]) (see [A New Heart and a New Spirit](/ezekiel/themes/new-heart-new-spirit/)). Set beside 30:6, Paul's “circumcision made without hands… the circumcision of Christ” ([[Col. 2:11]]) also names an agent other than the one circumcised.

<h2 id="disciple">The heart a disciple is given</h2>

A disciple who finds the first commandment beyond the heart stands where Israel stood at 29:4, and the book answers 29:4 with the LORD's promise of 30:6, given to those who “return unto the LORD thy God” ([[Deut. 30:2]]). Mormon says to “pray unto the Father with all the energy of heart, that ye may be filled with this love, which he hath bestowed upon all who are true followers of his Son” ([[Moro. 7:48]]);[@lds-scriptures] set beside 30:6, the love commanded is also love bestowed. “God commands us to love Him because of what He knows it will do for us.”[@christofferson-first-commandment] The choosing stays the disciple's: the same chapter says “choose life” ([[Deut. 30:19]]), and men “are free to choose liberty and eternal life, through the great Mediator of all men” ([[2 Ne. 2:27]]).[@lds-scriptures] The life the changed heart is for is the Lord who changes it: “for he is thy life.”
