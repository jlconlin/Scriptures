Moses sets “a blessing and a curse” before Israel ([[Deut. 11:26]]). Set beside one another, the passages that follow show the curse spoken and answered “Amen” twelve times, overtaking the nation, and coming upon it ([[Deut. 30:1]]); Moses' last word to Israel is “the blessing” ([[Deut. 33:1]]), and after [[Deut. 30:19]] the book has no word for curse.[@oshb-wlc] One Hebrew noun for curse, *qelalah*, stands in eleven verses, two of them in the laws: the hanged man “accursed of God” ([[Deut. 21:23]]) and the curse the LORD “turned” ([[Deut. 23:5]]).[@oshb-wlc] The step to Christ is Paul's ([[Gal. 3:13]]).

<h2 id="set-before">Set before them</h2>

The curse is first one of two ways: the blessing “if ye obey,” the curse “if ye will not obey” ([[Deut. 11:27–28]]). It has a mountain: “the curse upon mount Ebal” ([[Deut. 11:29]]). See the note there.

<h2 id="in-the-laws">In the laws: the curse of God, and the curse turned</h2>

“He that is hanged is accursed of God” ([[Deut. 21:23]]) is, in Hebrew, “the curse of God,” the book's noun.[@oshb-wlc] Moab hired Balaam “to curse thee”; “Nevertheless the LORD thy God would not hearken unto Balaam; but the LORD thy God turned the curse into a blessing unto thee, because the LORD thy God loved thee” ([[Deut. 23:4–5]]). The noun is again that of 21:23.[@oshb-wlc] Balaam was “bribed by Balak, king of Moab, to curse the Israelites,” and “the curse turned to blessing.”[@bd-balaam]

This curse is a hired enemy's, not the law's curse of chapters 27–28. The two verses share a noun, and both name God: at 21:23 the curse is “of God”; at 23:5 the LORD turns it. Set beside each other, they put a man under the curse of God and the LORD turning a curse to a blessing for love of His people. See [[Deut. 23:3]].

<h2 id="spoken">Spoken twelve times</h2>

Six tribes stand on Gerizim “to bless the people,” six on Ebal “to curse” ([[Deut. 27:12–13]]). Twelve sentences follow ([[Deut. 27:15–26]]), each answered “Amen,” a word found twelve times in the book, all here.[@oshb-wlc] The chapter records no sentence of blessing; its words are in [[Deut. 28:3–6]].

<h2 id="overtaking">Come upon thee, and overtake thee</h2>

The curse is no longer set before Israel: “all these curses shall come upon thee, and overtake thee” ([[Deut. 28:15]]). A curse “is the application of divine law that allows or brings judgments and their consequences upon a thing, person, or people primarily because of unrighteousness,” and curses “are a manifestation of God's divine love and justice.”[@gs-curse] Then: “when all these things are come upon thee, the blessing and the curse, which I have set before thee” ([[Deut. 30:1]]). After the return, “the LORD thy God will put all these curses upon thine enemies” ([[Deut. 30:7]]); the Hebrew word there is not 30:1's noun but *ʾalah*, the oath-curse of chapter 29.[@oshb-wlc] See [[Deut. 28:15]].

<h2 id="last-word">The last word</h2>

“And this is the blessing, wherewith Moses the man of God blessed the children of Israel” ([[Deut. 33:1]]); it ends, “Happy art thou, O Israel … O people saved by the LORD” ([[Deut. 33:29]]). Set beside chapter 27, where only the curse is given words and the people answer it twelve times, chapter 33 gives words only to blessing, tribe by tribe. The Song of chapters 31–32 still foretells the evils to come: “many evils and troubles shall befall them” ([[Deut. 31:17]]).[@lds-scriptures] So it is the word for curse that stops after 30:19, not the warning;[@oshb-wlc] the blessing is what Moses speaks last to Israel, and chapter 34 follows.

<h2 id="christ">Made a curse for us</h2>

The Church's footnotes at 21:23, “tree” and “accursed,” both point to [[Gal. 3:13]].[@lds-scriptures] Paul: “Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us … That the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ” ([[Gal. 3:13–14]]). The curse is borne, and the blessing comes by the one who bore it; “The Lord may remove curses because of the individual's or people's faith in Jesus Christ and obedience to the laws and ordinances of the gospel.”[@gs-curse]

Set beside Gal. 3:13–14, Deut. 23:5 shows a curse turned into a blessing by the LORD, “because the LORD thy God loved thee,” and John gives the same ground for the Son's being given: “For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son” ([[John 3:16]]). The footnote at “loved” in Deut. 23:5 is headed “God, Love of,” and John 3:16's includes “God, Love of.”[@lds-scriptures] Balaam's curse was an enemy's, turned before it fell; the law's curse fell, and Paul says Christ was made it. Deuteronomy 23:5 does not speak of the cross.

<h2 id="application">All things for your good</h2>

“As many as are of the works of the law are under the curse” ([[Gal. 3:10]]); “no man is justified by the law in the sight of God” ([[Gal. 3:11]]).[@lds-scriptures] Christ's being “made a curse for us” is how the blessing comes. The Church's footnote at Deut. 23:5, “blessing,” is [[D&C 90:24]]: “all things shall work together for your good, if ye walk uprightly and remember the covenant.”[@lds-scriptures] Its footnote on “all” returns to Deut. 23:5 and adds [[Rom. 8:28]], “to them that love God.”[@lds-scriptures] The edition's footnotes set the turned curse beside that promise, with its conditions: “if ye walk uprightly and remember the covenant,” “to them that love God.”
