Psalm 1 says the righteous prosper; other psalms see the wicked prosper. The order here is a reading order, not a date order, and no psalm corrects another. The same Hebrew words pass from one psalm to the next, and the righteous are promised three things: that their work will prosper, the earth, and God Himself. Where the page sets two passages side by side without a citation, the comparison is its own.

<h2 id="promise">The promise, and the sight</h2>

The tree of Psalm 1 is promised that “whatsoever he doeth shall prosper.” Among its cross-references is Joseph, whose master saw “that the LORD made all that he did to prosper in his hand” ([[Gen. 39:3]]).[@lds-scriptures]

Psalm 37 says, “fret not thyself because of him who prospereth in his way” ([[Ps. 37:7]]). The Hebrew verb is the one in Psalm 1:3.[@oshb-wlc] Set side by side, Psalm 37 admits of a wicked man what Psalm 1 promised the righteous. To the righteous it holds out an inheritance to wait for: “the meek shall inherit the earth; and shall delight themselves in the abundance of peace” ([[Ps. 37:11]]). Until then the righteous man may have less: “A little that a righteous man hath is better than the riches of many wicked” ([[Ps. 37:16]]).

<h2 id="envious">“I was envious”</h2>

Psalm 73’s speaker admits to the envy Psalm 37 forbids: “I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked… Verily I have cleansed my heart in vain” ([[Ps. 73:3]], [[Ps. 73:13]]). “Envious” is the verb of “neither be thou envious” ([[Ps. 37:1]]), and “prosperity” is the Hebrew word for “peace,” the word in “abundance of peace” ([[Ps. 37:11]]).[@oshb-wlc] Set beside Psalm 37, the speaker has done what it warned against, and he describes the wicked’s prosperity with the word Psalm 37 uses for the meek’s peace ([Psalm 73](/psalms/73/)). Jeremiah asks the same question, and Malachi’s people say the same thing ([[Jer. 12:1]]; [[Mal. 3:14]]).

<h2 id="portion">The sanctuary, and the portion</h2>

“Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end” ([[Ps. 73:17]]). The psalm does not say what happened there. “End” is the word of Psalm 37: “the end of that man is peace… the end of the wicked shall be cut off” ([[Ps. 37:37–38]]).[@oshb-wlc] Then: “My flesh and my heart faileth: but God is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever” ([[Ps. 73:26]]). The failing stands in the same verse as the confidence.

In the law the word for “portion” is a share of the land, and Aaron was to have none: “Thou shalt have no inheritance in their land, neither shalt thou have any part among them: I am thy part and thine inheritance” ([[Num. 18:20]]), the same noun.[@oshb-wlc] It is also the noun for “men of the world, which have their portion in this life” ([[Ps. 17:14]]).[@oshb-wlc] Psalm 17 names a portion in this life; Psalm 73 names a portion for ever. Read as a sequence, which is this page’s reading, what is offered is named first as how a man’s work fares (Psalm 1), then as where he lives (Psalm 37), then as whom he has (Psalm 73).

<h2 id="planted">Planted in the house</h2>

“Those that be planted in the house of the LORD shall flourish in the courts of our God” ([[Ps. 92:13]]). “Planted” is the verb of the tree in Psalm 1:3.[@oshb-wlc] Psalm 1 does not say where its tree stands. Set beside it, Psalm 92 plants the righteous in the house of the LORD, the place where Psalm 73 turned ([Psalm 92](/psalms/92/)).

<h2 id="christ">The meek, the earth, and the Redemption</h2>

Jesus makes Psalm 37:11 a beatitude: “Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth” ([[Matt. 5:5]]; [[3 Ne. 12:5]]). See [Psalm 37](/psalms/37/).

The Doctrine and Covenants sets that inheritance in the Redemption: “through the redemption which is made for you is brought to pass the resurrection from the dead,” and “the redemption of the soul is through him that quickeneth all things, in whose bosom it is decreed that the poor and the meek of the earth shall inherit it” ([[D&C 88:14]], [[D&C 88:17]]). That verse is cross-referenced to Matthew 5:5[@lds-scriptures], and “To inherit the earth means to inherit the celestial kingdom.”[@dc-student-manual-2017-88] The sources read carry Psalm 37 no further toward Christ than this; nothing here reads Psalms 37, 73 or 92 as spoken by Him or about Him.

Elder D. Todd Christofferson taught that God’s plan is not “a cosmic vending machine”: “What our Heavenly Father offers us is Himself and His Son, a close and enduring relationship with Them through the grace and mediation of Jesus Christ, our Redeemer.”[@christofferson-relationship-god] The talk does not cite the psalm, and its words differ. Set beside “God is… my portion for ever,” its sentence names God Himself as what is given. Paul’s words, which the talk goes on to quote in part, keep suffering in the promise: “heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ; if so be that we suffer with him” ([[Rom. 8:17]]).
