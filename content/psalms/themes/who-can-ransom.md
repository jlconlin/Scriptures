The psalms speak of death in different voices: fear, rescue already given, hope, God’s presence. This page follows those voices from fear of death to trust in God’s redemption, grouping passages by what they say, not by date or as a history of belief. Where it sets two passages side by side without a citation, the comparison is its own.

## A grave where no one praises

“For in death there is no remembrance of thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?” ([[Ps. 6:5]]). “The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence” ([[Ps. 115:17]]). Psalm 88 asks, “shall the dead arise and praise thee?” ([[Ps. 88:10]]; see [the unanswered prayer](/psalms/themes/the-unanswered-prayer/)). The “grave” of Psalm 6:5 is Sheol,[@oshb-wlc] “the abode of departed spirits”[@bd-sheol] (explained at [Psalm 6](/psalms/6/)). Other psalms say rescue from it is already given: “thou hast brought up my soul from the grave” ([[Ps. 30:3]]).

<h2 id="price">The price no one can pay</h2>

Psalm 49 aims at the rich ([[Ps. 49:6]]) and says no man can pay for a life, “That he should still live for ever, and not see corruption” ([[Ps. 49:7–9]]; see [Psalm 49](/psalms/49/)). The Hebrew for “corruption” in verse 9 is the word the King James also renders “corruption” in [[Ps. 16:10]].[@oshb-wlc]

<h2 id="two-shepherds">Two shepherds</h2>

“Like sheep they are laid in the grave; death shall feed on them” ([[Ps. 49:14]]). The Hebrew verb behind “feed” is the shepherd’s verb, the one in “The LORD is my shepherd” ([[Ps. 23:1]]),[@oshb-wlc] a contrast one older reading also draws[@delitzsch-psalms] (see [Psalm 49](/psalms/49/)).

Set beside Psalm 49:14, the sheep of the other shepherd “walk through the valley of the shadow of death” and fear no evil, “for thou art with me” ([[Ps. 23:4]]). The Hebrew behind “shadow of death” can also mean darkness or gloom, and the valley stands for “times of distress or danger,”[@scripture-helps-ps-1] so the verse is not only about dying. Another psalm: “if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there” ([[Ps. 139:8]]). Psalm 23 is taken up at [the law and the lost sheep](/psalms/themes/the-law-and-the-lost-sheep/).

## But God

“But God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave: for he shall receive me” ([[Ps. 49:15]]). Verse 7 said no man can “redeem”; here the same verb is God’s, and Hosea has it, translated “ransom,” in the Lord’s own voice: “I will ransom them from the power of the grave” ([[Hos. 13:14]]; [Hosea 13](/hosea/13/)).[@oshb-wlc] “Receive” is the verb of “afterward receive me to glory” ([[Ps. 73:24]]; that psalm’s story is at [my portion for ever](/psalms/themes/my-portion-for-ever/)).[@oshb-wlc]

Psalm 16 says the same kind of thing: “thou wilt not leave my soul in hell” ([[Ps. 16:10]]). Neither psalm says by what means. They trust God to do it.

<h2 id="christ">The one who was not left, and the ransom</h2>

Peter says David “is both dead and buried,” and that David “spake of the resurrection of Christ, that his soul was not left in hell, neither his flesh did see corruption. This Jesus hath God raised up” ([[Acts 2:29–32]]). “Corruption” is the word Psalm 49:9 used for what no payment could spare a man. See [Psalm 16](/psalms/16/).

Psalm 49:7 says none can “give to God a ransom” for a brother. Jesus says the Son of man came “to give his life a ransom for many” ([[Matt. 20:28]]). The two share only the King James’s English word “ransom.” To redeem is “To deliver, to purchase, or to ransom,” and “Jesus’ Atonement redeems all mankind from physical death.”[@gs-redeem] The redemption of Psalm 49:15 has been taught in general conference to be the Atonement of Jesus Christ, which does not come by “corruptible things, as silver and gold” ([[1 Pet. 1:18]]).[@curtis-redemption]

Where Psalm 49:14 has death for a shepherd, Hebrews calls the risen Lord “that great shepherd of the sheep,” “brought again from the dead” ([[Heb. 13:20]]).

Psalm 115:17 says only that the dead do not praise. The verse is cross-referenced to latter-day scripture:[@lds-scriptures] “Let the dead speak forth anthems of eternal praise to the King Immanuel” ([[D&C 128:22]]). Beside it, the dead in Joseph F. Smith’s vision “acknowledged the Son of God as their Redeemer and Deliverer from death and the chains of hell” ([[D&C 138:23]]).
