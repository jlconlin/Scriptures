Three psalms come at sacrifice from three sides: whether God needs feeding (Psalm 50), what He wants of the worshipper (Psalm 40), and what a guilty man can bring (Psalm 51). The answers keep returning to the heart without throwing out the altar, and the Psalms also ask God for what the worshipper cannot bring. The table’s order is the page’s own, not a sequence in time, and so is any comparison it makes without a citation. Hebrews then gives Psalm 40’s words to Christ, and the Book of Mormon joins His sacrifice to the broken heart He asks of His people.

## What God does not need

God says, “I will not reprove thee for thy sacrifices” ([[Ps. 50:8]]); the covenant was made “by sacrifice” ([[Ps. 50:5]]). But He adds, “If I were hungry, I would not tell thee: for the world is mine, and the fulness thereof” ([[Ps. 50:12]]), and asks “thanksgiving,” vows paid, and a call “in the day of trouble” ([[Ps. 50:14–15]]); [Psalm 50](/psalms/50/) explains the thank offering. A song, another psalm says, “shall please the LORD better than an ox or bullock” ([[Ps. 69:31]]).

None of this abolishes sacrifice. Other psalms simply offer it: “I will freely sacrifice unto thee” ([[Ps. 54:6]]); see also [[Ps. 27:6]], [[Ps. 66:13–15]] and [[Ps. 116:17]].

<h2 id="lo-i-come">“Lo, I come”</h2>

“Sacrifice and offering thou didst not desire… Then said I, Lo, I come… I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart” ([[Ps. 40:6–8]]). The speaker comes himself, delighting in God’s will. “Desire” (verse 6) and “delight” (verse 8) are one Hebrew verb.[@oshb-wlc]

Psalm 51 has the same verb three times: “thou desirest truth in the inward parts” ([[Ps. 51:6]]), “thou desirest not sacrifice” ([[Ps. 51:16]]) and “Then shalt thou be pleased” with the altar’s offerings ([[Ps. 51:19]]).[@oshb-wlc] Set side by side, the two psalms use the same word for the sacrifice God does not desire, the inward truth He desires, and the speaker’s delight in His will. Samuel says as much of obedience: “to obey is better than sacrifice” ([[1 Sam. 15:22]]).[@lds-scriptures]

<h2 id="broken-heart">The broken heart, and the altar after it</h2>

“The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise” ([[Ps. 51:17]]). Yet the psalm ends: “Then shalt thou be pleased with the sacrifices of righteousness… then shall they offer bullocks upon thine altar” ([[Ps. 51:19]]). Under the law “the broken heart was to accompany the animal sacrifice.”[@sc-knowhy-198] [Psalm 51](/psalms/51/) sets out two readings of the last verses.

<h2 id="only-god">What only God can give</h2>

The Psalms also ask God for what the worshipper cannot bring: “Create in me a clean heart, O God” ([[Ps. 51:10]]); “thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin” ([[Ps. 32:5]]); “there is forgiveness with thee… he shall redeem Israel from all his iniquities” ([[Ps. 130:4]], [[Ps. 130:8]]).

Set beside the sacrifice verses, these are requests of another kind: the psalm that says “thou desirest not sacrifice; else would I give it” ([[Ps. 51:16]]) is the psalm that asks God to create the clean heart. These passages do not say how He will do it; [Psalm 130](/psalms/130/) does not name the payment.

## The offering of Jesus Christ, and ours

“It is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins,” Hebrews says, and it gives the words of Psalm 40 to Christ: “when he cometh into the world, he saith… Lo, I come to do thy will, O God” ([[Heb. 10:4–9]]). Hebrews quotes Psalm 40 only through verse 8, and gives verse 6 as the Greek version of the psalm has it, “a body hast thou prepared me,” where the Hebrew has “ears” ([Psalm 40](/psalms/40/)).[@lxx-rahlfs] The speaker’s “mine iniquities” ([[Ps. 40:12]]) is not in what it quotes. “By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all” ([[Heb. 10:10]]). The sacrifices were “a similitude of the sacrifice that would be made of the Only Begotten Son of God.”[@bd-sacrifices]

The broken heart is the worshipper’s offering and does not take Christ’s place. He “offereth himself a sacrifice for sin… unto all those who have a broken heart and a contrite spirit” ([[2 Ne. 2:7]]), “an allusion to Psalms 51:16–17”: “To take advantage of Christ’s offering… people must offer up themselves as well.”[@sc-knowhy-275] The risen Lord says, “your sacrifices and your burnt offerings shall be done away… ye shall offer for a sacrifice unto me a broken heart and a contrite spirit” ([[3 Ne. 9:19–20]]).
