Psalms 78 and 106 retell the exodus and the wilderness and say two things: the people forgot, and God remembered. Psalm 105 tells only the second, Psalm 95 turns the story into a warning, and Psalm 107 gives thanks for the gathering.

<h2 id="retold">Retold, so as not to forget</h2>

Psalm 78 says why the story is told: “That the generation to come might know them… That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments” ([[Ps. 78:6–7]]). Psalm 105 opens the same way: “Remember his marvellous works that he hath done” ([[Ps. 105:5]]). It has been read as a covenant text.[@ricks-psalm-105] Psalm 78:6–7 has been studied alongside the sacrament’s “always remember.”[@cfm-ot-2026-35] See [Psalm 78](/psalms/78/).

<h2 id="forgot">They forgot</h2>

The verb for forgetting is the same in Psalm 78:7, 11 and 106:13, 21.[@oshb-wlc] “They soon forgat his works” ([[Ps. 106:13]]); “They forgat God their saviour, which had done great things in Egypt” ([[Ps. 106:21]]); “They remembered not his hand, nor the day when he delivered them” ([[Ps. 78:42]]). In the Bible, “forgot” is read as “to ignore” or “to take lightly,” not an accidental lapse.[@belnap-hesed]

Moses had warned of it: “Then thine heart be lifted up, and thou forget the Lord thy God” ([[Deut. 8:14]]).[@lds-scriptures] Blessings bring “the temptation to forget their source.”[@eyring-remember] See [Psalm 106](/psalms/106/).

<h2 id="harden">Harden not your heart</h2>

“To day if ye will hear his voice, Harden not your heart, as in the provocation, and as in the day of temptation in the wilderness” ([[Ps. 95:7–8]]). In the Hebrew, “provocation” is the place-name Meribah.[@lds-scriptures] The Exodus story is on [Proved in the Wilderness](/exodus/themes/proved-in-the-wilderness/).

Hebrews quotes the warning whole, “as the Holy Ghost saith” ([[Heb. 3:7–11]]), and says to believers, “Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief” ([[Heb. 3:12]]).[@lds-scriptures] The oath, “they should not enter into my rest” ([[Ps. 95:11]]), names a rest that is “the fulness of his glory” ([[D&C 84:24]]).[@lds-scriptures] Jacob 1:7 and Hebrews 3:8 both draw on Psalm 95:8.[@sc-knowhy-533] See [Psalm 95](/psalms/95/).

<h2 id="remembered">God remembered</h2>

“He hath remembered his covenant for ever” ([[Ps. 105:8]]); compare “God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob” ([[Ex. 2:24]]).[@lds-scriptures] “For he remembered that they were but flesh” ([[Ps. 78:39]]); “he remembereth that we are dust” ([[Ps. 103:14]]); “Who remembered us in our low estate” ([[Ps. 136:23]]).

Psalm 106 has both sides. The fathers “remembered not the multitude of thy mercies” ([[Ps. 106:7]]); of God, “he remembered for them his covenant, and repented according to the multitude of his mercies” ([[Ps. 106:45]]). Both verses use the same Hebrew noun for “multitude” and the same verb for “remembered.”[@oshb-wlc] Set beside each other, the verses have God remembering according to the same multitude of mercies the fathers did not remember. “Mercies” in both verses comes from hesed,[@oshb-wlc] which is “often used in the Old Testament in reference to God’s covenantal love.”[@scripture-helps-ps-102] The psalm also says: “Many times did he deliver them; but they provoked him with their counsel, and were brought low for their iniquity” ([[Ps. 106:43]]).

<h2 id="gathered">Gather us, and the gathered</h2>

Book Four ends with a prayer: “Save us, O LORD our God, and gather us from among the heathen” ([[Ps. 106:47]]), and a doxology, a formal declaration of God’s power and glory ([[Ps. 106:48]]); Book Five opens with “Let the redeemed of the LORD say so… And gathered them out of the lands” ([[Ps. 107:2–3]]).[@ot-manual-psalms] The verb “gather” is the same in both.[@oshb-wlc] “Thanks are here given in Psalm 107:3 for what was there desired in v. 47.”[@delitzsch-psalms] Ammon’s words in Alma 26:6–8 “likely borrow” from Psalms 106–107.[@sc-knowhy-133] See [Psalm 107](/psalms/107/) for 1 Nephi 22:25.

<h2 id="rock">The Rock, the rest, and remembering Him</h2>

Paul says of the fathers, “they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ,” and that these things “are written for our admonition” ([[1 Cor. 10:4]], [[1 Cor. 10:11]]). Hebrews says “There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God,” and ends with the warning: “Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest, lest any man fall after the same example of unbelief” ([[Heb. 4:9]], [[Heb. 4:11]]).

The sacrament prayer asks that those who partake “always remember him” ([[Moro. 4:3]]; [[D&C 20:77]]), and the sacrament prayer has been read as a covenant renewal, with “always remember him” among its terms.[@ricks-psalm-105]
