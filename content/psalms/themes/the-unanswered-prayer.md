The psalms of lament ask God why He is absent. Some turn to trust with no answer reported, and Psalm 88 does not turn at all. On the cross Jesus prayed the first line of Psalm 22 and a line of Psalm 31. Psalm 22 goes on to say that God did not hide His face from its sufferer. It also speaks of “my brethren,” a line Hebrews gives to Christ.

## How long, and why

Psalm 13 asks how long four times; two of the questions are about God’s absence: “How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD? for ever? how long wilt thou hide thy face from me?” ([[Ps. 13:1]]). Then it turns: “But I have trusted in thy mercy; my heart shall rejoice in thy salvation” ([[Ps. 13:5]]). No word from the Lord stands between the question and the trust; see the note at 13:5, [Psalm 13](/psalms/13/). Others end still asking ([[Ps. 38:21–22]]). Joseph Smith’s prayer asks it again: “O God, where art thou? And where is the pavilion that covereth thy hiding place?” ([[D&C 121:1]]), the verse the footnote at 13:1 points to.[@lds-scriptures]

<h2 id="psalm-88">The psalm that does not turn</h2>

Psalm 88 still calls on “LORD God of my salvation” ([[Ps. 88:1]]) and asks what Psalm 13 asked: “LORD, why castest thou off my soul? why hidest thou thy face from me?” ([[Ps. 88:14]]). The psalm has no vow of praise and no report of rescue, and ends: “Lover and friend hast thou put far from me, and mine acquaintance into darkness” ([[Ps. 88:18]]). Its questions about the dead are taken up in [Who Can Ransom a Life?](/psalms/themes/who-can-ransom/); see also [Psalm 88](/psalms/88/).

<h2 id="cross">Prayed from the cross</h2>

On the cross Jesus cried “Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani? that is to say, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?” ([[Matt. 27:46]]; [[Mark 15:34]]), the first line of [[Ps. 22:1]]. In Luke His last words are “Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit” ([[Luke 23:46]]), from [[Ps. 31:5]].

The Gospels also use the mockers’ taunt ([[Ps. 22:7–8]]; [[Matt. 27:43]]), the parted garments ([[Ps. 22:18]]; [[John 19:24]]) and the vinegar ([[Ps. 69:21]]; [[John 19:28–29]]); the notes at [Psalm 22](/psalms/22/) and [Psalm 31](/psalms/31/) carry the detail. None of these lines is a confession of sin. The confessions belong to the sufferers ([[Ps. 38:3–4]]; [[Ps. 41:4]]; [[Ps. 69:5]]); see the note at 69:5, [Psalm 69](/psalms/69/).

The cry “showed that Christ had truly ‘descended below all things’ (D&C 88:6), so that he could understand and succor his people (Alma 7:12).”[@hopkin-psalm-22] Joseph Smith’s question has been read as “connected to Jesus’s cry,” and the response he recorded was “The Son of Man hath descended below them all” ([[D&C 122:8]]).[@hopkin-psalm-22] The Doctrine and Covenants records that response; Psalm 13 records none.

<h2 id="turn">“Neither hath he hid his face”</h2>

Psalm 22 turns in verse 21 (see the note there, [Psalm 22](/psalms/22/)). Verses 23–25 “speak of the trial as already having passed,”[@hopkin-psalm-22] and of the one who cried the psalm says: “neither hath he hid his face from him; but when he cried unto him, he heard” ([[Ps. 22:24]]). On the cry itself, see the note “Did the Father forsake Him?” at [Psalm 22](/psalms/22/). The verb for “hid” is the same in 13:1 and 88:14.[@oshb-wlc]

Set beside each other, Psalms 13:1 and 88:14 ask why God hides His face (a phrase the laments use often), and Psalm 22:24 says He did not hide it from its own sufferer. Psalm 88 leaves its question unanswered.

Two verses earlier the sufferer says “I will declare thy name unto my brethren: in the midst of the congregation will I praise thee” ([[Ps. 22:22]]). Hebrews gives the line to Christ, who “is not ashamed to call them brethren, saying, I will declare thy name unto my brethren, in the midst of the church will I sing praise unto thee” ([[Heb. 2:11–12]]). Hebrews quotes “from the final section of the psalm” to stress “the victorious nature of Jesus’s Atonement.”[@hopkin-psalm-22] The circle widens to “all the ends of the world” ([[Ps. 22:27]]).

## The book called Praises

The book’s Hebrew name is “Praises,”[@bd-psalms] and it ends “Let every thing that hath breath praise the LORD” ([[Ps. 150:6]]). The last of its five sections (Psalms 107–150) praises “God for delivering Israel through the Messiah.”[@scripture-helps-ps-1]

The book that ends in praise also keeps Psalm 88 as it is; the praise at the end does not go back and answer each lament. The one who declares God’s name “in the midst of the church” ([[Heb. 2:12]]) is, in the Gospels, the one who cried out the first line of Psalm 22.
