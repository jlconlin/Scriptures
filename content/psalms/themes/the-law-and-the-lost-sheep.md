Two of the psalms that praise the law also ask for help: Psalm 19 to be cleansed, and Psalm 119, in its last verse, to be sought. This page follows that last verse to the shepherd of the Psalms, to the prophets and to the Savior. Where it sets the law psalms beside the shepherd psalms, the comparison of wording is its own, and it does not claim that one psalm answers another.

## Delight, and a plea

The blessed man’s “delight is in the law of the LORD” ([[Ps. 1:2]]). Psalm 19 calls the law “perfect, converting the soul” ([[Ps. 19:7]]), and that praise turns the psalmist to himself: “Who can understand his errors? cleanse thou me from secret faults” ([[Ps. 19:12]]; see [Psalm 19](/psalms/19/)).[@ot-minute-psalms-1-20] Psalm 119: “O how love I thy law!” ([[Ps. 119:97]]).

<h2 id="last-verse">The last verse</h2>

Psalm 119 has spoken of past straying: “Before I was afflicted I went astray” ([[Ps. 119:67]]). It has also spoken of the danger: “O let me not wander from thy commandments” ([[Ps. 119:10]]). What is new in the last verse is the sheep, and God being asked to seek:

> I have gone astray like a lost sheep; seek thy servant; for I do not forget thy commandments. ([[Ps. 119:176]])

“Lost” occurs nowhere else in the King James Psalms (its Hebrew word is elsewhere “perish,” as at [[Ps. 1:6]])[@oshb-wlc], and nowhere else in them is God asked to “seek” a person.[@lds-scriptures] In one older reading, the straying is not a turning away from God: the sheep’s home is still beside the Lord (see the note on [[Ps. 119:176]]).[@delitzsch-psalms]

<h2 id="shepherd">The shepherd who restores</h2>

“He restoreth my soul” ([[Ps. 23:3]]) has the same Hebrew root and noun as the law “converting the soul” in [[Ps. 19:7]].[@oshb-wlc] The similar wording has different subjects: the law in Psalm 19, the shepherd in Psalm 23. The root is common in Psalms, and the likeness is one of wording only. The good shepherd is explained at [Psalm 23](/psalms/23/); verse 4 is taken up in [Who Can Ransom a Life?](/psalms/themes/who-can-ransom/).

Psalm 95 calls the singers “the people of his pasture, and the sheep of his hand,” and its next words are about hearing: “To day if ye will hear his voice” ([[Ps. 95:7]]). God says of the wilderness generation, “It is a people that do err in their heart, and they have not known my ways” ([[Ps. 95:10]]). “Err” there is the Hebrew verb translated “I have gone astray” in [[Ps. 119:176]], one of six occurrences of that verb in Psalms.[@oshb-wlc] After the shared verb, the wording differs: the generation has “not known my ways”; the servant does “not forget thy commandments.”

## “I will seek that which was lost”

The LORD says through Ezekiel, “I will seek that which was lost” ([[Ezek. 34:16]]). The psalmist’s request and the LORD’s promise use the same two Hebrew words for “seek” and “lost” ([[Ps. 119:176]]).[@oshb-wlc] See [Ezekiel 34](/ezekiel/34/) and [One Shepherd](/ezekiel/themes/one-shepherd/).

Isaiah says, “All we like sheep have gone astray,” using the psalm’s verb again.[@oshb-wlc] He continues, “and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all” ([[Isa. 53:6]]). The psalm’s last verse is cross-referenced to this one, to [[1 Pet. 2:25]] and to [[Alma 5:37]];[@tg-sheep] see [Isaiah 53](/isaiah/53/).

<h2 id="christ">The lawgiver and the shepherd</h2>

Jesus tells of the man who goes “after that which is lost, until he find it” ([[Luke 15:4]]), and says of Himself, “the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost” ([[Luke 19:10]]). Ezekiel’s promise is cross-referenced to both verses.[@lds-scriptures]

“My sheep hear my voice” ([[John 10:27]]). Set beside [[Ps. 95:7]], the King James words are the same three: sheep, hear, voice. Psalm 95:7 is listed with [[John 10:14]] among the scriptures of Jesus Christ as the Good Shepherd.[@tg-jesus-christ-good-shepherd]

The risen Lord says of the law given to Israel, “I am he that gave the law,” and that it is fulfilled in Him ([[3 Ne. 15:5]]). He says, “I am the law, and the light” ([[3 Ne. 15:9]]), and of what He has taught, “therefore keep my commandments” ([[3 Ne. 15:10]]). Two things scripture says of Him are set together here, and the pairing is this page’s: He gave the law the psalmist loves, and He came to seek that which was lost (see [Psalm 119](/psalms/119/)).

So the prayer of [[Ps. 119:176]] holds two things at once: the request to be sought, and its reason, “for I do not forget thy commandments.”
