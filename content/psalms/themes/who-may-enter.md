Two psalms ask who may enter God’s presence, and both describe the person who may. Around them the Psalms long for the house from far off and say that God purges, chooses and brings the worshipper near. In Psalm 24 the one who comes in at the gates is the King of glory. Set beside these psalms, Hebrews says that Jesus entered “for us.” Where the page sets two passages side by side without a citation, the comparison is its own.

## The question and its answer

“Who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD? or who shall stand in his holy place?” ([[Ps. 24:3]]). The answer is a person: “He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart” ([[Ps. 24:4]]). See [Psalm 15](/psalms/15/) and [Psalm 24](/psalms/24/). The next verse says what such a person gets: “the blessing from the LORD, and righteousness from the God of his salvation” ([[Ps. 24:5]]).

<h2 id="longing">Far from the house</h2>

Elsewhere the psalmist wants in. “My soul thirsteth for God… when shall I come and appear before God?” ([[Ps. 42:2]]). “My soul longeth, yea, even fainteth for the courts of the LORD” ([[Ps. 84:2]]).

Another speaker is far off: “Woe is me, that I sojourn in Mesech, that I dwell in the tents of Kedar!” ([[Ps. 120:5]]). The Hebrew words in this verse are also those behind “abide… dwell… tabernacle” in [[Ps. 15:1]].[@oshb-wlc] Set beside that verse, the speaker is lodging in other tents than the one Psalm 15 asks about. Psalm 120 is the first of the fifteen songs of degrees, probably pilgrims’ songs for the way up to Jerusalem; see [Psalm 120](/psalms/120/).[@bd-degrees-songs-of]

<h2 id="brought-near">Brought near</h2>

The same book speaks of entry as something given. “I will come into thy house in the multitude of thy mercy” ([[Ps. 5:7]]). “Let them bring me unto thy holy hill, and to thy tabernacles” ([[Ps. 43:3]]). “Thou shalt purge them away. Blessed is the man whom thou choosest, and causest to approach unto thee” ([[Ps. 65:3–4]]).

Set beside the questions of [[Ps. 15:1]] and [[Ps. 24:3]], these verses speak of mercy and of being brought near.

<h2 id="king">The King at the gates</h2>

After asking who may go up, Psalm 24 has someone else come in: “Lift up your heads, O ye gates… and the King of glory shall come in” ([[Ps. 24:7]]); “The LORD of hosts, he is the King of glory” ([[Ps. 24:10]]). Psalm 118 has a worshipper at gates: “Open to me the gates of righteousness: I will go into them” ([[Ps. 118:19]]). Set beside each other, both passages have gates, and “come in” and “go into” are one ordinary Hebrew verb.[@oshb-wlc]

In Joseph Smith’s revision the King “shall redeem his people, and shall establish them in righteousness” (JST Ps. 24:10).[@jst-ps-24] Beside it, verse 5 of the psalm has the same English word: “righteousness from the God of his salvation” ([[Ps. 24:5]]).

Later in Psalm 118, “Blessed be he that cometh in the name of the LORD” ([[Ps. 118:26]]) is what the crowds said to Jesus ([[Matt. 21:9]]); see [Psalm 118](/psalms/118/).

<h2 id="forerunner">The forerunner</h2>

Hebrews says of Jesus: “the forerunner is for us entered” ([[Heb. 6:19–20]]); “by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place” ([[Heb. 9:12]]), which is “heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us” ([[Heb. 9:24]]). Hebrews does not quote Psalm 15 or 24 here. The comparison with “who shall stand in his holy place?” ([[Ps. 24:3]]) rests only on the King James Version’s shared words “holy place” and the thought of coming before God. On the Hebrews side, “the Atonement of Jesus Christ gives us access to God’s presence.”[@scripture-helps-nt-heb-7-13]

Clean hands and a pure heart are still required. Hebrews goes on: “boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus… Let us draw near with a true heart… our bodies washed with pure water” ([[Heb. 10:19–22]]). Elder David A. Bednar taught from Psalm 24 that worthy desires and good works, “as necessary as they are, can never produce clean hands and a pure heart. It is the Atonement of Jesus Christ that provides both a cleansing and redeeming power.”[@bednar-clean-hands] Nephi joins the psalm’s words to faith in Christ: “worship the Father in his name, with pure hearts and clean hands” ([[2 Ne. 25:16]]), “almost certainly a quote from Psalm 24.”[@sc-knowhy-51]

The psalms ask who may enter and describe that person. Hebrews says Jesus entered as “the forerunner,” and the cleansing the psalm asks for comes through His Atonement.[@bednar-clean-hands]
