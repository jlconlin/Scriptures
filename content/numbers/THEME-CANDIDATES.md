# Numbers: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.** The author has chosen none yet; Fable's and Codex's lists come at the end of the book (`AUTHORING.md`, “Theme candidates at the end of a book”).

## Leads seen while drafting the brief (2026-10-16)

Working leads for the proposers, not candidates; each must still pass §8 (“The insight is about Christ's work,” and “It applies the arc to the reader”). Deuteronomy's theme pages already cover Moses' refused prayer (*Let It Suffice Thee*), the curse turned into a blessing (*He Turned the Curse into a Blessing*, which follows Balaam from Deut. 23:5) and the prophet like Moses (*Hear Ye Him*); Exodus's *Proved in the Wilderness* and *Moses the Mediator* and Leviticus's *Them That Come Nigh Me* are near. A Numbers page links to them and must add an arc that is Numbers' own.

- **Look and live.** From the people bitten for speaking against God and Moses (21:5–6), to the serpent lifted on a pole (21:8–9), to the Savior's “so must the Son of man be lifted up” (`[[John 3:14–15]]`) and the Book of Mormon's readings: those who would not look “because of the simpleness of the way” (`[[1 Ne. 17:41]]`), Alma's “a type was raised up” (`[[Alma 33:19–22]]`), Nephi son of Helaman's “with a contrite spirit” (`[[Hel. 8:14–15]]`). What develops is what the look asks of the one who looks. Pierce (RSC) and Rappleye (*Interpreter* 50) trace it; KnoWhys #657, #721, #749.
- **Two counts, two generations.** The census at Sinai (1:46) and on the plains of Moab (26:51), with the line between them, “there was not left a man of them, save Caleb … and Joshua” (26:65); the refusal at Kadesh (14:29–35) and the children who would “know the land which ye have despised” (14:31). Where it reaches Christ is not yet clear: Hebrews 3–4 on the rest not entered, and Alma 12:36–37 on “the provocation,” are the leads (Thomas, “The Provocation in the Wilderness,” RSC, already on the site).
- **Who may come near.** The Levites set between the people and the sanctuary “that there be no plague” (8:19); Korah's “all the congregation are holy” (16:3) answered by “him whom he hath chosen will he cause to come near” (16:5); Aaron running with the censer to stand “between the dead and the living” (16:48); the rod that budded (17:8); and the priests bearing the iniquity of the sanctuary (18:1). Christ as the one high priest who stands between (Heb. 5:4–5; 7:25). Leviticus's *Them That Come Nigh Me* is near: a page would have to be the arc of mediation in Numbers, not a second page on access.
- **Seeing and not seeing.** Balaam the seer who cannot see the angel his ass sees (22:23–31), whose eyes the LORD opens (22:31; 24:3–4, 15–16) until he sees “him, but not now” (24:17); set beside the spies who saw the land and “were in our own sight as grasshoppers” (13:33) and the people told to “look upon” the fringe and remember (15:39) and to look upon the serpent (21:8). Pike (RSC) on the irony of the seer; the Church's heading reads 24:17 as of the Messiah.
- **The blessing the Lord will not revoke.** The blessing put on Israel through the priests, “I will bless them” (6:27), held against a hired curse (22:12; 23:8, 20; 24:9), and fulfilled in the Savior's blessing at Bountiful (`[[3 Ne. 19:25]]`; KnoWhy #212). Overlaps Deuteronomy's *He Turned the Curse into a Blessing*: only if the priestly blessing gives it an arc of its own.
