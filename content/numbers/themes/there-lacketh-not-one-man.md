Israel is counted at Sinai, sentenced at Kadesh in the count’s own terms, and counted again in Moab without the first roll; the officers back from Midian report that “there lacketh not one man of us.” The book is “so called from the double numbering or census of the people (Num. 1–4; 26).”[@bd-numbers] The text itself joins the first three: Moab’s count names Sinai and Kadesh ([[Num. 26:64–65]]), and the footnote there points to [[Num. 14:29]].[@lds-scriptures] The page sets the fourth beside them. It closes with what scripture says of the Good Shepherd.

## Numbered by name, and redeemed

The LORD tells Moses to “take ye the sum of all the congregation… with the number of their names, every male by their polls” ([[Num. 1:2]]), all “from twenty years old and upward” ([[Num. 1:3]]). The count ([Numbers 1](/numbers/1/)) stands under a ransom: “then shall they give every man a ransom for his soul unto the LORD, when thou numberest them” ([[Ex. 30:12]]); the silver was “a bekah for every man, that is, half a shekel… for every one that went to be numbered, from twenty years old and upward” ([[Ex. 38:26]]).

The firstborn are counted the same way, “the number of their names” ([[Num. 3:40]]), and the 273 beyond the Levites are redeemed at “five shekels apiece by the poll” ([[Num. 3:47]]). President John Taylor taught that the Lord “claimed those that He saved as righteously belonging to Him,” and that the Israelites were saved at the Passover by a propitiation that was a figure of “the contemplated atonement and propitiation of Christ.”[@ot-manual-numbers-1-12] See [Numbers 3](/numbers/3/) and [The Firstborn](/exodus/themes/the-firstborn/).

## The roll of the dead, and the roll of heirs

At Kadesh the sentence is spoken in the count’s own terms: “all that were numbered of you, according to your whole number, from twenty years old and upward” ([[Num. 14:29]]). Hebrews gives the reason: “they could not enter in because of unbelief” ([[Heb. 3:19]]).

On the plains of Moab the LORD says again, “Take the sum… from twenty years old and upward” ([[Num. 26:2]]), now “Unto these the land shall be divided for an inheritance according to the number of names” ([[Num. 26:53]]). “Among these there was not a man of them whom Moses and Aaron the priest numbered” at Sinai ([[Num. 26:64]]), “save Caleb… and Joshua” ([[Num. 26:65]]). The second count comes “now that the people of the Exodus generation were dead.”[@pike-balaam]

## A shepherd, and not one missing

Moses asks that the LORD “set a man over the congregation… that the congregation of the LORD be not as sheep which have no shepherd” ([[Num. 27:16–17]]). The LORD names Joshua ([[Num. 27:18]]).

Twelve thousand go to war, “a thousand of every tribe” ([[Num. 31:5]]). Their officers report: “Thy servants have taken the sum of the men of war which are under our charge, and there lacketh not one man of us” ([[Num. 31:49]]). This is the count of that army, not of Israel; it means “that no one was killed.”[@ot-seminary-teacher-num-67]

Set beside Numbers 26, the report uses the census’s own words. “Have taken the sum” is the idiom of “Take the sum” in 1:2 and 26:2, and “lacketh” is the passive of the verb behind “numbered” in 14:29 and 26:64: no man is “missed, lacking.”[@bdb] Of the first roll, “not a man of them”; of this count, not one man missing.

The officers bring gold “to make an atonement for our souls before the LORD” ([[Num. 31:50]]), “for a memorial” ([[Num. 31:54]]). Their “oblation” and “atonement” are cross-referenced to Ex. 30:12 and 30:15,[@lds-scriptures] where the first count was taken under a ransom “for his soul.” What the atonement is, [Between the Dead and the Living](/numbers/themes/between-the-dead-and-the-living/) and the note at [Numbers 31:50](/numbers/31/) take up.

## None of them is lost

Chapter 31 is summed up as “None in the armies of Israel are lost”;[@lds-scriptures] the verse’s own word is “lacketh.” Set beside it the Savior’s words: “those that thou gavest me I have kept, and none of them is lost, but the son of perdition” ([[John 17:12]]); “Of them which thou gavest me have I lost none” ([[John 18:9]]).

The first count was of names. Jesus “calleth his own sheep by name” ([[John 10:3]]) and says, “I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep” ([[John 10:14]]). Of one who will not repent He says, “if he repent not he shall not be numbered among my people, that he may not destroy my people, for behold I know my sheep, and they are numbered” ([[3 Ne. 18:31]]). Set beside the shepherd Moses asked for, the Savior is the Good Shepherd, and His followers are like sheep He watches over.[@gs-good-shepherd] For the one that strays, see [The Law and the Lost Sheep](/psalms/themes/the-law-and-the-lost-sheep/).

## Numbered among His people

Of those received into baptism, “they were numbered among the people of the church of Christ; and their names were taken, that they might be remembered and nourished by the good word of God” ([[Moro. 6:4]]). Set beside the count of names at Sinai, the church too takes the names of its people. “The worth of souls is great in the sight of God” ([[D&C 18:10]]). Sister Bonnie H. Cordon taught that “numbering… is not really about numbers; it is about making certain each person feels the love of the Savior through someone who serves for Him.”[@cordon-becoming-shepherd]
