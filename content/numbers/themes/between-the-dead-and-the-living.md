In Numbers, atonement is made by serving, offering, running incense into a plague, a deed, gold. One Hebrew verb, *kipper*, is behind “atonement” in each; it is used 16 times in 15 verses of the book, most of them in the laws of offerings.[@oshb-wlc] The verb’s last use sets a limit: the land cannot be cleansed of shed blood by any ransom. The sequence is this page’s own reading. Amulek reasons from that law that nothing “short of an infinite atonement” will suffice, and scripture names the one Mediator.

## A service, and an offering for error

The Levites are given “to do the service of the children of Israel… and to make an atonement for the children of Israel: that there be no plague” ([[Num. 8:19]]). Atonement here is a standing service. The priest’s atonement is then joined to forgiveness, “for it is ignorance” ([[Num. 15:25]]), with a limit already in the law: for the soul that sins “presumptuously,” “that soul shall be cut off” ([[Num. 15:30]]). Chapters [8](/numbers/8/) and [15](/numbers/15/) take these up.

## Between the dead and the living

When the plague began, Moses said to Aaron, “Take a censer, and put fire therein from off the altar, and put on incense, and go quickly unto the congregation, and make an atonement for them” ([[Num. 16:46]]). Aaron “made an atonement for the people. And he stood between the dead and the living; and the plague was stayed” ([[Num. 16:47–48]]). The verb is in verses 46–47; verse 48 says where he stood.[@oshb-wlc] These actions “represented Jesus Christ’s Atonement for us.”[@seminary-2018-num-67] [Pray unto the LORD](/numbers/themes/pray-unto-the-lord/) sets these verses among Moses’ intercessions, as an act where no prayer is recorded.

## A deed, and gold

Phinehas killed two in the tent when the plague was upon Israel ([[Num. 25:7–8]]); the LORD says he “was zealous for his God, and made an atonement for the children of Israel” ([[Num. 25:13]]), and the psalm says he “executed judgment: and so the plague was stayed” ([[Ps. 106:30]]). The verb is used of a deed, not an offering.

Then an offering of gold. After Midian the officers said, “there lacketh not one man of us,” and brought an oblation “to make an atonement for our souls before the LORD” ([[Num. 31:49–50]]); [There Lacketh Not One Man of Us](/numbers/themes/there-lacketh-not-one-man/) follows the census.

## No ransom for a life

“Ye shall take no satisfaction for the life of a murderer, which is guilty of death: but he shall be surely put to death” ([[Num. 35:31]]). “Satisfaction” is *kōper*, glossed “a price for ransom of a life.”[@bdb] Set beside it, Exodus allowed a sum “for the ransom of his life” (also *kōper*) when an ox had killed a man ([[Ex. 21:29–30]]);[@lds-scriptures] for a murderer none is taken.

Then the verb returns, for the last time in the book.[@oshb-wlc] The land “cannot be cleansed of the blood that is shed therein, but by the blood of him that shed it” ([[Num. 35:33]]). Set beside it, the psalm says no one has a ransom to give: “None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him” ([[Ps. 49:7]]) (also *kōper*).[@bdb] Set beside the earlier steps, where something was offered or done, here the only means named is the shedder’s own blood.

## The life of him who hath murdered

Amulek asks, “if a man murdereth, behold will our law, which is just, take the life of his brother? I say unto you, Nay… the law requireth the life of him who hath murdered; therefore there can be nothing which is short of an infinite atonement” ([[Alma 34:11–12]]). Set beside Numbers 35:31, 33, Amulek’s “the law requireth the life of him who hath murdered” is the same requirement as “he shall be surely put to death” and “by the blood of him that shed it”; the comparison is this page’s. One reading sets Amulek’s words beside Israel’s law that “every man shall be put to death for his own sin” ([[Deut. 24:16]]);[@sc-knowhy-142] another sees in them “a notion not found in the law of Moses but present in Mesoamerica.”[@gee-last-sacrifice] The answer, he says, is “not a sacrifice of man, neither of beast… but it must be an infinite and eternal sacrifice” ([[Alma 34:10]]).

## One Mediator

Hebrews sets the many priests, “not suffered to continue by reason of death,” against the one who “ever liveth to make intercession” ([[Heb. 7:23–25]]), and says “it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins” ([[Heb. 10:4]]). “There is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus; who gave himself a ransom for all” ([[1 Tim. 2:5–6]]). “Jesus Christ was the only one capable of making a perfect atonement for all mankind.”[@gs-atone-atonement] He pleads, “behold the blood of thy Son which was shed… Father, spare these my brethren that believe on my name” ([[D&C 45:4–5]]). See also [Moses the Mediator](/exodus/themes/moses-the-mediator/#mediator).

## Through the merits of the Holy Messiah

A disciple, like those in [[Ps. 49:7]], has no ransom to give for himself or his brother, for “there is no flesh that can dwell in the presence of God, save it be through the merits, and mercy, and grace of the Holy Messiah” ([[2 Ne. 2:8]]). The Atonement’s blessings come to an accountable person “only if he has faith in Jesus Christ, repents of his sins, receives the ordinances of salvation, and obeys the commandments of God.”[@gs-atone-atonement]
