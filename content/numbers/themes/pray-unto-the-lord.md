Five times in Numbers, when a judgment falls or threatens, Moses speaks to the LORD for others (with Aaron, once), and the text gives both the request and the answer. Between the fourth and the fifth, at [[Num. 16:46–48]], no prayer is recorded, only an act. At the first the fire is quenched. At the last the people ask in so many words that the serpents be taken away, and the answer is of another kind.

| Another shape | [[Num. 12:13–14]] | “Heal her now” / “shut out from the camp seven days” |
| Another shape | [[Num. 14:19–23]] | “Pardon” / “I have pardoned… But”; “Surely they shall not see the land” |

<h2 id="quenched">Quenched</h2>

“And the people cried unto Moses; and when Moses prayed unto the LORD, the fire was quenched” ([[Num. 11:2]]). No words of the prayer are given. The Hebrew word translated “prayed” occurs in Numbers only here and at [[Num. 21:7]].[@oshb-wlc] Set beside each other, the two verses have one shape: the people come to Moses, Moses prays to the LORD. The first ends with the fire gone; the last ends with a serpent on a pole. The fire was “extinguished very quickly, at the intercession of Moses.”[@keil-numbers] See [chapter 11](/numbers/11/).

<h2 id="another-shape">Granted, in another shape</h2>

Moses cries, “Heal her now, O God, I beseech thee” ([[Num. 12:13]]). The answer: “let her be shut out from the camp seven days, and after that let her be received in again” ([[Num. 12:14]]). He asks, “Pardon, I beseech thee, the iniquity of this people” ([[Num. 14:19]]), and hears, “I have pardoned according to thy word: But as truly as I live, all the earth shall be filled with the glory of the LORD” ([[Num. 14:20–21]]). Then: “Surely they shall not see the land which I sware unto their fathers” ([[Num. 14:23]]). “Israel was spared destruction but lost the privilege of immediately entering the promised land.”[@ot-manual-numbers-13-36] See [chapter 12](/numbers/12/) and [chapter 14](/numbers/14/).

<h2 id="get-you-up">Get you up</h2>

Moses and Aaron ask, “shall one man sin, and wilt thou be wroth with all the congregation?” ([[Num. 16:22]]). The answer narrows the judgment from the whole congregation to the tents of Korah, Dathan and Abiram: “Get you up from about the tabernacle of Korah, Dathan, and Abiram” ([[Num. 16:24]]). Moses tells the congregation, “Depart, I pray you, from the tents of these wicked men” ([[Num. 16:26]]). Later the LORD says again, “Get you up from among this congregation, that I may consume them as in a moment” ([[Num. 16:45]]), and “they fell upon their faces,” but no prayer is recorded. Moses says, “Take a censer… and go quickly unto the congregation,” and Aaron “stood between the dead and the living; and the plague was stayed” ([[Num. 16:46–48]]). The motives Moses “had hitherto pleaded” for sparing the congregation “were now exhausted.”[@keil-numbers] The atonement of that act is the subject of [Between the Dead and the Living](/numbers/themes/between-the-dead-and-the-living/). See [chapter 16](/numbers/16/).

<h2 id="not-taken-away">Not taken away</h2>

“The people came to Moses, and said, We have sinned… pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us. And Moses prayed for the people” ([[Num. 21:7]]). The LORD said, “Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live” ([[Num. 21:8]]). Numbers itself does not say whether the serpents left. “The Lord did not take the serpents away. But He did make a way for the people to be saved if they were bitten.”[@ot-stories-brass-serpent] The look is taken up in [Seek Not after Your Own Eyes](/numbers/themes/seek-not-after-your-own-eyes/) and [chapter 21](/numbers/21/).

<h2 id="intercessor">The Intercessor lifted up</h2>

Of Christ: “he ever liveth to make intercession” ([[Heb. 7:25]]). Of the serpent: “as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up” ([[John 3:14]]). In Numbers 21 Moses prays, and a serpent is raised for the people to look upon. Set beside these, Hebrews and John say both of one Person. Latter-day Saints may read prophetic intercession “as typological for the role of Jesus,”[@sears-intercession] and the lifting up of the serpent is “a type of His own ‘lifting up’ upon the cross.”[@bd-serpent-brazen] For Moses as mediator, see [Moses the Mediator](/exodus/themes/moses-the-mediator/#mediator).

<h2 id="borne">Not removed, but borne</h2>

As in Numbers 21, Paul and the people of Alma asked for relief, and the answer was not removal but strength to bear it. Paul “besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me,” and was told, “My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness” ([[2 Cor. 12:8–9]]). The Lord told Alma’s people, “I will also ease the burdens which are put upon your shoulders… even while you are in bondage,” so “that ye may know of a surety that I, the Lord God, do visit my people in their afflictions” ([[Mosiah 24:14]]). “The challenges and difficulties were not immediately removed from the people. But Alma and his followers were strengthened, and their increased capacity made the burdens lighter.”[@bednar-bear-up-burdens] Deuteronomy's [Let It Suffice Thee](/deuteronomy/themes/let-it-suffice-thee/) also takes up 2 Cor. 12:8–9.
