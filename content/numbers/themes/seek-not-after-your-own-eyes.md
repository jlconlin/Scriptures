In Numbers, Israel first sees the land and itself through its own eyes. After the sentence, the LORD gives the sights: a fringe He tells Israel to look upon, a serpent He commands to be made and looked upon, eyes He opens, a land He shows.

## Sent to see

Moses sends the twelve to “see the land, what it is” ([[Num. 13:18]]). The ten report that “we were in our own sight as grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight” ([[Num. 13:33]]); the Hebrew word translated “sight” is the word for “eyes.”[@oshb-wlc] The LORD’s sentence is also told in sight: “all those men which have seen my glory… Surely they shall not see the land” ([[Num. 14:22–23]]). The Church’s cross-reference at “see” leads to Moses, who “sought diligently to sanctify his people that they might behold the face of God; But they hardened their hearts” ([[D&C 84:23–24]]).[@lds-scriptures] The notes at [[Num. 13:33]] and [[Num. 14:20]] tell the rest.

## A tassel to look upon

The first law after the sentence reads: “it shall be unto you for a fringe, that ye may look upon it, and remember all the commandments of the LORD, and do them; and that ye seek not after your own heart and your own eyes” ([[Num. 15:39]]). Set beside the spies’ searching, the Hebrew verb for “seek” is the one used twelve times in chapters 13–14, each time for the spies’ searching; 15:39 is its last use in the book.[@oshb-wlc] The searching commanded of the land is, here, forbidden when its object is “your own heart and your own eyes.” See the note at [[Num. 15:39]].

## A serpent to look upon

Bitten Israelites are told, “every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live,” and “when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived” ([[Num. 21:8–9]]). Jesus likens the serpent’s lifting up to His own: “As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up” ([[John 3:14–15]]).[@scripture-helps-num] It is “a symbol of Himself being lifted up on the cross.”[@gs-serpent-brazen] Scripture names what the look was: “as many as should look upon the Son of God with faith, having a contrite spirit, might live” ([[Hel. 8:15]]), while those who perished would not look “because they did not believe that it would heal them” ([[Alma 33:20]]). The notes at [[Num. 21:8]] take up the serpent.

## Eyes the LORD opened

“Then the LORD opened the eyes of Balaam, and he saw the angel of the LORD” ([[Num. 22:31]]); the man sought out for his reputation as a soothsayer “could not see what even his donkey could.”[@scripture-helps-num] The verb for “opened” is used in Numbers only of Balaam, three times.[@oshb-wlc] Balaam now speaks as one “having his eyes open” ([[Num. 24:4]]) and says, “I shall see him, but not now: I shall behold him, but not nigh: there shall come a Star out of Jacob” ([[Num. 24:17]]). Balaam “prophesies of the Messiah: There will come a Star out of Jacob,” and “not now” means “His coming was to be in the future, long after Moses’ time.”[@lds-scriptures] Set beside 22:31, the eyes that see the Star “not now” are the eyes the LORD opened: both verses use the same verb for “opened” with “eyes.”[@oshb-wlc] Read the notes at [[Num. 22:28]] and [[Num. 24:17]].

## Moses shown the land

“Get thee up into this mount Abarim, and see the land which I have given unto the children of Israel” ([[Num. 27:12]]). Set beside 14:23, the same verb and object: the generation “shall not see the land,” and Moses, who also “will see but not enter the promised land,”[@lds-scriptures] is told to see it. The rest is in [Let It Suffice Thee](/deuteronomy/themes/let-it-suffice-thee/).

## The look that lives

Set beside the sentence at Kadesh ([[Num. 14:22–23]]), the next things Israel is told to see are objects the LORD commands to be looked upon ([[Num. 15:39]]; [[Num. 21:8]]). Jesus taught that the serpent was lifted up as He would be ([[John 3:14–15]]); and in Numbers the Messiah is foreseen by a man whose eyes the LORD opened ([[Num. 24:17]]).

## Where this reaches now

A disciple whose own eyes report the walls and the giants stands where the ten stood, and what Numbers offers that eye is something else to look at: “Look unto me in every thought; doubt not, fear not” ([[D&C 6:36]]), and to look to God is “to keep His commandments, cry unto Him continually for His support, counsel with Him in all your doings, and let your heart be full of thanks unto Him day and night.”[@christofferson-look]
