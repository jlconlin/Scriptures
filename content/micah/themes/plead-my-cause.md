Micah opens with the Lord as “witness against you” ([[Micah 1:2]]), and chapters 6–7 are called a covenant lawsuit, in Hebrew a *rîb*.[@pike-micah] Near the end a fallen one who has confessed says the Lord will “plead my cause” ([[Micah 7:9]]). What changes is the Lord’s part in the *rîb*: in 6:2 the case is His, against His people; in 7:9 it is hers, against her enemy, and He pleads it.[@keil-micah] The step from there to Christ’s advocacy is made by other scripture, not by Micah 7:9.

<h2 id="witness">Witness against, and no answer</h2>

“Hear” opens each of the book’s three blocks ([[Micah 1:2]]; [[Micah 3:1]]; [[Micah 6:1]]).[@correa-jonah] Legal words run through the book.[@pierce-micah] The rulers should “know judgment” but “abhor judgment, and pervert all equity” ([[Micah 3:1]]; [[Micah 3:9]]). So “they cry unto the LORD, but he will not hear them: he will even hide his face” ([[Micah 3:4]]). The Hebrew is literally “will not answer them.”[@oshb-wlc] Compare [[Mosiah 11:24]], where the next verse sets the condition: “except they repent … I will not hear their prayers” ([[Mosiah 11:25]]).[@lds-scriptures] Yet the rulers “lean upon the LORD, and say, Is not the LORD among us?” ([[Micah 3:11]]).

<h2 id="controversy">The controversy, and what He requires</h2>

“The LORD hath a controversy with his people” ([[Micah 6:2]]), and the mountains are its witnesses.[@keil-micah] Then He asks them: “testify against me” ([[Micah 6:3]]), which is “answer against me,” that is, “accuse me.”[@keil-micah] They ask what to pay: “shall I give my firstborn for my transgression?” ([[Micah 6:7]]). “Salvation is not earned.”[@renlund-do-justly] What He requires is “to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God” ([[Micah 6:8]]).

<h2 id="plead">“Until he plead my cause”</h2>

In chapter 7 Micah seems to be “speaking on behalf of the people.”[@correa-jonah] “I will wait for the God of my salvation: my God will hear me” ([[Micah 7:7]]). “I will bear the indignation of the LORD, because I have sinned against him, until he plead my cause, and execute judgment for me” ([[Micah 7:9]]). The cause is Israel’s conflict with the enemy of 7:8 and 7:10, which the Lord “conducts … and secures its rights.”[@keil-micah] In Israel’s restoration “He will plead her cause.”[@ot-manual-11]

Set beside chapter 6, three points change what the verse shows.

- **The lawsuit word.** The noun *rîb* stands in Micah only at 6:2 (twice) and 7:9, and the verb only at 6:1 and 7:9.[@oshb-wlc] The word for the Lord’s case against His people is the word for the case He pleads for her. She says she will bear His indignation.
- **Do justice.** “To do justly” (6:8) and “execute judgment for me” (7:9) both join the verb “do” to *mišpāṭ*, “judgment,” the only two places in Micah where they meet.[@oshb-wlc] What He requires of man is to do justice; what the fallen one waits for is that He will do her justice.
- **Hearing.** The criers of chapter 3 “behaved themselves ill”; the speaker of chapter 7 says “I have sinned against him,” and is sure of being heard.

<h2 id="mercy">He delighteth in mercy</h2>

“Who is a God like unto thee, that pardoneth iniquity … because he delighteth in mercy” ([[Micah 7:18]]). The book ends with “His willingness to forgive all who repent.”[@scripture-helps-micah] “To love mercy as God does is inseparably connected to dealing justly.”[@renlund-do-justly]

<h2 id="advocate">The Advocate</h2>

Other prophets say it: “thy God that pleadeth the cause of his people” ([[Isa. 51:22]]); “he shall throughly plead their cause” ([[Jer. 50:34]]), in both the verb of Micah 7:9.[@oshb-wlc] In this dispensation the Lord says: “Listen to him who is the advocate with the Father, who is pleading your cause before him” ([[D&C 45:3]]). Set beside Micah 7:9, “pleading your cause” is the same English, and in both the one who pleads is the Lord. D&C 45 does not cite Micah.[@lds-scriptures] “We have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous” ([[1 Jn. 2:1]]), who “pleads our cause with Him.”[@gs-advocate]

If the sinner repents, “the Savior pays the penalty through the Atonement, invoking mercy.”[@gs-justice] In Micah the One with the controversy is the One who pleads. In D&C 45 the Advocate says: “Father, spare these my brethren that believe on my name” ([[D&C 45:5]]).

<h2 id="disciple">Where this reaches now</h2>

A cry that presumes on the Lord’s presence while refusing what He requires goes unheard: “except they repent … I will not hear their prayers” ([[Mosiah 11:25]]). He hears the one who confesses: “I, the Lord, forgive sins unto those who confess their sins before me and ask forgiveness” ([[D&C 64:7]]); in the Joseph Smith Translation, “if any man sin and repent, we have an advocate with the Father” (JST, [[1 Jn. 2:1]]).[@lds-scriptures] Doing justly includes that: a just person “repents when falling short, and keeps on trying.”[@renlund-do-justly] A disciple can say that “my Advocate, him who did no sin, whose blood was shed, will plead my cause.”[@ensign-2014-advocate]
