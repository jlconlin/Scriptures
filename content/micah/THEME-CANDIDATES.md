# Micah: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.** The candidates for the author are Fable's and Codex's lists (`.cache/batch/micah/reports/themes-fable.md` and `themes-codex.md`), shown to the author numbered; only the author's choice is recorded here.

## Author's selection (2026-10-23)

**One page is chosen** (author, 2026-10-23: “Yes, M1 sounds like a good candidate”): **the Lord's lawsuit becomes the Lord's advocacy**, Fable's first proposal, which Codex's first (“The Lord hath a controversy with his people”) also found. The Lord who is witness against His people (1:2) will not hear the rulers who abhor judgment (3:4, 7, 11), brings His controversy and asks them to testify against Him (6:1–8), and is at last the one who “plead[s] my cause, and execute[s] judgment for me” (7:7–10, 18): *rîb* only at 6:2 and 7:9, *mishpāṭ* from 3:1 to 7:9. To Christ: the Advocate “pleading your cause” ([[D&C 45:3–5]]), as a comparison of the page's own. Must not claim that 7:9 is about Christ's advocacy, that the silence of 3:4 is final, or that D&C 45 quotes Micah.

The other numbered proposals (M2–M7: the flock of thine heritage, the depths of the sea, the man who shall be the peace, the remnant gathered, the mountain of the house, out of thee shall he come forth) were not chosen.
