## Author's selection (2026-11-12)

One page (author: “Yes, page 1 only and fold in the page 2 stuff”). Both readers put it first; the second candidate, “Remember Me, O My God,” is not a page of its own and supplies this page's last movement.

### 1. “And Forsookest Them Not” (from [[Neh. 9:17]])

**The arc.** The prayer of chapter 9 sets God's faithfulness against Israel's cycle: three times He “forsookest them not” (9:17, 19, 31; one Hebrew verb, *ʿāzab*). The people answer in the same verb when they seal their covenant: “we will not forsake the house of our God” (10:39). Then, while Nehemiah is away, the cycle of 9:28 runs again in the people who confessed it: “Why is the house of God forsaken?” (13:11); “Did not your fathers thus?” (13:18). **Folded in from “Remember Me”:** the book opens asking God to remember His word (1:8) and closes asking Him to remember a man (13:31), with a people between who “neither were mindful” (9:17, the same verse that holds “forsookest them not”); and Nehemiah's own last prayers move from his deeds, “wipe not out my good deeds” (13:14; the verb of 4:5, “let not their sin be blotted out”), to God's mercy, “spare me according to the greatness of thy mercy” (13:22), to “Remember me, O my God, for good” (13:31).

**Thesis.** A renewed promise does not end the need for mercy: the covenant in Nehemiah is kept by a God who does not forsake, not by a people who never fail, and the book's last prayers rest on that.

**To Christ:** [[Mosiah 3:7–11]], [[Mosiah 4:1–3]], [[Mosiah 5:2–8]] (Benjamin's assembly names the source of the mercy; Szink and Welch set it beside Nehemiah 8–13); [[3 Ne. 15:5]]; [[2 Ne. 2:8]]; [[Jer. 31:31–34]]; the same verb in [[Ps. 22:1]] with [[Matt. 27:46]] and Elder Holland's “None Were with Him” (April 2009); [[Isa. 43:25]] (both verbs of the “remember me” prayers, the other way round). **Applies:** disciples renew covenants at the sacrament ([[D&C 20:77]]) having broken them before, and depend on the mercy of the One who was forsaken so that they would not be.

**Must not claim:** that the Psalm 22 join is anyone's but the page's (say so once); that *ʿāzab* never has God as its subject against Israel (9:28, “leftest thou them,” is the same verb, and the page says so); that Nehemiah 8 says why the people wept or that they received a remission of sins, as Benjamin's people did; that chapter 13 records the people's repentance, or that Nehemiah's reforms atone; that Nehemiah was wrong to ask to be remembered for his deeds; anything that rules on the marriages of 13:23–31 (the brief). Check every Hebrew count with `hebrew.mjs` before the page leans on it (Fable's: H5800 nine times, H2617 five, H2142 nine, H4229 twice).

## Not chosen

Praying, then working (chapters 1, 2, 4, 6); Tobiah inside the house (2, 6, 13); the covenant assemblies beside Joshua, Josiah and Benjamin; joy; the registers and the Book of Life; filling the city; the poor and Zion; the Sabbath and Jeremiah 17. Each is already carried by the chapter notes or lives mostly in one chapter.
