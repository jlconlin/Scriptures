The Lord told Moses of “a good land and a large, unto a land flowing with milk and honey” ([[Ex. 3:8]]). It was a relatively small land, and its position “as a land bridge between three continents gives it the title ‘crossroads of the East.’”[@ogden-1980-testing-ground] This guide sets out the shape of the land, the roads across it, the peoples around it, places the books name, and the land as the covenant's inheritance. For when things happened, see [A Timeline of the Old Testament](/old-testament/guides/israels-story/).

<h2 id="regions">The shape of the land</h2>

**Size.** “From Dan even to Beer-sheba” ([[Judg. 20:1]]) names the whole of Israel, Dan being its northern limit and Beersheba its southern.[@lds-scriptures][@bd-dan][@bd-beersheba] That is “roughly 150 miles,” and about 75 miles across at the widest.[@ot-manual-josh] The wider district between Syria and Egypt is “nearly 180 miles long and about 85 miles in average breadth.”[@bd-palestine] Deuteronomy names five regions: “in the plain, in the hills, and in the vale, and in the south, and by the sea side” ([[Deut. 1:7]]).[@lds-scriptures]

**From the sea eastward.** The Maritime Plain runs the length of the coast and includes Philistia and Sharon, the part from Carmel to Joppa.[@bd-palestine][@bd-sharon] Inland lies the Shephelah, “the Low Hills,”[@bd-palestine] between the coastal plain and the hills of Judah; its low hills and valleys allowed “more extensive agriculture.”[@pierce-micah] The King James Version prints it as “the plain,” “the valley” and “the vale” ([[Jer. 17:26]]; [[Jer. 32:44]]; [[Jer. 33:13]]).[@oshb-wlc] Beyond it is the hill country, broken by the plain of Esdraelon.[@bd-palestine] Esdraelon, the valley of Jezreel, separates the hills of Samaria from those of Galilee and is “the most fertile part of Palestine.”[@bd-esdraelon] Galilee, about 60 miles by 30, holds “some of the best land.”[@bd-galilee] In the south lies the Negev, “dry or parched land,” which the King James Version calls “the south country.”[@bd-negev]

**The Jordan.** The Jordan is formed by springs on Mount Hermon, falls to the Sea of Galilee and then to the Dead Sea, which is 1,290 feet below the Mediterranean and has no outlet.[@bd-jordan-river][@bd-dead-sea] The Judean hill country rises to 2,000–3,000 feet.[@bible-maps-14] In the Bible, “up” and “down” “usually indicated elevation”: Jerusalem stands high, so one goes up to it.[@interpreter-halverson-geographically] East of the river are Bashan, a district “rich in grain production,” Gilead, a wooded highland, then Moab, and Edom with Mount Seir.[@bd-bashan][@bd-gilead][@bd-seir] In the far north are Lebanon and Mount Hermon, 9,400 feet high.[@bd-lebanon][@bd-hermon] Bible Maps [1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bible-maps/map-1?lang=eng) and [14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bible-maps/map-14?lang=eng) label these regions, and the second prints their elevations.[@bible-maps-1]

**Rain.** The land “is not as the land of Egypt,” watered by foot; it is “a land of hills and valleys, and drinketh water of the rain of heaven” ([[Deut. 11:10–11]]).[@lds-scriptures] Moses calls it “a land which the Lord thy God careth for” ([[Deut. 11:12]]), “a land of wheat, and barley, and vines, and fig trees, and pomegranates” ([[Deut. 8:8]]).[@lds-scriptures] Israel sits where “in some years parts of the land may be untouched by rain,” and famine in the Bible “simply means it didn’t rain that year”; “thus God proved his people even through climate and rainfall.”[@ogden-1980-testing-ground] The prophets name “the former rain, and the latter rain” ([[Joel 2:23]]); Jeremiah speaks of “no latter rain” ([[Jer. 3:3]]), Hosea of “the latter and former rain” ([[Hosea 6:3]]).

<h2 id="roads">The roads</h2>

The verses name a few. Isaiah speaks of “the way of the sea, beyond Jordan, in Galilee of the nations” ([[Isa. 9:1]]). Israel asked Edom, “we will go by the king's high way” ([[Num. 20:17]]).[@lds-scriptures] God “led them not through the way of the land of the Philistines, although that was near” ([[Ex. 13:17]]). The Church's Bible Maps label no road by name; the only route on maps 1–10 is the “possible route of the Exodus.”[@bible-maps-2]

Galilee “was crossed by important highways leading to Damascus, Egypt, and eastern Palestine.”[@bd-galilee] Esdraelon was crossed “by several important highways,” which is why it so often became a battleground.[@bd-esdraelon] Megiddo lies “on the caravan route from Egypt to Damascus,” where Josiah died.[@bd-megiddo] The Shephelah held the roads by which Judah reached “the coastal highway.”[@pierce-micah] [Bible Map 5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bible-maps/map-5?lang=eng) shows the Assyrian Empire over this ground.[@bible-maps-5] The story of this land “is essentially a history of the struggle among these mightier civilizations to control this land bridge.”[@ogden-1980-testing-ground]

Matthew applies Isaiah’s Zebulun, Naphtali and “Galilee of the nations” ([[Isa. 9:1]]) to Jesus ([[Matt. 4:13–15]]).[@sc-knowhy-869] See [[Isa. 9]].

<h2 id="inheritance">Tribes, kingdoms and inheritance</h2>

**The tribes.** The Lord gave Moses the borders of “the land of Canaan with the coasts thereof” ([[Num. 34:2]]).[@lds-scriptures] The Reubenites and Gadites “received their inheritance… beyond Jordan eastward,” and the rest was divided “unto the nine tribes, and the half tribe of Manasseh” ([[Josh. 13:7–8]]).[@lds-scriptures] Levi “had no inheritance in Canaan,” but had the tithe and 48 cities.[@bd-levites] Bible Map 3 labels the tribes.[@bible-maps-3]

**The kingdoms.** Solomon “reigned over all kingdoms from the river unto the land of the Philistines, and unto the border of Egypt” ([[1 Kgs. 4:21]]),[@lds-scriptures] the reach Bible Map 4 draws.[@bible-maps-4] The division came about 925 B.C.: ten tribes in the north, headquartered at Shechem, and Judah and Benjamin in the south at Jerusalem.[@bd-israel-kingdom]

**The covenant.** “Unto thy seed have I given this land” ([[Gen. 15:18]]); “all the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession” ([[Gen. 17:8]]). The Abrahamic covenant includes the promise that Abraham's posterity would receive “certain lands as an eternal inheritance.”[@bd-abraham-covenant] Land “was among the blessings of Israel’s covenant with the Lord,” and the people were exiled from it, “yet there was hope of return.”[@keller-land-lands] Moses promised that the Lord would “gather thee from all the nations” and “bring thee into the land which thy fathers possessed” ([[Deut. 30:3–5]]).[@lds-scriptures] There are “many promised lands.”[@gs-promised-land] “The tribe of Judah will return to the city of Jerusalem and the area surrounding it.”[@gospel-principles-42]
