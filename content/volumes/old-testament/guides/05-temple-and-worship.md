“Let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them” ([[Ex. 25:8]]). The Lord “has always commanded His people to build temples,” and the tabernacle Moses built was “a portable temple.”[@gs-temple-house-of-the-lord] This guide follows the house of the Lord through the Old Testament: the tabernacle, Solomon's temple and the two that followed, the priests and Levites who served in them, how Israel worshipped there, what the prophets said when the worship went hollow, and the temple Ezekiel saw.

<h2 id="tabernacle">The tabernacle</h2>

The Lord told Moses to make the tabernacle “after the pattern” He showed ([[Ex. 25:9]]). It stood in a court 100 cubits by 50, with the altar of burnt offering and a brass laver for the priests' washing before the tent door.[@bd-tabernacle] A veil divided the tent in two. In the Holy Place were the altar of incense, the candlestick and the table of shewbread; in the Holy of Holies, “only one piece of furniture,” the ark, whose lid, the mercy seat, was “the place of the manifestation of God's glory” and “God's throne in Israel.”[@bd-tabernacle] The three spaces, the court, the Holy Place and the Holy of Holies, each “represented an increasing proximity to God's holy presence.”[@scripture-helps-ex-35]

The veil “symbolized the ‘separation between God and man.’”[@scripture-helps-ex-35] Hebrews says Christians may “enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, by a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh” ([[Heb. 10:19–20]]).[@sc-knowhy-698]

Three Hebrew words name the house. “Sanctuary” in Exodus 25:8 is *miqdash*; “tabernacle” in 25:9 is *mishkan*; and in 40:34 “the tent of the congregation” is *ohel moed*, “tent of meeting.”[@oshb-wlc][@lds-scriptures] The verb “dwell” (*shakan*) “implies a nonpermanent residence,” so the tabernacle may be better understood as “a meeting place.”[@belnap-dwell-among]

When the work was finished, “a cloud covered the tent of the congregation, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle” ([[Ex. 40:34]]). When Israel camped, the tabernacle stood “in the precise center of the camp.”[@ensign-tabernacle-2018] After the conquest it was set at Shiloh, and in Solomon's reign it was at Gibeon; “after the building of the temple it entirely disappears from the history.”[@bd-tabernacle]

“Our modern temples share similarities with Israel's tabernacle, but they certainly don't match its description in Exodus. And we don't kill animals in our temples—the Savior's Atonement ended animal sacrifice almost 2,000 years ago.”[@cfm-ot-2026-tabernacle] The ark and the high priest are compared with Christ, as God's presence and as Mediator. The washing, anointing, clothing, covenants and veil of today's temple are compared with the tabernacle's.[@cfm-ot-2026-tabernacle] For the furnishings one by one, see [Exodus 25–40](/exodus/25/) and the themes [“That I May Dwell among Them”](/exodus/themes/that-i-may-dwell-among-them/) and [“What Israel Builds”](/exodus/themes/what-israel-builds/).

<h2 id="temples">Solomon's temple, and the two after it</h2>

| House | Built | What it held | What became of it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tabernacle | Set up on the first day of the first month of the second year after the Exodus[@bd-tabernacle] | Ark, mercy seat, one candlestick, one table[@bd-tabernacle] | Shiloh, then Gibeon; gone after the temple[@bd-tabernacle] |
| Solomon's temple | Begun in Solomon's fourth year, finished in his eleventh ([[1 Kgs. 6:1]], [[1 Kgs. 6:38]]) | The old ark and mercy seat; new cherubim; ten candlesticks and ten tables[@bd-temple-of-solomon] | Burned by Nebuchadnezzar ([[2 Kgs. 25:9]]); 587 B.C.[@bd-temple] |
| Zerubbabel's temple | Dedicated 516 B.C.;[@bd-temple-of-zerubbabel] also dated “around 515”[@scripture-helps-ezra] | About Solomon's size; no ark, so the Holy of Holies was empty[@scripture-helps-ezra] | Rebuilt by Herod from 17 B.C.[@bd-temple-of-herod] |
| Herod's temple | From 17 B.C.; the design not complete until A.D. 64[@bd-temple-of-herod] | No ark; one candlestick and one table, as in the tabernacle[@bd-temple-of-herod] | Destroyed by the Romans, A.D. 70[@bd-temple-of-herod] |

Solomon's temple “was built after the model of the tabernacle, the dimensions of each part being exactly double.”[@bd-temple-of-solomon] David “gave to Solomon his son the pattern” ([[1 Chr. 28:11]]), and the house rose at Jerusalem “in mount Moriah” ([[2 Chr. 3:1]]). It was small because “the worshippers remained outside; only the priests went within.”[@bd-temple-of-solomon] It was dedicated at the Feast of Tabernacles; “the cloud filled the house of the LORD” and “the priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud” ([[1 Kgs. 8:10–11]]).[@bd-temple-of-solomon] Solomon prayed: “will God indeed dwell on the earth? behold, the heaven and heaven of heavens cannot contain thee; how much less this house that I have builded?” ([[1 Kgs. 8:27]]). He asked that when Israel prayed “toward this place,” God would hear in heaven ([[1 Kgs. 8:30]]). The Lord answered, “I have hallowed this house … to put my name there for ever.”[@scripture-helps-1-kings-24]

The house was often stripped, polluted and cleansed; “finally it was burned to the ground and utterly destroyed by Nebuchadnezzar.”[@bd-temple-of-solomon] For the year, see [the timeline](/old-testament/guides/israels-story/#dates). Isaiah had named Cyrus as the one who would say “to the temple, Thy foundation shall be laid” ([[Isa. 44:28]]). When the foundation was laid, “many of the priests and Levites … who were ancient men, that had seen the first house … wept with a loud voice; and many shouted aloud for joy” ([[Ezra 3:12]]). Haggai asked, “Who is left among you that saw this house in her first glory?” and promised, “The glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former” ([[Hag. 2:3]], [[Hag. 2:9]]). “The desire of all nations” ([[Hag. 2:7]]) “can refer to the Savior,” with “multiple fulfillments.”[@scripture-helps-hag-1-2]

The tabernacle's completion, Solomon's dedication and the Kirtland Temple's have been set side by side as one pattern: “God comes to His new house in glory.”[@sc-knowhy-806] President Nelson said Solomon's dedicatory prayer provides “patterns that are employed for temples today.”[@sc-knowhy-814]

<h2 id="priests">The priests and the Levites</h2>

Aaron and his sons were taken, “that he may minister unto me in the priest’s office” ([[Ex. 28:1]]). The Lord took the Levites “instead of all the firstborn” ([[Num. 3:12]]) (see [The Firstborn](/exodus/themes/the-firstborn/)). The Levites were given to Aaron and his sons “to do the service of the children of Israel in the tabernacle” ([[Num. 8:19]]). They assisted the priests, acted as musicians, and had no inheritance in Canaan but the tithe and 48 cities.[@bd-levites] Only Aaron and his sons could be priests, and the high priest was chosen from Aaron's firstborn line.[@bd-aaronic-priesthood] A priest was a mediator who offered sacrifice and incense, taught the law and blessed the people;[@bd-priests] the blessing begins “The LORD bless thee, and keep thee” ([[Num. 6:24]]), and the priests “shall put my name upon the children of Israel” ([[Num. 6:27]]). The description of Jesus blessing the people in 3 Nephi 19 parallels words of that blessing.[@sc-knowhy-212]

The high priest wore a gold plate engraved “HOLINESS TO THE LORD” ([[Ex. 28:36]]); his main duty was the service of the Day of Atonement.[@bd-high-priest] On that day Aaron might not enter within the veil “at all times … that he die not” ([[Lev. 16:2]]). Hebrews teaches that “Jesus Christ is the great High Priest, of whom all the others were pre-figures.”[@bd-high-priest]

The Aaronic Priesthood was “of lesser power and authority than the priesthood of Melchizedek,” given after Israel failed to keep the gospel law under Moses; its limit to Levi's tribe “was lifted when the law of Moses was fulfilled.”[@bd-aaronic-priesthood]

The prophets charged the priests with failing in this work. Malachi: “if ye offer the blind for sacrifice, is it not evil?” ([[Mal. 1:8]]); the animals were to be without blemish.[@scripture-helps-mal] Hosea: “My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge” ([[Hos. 4:6]]). Ezekiel: the priests “have put no difference between the holy and profane” ([[Ezek. 22:26]]). See [Malachi's “Purified Offering”](/malachi/themes/purified-offering/).

<h2 id="worship">How Israel worshipped</h2>

**Sacrifice.** Two lambs a day were offered ([[Ex. 29:38]]), “a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle” ([[Ex. 29:42]]). Altar sacrifices were “sin offerings, burnt offerings, and peace offerings”;[@bd-sacrifices] the earliest sacrifices were “in a similitude of the sacrifice that would be made of the Only Begotten Son of God.”[@bd-sacrifices] See [Law and Covenant](/old-testament/guides/law-and-covenant/#sacrifice).

**The feasts.** “Three times in a year shall all thy males appear before the LORD thy God in the place which he shall choose” ([[Deut. 16:16]]), at unleavened bread, weeks and tabernacles; this “was not and could not be generally or even frequently observed” in the Old Testament period.[@bd-feasts] Psalms 113–118 (the Hallel) were sung at the Passover “in later times.”[@bd-feasts] See [Law and Covenant](/old-testament/guides/law-and-covenant/#feasts).

**The psalms.** David set Levites “over the service of song in the house of the LORD” ([[1 Chr. 6:31]]). The sons of Korah and Asaph were Levite musicians, and “the psalms associated with the sons of Korah were likely sung as part of temple worship.”[@scripture-helps-ps-49] At Solomon's dedication, “the trumpeters and singers were as one” and then “the house was filled with a cloud” ([[2 Chr. 5:13]]); the singing has been read as what “directly led to the presence of God entering into the temple.”[@hopkin-psalms-sung] The Psalms “have been called the ‘hymn book’ of the ancient temple.”[@sc-knowhy-437] Psalms 120–134, titled “A Song of degrees,” are often called “pilgrim songs”; they have also been called a “songbook for pilgrimage.”[@larsen-ascending-hill] “I was glad when they said unto me, Let us go into the house of the LORD” ([[Ps. 122:1]]). “How amiable are thy tabernacles, O LORD of hosts! My soul longeth, yea, even fainteth for the courts of the LORD” ([[Ps. 84:1–2]]). “Who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD? or who shall stand in his holy place?” ([[Ps. 24:3]]); “the mountain of the Lord” in the Old Testament “often refers to the temple.”[@scripture-helps-ps-1] See [Who May Enter?](/psalms/themes/who-may-enter/).

**Prayer toward the house.** Solomon asked that the prayer of a captive Israel “toward their land … the city which thou hast chosen, and the house which I have built” be heard ([[1 Kgs. 8:48]]). Daniel “kneeled upon his knees three times a day,” his windows “open in his chamber toward Jerusalem” ([[Dan. 6:10]]).

**More than sacrifice.** “To obey is better than sacrifice” ([[1 Sam. 15:22]]). “The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart” ([[Ps. 51:17]]). “I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings” ([[Hos. 6:6]]); the Lord “placed more value on their heartfelt devotion to Him and compassion for others,” while sacrifices “were still important.”[@scripture-helps-hosea] Micah answers his own question about rams and oil: “do justly, and … love mercy, and … walk humbly with thy God” ([[Micah 6:8]]). Jeremiah warned against “lying words, saying, The temple of the LORD, The temple of the LORD” ([[Jer. 7:4]]); his people trusted the temple as Israel had once trusted the ark at Shiloh.[@halverson-swine] See [The Sacrifice God Desires](/psalms/themes/the-sacrifice-god-desires/) and [Mercy, Not Sacrifice](/hosea/themes/mercy-not-sacrifice/).

**The synagogue.** The synagogue was a Jewish meetinghouse with an ark of the scrolls, a reading desk and seats; its Sabbath service included readings from the law and the prophets and a sermon.[@bd-synagogue] How it began is “unknown.”[@cohen-temple-synagogue]

<h2 id="ezekiel">The temple Ezekiel saw, and the temple to come</h2>

In the twenty-fifth year of the captivity, “in the visions of God brought he me into the land of Israel, and set me upon a very high mountain” ([[Ezek. 40:1–2]]). A guide with a measuring reed shows him “a glorious, complete temple,” and the vision ends with the city's new name, “The LORD is there” ([[Ezek. 48:35]]).[@rennaker-holiness] “The glory of the LORD filled the house” ([[Ezek. 43:5]]), and “waters issued out from under the threshold of the house eastward” ([[Ezek. 47:1]]). Joseph Smith spoke of “water come out from under the temple”; Elder Renlund reads the waters as temple blessings that grow and heal.[@scripture-helps-ezek] The river has been linked with “the pure river of water of life” (Revelation 22:1).[@cowan-ot-temples] See [Waters out of the Sanctuary](/ezekiel/themes/waters-out-of-the-sanctuary/) and [Ezekiel 40–48](/ezekiel/40/).

The sources differ on what the temple is for. Elder Talmage: whether Ezekiel “considered the design so shown as one to be subsequently realized, or as but a grand yet unattainable ideal, is not declared.” President Joseph Fielding Smith: the temple “will be used for ordinance work after the gathering of Israel,” and “the law of sacrifice will have to be restored,” so that the sons of Levi “offer such a sacrifice again.”[@ot-manual-ezek-25-48] Another reading: “no provision is made in it for any but the most simple rites, and these are not those of the sacred endowments as we know them.”[@cowan-ot-temples] Animal sacrifice ended with the Atonement.[@cfm-ot-2026-tabernacle] This guide does not choose among them.

Isaiah saw that “the mountain of the LORD's house shall be established in the top of the mountains” ([[Isa. 2:2]]). The prophecy “has multiple fulfillments”: a temple in Jerusalem in the last days, and temples throughout the world.[@scripture-helps-isa-1-12] It has also been read as a temple of Judah “upon Mount Moriah, in the last days.”[@chadwick-temple-prophecy] Malachi: “the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple” ([[Mal. 3:1]]), which “likely has multiple fulfillments,” including Kirtland in 1836.[@scripture-helps-mal]

Temples “are literally houses of the Lord,” where “individuals make sacred covenants with God,” and “their principal purpose is to provide ordinances necessary for the children of God to enable them to return to dwell with Him.”[@gospel-topics-temples] “Building and properly using a temple is one of the marks of the true Church in any dispensation.”[@bd-temple]
