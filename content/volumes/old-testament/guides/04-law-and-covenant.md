A covenant between God and man is not an agreement between equals: “God in His good pleasure fixes the terms, which man accepts.”[@bd-covenant] He then promises blessings for obedience.[@gs-covenant] The Hebrew word, *bĕrît*, covers agreements in which one or both parties commit to stipulations, and stresses relationship and obligation.[@hopkin-covenant] This guide sets out the covenants in order, what Torah and the law of Moses were, sacrifice, the feasts, clean and unclean, and the blessings, cursings and renewals that close the story.

<h2 id="covenants">The covenants, in order</h2>

| Covenant | With whom | Token or mark | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adam | Adam and Eve[@scripture-helps-everlasting-covenant] | — | [[Moses 5:5–8]]; [[Moses 6:52]] |
| Noah | Noah, his seed, every living creature | the bow in the cloud | [[Gen. 9:8–17]] |
| Abraham | Abraham and his seed | circumcision | [[Gen. 12]]; [[Gen. 15]]; [[Gen. 17]]; [[Gen. 22]]; [[Abr. 2:8–11]] |
| Sinai | all Israel | blood on altar and people; the Sabbath | [[Ex. 19]]; [[Ex. 24]]; [[Ex. 31:16]] |
| Levi | Phinehas and the priests | “an everlasting priesthood” | [[Num. 25:10–13]]; [[Mal. 2:4–8]] |
| David | David and his house | a throne “for ever” | [[2 Sam. 7]]; [[Ps. 89]] |
| New covenant | the house of Israel and Judah | the law written in the heart | [[Jer. 31:31–34]]; [[Ezek. 36:25–28]] |

**Adam.** An angel tells Adam that the offering of the firstlings is “a similitude of the sacrifice of the Only Begotten of the Father” ([[Moses 5:7]]), and the Lord tells him to believe, repent and be baptized ([[Moses 6:52]]). After periods of apostasy the Lord reestablished this everlasting covenant with Enoch, Noah, Abraham and Moses; it is “new” because it is reestablished, “everlasting” because it is eternal.[@scripture-helps-everlasting-covenant]

**Noah.** “I establish my covenant with you, and with your seed after you” ([[Gen. 9:9]]), and the bow is “a token of a covenant between me and the earth” ([[Gen. 9:13]]). In the Joseph Smith Translation this renews the covenant made with Enoch.[@hopkin-covenant]

**Abraham.** God promises “a great nation” and that “in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed” ([[Gen. 12:2–3]]); makes a covenant “in the same day” ([[Gen. 15:18]]); and calls it “an everlasting covenant, to be a God unto thee, and to thy seed after thee” ([[Gen. 17:7]]). Circumcision was its token.[@bd-circumcision] The Book of Abraham adds that his seed “shall bear this ministry and Priesthood unto all nations” ([[Abr. 2:9]]). Its promises include Christ through his lineage, land as an eternal inheritance, and adoption of Gentiles into the house of Israel.[@bd-abraham-covenant] It includes the gospel, the higher priesthood and celestial marriage.[@gs-abrahamic-covenant] The promises to the seed appear to be granted without condition, yet must be accepted by faith.[@hopkin-covenant] More in [The Covenant with Abraham](/genesis/themes/covenant-with-abraham/).

**Sinai.** “If ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant, then ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me” and “a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation” ([[Ex. 19:5–6]]). The people consent, and Moses sprinkles blood on them: “Behold the blood of the covenant” ([[Ex. 24:8]]). The Sabbath is “a perpetual covenant” ([[Ex. 31:16]]). Latter-day Saints see the Mosaic covenant as a modified renewal of what came before, shaped by Israel’s refusal to enter God’s presence at Sinai. They see the priesthood authority it provided as subsumed within the broader priesthood authority of the Abrahamic covenant.[@hopkin-covenant] See [What Was Offered at Sinai](/exodus/themes/offered-at-sinai/) and [The Blood of the Covenant](/exodus/themes/blood-of-the-covenant/).

**Levi.** “Behold, I give unto him my covenant of peace… the covenant of an everlasting priesthood” (Num. 25:12–13).[@lds-scriptures] Malachi recalls it: “My covenant was with him of life and peace” ([[Mal. 2:5]]), and says it was corrupted.[@hopkin-covenant] See [From Polluted Offerings](/malachi/themes/purified-offering/).

**David.** “Thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever” (2 Sam. 7:16).[@lds-scriptures] Psalm 89 restates it: “I have made a covenant with my chosen, I have sworn unto David my servant” ([[Ps. 89:3]]). Psalm 132:11–12 adds that David’s earthly kingdom would endure only if his descendants stayed faithful.[@scripture-helps-2-sam]

**The new covenant.** “I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel” ([[Jer. 31:31]]): “I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts” ([[Jer. 31:33]]); “A new heart also will I give you” ([[Ezek. 36:26]]). “New” means the covenant, “the fulness of [the] gospel,” revealed anew in the last days.[@scripture-helps-jer-31-33] Latter-day Saints read it as the Abrahamic covenant made new through Christ.[@hopkin-covenant] The new and everlasting covenant and the Abrahamic covenant “are essentially the same.”[@nelson-everlasting-covenant] All saving priesthood ordinances come with covenants.[@gospel-topics-covenant] See [The Covenant Written in the Heart](/jeremiah/themes/new-covenant/) and [A New Heart and a New Spirit](/ezekiel/themes/new-heart-new-spirit/).

<h2 id="torah">Torah, and what the law of Moses was for</h2>

*Torah* comes from a verb meaning to point out or direct; it is divine direction and instruction, and includes particularly the five books of Moses.[@bd-torah] The Pentateuch is the “fivefold book” that contains the Torah. It was written by Moses, who used several documentary sources, though scribes and copyists have left their traces; latter-day revelation confirms Moses as the author (1 Ne. 5:10–11; Moses 1:40–41).[@bd-pentateuch] The laws are spread across Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy.[@belnap-law-moses] The law has been described as “a cobbling together of a number of older legal and cultic traditions,” among them a Holiness Code (Leviticus 1–7 and 17–27) and the Deuteronomic code.[@belnap-law-moses]

The law of Moses replaced a higher law Israel had failed to obey.[@bd-law-of-moses] Moses “sought diligently to sanctify his people that they might behold the face of God” (D&C 84:23). “But they hardened their hearts and could not endure his presence” (D&C 84:24). The lesser priesthood continued, holding “the preparatory gospel,” which is “the gospel of repentance and of baptism, and the remission of sins, and the law of carnal commandments” (D&C 84:26–27).[@lds-scriptures] In the Joseph Smith Translation the second tables come “after the law of a carnal commandment” (JST Ex. 34:2).[@lds-scriptures] The law still included faith, repentance, baptism and the Ten Commandments, and was “a preparatory gospel to bring its adherents to Christ.”[@bd-law-of-moses] Following D&C 84, the law can be grouped into the preparatory gospel and the law of carnal commandments (offerings, and performances such as dietary and purification laws), “added because of transgressions.”[@brandt-law-moses]

The Book of Mormon reads the law as pointing to Christ: “for this intent we keep the law of Moses, it pointing our souls to him” ([[Jacob 4:5]]); “this is the whole meaning of the law, every whit pointing to that great and last sacrifice” ([[Alma 34:14]]).[@sc-knowhy-424] Paul: “the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ” (Gal. 3:24).[@lds-scriptures] Christ says “the law is fulfilled that was given unto Moses” ([[3 Ne. 15:4]]), and the law of Moses is not the same as the law of sacrifice, whose principles continue.[@ballard-sacrifice]

Leviticus’s dominant thought is “the presence of a holy God in the midst of a holy people.”[@bd-leviticus] “Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy” (Lev. 19:2).[@lds-scriptures] See [That I May Dwell among Them](/exodus/themes/that-i-may-dwell-among-them/) and, for the psalms’ praise of the law, [The Law and the Lost Sheep](/psalms/themes/the-law-and-the-lost-sheep/).

<h2 id="sacrifice">Sacrifice</h2>

The law of sacrifice began with Adam’s firstlings.[@bd-sacrifices] Under Moses the altar sacrifices were of three kinds, sin, burnt and peace offerings, with rules in Lev. 1–7.[@bd-sacrifices][@gs-law-of-moses] Each animal sacrifice had six acts, among them the offerer’s presenting the animal, laying on hands, slaying it, and the priest’s application of the blood.[@bd-sacrifices] The hand “upon the head of the burnt offering” (Lev. 1:4) made the animal the offerer’s representative and substitute.[@bd-sacrifices] “It is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul” (Lev. 17:11).[@lds-scriptures] When sin, burnt and peace offerings came together, the order was sin first, burnt second, peace third: atonement, sanctification, fellowship.[@bd-sacrifices]

What it pointed to: the sacrifices offered from Adam on symbolized the Only Begotten’s sacrifice,[@gs-sacrifice] and like Christ, the animal was without blemish and its life’s blood was spilled.[@ballard-sacrifice] His death ended animal sacrifice as a gospel ordinance,[@gs-sacrifice] and the sacrament replaced it.[@scripture-helps-ex-35]

**The Day of Atonement** (Lev. 16) appears to be the only fast ordered by the law.[@bd-fasts] Wearing white linen, the high priest entered the Holy of Holies on the only day he could.[@scripture-helps-ex-35] Two goats were chosen by lot, one for the LORD and one for Azazel ([[Lev. 16:8]]).[@bd-fasts] The second goat has been read two ways: its ritual “signified the sending away of the sins of the people now expiated to the Evil One”;[@bd-fasts] or the scapegoat “can symbolize Jesus Christ.”[@scripture-helps-ex-35] The jubilee year was announced by a trumpet on this day.[@bd-jubilee-year-of] On empty sacrifice, see [The Sacrifice God Desires](/psalms/themes/the-sacrifice-god-desires/).

<h2 id="feasts">The feasts and the sabbath years</h2>

“Three times in a year shall all thy males appear before the LORD thy God… in the feast of unleavened bread, and in the feast of weeks, and in the feast of tabernacles” (Deut. 16:16).[@lds-scriptures] This requirement was not generally observed in Old Testament times.[@bd-feasts]

| Feast | When | What it remembered or marked | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sabbath | weekly | God’s rest at creation; the redemption from Egypt[@bd-sabbath] | [[Ex. 20:8–11]] |
| Passover | 14th Abib[@bd-feasts] | the passing over of Israel’s houses; redemption from Egypt[@bd-feasts] | [[Ex. 12]] |
| Unleavened Bread | seven days[@bd-feasts] | joined to Passover[@bd-feasts] | [[Ex. 12:15]] |
| Firstfruits | morrow after the Sabbath[@bd-feasts] | a sheaf of the barley harvest waved[@bd-feasts] | Lev. 23:10–14 |
| Weeks (Pentecost) | fifty days after Passover[@bd-feasts] | the wheat harvest[@bd-feasts] | Lev. 23:16 |
| Trumpets | first of the seventh month | “a memorial of blowing of trumpets” | Lev. 23:24 |
| Day of Atonement | 10th of the seventh month[@bd-fasts] | national fast and atonement[@bd-fasts] | Lev. 16 |
| Tabernacles | 15th to 21st of the seventh month[@bd-feasts] | the wilderness sojourn and the ingathering[@bd-feasts] | Lev. 23:34 |
| Sabbatical year; jubilee | every 7th; every 50th year | the land’s rest, debts released[@bd-sabbatical-year]; land returned, bondmen freed[@bd-jubilee-year-of] | Lev. 25 |

**Passover.** The unblemished lambs are a symbol of the Lamb of God.[@gs-passover] The lamb’s blood is set beside Christ’s: as it saved the Hebrews from death, His saves all from physical and spiritual death.[@sc-knowhy-845] See [The Firstborn](/exodus/themes/the-firstborn/).

**Weeks.** It is also read as a celebration of the giving of the law at Sinai.[@sc-knowhy-90]

**Trumpets.** The law names the day and calls it a sabbath, “a memorial of blowing of trumpets, an holy convocation” (Lev. 23:24), but gives no reason for it.[@lds-scriptures]

**Tabernacles.** In the sabbatical year the whole law was read at this feast.[@bd-sabbatical-year]

**Sabbatical year and jubilee.** The seventh year the land kept “a sabbath for the LORD” (Lev. 25:4); there is no Old Testament record of a jubilee being kept.[@bd-jubilee-year-of] *Yōbēl* means “trumpet.”[@sc-knowhy-136]

<h2 id="clean-unclean">Clean and unclean</h2>

The categories covered food, childbirth, death and leprosy. Uncleanness was ceremonial, not moral; touching a dead body or bearing children was not “morally evil.” Except for the prohibition against blood, which goes back to Noah, these regulations belonged to the law of carnal commandments. They were fulfilled by the Atonement and are no longer required.[@bd-clean-unclean] “Ye shall therefore put difference between clean beasts and unclean” (Lev. 20:25).[@lds-scriptures]

The reason given is holiness: “ye shall therefore be holy, for I am holy” (Lev. 11:45).[@lds-scriptures] Uncleanness cut a person off from the covenant people for a time.[@sc-knowhy-479] Spiritually, clean means free from sin.[@gs-clean-unclean] Swine and other animals once banned are not prohibited under the current health code, D&C 89.[@lund-ot-laws]

<h2 id="renewals">Blessings, cursings, and the covenant renewed</h2>

Deuteronomy means “repetition of the law.” The book contains Moses’s last discourses on the plains of Moab, with a solemn renewal of the covenant in chapters 27–30.[@bd-deuteronomy] Its shape resembles an ancient Near Eastern treaty: history, stipulations, blessings and curses, and periodic reading.[@belnap-law-moses] Leviticus 26 pairs them too: blessings, “If ye walk in my statutes” (Lev. 26:3), and curses, “But if ye will not hearken unto me” (Lev. 26:14).[@lds-scriptures] “I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life” (Deut. 30:19).[@lds-scriptures] The next generation renewed the covenant before entering the land.[@scripture-helps-deut]

The covenant was renewed again and again. Joshua built an altar on Mount Ebal, and the tribes stood on Gerizim and Ebal, a covenant renewal similar to Sinai;[@scripture-helps-josh] at Shechem “Joshua made a covenant with the people that day” (Josh. 24:25). Josiah “made a covenant before the LORD… And all the people stood to the covenant” (2 Kgs. 23:3). After the exile the people “entered into a curse, and into an oath, to walk in God’s law” (Neh. 10:29).[@lds-scriptures] Many of the afflictions the Zeniffites suffered when they broke their covenants are those promised to Israel if they broke theirs.[@sc-knowhy-369]

The Old Testament’s last word is “Remember ye the law of Moses my servant” ([[Mal. 4:4]]). The Lord who gave the law says, “the law in me is fulfilled” ([[3 Ne. 15:5]]). All who accept Jesus Christ and keep sacred covenants with Him become part of the house of Israel.[@scripture-helps-everlasting-covenant] For the order of events, see [A Timeline of the Old Testament](/old-testament/guides/israels-story/).
