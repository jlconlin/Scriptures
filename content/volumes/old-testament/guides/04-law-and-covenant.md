A covenant between God and man is not an agreement between equals: “God in His good pleasure fixes the terms, which man accepts.”[@bd-covenant] He then promises blessings for obedience.[@gs-covenant] The Lord “has always commanded His people to build temples,” and the tabernacle Moses built was “a portable temple”:[@gs-temple-house-of-the-lord] “Let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them” ([[Ex. 25:8]]). This guide sets out the covenants, the law, the tabernacle and temples, the priests, sacrifice, the feasts, and the temple Ezekiel saw.

<h2 id="covenants">The covenants, in order</h2>

| Covenant | With whom | Token or mark | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adam | Adam and Eve[@scripture-helps-everlasting-covenant] | — | [[Moses 5:5–8]]; [[Moses 6:52]] |
| Noah | Noah, his seed, every living creature | the bow in the cloud | [[Gen. 9:8–17]] |
| Abraham | Abraham and his seed | circumcision | [[Gen. 12]]; [[Gen. 15]]; [[Gen. 17]]; [[Gen. 22]]; [[Abr. 2:8–11]] |
| Sinai | all Israel | blood on altar and people; the Sabbath | [[Ex. 19]]; [[Ex. 24]]; [[Ex. 31:16]] |
| Levi | Phinehas and the priests | “an everlasting priesthood” | [[Num. 25:10–13]]; [[Mal. 2:4–8]] |
| David | David and his house | a throne “for ever” | [[2 Sam. 7]]; [[Ps. 89]] |
| New covenant | the house of Israel and Judah | the law written in the heart | [[Jer. 31:31–34]]; [[Ezek. 36:25–28]] |

After the Fall God made covenants with Adam and Eve, and after periods of apostasy the Lord reestablished this everlasting covenant with Enoch, Noah, Abraham and Moses; it is “new” because it is reestablished, “everlasting” because it is eternal.[@scripture-helps-everlasting-covenant] Circumcision was the token of Abraham's covenant,[@bd-circumcision] whose promises include Christ through his lineage, land as an eternal inheritance, and adoption of Gentiles into the house of Israel.[@bd-abraham-covenant] At Sinai Moses sprinkles blood on the people: “Behold the blood of the covenant” ([[Ex. 24:8]]). Latter-day Saints see the Mosaic covenant as a modified renewal of what came before, shaped by Israel’s refusal to enter God’s presence at Sinai; Malachi recalls the covenant with Levi, “My covenant was with him of life and peace” ([[Mal. 2:5]]), and says it was corrupted.[@hopkin-covenant] Psalm 132:11–12 adds that David’s earthly kingdom would endure only if his descendants stayed faithful.[@scripture-helps-2-sam] Of the new covenant, “I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts” ([[Jer. 31:33]]), “new” means the covenant, “the fulness of [the] gospel,” revealed anew in the last days,[@scripture-helps-jer-31-33] and the new and everlasting covenant and the Abrahamic covenant “are essentially the same.”[@nelson-everlasting-covenant] See [The Covenant with Abraham](/genesis/themes/covenant-with-abraham/) and [What Was Offered at Sinai](/exodus/themes/offered-at-sinai/).

<h2 id="torah">Torah, and what the law of Moses was for</h2>

*Torah* comes from a verb meaning to point out or direct; it is divine direction and instruction, and includes particularly the five books of Moses.[@bd-torah] The law of Moses replaced a higher law Israel had failed to obey.[@bd-law-of-moses] Moses “sought diligently to sanctify his people that they might behold the face of God” (D&C 84:23). “But they hardened their hearts and could not endure his presence” (D&C 84:24). The lesser priesthood continued, holding “the preparatory gospel” (D&C 84:26).[@lds-scriptures] The law still included faith, repentance, baptism and the Ten Commandments, and was “a preparatory gospel to bring its adherents to Christ.”[@bd-law-of-moses] “For this intent we keep the law of Moses, it pointing our souls to him” ([[Jacob 4:5]]).[@sc-knowhy-424] Christ says “the law is fulfilled that was given unto Moses” ([[3 Ne. 15:4]]), and the law of Moses is not the same as the law of sacrifice, whose principles continue.[@ballard-sacrifice] Leviticus’s dominant thought is “the presence of a holy God in the midst of a holy people.”[@bd-leviticus]

<h2 id="clean-unclean">Clean and unclean</h2>

The categories covered food, childbirth, death and leprosy. Uncleanness was ceremonial, not moral; touching a dead body or bearing children was not “morally evil.” Except for the prohibition against blood, which goes back to Noah, these regulations belonged to the law of carnal commandments; they were fulfilled by the Atonement and are no longer required.[@bd-clean-unclean] The reason given is holiness: “ye shall therefore be holy, for I am holy” (Lev. 11:45).[@lds-scriptures] Uncleanness cut a person off from the covenant people for a time.[@sc-knowhy-479]

<h2 id="renewals">Blessings, cursings, and the covenant renewed</h2>

Deuteronomy means “repetition of the law.” The book contains Moses’s last discourses on the plains of Moab, with a solemn renewal of the covenant in chapters 27–30.[@bd-deuteronomy] Its shape resembles an ancient Near Eastern treaty: history, stipulations, blessings and curses, and periodic reading.[@belnap-law-moses] “I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life” (Deut. 30:19).[@lds-scriptures] The covenant was renewed again and again: at Shechem “Joshua made a covenant with the people that day” (Josh. 24:25); Josiah “made a covenant before the LORD… And all the people stood to the covenant” (2 Kgs. 23:3); after the exile the people “entered into a curse, and into an oath, to walk in God’s law” (Neh. 10:29).[@lds-scriptures]

<h2 id="tabernacle">The tabernacle</h2>

The Lord told Moses to make the tabernacle “after the pattern” He showed ([[Ex. 25:9]]). It stood in a court, with the altar of burnt offering and a brass laver for the priests' washing before the tent door.[@bd-tabernacle] A veil divided the tent in two. In the Holy Place were the altar of incense, the candlestick and the table of shewbread; in the Holy of Holies, “only one piece of furniture,” the ark, whose lid, the mercy seat, was “the place of the manifestation of God's glory” and “God's throne in Israel.”[@bd-tabernacle] The three spaces, the court, the Holy Place and the Holy of Holies, each “represented an increasing proximity to God's holy presence.”[@scripture-helps-ex-35] When the work was finished, “a cloud covered the tent of the congregation, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle” ([[Ex. 40:34]]). After the conquest it was set at Shiloh, and in Solomon's reign it was at Gibeon; “after the building of the temple it entirely disappears from the history.”[@bd-tabernacle] For the furnishings one by one, see [Exodus 25–40](/exodus/25/) and [“That I May Dwell among Them”](/exodus/themes/that-i-may-dwell-among-them/).

<h2 id="temples">Solomon's temple, and the two after it</h2>

| House | Built | What it held | What became of it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tabernacle | Set up on the first day of the first month of the second year after the Exodus[@bd-tabernacle] | Ark, mercy seat, one candlestick, one table[@bd-tabernacle] | Shiloh, then Gibeon; gone after the temple[@bd-tabernacle] |
| Solomon's temple | Begun in Solomon's fourth year, finished in his eleventh ([[1 Kgs. 6:1]], [[1 Kgs. 6:38]]) | The old ark and mercy seat; new cherubim; ten candlesticks and ten tables[@bd-temple-of-solomon] | Burned by Nebuchadnezzar ([[2 Kgs. 25:9]]); 587 B.C.[@bd-temple] |
| Zerubbabel's temple | Dedicated 516 B.C.;[@bd-temple-of-zerubbabel] also dated “around 515”[@scripture-helps-ezra] | About Solomon's size; no ark, so the Holy of Holies was empty[@scripture-helps-ezra] | Rebuilt by Herod from 17 B.C.[@bd-temple-of-herod] |
| Herod's temple | From 17 B.C.; the design not complete until A.D. 64[@bd-temple-of-herod] | No ark; one candlestick and one table, as in the tabernacle[@bd-temple-of-herod] | Destroyed by the Romans, A.D. 70[@bd-temple-of-herod] |

Solomon's temple “was built after the model of the tabernacle, the dimensions of each part being exactly double,” and it was small because “the worshippers remained outside; only the priests went within.”[@bd-temple-of-solomon] It rose at Jerusalem “in mount Moriah” ([[2 Chr. 3:1]]). At its dedication “the priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud” ([[1 Kgs. 8:10–11]]), and Solomon asked that when Israel prayed “toward this place,” God would hear in heaven ([[1 Kgs. 8:30]]). When the second temple's foundation was laid, “many of the priests and Levites … who were ancient men, that had seen the first house … wept with a loud voice; and many shouted aloud for joy” ([[Ezra 3:12]]).

<h2 id="priests">The priests and the Levites</h2>

Aaron and his sons were taken, “that he may minister unto me in the priest’s office” ([[Ex. 28:1]]). The Lord took the Levites “instead of all the firstborn” ([[Num. 3:12]]) (see [The Firstborn](/exodus/themes/the-firstborn/)). The Levites were given to Aaron and his sons “to do the service of the children of Israel in the tabernacle” ([[Num. 8:19]]). They assisted the priests, acted as musicians, and had no inheritance in Canaan but the tithe and 48 cities.[@bd-levites] Only Aaron and his sons could be priests, and the high priest was chosen from Aaron's firstborn line.[@bd-aaronic-priesthood] A priest was a mediator who offered sacrifice and incense, taught the law and blessed the people;[@bd-priests] the blessing begins “The LORD bless thee, and keep thee” ([[Num. 6:24]]), and the priests “shall put my name upon the children of Israel” ([[Num. 6:27]]).

The high priest wore a gold plate engraved “HOLINESS TO THE LORD” ([[Ex. 28:36]]); his main duty was the service of the Day of Atonement.[@bd-high-priest] On that day Aaron might not enter within the veil “at all times … that he die not” ([[Lev. 16:2]]). Hebrews teaches that “Jesus Christ is the great High Priest, of whom all the others were pre-figures.”[@bd-high-priest]

The Aaronic Priesthood was “of lesser power and authority than the priesthood of Melchizedek,” given after Israel failed to keep the gospel law under Moses; its limit to Levi's tribe “was lifted when the law of Moses was fulfilled.”[@bd-aaronic-priesthood]

The prophets charged the priests with failing in this work. Malachi: “if ye offer the blind for sacrifice, is it not evil?” ([[Mal. 1:8]]). Ezekiel: the priests “have put no difference between the holy and profane” ([[Ezek. 22:26]]).

<h2 id="sacrifice">Sacrifice</h2>

Every animal sacrifice under the law had six acts: the offerer presented it at the sanctuary door himself; he laid his hands on it, which made it his “representative and substitute”; he slew it; the priest poured out or sprinkled its blood “to make an atonement”; the priest burned it on the altar, an act that “symbolized the consecration of the worshipper”; and, for a peace offering only, the offerer, his household and the poorer Levites ate the rest of the flesh.[@bd-sacrifices] The sacrifices offered from Adam on symbolized the Only Begotten’s sacrifice, and His death ended animal sacrifice as a gospel ordinance.[@gs-sacrifice]

**The Day of Atonement** (Lev. 16) appears to be the only fast ordered by the law.[@bd-fasts] Wearing white linen, the high priest entered the Holy of Holies on the only day he could.[@scripture-helps-ex-35] Two goats were chosen by lot, one for the LORD and one for Azazel ([[Lev. 16:8]]).[@bd-fasts] The second goat has been read two ways: its ritual “signified the sending away of the sins of the people now expiated to the Evil One”;[@bd-fasts] or the scapegoat “can symbolize Jesus Christ.”[@scripture-helps-ex-35]

<h2 id="feasts">The feasts and the sabbath years</h2>

“Three times in a year shall all thy males appear before the LORD thy God… in the feast of unleavened bread, and in the feast of weeks, and in the feast of tabernacles” (Deut. 16:16).[@lds-scriptures] This requirement was not generally observed in Old Testament times.[@bd-feasts]

| Feast | When | What it remembered or marked | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sabbath | weekly | God’s rest at creation; the redemption from Egypt[@bd-sabbath] | [[Ex. 20:8–11]] |
| Passover | 14th Abib[@bd-feasts] | the passing over of Israel’s houses; redemption from Egypt[@bd-feasts] | [[Ex. 12]] |
| Unleavened Bread | seven days[@bd-feasts] | joined to Passover[@bd-feasts] | [[Ex. 12:15]] |
| Firstfruits | morrow after the Sabbath[@bd-feasts] | a sheaf of the barley harvest waved[@bd-feasts] | Lev. 23:10–14 |
| Weeks (Pentecost) | fifty days after Passover[@bd-feasts] | the wheat harvest[@bd-feasts] | Lev. 23:16 |
| Trumpets | first of the seventh month | “a memorial of blowing of trumpets” | Lev. 23:24 |
| Day of Atonement | 10th of the seventh month[@bd-fasts] | national fast and atonement[@bd-fasts] | Lev. 16 |
| Tabernacles | 15th to 21st of the seventh month[@bd-feasts] | the wilderness sojourn and the ingathering[@bd-feasts] | Lev. 23:34 |
| Sabbatical year; jubilee | every 7th; every 50th year | the land’s rest, debts released[@bd-sabbatical-year]; land returned, bondmen freed[@bd-jubilee-year-of] | Lev. 25 |

The unblemished Passover lambs are a symbol of the Lamb of God.[@gs-passover] There is no Old Testament record of a jubilee being kept.[@bd-jubilee-year-of]

<h2 id="worship">How Israel worshipped</h2>

Two lambs a day were offered ([[Ex. 29:38]]), “a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle” ([[Ex. 29:42]]). David set Levites “over the service of song in the house of the LORD” ([[1 Chr. 6:31]]), and the Psalms “have been called the ‘hymn book’ of the ancient temple.”[@sc-knowhy-437] “Who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD? or who shall stand in his holy place?” ([[Ps. 24:3]]); “the mountain of the Lord” in the Old Testament “often refers to the temple.”[@scripture-helps-ps-1] “To obey is better than sacrifice” ([[1 Sam. 15:22]]); “The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart” ([[Ps. 51:17]]); “I desired mercy, and not sacrifice” ([[Hos. 6:6]]). The Lord “placed more value on their heartfelt devotion to Him and compassion for others,” while sacrifices “were still important.”[@scripture-helps-hosea] See [Psalm 24](/psalms/24/), [Psalm 51](/psalms/51/) and [Mercy, Not Sacrifice](/hosea/themes/mercy-not-sacrifice/).

<h2 id="ezekiel">The temple Ezekiel saw</h2>

In the twenty-fifth year of the captivity, “in the visions of God brought he me into the land of Israel, and set me upon a very high mountain” ([[Ezek. 40:1–2]]). A guide with a measuring reed shows him “a glorious, complete temple,” and the vision ends with the city's new name, “The LORD is there” ([[Ezek. 48:35]]).[@rennaker-holiness] “Waters issued out from under the threshold of the house eastward” ([[Ezek. 47:1]]); Elder Renlund reads the waters as temple blessings that grow and heal.[@scripture-helps-ezek] See [Ezekiel 40–48](/ezekiel/40/) and [Ezekiel 47](/ezekiel/47/).

The sources differ on what the temple is for. Elder Talmage: whether Ezekiel “considered the design so shown as one to be subsequently realized, or as but a grand yet unattainable ideal, is not declared.” President Joseph Fielding Smith: the temple “will be used for ordinance work after the gathering of Israel,” and “the law of sacrifice will have to be restored,” so that the sons of Levi “offer such a sacrifice again.”[@ot-manual-ezek-25-48] Another reading: “no provision is made in it for any but the most simple rites, and these are not those of the sacred endowments as we know them.”[@cowan-ot-temples] Animal sacrifice ended with the Atonement.[@cfm-ot-2026-tabernacle] This guide does not choose among them.

Temples “are literally houses of the Lord,” where “individuals make sacred covenants with God.”[@gospel-topics-temples] “Building and properly using a temple is one of the marks of the true Church in any dispensation.”[@bd-temple]
