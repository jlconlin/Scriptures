This guide covers Judah from Manasseh to Cyrus's taking of Babylon. Judah had been tributary to Assyria in Manasseh's day.[@ot-manual-kings-a] Assyria fell,[@bd-assyria] and Judah was “caught in the power struggle between Egypt and Babylonia.”[@ot-manual-kings-g] Nebuchadnezzar took Jerusalem in stages and carried its people to Babylon, where they lived until Persia conquered the empire in 539 BC.[@scripture-helps-ezra] The return from exile belongs to the next period. For the whole sweep see [A Timeline of the Old Testament](/old-testament/guides/israels-story/#babylon) and its section on [the exile](/old-testament/guides/israels-story/#exile).

<h2 id="nations">The nations</h2>

| Nation | Who and where | With Judah |
|---|---|---|
| Babylon (Chaldea) | The capital of Babylonia; Chaldea was the part south and east of Babylon[@bd-babylon][@bd-chaldea] | Nebuchadnezzar subdued Judah, besieged Jerusalem and took it[@bd-nebuchadnezzar] |
| Egypt | Necho slew Josiah at Megiddo;[@bd-necho] Apries, “Pharaoh Hophra,” offered help later[@bd-assyria] | Egypt made Judah its vassal;[@bd-assyria] Hophra is to be given to his enemies as Zedekiah was ([[Jer. 44:30]]) |
| Media | Between the Caspian Sea and Elam[@bd-media] | King Cyaxares joined Nabopolassar in overthrowing Nineveh;[@bd-media] “the Lord hath raised up the spirit of the kings of the Medes” against Babylon ([[Jer. 51:11]]) |
| Assyria | Judah's overlord in Manasseh's day[@ot-manual-kings-a] | Nineveh fell to the Medes and Babylonians;[@bd-assyria] Egypt marched north to help its ally Assur-uballit II of Assyria[@schade-kingdom-judah] |
| Edom | South of the Dead Sea; “great mutual hatred” with Israel[@bd-edom] | Obadiah says Edom “wast as one of them” when strangers entered Jerusalem ([[Obad. 1:11]]); the Bible Dictionary dates that capture to 586 or to Jehoram's day[@bd-obadiah] |
| Ammon and Moab | Moab east of the Dead Sea;[@bd-moab] Ammon east of Mount Gilead[@bd-ammon] | The Lord sent bands of Moabites and Ammonites against Judah ([[2 Kgs. 24:2]]); Gedaliah was warned that Baalis king of the Ammonites had sent Ishmael to slay him ([[Jer. 40:14]]) |
| Tyre | An important commercial and seaport town of Syria[@bd-tyre] | “I will bring upon Tyrus Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon” ([[Ezek. 26:7]]); [[Jer. 27:3]] |
| Persia | Its empire reached “from Afghanistan to the Mediterranean”[@bd-persia] | Cyrus united the Medes and Persians and conquered Babylon[@bd-persia] |

<h2 id="books">The books of this period</h2>

- [[2 Kgs. 21–25]] and [[2 Chr. 33–36]]: the history from Manasseh to the fall and Cyrus's decree.
- Zephaniah in Josiah's reign,[@bd-zephaniah] Nahum, probably written between the fall of Thebes (about 660) and the fall of Nineveh,[@bd-nahum] and Habakkuk, whose date is uncertain, possibly Josiah's or Jehoiakim's.[@bd-habakkuk]
- [Jeremiah](/jeremiah/), 626–586 BC;[@bd-jeremiah] [Lamentations](/lamentations/), written some years after the fall.[@bd-lamentations] For Jeremiah's own setting, see [Jeremiah's World](/jeremiah/guides/jeremiahs-world/).
- [Ezekiel](/ezekiel/), a priest carried to Babylon in about 597 who prophesied there from about 592 to 570;[@scripture-helps-ezek] [Daniel](/daniel/), who “prophesied within the royal courts of Babylon.”[@scripture-helps-ezek]
- Obadiah, dated either to Jehoram's day or, more probably, after 586.[@bd-obadiah]
- [[1 Ne. 1]]: “in the commencement of the first year of the reign of Zedekiah” ([[1 Ne. 1:4]]). The Lord warned Lehi to flee before the city was destroyed.[@scripture-helps-2-kings]
