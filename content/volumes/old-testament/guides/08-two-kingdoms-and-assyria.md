This guide covers the years from the division of David's kingdom, under Rehoboam and Jeroboam, to the end of Hezekiah's reign. The northern kingdom, Israel, with its capital at Samaria, lasted over 200 years[@bd-israel-kingdom] and fell to Assyria in 721 BC, though the date may be 722.[@ot-manual-12] Judah, where David's house held the throne,[@bd-judah-kingdom] paid Assyria tribute until Hezekiah rebelled;[@scripture-helps-2-kings] Sennacherib came to Palestine in 701.[@bd-assyria] “Now in the fourteenth year of king Hezekiah did Sennacherib king of Assyria come up against all the fenced cities of Judah, and took them” ([[2 Kgs. 18:13]]), yet Jerusalem was at length delivered.[@bd-hezekiah]

The division is dated 975,[@bible-chron-ot] 931[@ot-manual-kings-a] or about 925 BC.[@bd-israel-kingdom] For the whole sweep see [A Timeline of the Old Testament](/old-testament/guides/israels-story/#two-kingdoms) and its section on [Assyria](/old-testament/guides/israels-story/#assyria).

<h2 id="nations">The nations</h2>

| Nation | Who and where | With Israel and Judah |
|---|---|---|
| Assyria | The Tigris valley, centered on Nineveh;[@bd-assyria] “ruled by terror,” and deported whole populations[@gs-assyria] | Took the north and laid Judah under tribute; “the rod of mine anger” ([[Isa. 10:5]]); [[2 Kgs. 17]]; [[2 Kgs. 18–19]] |
| Syria | Aram; its capital Damascus[@bd-damascus] | Allied with Israel against Assyria in Ahab's day;[@ot-manual-kings-a] Rezin and Pekah threatened Judah;[@scripture-helps-2-kings] [[2 Kgs. 16:5–9]] |
| Egypt | Shishak took Jerusalem under Rehoboam[@bd-egypt] | Assyria's rival; allying with it against Assyria was Judah's policy, which Isaiah opposed;[@bd-egypt] [[1 Kgs. 14:25–26]]; [[Isa. 30:1–5]] |
| Cush | South of Egypt, including modern Nubia[@bd-ethiopia] | The Ethiopian So made himself lord of Egypt and stirred up revolt;[@bd-assyria] [[2 Kgs. 17:4]]; [[2 Kgs. 19:9]] |
| Philistines | The coast from Joppa to the Egyptian desert; five chief cities[@bd-philistines] | Conquered by Tiglath-pileser in 734;[@bd-philistines] rose again in 711 and 701;[@bd-assyria] [[Isa. 20]] |
| Moab | East of the Dead Sea; akin to Israel[@bd-moab] | Omri put it under tribute;[@ot-manual-kings-a] “constant warfare”;[@bd-moab] [[2 Kgs. 3:6–27]] |
| Edom | South of the Dead Sea; Esau's descendants[@bd-edom] | “Great mutual hatred”;[@bd-edom] Edomites and Philistines attacked Ahaz;[@ot-manual-kings-a] [[2 Chr. 28:17–18]] |
| Tyre and Sidon | The Phoenician coast; Tyre a commercial seaport[@bd-tyre] | Allied with David and Solomon;[@bd-tyre] Ahab married a Sidonian;[@ot-manual-kings-a] [[1 Kgs. 16:31]]; [[Amos 1:9]] |
| Babylon | On the Euphrates; part of the Assyrian empire in this period[@bd-babylon] | Merodach-baladan, who rebelled against Assyria, wrote to Hezekiah;[@ot-manual-f] [[2 Kgs. 20:12–13]]; [[Isa. 39]] |

<h2 id="books">The books of this period</h2>

- [[1 Kgs. 12–22]], [[2 Kgs. 1–20]] and [[2 Chr. 10–32]]: the two kingdoms' history; 1 Kings 12 tells of the division.[@scripture-helps-1-kings]
- Jonah, [Amos](/amos/) and [Hosea](/hosea/), under Jeroboam II.[@bd-jonah][@bd-jeroboam]
- Micah, in the days of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah ([[Micah 1:1]]); his ministry continued through Ahaz's reign and into Hezekiah's.[@ot-manual-kings-a]
- [Isaiah](/isaiah/), a prophet in Jerusalem 740–701 BC.[@bd-isaiah] For his own setting, see [Isaiah's World](/isaiah/guides/isaiahs-world/).
