The Old Testament “is a record of God's dealings with His covenant children from the Creation until about four hundred years before the birth of Jesus Christ.”[@scripture-helps-ot-welcome] The Eighth Article of Faith adds: “We believe the Bible to be the word of God as far as it is translated correctly” ([[A of F 1:8]]).[@lds-scriptures] This guide says how that record was written, kept, translated and printed.

<h2 id="written">Written, and written again</h2>

The books were written by hand. “Moses wrote all the words of the LORD” and read “the book of the covenant” to the people ([[Ex. 24:4]], [[Ex. 24:7]]); having finished writing the law “in a book,” he had it put “in the side of the ark of the covenant” ([[Deut. 31:24–26]]).[@lds-scriptures] The prophets “wrote or dictated to scribes who recorded (such as Jeremiah to Baruch, Jer. 36).”[@bd-canon] In Jeremiah 36 “Baruch wrote from the mouth of Jeremiah all the words of the LORD” ([[Jer. 36:4]]). The king had the roll burned “until all the roll was consumed” ([[Jer. 36:23]]). Then “took Jeremiah another roll,” and Baruch wrote again, “and there were added besides unto them many like words” ([[Jer. 36:32]]). In the days of the monarchy “scribe” was the title of a court official; after the captivity it was given to Ezra and to others who acted as teachers of the law.[@bd-scribe]

Later scenes show the written law being found and read. Hilkiah said, “I have found the book of the law,” and Josiah “read in their ears all the words of the book of the covenant” ([[2 Kgs. 22:8]]; [[2 Kgs. 23:2]]).[@lds-scriptures] Ezra was “a ready scribe in the law of Moses” ([[Ezra 7:6]]), and when the law was read to the returned people, “they read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense” ([[Neh. 8:8]]); “gave the sense” is explained as “gave a commentary by the power of the Holy Ghost.”[@lds-scriptures]

Papyrus was the ordinary writing material, and in Babylonia books were written on clay tablets.[@bd-writing] The Old Testament books were likely written on animal skin or papyrus and kept as scrolls, mostly in Hebrew; no manuscript by an author survives, and the oldest existing manuscripts of Old Testament texts date to the third century B.C.[@scripture-helps-ot-welcome] The Great Isaiah Scroll was twenty-three feet six inches long and lacked verses and chapters; it was written in Hebrew without vowels or punctuation.[@dss-roundtable-2007] Stephen Langton divided the Bible into chapters early in the thirteenth century; Robert Estienne set verse numbers within them in the sixteenth, and the Geneva Bible of 1560 brought them into English.[@jackson-kjv-jst]

<h2 id="hebrew-bible">The Hebrew Bible and its order</h2>

Anciently the books were grouped in three: the Law, the Prophets and the Writings.[@scripture-helps-ot-welcome][@bd-bible] Jesus named them: “the law of Moses, and in the prophets, and in the psalms” ([[Luke 24:44]]).[@lds-scriptures] Christian Bibles group the books by subject instead: the Law (Genesis to Deuteronomy), the History (Joshua to Esther), the Poetry (Job to Song of Solomon) and the Prophets (Isaiah to Malachi), whose books “are not in historical order.”[@scripture-helps-ot-welcome] [A Timeline of the Old Testament](/old-testament/guides/israels-story/) sets them in time.

The Hebrew text is called the Masoretic Text, and its date is given two ways. Soon after A.D. 70, the scholars of the Jewish school at Tiberias agreed on one form of text, “afterwards called the Masoretic text”;[@bd-bible] on the other account, the Masoretic text “was formed in approximately the seventh through the tenth centuries AD by a group of Jewish scholars known as the Masoretes.”[@scripture-helps-ot-welcome] About a thousand years after the Hebrew Bible closed, the vowel marks were added.[@spackman-translations-differ]

Other witnesses are ancient translations, the versions; before the Dead Sea Scrolls were found, some copies of them were older than the oldest copies of the Masoretic text.[@spackman-translations-differ] One is the Samaritan Pentateuch: a grandson of the high priest Eliashib, ejected by Nehemiah, took refuge with the Samaritans, “taking with him a copy of the Pentateuch.”[@bd-samaritans] Another is the Septuagint, the Greek translation “made in the first instance for the use of Greek-speaking Jews living in Alexandria” in the reign of Ptolemy Philadelphus (284–246 B.C.); most of the New Testament's quotations from the Old are taken from it, and it included the books we call the Apocrypha.[@bd-septuagint] The Septuagint and the Samaritan Pentateuch “differ materially from the Masoretic text and in some cases have no doubt preserved older and truer readings.”[@bd-bible] For the Apocrypha, the Lord told Joseph Smith that it “is mostly translated correctly” but contains “interpolations by the hands of men” ([[D&C 91:1–2]]).[@lds-scriptures] Because the three texts “do not agree together,” many early dates cannot be fixed (see [How sure the dates are](/old-testament/guides/israels-story/#dates)).[@bible-chron-intro]

The Bible names books it does not contain, among them the book of Nathan the prophet and the book of Jehu, and the Book of Mormon names writings of Zenock, Zenos and Neum.[@bd-lost-books]

<h2 id="dead-sea-scrolls">The Dead Sea Scrolls</h2>

In 1947, near Qumran, goat herders found scrolls in caves; copies or fragments of every Old Testament book but Esther have been found, and Deuteronomy, Isaiah and Psalms are the most numerous.[@bd-dead-sea-scrolls] The remains of thirty-six copies of Psalms, thirty of Deuteronomy and twenty-one of Isaiah were found.[@dss-roundtable-2007] The oldest biblical texts among them are more than 1,000 years older than the oldest complete Masoretic Text.[@pike-parry-dss-2014]

What they show is not simple. Some show “significant textual variants ... including significant additions to and deletions from individual books,” while others “closely match the Masoretic text.”[@seely-masada] On one reading they are evidence “that the Old Testament text was corrupted at least by that time.”[@bd-bible] “There are no uniquely LDS doctrines purely preserved in the Dead Sea Scrolls.”[@pike-parry-dss-2014] For the Great Isaiah Scroll, see [Isaiah's World](/isaiah/guides/isaiahs-world/#archaeology-that-illuminates-isaiah).

<h2 id="english">From Hebrew to English</h2>

| Date | Version |
|---|---|
| Earlier | Jerome's Latin Vulgate, “the official Bible of the Catholic Church for more than 1,000 years”[@lambert-mays-kjv-400] |
| 1382 | Wycliffe's, the first English version of the whole Bible, made from the Latin[@bd-bible-english] |
| 1526 | Tyndale's New Testament, from the Greek; he has “the honor of making the first translation of the Bible into English from the languages in which it was originally written”[@lambert-mays-kjv-400][@bd-bible-english] |
| 1535–68 | Coverdale's (1535), the Great Bible (1539), the Genevan Bible (1560), the Bishops' Bible (1568)[@bd-bible-english] |
| 1604–11 | The King James Version[@bd-bible-english] |

At the Hampton Court Conference of 1604 the king agreed to a new translation. Its translators were to follow the Bishops' Bible “as little altered as the truth of the original will permit”; it was published in 1611.[@bd-bible-english] By design it was “more a revision of previous Bibles than a new translation,” with the Hebrew and Greek consulted.[@blumell-whitchurch-coming-forth] On one account the King James Version “follows the Masoretic text except in a very few passages”;[@bd-bible] on another, modern English versions, “including the King James Version, have been influenced by the Septuagint and Masoretic text.”[@scripture-helps-ot-welcome] It “is not the Bible. Like all modern versions, it is a translation of the Bible.”[@jackson-kjv-jst]

Why the Church uses it in English: Joseph Smith studied it, and “its vocabulary and style of language can be seen throughout the Book of Mormon and the Doctrine and Covenants.”[@scripture-helps-ot-welcome] Though “other Bible versions may be easier to read,” “in doctrinal matters latter-day revelation supports the King James Version in preference to other English translations,” and “no original manuscripts of any portion of the Bible are available for comparison to determine the most accurate version. However, the Lord has revealed clearly the doctrines of the gospel in these latter days. The most reliable way to measure the accuracy of any biblical passage is not by comparing different texts, but by comparison with the Book of Mormon and modern-day revelations.”[@church-news-1992-kjv] The Church's footnotes give “many alternative phrases that make the KJV wording clear.”[@pinnock-kjv-1987] The Church uses other translations in other languages, and “other Bible translations may also be used.”[@gospel-topics-bible] “Neither is the Bible, as it is currently available, without error”; as it was “compiled, organized, translated, and transcribed, errors were introduced into the text.”[@gospel-topics-bible]

<h2 id="jst">The Joseph Smith Translation</h2>

Beginning in the summer of 1830, Joseph Smith worked on a revision of the King James Bible, which he considered a “branch of [his] calling”; he dictated to Oliver Cowdery, Sidney Rigdon and other scribes, using a King James Bible as the starting point and no Hebrew or Greek sources.[@church-history-topics-jst] Most of the work was done by July 1833.[@scripture-helps-ot-welcome] Several sections of the Doctrine and Covenants “were received in direct consequence of the work (D&C 76–77; 91).”[@bd-jst]

“Because the Lord revealed to Joseph certain truths that the original authors had once recorded,” the word “translation” is used “in a broader and different way than usual, for Joseph's translation was more revelation than literal translation from one language into another,” and the Lord “inspired the Prophet Joseph Smith to restore truths to the King James Bible text that had become lost or changed.”[@jst-appendix-intro] The revisions include “long revealed passages” that “sometimes dramatically expanded the biblical text,” as in the book of Moses; passages “heavily revised,” such as Matthew 24; and “many smaller changes that improved grammar, clarified meaning, modernized language, corrected points of doctrine, or alleviated inconsistencies.”[@church-history-topics-jst] The Joseph Smith Translation “to some extent assists in restoring the plain and precious things that have been lost from the Bible (see 1 Ne. 13–14).”[@bd-jst]

The Book of Moses is “an extract from the translation of the Bible as revealed to Joseph Smith the Prophet, June 1830–February 1831,” and Joseph Smith—Matthew an extract from the work of 1831.[@lds-scriptures] Shorter revisions are in the footnotes, and longer ones in the appendix.[@scripture-helps-ot-studying] The Reorganized Church (now Community of Christ) published the revisions in 1867. For a century the Church did not publish an edition. A collaboration with the Reorganized Church let the Church print excerpts in 1979 and led to the publication of the complete manuscripts in 2004.[@church-history-topics-jst] The name “Joseph Smith Translation” was devised for the 1979 edition, since “NT” already meant New Testament.[@woods-lds-edition] It is “not the official Bible” of the Church but “an invaluable aid.”[@bd-jst]

<h2 id="study-helps">The Church's edition and its helps</h2>

In the early 1970s three different Bibles were in use among Church members, and a letter of 27 October 1972 from Spencer W. Kimball, then President of the Quorum of the Twelve, asked three BYU professors to prepare one with a concordance, dictionary, atlas and index and with footnotes related to the other scriptures.[@woods-lds-edition] Elder Bruce R. McConkie wrote the chapter headings, and “everything, before it was set in type, was officially approved by the Brethren.”[@woods-lds-edition] The Bible Dictionary used a Cambridge University Press dictionary as its base, to which Robert J. Matthews added the Latter-day Saint aspect.[@woods-lds-edition] The edition appeared in 1979.[@church-news-1992-kjv]

In 2013 the Church made new printing masters; “more than 99 percent” of the adjustments were in the study helps, and the chapter headings were adjusted “to more clearly reflect the text and avoid interpretation.”[@about-scriptures-adjustments][@approved-adjustments-2013] In 2025 some Doctrine and Covenants section introductions were adjusted.[@about-scriptures-adjustments]

The helps say what they are. The Bible Dictionary “is not intended as an official statement of Church doctrine,” and many entries “are subject to reevaluation as new research or revelation comes to light.”[@bd-introduction] The Topical Guide is “not intended to be comprehensive,” and urges the reader to look up each scripture in its context.[@tg-introduction] The Guide to the Scriptures covers “selected doctrines, principles, people, and places.”[@gs-introduction] The maps help the reader “better understand the scriptures” by knowing the geography.[@bible-maps-intro] The Book of Mormon, the Joseph Smith Translation and the Pearl of Great Price “support and clarify Old Testament teachings.”[@scripture-helps-ot-studying]

<h2 id="restoration">What the Restoration adds</h2>

Lehi searched “the records which were engraven upon the plates of brass” and found “the five books of Moses,” “a record of the Jews from the beginning, even down to the commencement of the reign of Zedekiah,” and “the prophecies of the holy prophets” ([[1 Ne. 5:10–13]]).[@lds-scriptures] A collection of Old Testament documents was available in Jerusalem in 600 B.C., written on the plates of brass.[@bd-canon]

The Lord told Moses of a day when men “take many of them from the book which thou shalt write”; then, “I will raise up another like unto thee; and they shall be had again among the children of men” ([[Moses 1:41]]).[@lds-scriptures] In vision Nephi saw a book that “proceedeth out of the mouth of a Jew” and from which “these things go forth from the Jews in purity unto the Gentiles”; afterward, “many parts which are plain and most precious” were taken away ([[1 Ne. 13:23–29]]). Of the last records the angel said they “shall make known the plain and precious things which have been taken away” ([[1 Ne. 13:40]]).[@lds-scriptures]

The Bible “is the word of God”: “We love and revere the Bible.”[@holland-words-never-cease] Its “4,000 years of sacred and secular history were recorded and preserved by the prophets, apostles, and inspired churchmen,” and Wycliffe, Tyndale and Gutenberg were “prompted against much opposition” to translate and publish it.[@ballard-miracle-bible] The scriptures of the Restoration “do not compete with the Bible; they complement the Bible,” and Saints hold “the Bible (the stick of Judah) and the Book of Mormon (the stick of Ephraim) bound as one in their hands.”[@nelson-scriptural-witnesses] “The first testament of Christ is the Bible's Old Testament, which predicted and prophesied of the coming of the Savior, His transcendent life, and His liberating Atonement.”[@ballard-miracle-bible]
