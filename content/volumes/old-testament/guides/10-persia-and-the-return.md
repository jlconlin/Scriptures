This guide covers Judah under the Persian kings, from Cyrus's taking of Babylon (539 or 538 BC) to Malachi's time (about 430). Persia conquered the Babylonian kingdom,[@scripture-helps-ezra] and Judah remained a Persian province until 334, when it passed to Alexander the Great.[@bd-persia] For the whole sweep see [A Timeline of the Old Testament](/old-testament/guides/israels-story/#return). The exile before it is in [Babylon and the Exile](/old-testament/guides/babylon-and-the-exile/).

<h2 id="nations">The nations</h2>

| Nation | Who and where | With Judah |
|---|---|---|
| Persia and Media | Susa (Shushan) was the capital under Cyrus[@bd-shushan] | Nehemiah served the king “in Shushan the palace” ([[Neh. 1:1]]) |
| The Samaritans | Descended from foreign colonists and from Israelites who escaped the captivity;[@bd-samaritans] Sanballat was governor of Samaria[@scripture-helps-ezra] | Became “bitter opponents of the Jews”[@bd-samaritans] |
| Ammon | Tobiah was an Ammonite official, influential in Jerusalem[@scripture-helps-ezra] | “Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, and Geshem the Arabian” mocked the wall-building ([[Neh. 2:19]]) |
| The Dispersion | Israelites who stayed abroad after Cyrus's decree[@bd-dispersion] | Most of the exiles stayed in Babylon[@ot-manual-kings-j] |
| Edom | South of the Dead Sea; “great mutual hatred” with Israel[@bd-edom] | “I loved Jacob, And I hated Esau” ([[Mal. 1:2–3]]) |
| Greece | Alexander ruled an empire “from Greece to the Indus”[@bd-alexander] | Defeated Persia in 334[@ot-manual-kings-k] |

<h2 id="books">The books of this period</h2>

- [[2 Chr. 36:22–23]]: Chronicles ends with Cyrus's proclamation.[@bd-chronicles]
- [[Isa. 44:28]] names Cyrus: “That saith of Cyrus, He is my shepherd.”[@lds-scriptures] The prophecy was made about 180 years before Cyrus let the Jews return.[@gs-cyrus]
- Ezra 1–6 describes events from 60 to 80 years before Ezra came;[@bd-ezra] [[Ezra 7–10]] follows him.
- Nehemiah continues Ezra; its four parts run from the walls to Nehemiah's second visit.[@bd-nehemiah]
- Esther: the book has no direct reference to God, yet shows His overruling providence.[@bd-esther-book]
- Haggai and Zechariah, who urged the rebuilding.[@bd-haggai][@bd-temple-of-zerubbabel]
- Malachi, whose exact timing is uncertain.[@scripture-helps-mal] Its chapters 3 and 4 were quoted by the Savior to the Nephites ([[3 Ne. 24–25]]).[@bd-malachi]
