# Old Testament guides: the plan

A project note (the build ignores it). It holds the author's decisions about guides for the Old Testament as a volume and the plan for writing them. When the work is done, what remains true moves to `STANDARDS.md` §8 and `DEVELOPMENT.md`, and this file is cut to the volume's standing decisions (`DECISIONS.md`, “Process notes are temporary”).

## State

The code, the scripts, the agents' guide sections and the workflow are built and described in `DEVELOPMENT.md` (“Volume guides,” “A page in preview”) and `STANDARDS.md` §8 (“How a guide is written”); the author approved the look (2026-10-12: “Yes, I like the look”). **Guide 1, Women and Marriage, is live** (2026-10-12; author: “Let's go live I guess”), with back-links from 88 notes in Genesis, Exodus, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Hosea and Psalms. It was written three times in a day: a first page that explained the customs but, the author said, “doesn't really explain how this is beneficial or protecting of women,” and was too long (4,000 words); a rewrite that opens with the Church's teaching of equal partnership and adds a section “Valued, and more than property”; and an expansion of that section with “more evidence to support the strength and value of women” (a third research pass: KnoWhy 639 with Carol Meyers, BDB on *chayil*, Olson, the Hiltons, the Shannons, Frymer-Kensky). What that taught, for guides 2–7: research the *argument* the author wants the page to carry, not only the facts, before the outline; and the first outline should be about 2,000 words (`STANDARDS.md` §8). The author's reservation stands (“better than before, but not entirely what I was hoping for”): the page is open to change as he reads it. **Guide 2, A Timeline of the Old Testament (file `02-israels-story`), is live** (2026-10-13; author: “go ahead and go live with this. I still reserve the right to modify this in the future”): the patriarchs to the Persian period and one timeline with the books set on it. What research found: the Church's own pages print different dates for the same events (Samaria 721 or 722, Jerusalem 587 or 586, Cyrus's decree 537, 536 or 538, the division 975, 931 or about 925), and no Church source dates the Exodus or the patriarchs; the page reports the figures rather than reconciling them, gives the Bible Chronology table's figure in parentheses for the main events, and says what cannot be dated. What the volume guide absorbed from the book guides (the coordinator's judgment under the decision below, which the author can overturn): Isaiah's World keeps its map, nations, everyday things and archaeology (they belong to guide 6) and the rulers of Isaiah's own day; three rows of its table that were not about Isaiah (Nineveh's fall, Lehi's departure, the second temple) became a link. Jeremiah's World keeps its last-forty-years timeline, which is finer than the volume guide's, and links to it twice. Daniel's and Hosea's intros each got the linking sentence. Chapter notes that explain a dated event link to the guide's section. At the author's request (2026-10-13: “Can have a visual timeline? Where we have scripture events on one side and secular events on the other side?”) the page opens with the timeline, a two-sided figure (author, from the preview: the heading must tell the reader this is where the timeline is, so the title names it), Israel on the left and the nations on the right, from the division to Alexander (HTML and CSS, `.tl` in `site.css`; one column on a phone). The author allowed dates from sources other than the Church's; the figure's dates are those the page's sections already carry.

**Guides 3 to 7 are live** (2026-10-13; author: “Let's push and go live”), written the night before (2026-10-13, overnight, at the author's word for the remaining Old Testament themes and guides: “Themes not threads… And guides”). Each was researched by a Sonnet `theme-researcher` from a script-started dossier, outlined by the coordinating session (Fable), then written, reviewed, repaired and read by Codex through `write-guide.js`; the author has read none of them. The files: `03-hebrew-poetry` (Hebrew Poetry and the Prophets' Speech, about 1,540 words), `04-law-and-covenant` (The Law and the Covenant, about 2,050), `05-temple-and-worship` (The Temple and Worship, about 2,120), `06-the-land` (The Land, about 1,670), `07-how-it-came-to-us` (How the Old Testament Came to Us, about 2,160). What the research found and the pages keep plain: no Church source uses a moral/civil/ceremonial division of the law (the pages use D&C 84's words); the Church's sources differ on the scapegoat (the Bible Dictionary: sins sent to the Evil One; Scripture Helps: “can symbolize” Christ), on whether sacrifice returns in Ezekiel's temple (Joseph Fielding Smith against the Guide to the Scriptures and *Come, Follow Me*), on when the Masoretic text was fixed, on what the King James Version's base text is, and on what the Dead Sea Scrolls show; each page reports both sides and settles nothing. No Church source draws the roads of the land, so the land guide gives only the verses and Ogden's “crossroads.” **Still to do, now that they are live:** the trimming the decision below calls for (How to Read Isaiah's keys 2 to 5 and 8, the two “World” guides' overlapping sections, the glossaries' “Places and peoples” cut to a paragraph and a link), the one-sentence links from Daniel's and Hosea's intros, and chapter back-links to the five new guides (none yet; the ten theme pages written the same night have theirs). The Psalms and Genesis book guides were held: two of them overlap guides 4, 5 and 6, so they are outlined after the author has read these.

## What the author decided (2026-10-12)

- **Every volume gets a timeline in the same style** (author, 2026-10-13: “We'll want to have timelines for the Book of Mormon and Doctrine & Covenants too. Maybe for the New Testament. Should have a similar style”): the two-sided figure of the Old Testament's timeline (`.tl` in `site.css`, `DEVELOPMENT.md`), the record's own people on one side and the surrounding world on the other, colored by people and power, with a distinct grey for a people apart. Written when that volume's guides are planned.

- **Guides belong to the volume when their content is true for more than one book.** The kingdoms and the exile, the geography, the law and the covenant, the temple, Hebrew poetry, how the prophets speak, and the household and marriage customs are the Old Testament's background, not a book's. A book's guides keep what is particular to the book (how to read Psalms, Ezekiel's temple visions) and link to the volume's guides instead of repeating them. When a volume guide is written, the sections of Isaiah's and Jeremiah's guides that cover the same ground (the two “World” guides whole, keys 2 to 5 and 8 of *How to Read Isaiah*, the “Places and peoples” of both glossaries) are cut to a paragraph that says what matters for that book and links to the volume guide. The author authorized changing live books (2026-10-12; `AUTHORING.md`, “Live books are open to change”).
- **Volume guides do not wait for the volume to be finished.** They describe the world, not the books, so they can be written now and linked from each book's chapters as it comes. A book with no guides of its own has no Guides page (author, 2026-10-18).
- **The first volume guide is on the women of the Old Testament** and the marriage customs they lived under. The author's wife reads the Old Testament's women as objects, and the site has nothing to answer that. The guide is sourced like any guide (`STANDARDS.md` §4 in full; a guide makes no connections of its own). It does not argue that the customs promoted women; it says what the customs did, where the law protected women, where the narrator takes the women's side, what the hard texts are, and what the Restoration teaches.
- **A book gets guides of its own only when it has two or more topics a book page cannot hold**, each needing sections, a table or a map. A single topic goes into the book page's intro, lengthened a little if it must, or into a volume guide. Daniel's and Hosea's intros already carry their setting in about 200 words each; when volume guide 2 exists, each gets one sentence linking to its period.
- **No glossaries beyond Isaiah's and Jeremiah's** (author, 2026-10-16, asked for one per book; 2026-10-18, after mockups on the branch `claude/glossary-mockup`, set them aside; 2026-10-23, closing it: “I haven't found a solution that adds real value. We already have context pages that name people and places”). A written glossary mostly repeated the Bible Dictionary, the World guides and the chapter notes, and a generated index of names did what search does (author: “I can just use the search to find that”). What a reader lacks is the period around a name, without losing their place, and the Context panel and the period guides give that (`CONTEXT.md`). Isaiah's *Names, Places & Symbols* and Jeremiah's *Names, Places & Images* stay as they are, apart from the trimming of their “Places and peoples” above; their entries keep their anchors, but no pass links the chapters to them. If a name is often searched for and the results only mention it, the fix is in search, not a glossary.
- **The author approves the outline of the women guide before it is written.** Other guides follow the usual course (outline by the coordinating session, the page shown in the preview).

## The guides

**Old Testament (volume), in the order to write them:**

1. **Women and marriage in the Old Testament.** The household (the father's house), betrothal and the bride-price, dowry, the marriage contract, levirate marriage and plural marriage; what the law protected (a wife's right to food, clothing and marital relations, limits on divorce, the captive bride, Zelophehad's daughters inheriting); where the text sides with the women (Sarah and Rebekah directing the covenant line, Hagar naming God, Tamar “more righteous than I,” Hannah, Abigail, Deborah, Huldah, Ruth, Esther, the wise women of Samuel); the hard texts named plainly (Lot's daughters, Dinah, Judges 19, the marriage metaphors of Hosea and Ezekiel 16 and 23); the Restoration's teaching on Eve and on plural marriage (Gospel Topics essay; D&C 132; Jacob 2). A table of the women and where to read them. Links from the chapters that touch it: Genesis 16, 19, 21, 24, 29, 30, 34, 38; Hosea 1–3; Ezekiel 16, 23; Psalms where relevant.
2. **Israel's story in one place.** The patriarchs to the return: judges, the united kingdom, the two kingdoms, Assyria, Babylon, Persia; one timeline with the books set on it. Future “World” guides shrink to what is particular to the book.
3. **Hebrew poetry and the prophets' speech.** Parallelism, acrostics, the lament, the oracle, the woe, the covenant lawsuit, the call narrative. Psalms needs it first.
4. **The law and the covenant.** What Torah is, the covenants from Noah to Sinai, sacrifice, the festivals, clean and unclean. Needed by Leviticus, Deuteronomy, Psalms and the prophets.
5. **The temple and worship.** Tabernacle, Solomon's temple, the priests and Levites, the psalms in worship, Ezekiel's temple.
6. **The land.** The regions, the roads, the neighbors, the places the books keep naming.
7. **How the Old Testament came to us.** The Hebrew Bible and its order, the King James Version, the Joseph Smith Translation, the Dead Sea Scrolls, the Church's edition and its study helps.

Guides 2 to 7 were to be written as a book needed them; the author asked for the rest overnight on 2026-10-13, and all seven are live.

**Book guides still worth writing:**

- **Psalms** (the largest gap, 150 chapters with none): *How to read the Psalms* (the five books, the titles and their terms, the kinds of psalm, Davidic authorship) and *The Psalms in Israel's worship* (or fold that into volume guide 5). Christ in the Psalms is already carried by the theme pages.
- **Genesis**: *The patriarchs' world* (the household, inheritance, the birthright, nomadic life, Mesopotamia to Egypt) and *Genesis beside Moses and Abraham* (the JST and the Pearl of Great Price, chapter by chapter).
- **Ezekiel**: *Ezekiel's exile and his temple* (the deportations, the priestly background, the visions' architecture).
- **Daniel, Hosea, Joel, Malachi, Lamentations, Moses, Abraham, Mosiah**: none. The book page and the chapter settings carry the context, the volume guides carry the rest, and Abraham has its facsimile pages.

## The workflow, and what each model does

Guides reuse the theme pipeline (`STANDARDS.md` §8, “How a theme page is written”) with instructions for a guide: the three `theme-*` agents each get a section “When the page is a guide” (background rather than an arc; §4 in full, no connections of the page's own; not held to 1,000 words; the owner may be a volume) rather than three new agent files. The pipeline, and the model for each step:

| Step | Who | Model | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| The code change, the docs, the agents' new sections | a session | Opus | Ordinary engineering against a documented codebase; Fable is not needed. |
| Research: the dossier of verbatim quotes | `theme-researcher` | Sonnet | Reading and recording, no judgment, as for themes. |
| Outline: what the guide says, section by section, and which source carries each claim | the coordinating session | Fable for the women guide; Opus for the rest | The women guide is the one page where framing can go wrong in a way the author's reader will feel. The standard already keeps this step with the most capable model and out of the agents' hands. |
| The author approves the outline (women guide only) | the author | | |
| Writing the page and its ledger from the outline and dossier | `theme-writer` | Sonnet | Adds nothing of its own, so the cheaper model is enough, as for themes. If the reviewer's list comes back long, switch the writer to Opus and compare with `batch-usage.cjs`. |
| Review: ledger check first, then the sources the script can't judge | `theme-reviewer` | Opus | Judgment, as now. |
| Language pass | Codex, then an Opus agent applies what holds up | as `codex-pass` | Unchanged. |
| Chapter back-links: one sentence in a note pointing to the guide section | a small agent | Sonnet | Mechanical, from a list `occurrences.mjs --site` makes. |
| The author reads the page in the preview | the author | | |

Haiku is not used: the author's test found its answers thin and review did not close the gap (`AUTHORING.md`).
