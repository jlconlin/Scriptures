# The four Gospels: what their briefs share

A project note (the build ignores it). It holds what the author has decided about Matthew, Mark, Luke and John together, before any of them has a brief. Each Gospel's `BRIEF.md` points here for these and keeps only its own questions, as `content/2-chronicles/BRIEF.md` points to 1 Chronicles'. The guides are planned in `GUIDES.md` beside this file.

## What the author decided (2026-11-20)

- **The Gospels are finished by 2027-01-01.** The chapters come first. Theme pages and guides that are not ready by then follow in January.
- **Organized by book and chapter, as every book is** (author: “This is mostly a reference website and that's how people (I.e., me) will look it up”). The same holds for every New Testament book. A harmony is a guide (`GUIDES.md`), never the way the text is arranged.
- **Every chapter answers its own reader's questions** (author: “the purpose is to anticipate questions a reader might have, and answer those questions”). A story told in more than one Gospel is explained in each of them, in that Gospel's wording. A reader in Matthew 14 finds what the feeding of the five thousand means in Matthew 14, not a link to Mark 6. This is not how Chronicles is written beside Samuel and Kings, where a parallel passage points to the earlier note: few readers look up 1 Chronicles 18 for itself, and readers look up each Gospel's telling all the time.
- **The research is shared; the answers are not.** Each story told more than once is researched once, and the writers of every account work from the same sources and findings, so that the notes agree and the research is not paid for four times. The reader never sees this.
- **Each account also answers what only its reader would ask:** what this evangelist adds, leaves out or places differently, where a source says what it means (why John alone has the boy with the loaves and the crowd that would make Jesus king; why Matthew counts the men “beside women and children”).
- **A link is for going further, never for the answer.** Each section names the other tellings of its story. Background too long for a note is in a volume guide.
- **Some repetition between the Gospels' chapters is accepted.** The same question is answered wherever it is asked.
- **A table of parallels is built first,** from the Church's Harmony of the Gospels: every story, and where each Gospel tells it. The writers work from it, the sections' links to the other tellings are made from it, and the harmony guide shows it.
- **The images wait until the four books are done** (author: “We'll do the images after the books are done”). Nothing about them is decided. The usual stage, an image at the end of each book's last batch, does not run for these four.
