# The four Gospels: what their briefs share

A project note (the build ignores it). It holds what the author has decided about Matthew, Mark, Luke and John together, before any of them has a brief. Each Gospel's `BRIEF.md` points here for these and keeps only its own questions, as `content/2-chronicles/BRIEF.md` points to 1 Chronicles'. The guides are planned in `GUIDES.md` beside this file.

## What the author decided (2026-11-20)

- **The Gospels are finished by 2027-01-01.** The chapters come first. Theme pages and guides that are not ready by then follow in January.
- **Organized by book and chapter, as every book is** (author: “This is mostly a reference website and that's how people (I.e., me) will look it up”). The same holds for every New Testament book. A harmony is a guide (`GUIDES.md`), never the way the text is arranged.
- **Every chapter answers its own reader's questions** (author: “the purpose is to anticipate questions a reader might have, and answer those questions”). A story told in more than one Gospel is explained in each of them, in that Gospel's wording. A reader in Matthew 14 finds what the feeding of the five thousand means in Matthew 14, not a link to Mark 6. This is not how Chronicles is written beside Samuel and Kings, where a parallel passage points to the earlier note: few readers look up 1 Chronicles 18 for itself, and readers look up each Gospel's telling all the time.
- **The research is shared; the answers are not.** Each story told more than once is researched once, and the writers of every account work from the same sources and findings, so that the notes agree and the research is not paid for four times. The reader never sees this.
- **Written in canonical order, one book at a time:** Matthew, Mark, Luke, John (author: “I prefer canonical order”). The books are not written at once: a chapter is the unit a writer holds together, and the rule is one stage at a time (`AUTHORING.md`).
- **A story is researched with every account of it open, whichever book is being written,** and the findings are saved as one file for the story: the sources read, what they say, and how the accounts differ. The file holds findings and quotations, never finished prose, so that each writer writes from their own Gospel's wording and a later note is not a copy of Matthew's. Matthew's writers do most of this research, since Matthew tells nearly every story Mark does and much of what Luke does.
- **A later writer corrects and deepens the story's file,** reading it with fresh eyes. When a file changes, the notes already written from it are repaired; a script lists which notes rest on a changed file.
- **A small trial first:** the first few chapters of Matthew, before the rest are run. If Mark's chapters come back as near-copies of Matthew's, or the repairs to finished chapters pile up, the arrangement is rethought before Luke.
- **Each account also answers what only its reader would ask:** what this evangelist adds, leaves out or places differently, where a source says what it means (why John alone has the boy with the loaves and the crowd that would make Jesus king; why Matthew counts the men “beside women and children”).
- **A link is for going further, never for the answer.** Each section names the other tellings of its story. Background too long for a note is in a volume guide.
- **Some repetition between the Gospels' chapters is accepted.** The same question is answered wherever it is asked.
- **The table of parallels is the author's own chronology, not the Church's** (author: “I dno't want you to use the Church's chronology. I want you to use mine”): `Appendices/Chronology.tex` in the author's repository `jlconlin/WitnessesofChrist` (read with `gh api repos/jlconlin/WitnessesofChrist/contents/Appendices/Chronology.tex -H "Accept: application/vnd.github.raw"`). It has 19 periods and 636 events, each with its place and its verses in each Gospel; 235 events are told in more than one. It is built first: a script committed under `scripts/` converts it to a data file in this repository, so the site builds without the other repository and the conversion can be run again when the author changes the original, which stays the master. Its events are finer than a story (“Sends apostles on a ship,” one verse), so the story files group neighboring events; the grouping is proposed at setup. The writers work from it, the sections' links to the other tellings are made from it, and the harmony guide shows it. The author has agreed that it appears on the site (“Yes, my chronology can appear on the site”), that its errors are fixed as they are found, and that its events are consolidated so there are fewer; the import script lists every verse in two rows and every verse in none, and the list goes to the author before anything in the original is changed. **The site never shows the chronology at the original's level of detail** (author, of the 636 events: “I don't want every little detail like it is in that file. That's not helpful”; the detail is for the author's book). The fine rows are kept in the data only to make each section's links to the other tellings exact; the harmony guide shows the consolidated stories and nothing finer. Naming the private repository here is the author's choice (“it's fine to keep it”).
- **Anticipate the reader's questions, find answers to them, and cite what answers** (author, 2026-11-20, replacing two rules made earlier the same day, “Talmage never for history” and a count of his citations: “Does Talmage (or any other source) answer the question? Cite it”; “*anticipate questions and look for answers to those questions.* you are *not* retelling what the scriptures already say. You are adding insight to help the reader understand”). What follows from it:
  - **The question comes first, always.** A note exists because a reader would stop and ask; research is the search for that answer, and nothing else. A note that retells the verse, or passes on what a source happens to say about it, is cut.
  - **Any source that answers the question is cited:** Talmage, the Bible Dictionary and Ellicott included, for history as well as doctrine. No source is barred from a kind of claim. Where a newer study corrects an older work, the newer leads and the note says so; where sources disagree, the note says that plainly and still gives one clear answer.
  - **Not every source, and not every source for every chapter** (author: “‘All’ is indeed too much”; “you don't have to read every source for every chapter”). Cite the sources read that answer the question. Don't hunt for more to add to a note that is answered, and don't open a work because it covers the chapter.
  - **Don't always go to the same source first** (author: “we should be careful about always going to the same source first”; of the repaired trial chapters, “Bible dictionary should not be the first place to look”). The works that cover every verse in order and load in one call (*Jesus the Christ*, the Bible Dictionary, Ellicott) are the ones a writer reaches for by habit, and a writer who starts there asks that work's questions and tells the story its way. Take the question to where it is best answered; for most, that is the Latter-day Saint studies or the ancient source, and those works after. A brief to a writer never names one of them first.
  - **Talmage is opened last** (2026-11-21, after Matthew: the rule above did not hold, `State`). *Jesus the Christ* follows the Gospel story in order, so a writer who opens it early finds an answer to nearly every question and looks no further. A writer lists the reader's questions, then looks for answers in the sources that belong to each question (the Joseph Smith Translation, the Greek, conference talks, the Religious Studies Center's studies of the passage, the Bible Dictionary), and only then opens Talmage, for the questions still unanswered and for what he says that the others do not. The same holds for any work that covers every verse in order (Ellicott; Huntsman and Hatch's chapters for the last week).
  - **A source is cited for what it adds** (2026-11-21). Where another source already answers the note's question and a second only agrees, the second is not cited. This is a rule for every source, not for Talmage alone. The checker reports each note where a citation adds nothing the note's other sources don't already give, and the reviewer decides; it is a judgment on the note, never a count. A Gospel's brief repeats both rules, since the writer, the checker and the reviewer read the brief.
  - **Matthew stays as written** (author, 2026-11-21: “If only 36 notes are Talmage only then we are probably okay. We need to be careful for the future”).
  - **No count of any source in the rules or the checks** (author: “I hate adding a count as it feels artificial”). The reviewer tests each citation by whether it answers the note's question. After a batch the coordinator looks at how often each source appears and tells the author if one dominates; that is a measurement, never a target given to a writer. The older warning when more than a third of a chapter's notes cited outside commentary was removed from `check-content` the same day, for every book (author: “Yes”).
  - **What the trial did under the earlier rules stays** (author: “Don't change what was done”): Matthew 2 to 4 were repaired under “never for history,” which cut some statements only Talmage supported.
- **The outside commentary of last resort is Ellicott's *Commentary for English Readers*,** in Keil's place and under Keil's rule (`AUTHORING.md`): last, one voice, where nothing nearer answers. The *Expositor's Greek Testament* is a second voice on a point of wording. Meyer is allowed but is mostly argument with other critics. Edersheim's *Life and Times of Jesus the Messiah* may be used for a Jewish custom, with the caution stated that it leans on rabbinic writings later than Jesus's day. None of these has been opened with `source.mjs` yet: the first brief's research confirms each can be read before it is relied on.
- **The Greek needs what the Hebrew has:** a Greek text keyed to Strong's numbers and Thayer's lexicon in BDB's place, set up before the first chapters (`DEVELOPMENT.md` when built).
- **A theme page follows an arc that is this Gospel's own.** `PAGES.md` (“What earns a theme page”) holds in full, but its two tests about Christ pass every candidate here, so the others do the sorting: a connection the reader would not see chapter by chapter, something that changes from the first passage to the last, a thesis argued from the text. The arc is the shape this evangelist gives the story (examples of the kind, not proposals: “God with us” at [[Matt. 1:23]] and “I am with you alway” at [[Matt. 28:20]]; Mark's two healings of blind men on either side of the disciples' failures to understand, [[Mark 8:22]] to [[Mark 10:52]]; John's “hour,” not yet come from Cana until [[John 12:23]]). A subject that runs across all four is not a theme page: it would be a doctrinal harmony, a volume page that is not planned (`GUIDES.md`).
- **Theme candidates are proposed once, after all four are written,** in one pass that reads all four, since only then can an arc be judged a Gospel's own. `propose-themes` and Codex's review of candidates do not run at the end of each of these books.
- **The images wait until the four books are done** (author: “We'll do the images after the books are done”). Nothing about them is decided. The usual stage, an image at the end of each book's last batch, does not run for these four.
- **A saying Jesus gave on more than one occasion stays two stories** (2026-11-20). Luke has sayings of the Sermon on the Mount in later chapters (the Lord’s Prayer in Luke 11, “consider the lilies” in Luke 12, and others), and the author’s chronology has each as its own event. They are not joined to Matthew 5–7 in `stories.yaml`, they share no research file, and the harmony shows them apart. Each chapter’s `parallels` lists the other place by hand, with a note ending “said on another occasion” (`stories.mjs` does not print these lines); Matthew 5–7 have them, and Luke’s chapters get the matching lines when they are written.

## How a story's research is shared

For the writer, the checker and the reviewer of a Gospel chapter. Where this differs from an agent's own instructions, this holds: a writer may write the research files named here, and “say a thing once” does not apply between two Gospels' accounts of one story.

- **The stories** are `stories.yaml` beside this file: the author's chronology gathered into 165 stories, each with its verses in each Gospel. `node scripts/stories.mjs <gospel> <chapter>` prints a chapter's stories, where else each is told, its research file, and the `parallels` lines for the chapter file.
- **Read every account before writing your questions:** `node scripts/stories.mjs <id> --text` prints them all. What a reader of your chapter asks often comes from what the other accounts have and yours does not.
- **A story told in more than one Gospel has a research file,** `stories/<id>.yaml` beside this file. A story told in one Gospel has none: its research is the chapter's own ledger.
- **If the file exists, read it before you research.** Its findings are another writer's reading and save you the search, not the reading: a finding you use is opened again with `source.mjs` (the page is cached) and goes into your chapter's own ledger as a row of yours, as `STANDARDS.md` §5 requires. A finding you find wrong or thin you correct in the file, with your chapter in its `by`, and say so in your report; the notes already written from it are then repaired by the coordinator (`node scripts/stories.mjs --stale`).
- **If the file does not exist, you write it** once your research is done: what you read that bears on the story in any Gospel, not what is only your own chapter's wording.
- **The file holds findings and quotations, never prose for a note.** Each writer writes from their own Gospel's wording, so that a later note is not a copy of an earlier one.
- **Your chapter answers its own reader in full.** Explain the story in your notes even where another Gospel's chapter already has; a `[[reference]]` to the other account is for going further. Then answer what only your reader would ask: what this evangelist adds, leaves out or places differently. That an account has or lacks a detail is what the verses say and needs no row; what the difference means needs a source, or is left unsaid.
- **Fill the chapter's `parallels`** with the other tellings of its stories, from the lines `stories.mjs` prints.
- **Where the story falls in the author's chronology** (its `period`) may be said in `setting` or `when` as the order this site follows, never as settled fact where the Gospels differ.

```yaml
# content/volumes/new-testament/stories/<id>.yaml
story: the-feeding-of-the-five-thousand
differences:   # what the accounts themselves show, each with its verses; the text is the evidence
  - 'Only John says the loaves were a lad’s ([[John 6:9]]) and that the crowd would have made Jesus a king ([[John 6:15]]).'
findings:      # what was read: a ledger row (STANDARDS.md §5) without `where`, and who added or last corrected it
  - claim: 'Short statement of the fact'
    key: a-key-in-sources-yaml
    url: 'https://…'
    quote: 'Verbatim, about 40 words at most'
    by: matthew 14
open:          # questions a reader of another account will ask that you did not research
  - 'Why does Mark say the people sat down in ranks, by hundreds and by fifties ([[Mark 6:40]])?'
```

## State (2026-11-20)

- **The chronology is imported:** `chronology.yaml` beside this file, 635 events in 19 periods, written by `node scripts/chronology.mjs` (`DEVELOPMENT.md`). The King James text of the four Gospels is in `data/kjv/`, imported for the verse counts and not yet compared with the Gospel Library (`check-kjv.mjs`, when each book is set up).
- **A first grouping into stories is proposed:** `stories.yaml`, 165 stories, 83 of them told in more than one Gospel. It is written by hand from here on, and the author has not read it. Every event lies whole inside one story. Where the grouping took a side: the Sermon on the Mount is six stories, and Luke's sermon on the plain a seventh; a story follows the author's placement where the Gospels differ (two rejections at Nazareth; the anointing at Bethany in the last week, with John 12:1–11); what the chronology gives twice is in two stories (Matthew 11:25–30, Matthew 10:1–4).
- **What the import found in the original** is in `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` (“The Gospels”).
- **The stories tooling is built:** `scripts/stories.mjs`, and “How a story's research is shared” above. No research file is written yet. The full-detail `chronology.yaml` is kept out of this public repository (`.gitignore`) until the author answers whether it may be committed; nothing reads it but `chronology.mjs`.
- **Matthew is set up in preview on the branch `matthew`** (the worktree `.claude/worktrees/matthew`), with its brief, and **chapters 1 to 4 are written and reviewed as the trial.** The Greek tools are built (`scripts/greek.mjs`).
- **What the trial showed.** The four writers made nine research files in `stories/`, each with the accounts' differences, 5 to 16 findings, and 3 to 5 questions left for the other Gospels' writers; every chapter filled its `parallels`. All four chapters came back ready from review, with no question for the author. **Talmage was cited in 22 of the 50 notes** (10 of chapter 2's 12), which the author judged too much; the rule was first tightened to “never for history” and chapters 2 to 4 were repaired under it, checked and reviewed again the same day, leaving him in **5 of 50** (one note each in chapters 2 and 3, three in chapter 4, all doctrine or his own reading). That is fewer than the dozen the coordinator forecast, because the reviewers cut citations the repair had kept; whether it is now too few is for the author's reading. The author then replaced that rule with the one above (cite what answers the question; no counts), for the chapters still to be written. Outside commentary is in 7 notes, and chapter 3's 4 of 13 is just under the limit: history taken from Talmage tends to land on Ellicott.
- **Two gaps in the tooling.** `batch-commit.mjs` does not commit the story files, so the coordinator commits `content/volumes/new-testament/stories/` after each batch. And no script confirms a story file's quotations on their pages: a finding is confirmed only when a chapter uses it, in that chapter's own ledger.
- **Matthew is written: all 28 chapters, reviewed and committed on the branch `matthew`** (2026-11-20), in preview, not merged to `main`. The author answered the trial with “A quick check looks fine. Keep going.” Its writers left 82 research files in `stories/`.
- **How often Talmage is cited, measured after the book** (a measurement, not a target). 415 notes: *Jesus the Christ* is among the sources of 235 (57%), Ellicott of 100 (24%), Huntsman and Hatch’s chapters of 78, the Bible Dictionary of 51, Thayer of 48. Chapters 1 to 7 cite Talmage in 9 of 98 notes; chapters 8 to 28 in 226 of 317 (71%), and in 36 notes he is the only source besides scripture. The rule “don’t always go to the same source first” did not hold with the writers from chapter 8 on. The author left Matthew as it is and approved two rules for the books that follow: “Talmage is opened last” and “A source is cited for what it adds” (above). Mark’s first chapters show whether they hold.
- **The writers’ output is cut off on long chapters.** The writers of Matthew 5, 10, 17, 18, 24, 26 and 27 failed with “Output blocked by content filtering policy” before saving the chapter file, some several times (26 four times). A writer told to save the chapter in pieces (a small skeleton first, then two or three notes at a time by Edit) finished 26 at the first try. That the size of one output is the cause is the coordinator’s inference. Give the same instruction (the workflow’s `focus`, or the writer’s brief) for the long chapters of Mark, Luke and John.
- **Mark and Luke are written and live** (Mark 2026-11-21; Luke 2026-11-22, author: “Go live on main”). Luke's writers were stopped by the content filter only twice (chapters 7 and 17), each written on the second try. Luke's brief records how often Brown's commentary is cited and the repair the author asked for.
