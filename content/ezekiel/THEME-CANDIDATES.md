# Ezekiel: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). The author selected six on 2026-10-07. The earlier shortlist and chapter reports below remain research leads, not additional page assignments.

## Selected for development (author, 2026-10-07)

**Do not write the pages yet.** The author will separately authorize development. A theme must trace an arc across chapters that helps the reader understand Jesus Christ; a repeated image or phrase alone does not earn a page. Under `STANDARDS.md` §8 the page may make a text-grounded connection of its own, while facts and doctrinal claims remain sourced. These numbers are the selection's identifiers, not a writing order.

1. **The Lord Himself is their sanctuary (earlier shortlist 1 + the new sanctuary proposal 9).** Ezekiel sees the glory of the Lord among the exiles by the Chebar (1:28; 3:23). The temple is defiled and the glory leaves Jerusalem (8:4–6; 9:3; 10:18–19; 11:22–23), yet the Lord says He *has been* a sanctuary to the exiles in the countries where they live (11:15–16), and promises a sanctuary among His gathered people forever (37:26–28). The LORD is Jehovah, identified in the book brief and chapter 1 with the premortal Christ. **Point about Christ:** His people are not beyond His presence when they lose the temple and the land. **Boundary with page 6:** follow His presence *with the exiles while the house is lost*; point ahead to the promised permanent dwelling, but leave the new temple's design, the glory's entry, and the life-giving river to page 6. The old shortlist's geographical account of the glory's departure and return is a research lead for both pages, not a separate third page.
2. **Failed shepherds and the one shepherd (earlier shortlist 4).** The crown is removed “until he come whose right it is” (21:25–27); Israel's shepherds fail and the Lord says He Himself will search for His flock (34:1–16); He promises “my servant David,” one shepherd over a reunited people (34:23–24; 37:22–25). *Scripture Helps* and the chapter 34 note identify this David as Jesus Christ, the Good Shepherd of John 10. Link to Jeremiah's existing page *The Shepherds and the Branch* rather than repeat it. **Caution:** the prince (*nasi*) of the temple vision (44–46) bears the same title used of David but brings a sin offering for himself; do not identify him with Christ without support from a source read.
3. **A new heart and the Spirit within (earlier shortlist 5).** The Lord promises one heart and a new spirit to the exiles (11:19–20), commands the people to make a new heart (18:31), then promises to give a heart of flesh and put His Spirit within them (36:25–27; 37:14; 39:29). **Point about Christ:** trace what the covenant Lord gives His people, beyond the command to change; develop the Savior's role from scripture and Church sources rather than from the repeated word *heart* alone. Distinguish Ezekiel's promise-and-gift arc from Jeremiah's published page *The Covenant Written in the Heart*; link to it where the passages meet.
4. **No one stands in the gap; Christ intercedes (new proposal 10).** False prophets have not stood in the breach (13:5), and the Lord seeks someone to stand before Him for the land but finds none (22:30). Isaiah's related search says the Lord's own arm brings salvation when there is no intercessor (Isa. 59:16); Heb. 7:25 and D&C 45:3 name Jesus Christ as intercessor and advocate. The chapter 22 Witness of Christ already makes this comparison, and Isaiah's page *The Arm of the Lord* follows it further. **Page test:** research must show a larger arc than those existing explanations and link to them rather than repeat them. Do not claim Ezekiel 22:30 itself names Jesus or that the Lord found no one in every age.
5. **The stranger wronged, then given an inheritance (new proposal 11).** Jerusalem is charged with oppressing the stranger (22:7, 29); in the restored land, resident strangers who raise families among Israel receive an inheritance with the tribes (47:22–23). Set the two passages beside Christ's work of bringing those once called strangers near and making them fellow citizens (Eph. 2:12–19) as a clearly marked theme-page comparison, not as Ezekiel's stated interpretation. **Research test:** check the Hebrew and the different settings and uses of *stranger*; do not erase the conditions in 47:22 or imply that temple-entry rules in 44:7–9 are simply reversed.
6. **From a defiled house to a source of life (new temple theme).** The Lord's house is defiled and His glory leaves (8–11). In the later vision a measured, holy house is set apart (40–42), the glory returns and the Lord calls it “the place of my throne” (43:1–12), and water from it grows into a river that heals the sea and supports life (47:1–12); the city is named “The LORD is there” (48:35). **Point about Christ:** the returned glory is Jehovah's presence; John names the Lamb at the source of a river of life (Rev. 22:1), and the chapter 47 note relates living water to the Savior's invitation in John 7:37. The page should show how Christ's restored presence gives life beyond the house's walls. Preserve the Church's reading of Ezekiel's temple and Dead Sea waters as future realities (`BRIEF.md`, “The temple of chapters 40–48”); do not replace them with a metaphor. **Boundary with page 1:** this page follows the restored *house and what flows from it*; page 1 follows the Lord as sanctuary for exiles before that house exists. Link between them rather than telling the same departure and return twice.

**Not selected as separate pages:** the recognition/name refrain (earlier 2); watchman and personal responsibility (3); covenant warnings and blessings (6); the unfaithful wife (7); Ezekiel as a sign (8). Their passages may inform a selected page where they serve its arc. The author rejected a Christ connection based only on repetition; the theme must offer more than the chapter notes or existing Jeremiah and Isaiah theme pages.

## Earlier short list (historical; superseded by the selection above)

Sorted from what the 48 chapters reported below, by how much of the book each runs through and how often a reader will meet it. These were candidates before the author's selection; keep them as research leads.

1. **The glory of the Lord, leaving and returning.** Reported by eighteen chapters. Seen by the river (1:28) and in the plain (3:23); in the temple (8:4); moving to the threshold (9:3; 10:4), the east gate (10:18–19) and the mountain east of the city (11:22–23); returning by the east gate (43:1–7; 44:1–4); and the city's last name, “The LORD is there” (48:35). The book's spine.
2. **“Ye shall know that I am the LORD,” and “for my name's sake.”** The refrain comes about seventy times: after judgment (6:7 on), to the nations (25–32; 38–39), and after mercy (16:62; 34:27; 37:13). With it, the name profaned and sanctified before the nations (20:9–44; 36:20–23; 39:7, 25–27).
3. **The watchman, and each answering for his own sins.** 3:16–21; 14:12–20; chapter 18; 33:1–20; with “I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked” and turning (*shuv*) as the book's word for repentance.
4. **The shepherds, “my servant David,” and the prince.** The failed shepherds and the one shepherd (34; 37:22–25); the crown removed “until he come whose right it is” (21:25–27); the prince of the temple vision (44–46). It would stand beside Jeremiah's page The Shepherds and the Branch.
5. **The new heart and the spirit put within.** Promised (11:19–20), commanded (18:31), given (36:26–27; 37:14), poured out (39:29); “they shall be my people, and I will be their God.” Beside Jeremiah's page The Covenant Written in the Heart.
6. **The covenant's warnings carried out, and its blessings after.** Leviticus 26 in Ezekiel's words: the staff of bread, sword, famine, beasts and pestilence, “pine away in their iniquity” (4–6; 14:13–21; 24:23; 33:10, 27), then the covenant of peace (34:25–28; 36:8–11; 37:26).
7. **The unfaithful wife.** Chapters 16 and 23 (with 6:9 and the history retold in 20): idolatry and foreign alliances as adultery, and the everlasting covenant remembered (16:60–63). Needs the most care in the writing.
8. **Ezekiel himself as the sign.** The acted signs (4–5; 12; 24:15–27; 37:15–20) and the prophet's silence from his call to the fall of the city (3:26–27; 24:27; 33:22).

Smaller, and probably notes rather than pages: holy and common, a priest's vocabulary (22:26; 42–48); Ezekiel in the book of Revelation; trees and vines for kings (15; 17; 19; 31, beside Zenos); Egypt, the help that fails (17; 29–32); the laments (19; 26–28; 32).
