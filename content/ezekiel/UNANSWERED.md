# Ezekiel: reader questions no source answered

Questions a reader would ask that no source read answered, by chapter: a list for a later deepening pass. Kept out of `BRIEF.md` so the brief stays brief. `scripts/batch-notes.mjs` appends here.

*(`scripts/batch-notes.mjs` adds to the end of this file, so this stays the last section.)*
- **1:** Why the account changes from 'I' to 'Ezekiel... him' in verses 2-3 (the note records the change and says the text does not explain it); What the sound 'as the voice of the Almighty' is (verse 24); Why the creatures have straight feet, calf's soles, joined wings and human hands (verses 7-11); the Student Manual offers meanings, which the brief sets aside for Scripture Helps; What 'the hand of the LORD was upon him' means (verse 3); Keil treats it, and it could go at 3:14, 3:22 or 37:1; What 'terrible' crystal means in verse 22, in 1611 English or in the Hebrew; no lexicon page was read; Whether the rainbow of verse 28 is meant as the covenant sign of Genesis 9; no source read joins the two.
- **2:** 2:3: why the Hebrew has the plural 'nations' (goyim) for Israel. The note says the text does not explain it; Keil has an explanation the writer left out to stay within the commentary limit; 2:9: whose hand holds out the roll. No source read says; 2:2: whether 'the spirit' is the Holy Ghost. Only Keil's 'spirit-power which proceeds from God' is given.
- **3:** Why Ezekiel sits seven days at Tel-abib (3:15): no source read answers it; the note says the text does not say; Whom the anger of 3:14 is against, and whether it is Ezekiel's own: the note says the verse does not say; Why the New Revised Standard Version reads 3:12 without spoken words: no source read; the note only reports the two readings.
- **4:** 4:7: what the uncovered arm signifies. Keil and Clarke do not comment on it, and BDB only lists it beside Isaiah 52:10; 4:14-15: why the Lord granted Ezekiel's request and changed the command. No source read explains it; 4:4-8: how a man lying bound for 430 days could also bake bread and tend a model siege, and how 430 days fit between the dates of 1:2 and 8:1 (about fourteen months); 4:5-6: what the 390 and 40 years count. The note gives three attempts and says none is established.
- **5:** Was the sign of the razor and hair actually performed, or only commanded or seen in vision (5:1-4)? No source read says; the setting states only that the text gives the command; What do the scales of verse 1 signify? Only Clarke answers (divine justice and exactness), and he is not cited for it; the note says the text gives them no further meaning; Does 'that which I have not done, and whereunto I will not do any more the like' (5:9) look beyond 586 B.C.? No Church or BYU source read addresses it.
- **6:** 6:7 – what the refrain 'ye shall know that I am the LORD' means across the book, and how often it comes: no source read treats it, so the note stays with what this chapter's verses say and one later occurrence (39:28). The brief's 'about seventy' has no source row; 6:14 – where Diblath was: the sources say the place is unknown, and the Riblah correction is not settled; 6:9 – whether the Lord or the people's heart is 'broken': no Church source addresses it; the note gives the King James reading first and the others after it.
- **7:** Ezek. 7:11: what the last clauses mean ('nor of any of theirs: neither shall there be wailing for them'); the Hebrew is marked uncertain and no source read settles it; Ezek. 7:3-9: why the Septuagint is shorter and differently ordered here; Brenton's page shows the difference but nothing readable explains it; Ezek. 7:27: who 'the king' and 'the prince' are; the setting names Zedekiah, but the chapter has no note on the two titles; Ezek. 7:7: whose glad 'shouting on the mountains' is meant; the lexicon gives the word's sense but no source read says whose shouting it is.
- **8:** Why did the elders think the Lord had forsaken the land (8:12)? No source read answers; Why is incense in the elders' hands itself an offence (8:11)? No source read answers; Who are the twenty-five men of 8:16? The text does not say; Keil's identification (the heads of the priestly courses with the high priest) was left out by the writer; Why is each scene 'greater' than the last (8:6, 13, 15)? No source read ranks them.
- **9:** Why there are seven figures (six with weapons and one in linen): nothing readable says; Which cherub is meant in 9:3 (the ark's or the vision's): the note says the verse does not say; Keil argues for the vision's cherubim but was not used; How many were marked, and whether the marked all survived the fall of the city: the text does not say.
- **10:** Why are the creatures' whole bodies, and not only the wheel rims, full of eyes in 10:12? The 10:15 note points out the difference and no source read explains it for Ezekiel's vision; Why does the glory halt at the threshold and again at the east gate (10:4, 10:19)? The note says the text gives no reason; Keil's remark that God judges from the threshold is on 9:3 and was not used.
- **11:** Why the glory departs in stages and pauses on the mountain (11:22-23): no source read explains it; Where the glory goes after the mountain: the text does not say. Shannon's article says the Lord's presence settles among the exiles but does not tie that to 11:23; Whether Pelatiah actually died in Jerusalem at that hour (11:13): the text does not say; only Keil's conjecture was found, and it was removed; Whether the twenty-five men of 11:1 are those of 8:16, and why these two princes are named: the text does not say. Keil calls them representatives of the civil authorities and says nothing more is known of the two; he is not cited for it.
- **12:** Why does Ezekiel call Zedekiah "prince" (nasi) and not king in 12:10 and 12:12? The lexicon points to a study that is not readable here; Was the second sign (eating bread with quaking, 12:17-20) actually performed? The text does not say; Who are "the people of the land" that Ezekiel is told to address in 12:19: the exiles around him or those still in Judah?; Who are "they" in "they shall know that I am the LORD" at the end of 12:16: the survivors or the nations?; Was a breach in fact dug in Jerusalem's wall when Zedekiah fled? The histories name only the gate between two walls.
- **13:** 13:11-13: what the storm of rain, hail and wind stands for historically; two commentaries say the Chaldean army, but they were left out to keep within the commentary limit, and the note says only that the storm is the Lord's; 13:18: how the bands and veils were used, and on whom; no source read says; 13:18: why the Hebrew has 'my hands'; only the figurative reading Keil adopts addresses it; 13:19: whether the life and death the women pronounced were literal; only the Student Manual's '(spiritually)' was found, given as a reading; 13:9: what future the register and the entry into the land look to; Keil answers (a remnant gathered into a new kingdom) but is not cited in that note, so the note stops at the register of Ezra 2:62.
- **14:** 14:14: why the three are listed in the order Noah, Daniel, Job. The text does not say; only Keil's explanation (how many each delivered) was found and it is given as one explanation; 14:22: whether the 'remnant' brought out are the guilty or a more righteous few. Only the older commentaries (Keil, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown) answer; no Church source read addresses it, and the Topical Guide page behind the footnote would not load for the checker; 14:4: what 'answer him according to the multitude of his idols' means. No Church or BYU source read explains it; the note gives verses 7-8 and the Septuagint's wording only.
- **15:** Which earlier fire verse 7 means: the text does not say, and the note gives two readings as readings; What the unfaithful acts of verse 8 were: the chapter does not say, and no source read answers it for this verse; Whether the 'branch among the trees of the forest' is a wild vine: one commentary says so, but it was left out to keep that commentary to two notes.
- **16:** 16:6: why 'Live' is said twice. No source read explains it; the note now says so; 16:27: who the 'daughters of the Philistines' are and when Judah was delivered to them. Keil has a reading; it was left out to keep him within a third; 16:16: what the last clause means ('the like things shall not come, neither shall it be so'); 16:57: why some translations read Edom where the King James has Syria; 16:53–55: how Sodom's restoration is to happen. No Church source read addresses it; only Keil's reading is given; 16:61: what 'not by thy covenant' means. The note reports that translations differ and gives no interpretation.
- **17:** What the east wind of verse 10 is: only Keil answers, and a note would put him in a fifth note; explore points to 19:10–14; Why the Lord chose a riddle: no source read says; Whether the embassy to Egypt had already gone when Ezekiel spoke: no source read dates it.
- **18:** Whether the three generations of verses 5-18 have historical models (Hezekiah, Manasseh, Josiah): only Jamieson-Fausset-Brown names kings, and it was left out to keep outside commentaries within a third of the notes; Which half of verses 21-24 the people called 'not equal': the text does not say and no source read answers; Why the proverb is said 'concerning the land of Israel' (verse 2): not treated; What 'die' means in verse 4 beyond the Student Manual's 'spiritual life and death' and Keil's reading from the law: no Church source read defines the word in this verse; 'Come near to a menstruous woman' (verse 6): no note; the Church footnote (Lev. 18:19) is beside the verse.
- **19:** 19:1: why the kings are called 'princes of Israel' and not kings of Judah; chapter 12 found no readable source for the title, and this chapter has no note on it; 19:10-12: why the picture changes from a lioness to a vine; no source read explains the change, and the chapter's own Genesis 49 explanation was cut; 19:5-9: which king the second young lion is; the Church's footnotes, the Student Manual and Keil each name a different one; 19:10: what 'in thy blood' means; the lexicon calls the text probably faulty, and the renderings differ; 19:14: what 'shall be for a lamentation' looks ahead to; the note gives only the translations' tenses.
- **20:** 20:1: what the elders asked; the text does not say; 20:12: why the Sabbath is singled out above the other laws; the note gives Ex. 31:13 and the chapter's own use, and no source read says more; 20:38: whether the rebels who leave the land of exile but never enter Israel have a historical counterpart; no source read says; 20:37: why the Septuagint has 'by number' where the Hebrew has 'the bond of the covenant'; the lexicon calls the Hebrew text dubious, and nothing read explains the difference; 20:1: whether the date (fifth month, tenth day) is meant to be heard beside the burning of the temple on the same day of the year (Jer. 52:12); no source read joins them.
- **21:** To whom 'Return it to its sheath' (21:30) is spoken: only Keil answers, and the note says the verse does not say; Why the Ammonites were a target (21:20): no source read says; the setting gives only Jer. 27:3; What striking the thigh and clapping the hands signify (21:12, 14): the Student Manual (26-57) answers ('great alarm and horror'), but citing it would pass the manual's third; the note gives Jer. 31:19 and points to the note on 6:11; Who 'they' are that 'divine a lie unto thee' (21:29), and what the 'it' is that is overturned in 21:27: no source read says.
- **22:** 22:3: what 'maketh idols against herself' means. Keil discusses the preposition; he was left out to stay within the commentary limit; 22:16: what an inheritance taken 'in thyself' would be on the King James reading. Jamieson-Fausset-Brown has an explanation that was not used; the note says the verse does not say; 22:24: which reading of the first word is right ('not cleansed', the Greek 'not rained upon', or Keil's 'not shined upon'); 22:25: whether 'prophets' or 'princes' is original. Keil calls the one-letter change to 'princes' very plausible but is not cited; 22:29: who 'the people of the land' are as a class; 22:31: whether the past tenses mean the word was spoken after the city fell; 22:30: whether any Latter-day Saint source applies the empty gap to the Savior. One web search of the Church's site, the Religious Studies Center and BYU Studies found none.
- **23:** 23:23: who Shoa and Koa are. The note says the text says nothing more; no lexicon entry was read for them; 23:14: where the wall with the Chaldean pictures was and which embassy verse 16 means. Only Keil's conjecture was found, and it is not used; 23:45-49: why the sentence on both sisters is spoken as future when Samaria had fallen long before. Keil has an answer; it was left out to keep him within a third of the notes; 23:38-39: whether 'the same day' points to a known practice in Jerusalem beyond Manasseh's reign. No source read says.
- **24:** 24:16-18: why the Lord took Ezekiel's wife, beyond the sign the text says her death is; no source read says more, and the brief asks for restraint; 24:3: whether the parable of the pot was also acted out; the note says only that the text calls it a parable (Keil says it was not performed but is not cited, to keep him within a third); 24:14: who 'they' are in 'shall they judge thee'; the note gives the Hebrew plural and the versions, and no source read identifies them; 24:18: why the Septuagint has no mention of the wife's death; Brenton's page shows it and nothing read explains it; 24:10: what 'spice it well' means; the translations differ and the chapter has no note on it.
- **25:** Who the "men of the east" were historically (25:4, 10): only the Hebrew name and Judges 6:3 are given; no source cited in the chapter identifies them; How and when the judgment on the Philistines was carried out (25:15-17): no source read; What wrong Edom and the Philistines were avenging (25:12, 15): the text does not say.
- **27:** Where each place in verses 12-24 was (Helbon, Canneh, Chilmad, Raamah and the rest). Only Keil gives a gazetteer, and the writer left it out as a compendium. The Student Manual (27-7) does give 'Togarmah is Armenia' and 'Phut is Libya', which the chapter does not use; Why the picture changes from a ship to a city with walls and towers in verses 10-11. No source read explains it, and the unsourced remark was cut.
- **28:** Why the title changes from 'prince' (verse 2) to 'king' (verse 12): the note says the text does not explain it; What the 'stones of fire' (verses 14, 16) are: only Keil has a conjecture, and it was left out; What 'thy sanctuaries' in verse 18 are: only Keil answers; Why Zidon is charged with no particular offence.
- **29:** Whether and when Nebuchadnezzar actually invaded Egypt (29:19-20): the chapter points to the note on Jer. 43:11, which says it is disputed; Whether the forty years of desolation (29:11-13) ever happened: no source read shows a time when Egypt lay empty; What 'thou shalt not be brought together, nor gathered' (29:5) implies: no note; What the 'opening of the mouth' promised to Ezekiel in the twenty-seventh year (29:21) refers to, beyond the idiom: the note gives the dates and stops. The writer's report says Keil has a line on it, which was not added.
- **30:** 30:13, 'there shall be no more a prince of the land of Egypt': was that so, and in what sense? Keil's page takes it as 'a native prince', but the chapter does not answer it, since adding him would pass the third; 30:12, 'I will make the rivers dry': not explained; the Church footnote gives Isa. 19:5; Whether and when Nebuchadrezzar actually invaded Egypt: no source was read for it here; the chapter points to the note on 29:20.
- **31:** What the waters that feed the cedar stand for (31:4): the note says the text does not say; Why lying 'with the uncircumcised' is a particular disgrace for an Egyptian king (31:18): no source read explains it; How and when the word against Pharaoh Hophra was fulfilled: nothing was read for this chapter; it belongs to chapters 29-30 and 32.
- **32:** Who 'the princes of the north' are (32:30): the two outside commentaries read disagree, and no Church source says; Why Babylon is absent from the list of fallen nations (32:22-30): no source read addresses it; What 'their iniquities shall be upon their bones' means (32:27): the note only quotes the clause; Whether the king of Babylon's sword did come on Egypt (32:11): not answered here; the writer's report says the note on 29:20 treats it.
- **33:** Why the people talk 'by the walls and in the doors of the houses' (33:30): the writer left out the two commentators' differing guesses as a curiosity, and I did not add them; What 'this' is in 33:33 rests on one outside commentary alone; no Church source read treats the verse.
- **34:** 34:12, 'the cloudy and dark day': what day is meant. The Church footnote gives Joel 2:2; only Keil discusses it; 34:26, 'my hill': only Keil identifies it (Mount Zion), and citing him would put commentaries over a third of the notes; 34:23 and 34:15: how the Lord and 'my servant David' can both be the shepherd. No source read raises the question directly; the note sets the verses beside John 10:11 and Pike's sentence; 34:24: why 'prince' and not 'king' here. The text does not say, and only Keil offers a reason.
- **35:** 35:9: why 'thy cities shall not return' where modern translations have 'shall not be inhabited'. Keil discusses it; the writer left it out as a detail the chapter does not need; 35:14-15: when and by whom Edom was laid waste. Nothing was read beyond the Student Manual's one sentence; the note links to Mal. 1:4.
- **36:** 36:35, 'like the garden of Eden': no source read says more than the cross-references (Isa. 51:3 and Joel 2:3 are in explore); 36:11, 'do better unto you than at your beginnings': what the 'better' is; 36:20: why the Joseph Smith Translation reads 'out of this land'; 36:37: what changed so that the Lord will now be inquired of (only the order of the text is given); 36:31: why the loathing comes after the blessing (the note gives the order here and in 16:63 and 20:43, with no source on the reason).
- **37:** Verse 9, 'these slain': why the bones are called slain and not simply dead. Only Keil treats it, and he uses it to argue against the Resurrection reading; Verse 7, the 'noise' and 'shaking': whether a rattling of bones or an earthquake. Only Keil discusses it; the plain words follow the ESV's 'rattling'; How long Israel had been divided when Ezekiel spoke: no date for the division was read, so note 37:22 says only 'since the days of Rehoboam'.
- **38:** 38:7 'be thou a guard unto them': the lexicon calls the word doubtful here and no source read explains it; 38:14 'shalt thou not know it?': the Greek has 'arise'; not treated; 38:8 'thou shalt be visited': only the Church footnote ('OR mustered') answers it, so no note; 38:22 what 'pestilence and blood' will be: no source read says.
- **39:** 39:3: why the bow is in the left hand and the arrows in the right (only an outside commentary that reads the chapter of Antiochus answers; not used); 39:7: what 'the Holy One in Israel' signifies beside Isaiah's 'Holy One of Israel' (no source read); 39:11: which sea is meant (only the Dead Sea identification, given as one reading); 39:25: when the 'Now' of the verse is; answered only by one sentence in the thread from an outside commentary, with no note.
- **40:** Why there is no west gate (only Elder Talmage, in the Student Manual, mentions it; the writer left it out to keep the manual within a third of the notes); Why the longer cubit is used (40:5): the note says the text does not say; Keil's answer from 2 Chronicles 3:3 was not used; What the thirteen cubits of verse 11 and the sixty-cubit posts of verse 14 describe: Keil calls them difficult, and the brief says not to walk through every measurement; What the palm trees on the gateposts mean in themselves: no source read gives a meaning beyond the Eden reading of the house's cherubim and palms; Whether the numbers seven and eight in the steps mean anything: no source read says.
- **41:** 41:18-20: what the palm trees mean. No citable source read explains them; the note says the chapter gives them no meaning; 41:12: what the large building at the west is for. The text does not say; Keil only relays another writer's guess; 41:22: whether the wooden piece is the altar of incense or the table of shewbread. The verse does not say; the Church footnote points to Ex. 30:1; 41:25-26: what the 'thick planks' are. Not treated; the writer found the Hebrew word called obscure; 41:8: what 'great cubits' are. Not treated beyond the setting's sentence on the long cubit of 40:5.
- **42:** What the three-story, set-back shape of the chambers signifies (42:3-6): no source read gives a meaning, and the note says so; What would follow for a person 'sanctified' by contact with the holy garments or portions (42:14; 44:19): neither passage says, and no source read answers.
- **43:** 43:8: whether 'their threshold by my thresholds' means the royal palace built against the temple. No source read says so; only the idol-shrine reading is given; 43:17: why the altar's stairs face east. Gill and Poole answer, but they were left out to keep outside commentary at a third of the notes; 43:19–25: why the first sin offering is a bullock and the rest are goats.
- **44:** 44:3: who the prince is. No Church source read says; 44:7-8: what occasion is meant by bringing strangers into the sanctuary. The text names none; 44:22: why the high priest's marriage rule is extended to every priest. Nothing read explains it; 44:25-26: why priests may not go near the dead, and what the extra seven days are for. Nothing read explains them; 44:18: why sweat matters. Only outside readings were found, and they are given as readings; 44:12: what 'lifted up mine hand' signifies as a gesture. The note gives only the modern rendering 'sworn'.
- **45:** Ezek. 45:12: why the maneh is given as three sums (twenty, twenty-five, fifteen shekels); Keil calls the clause very obscure and no source read explains it; Ezek. 45:22: who the prince is; no Church source read identifies him, and the note leaves him unidentified with a pointer to 44:3; Ezek. 45:21-25: why Pentecost, the day of trumpets and the Day of Atonement are missing from this calendar; the text gives no reason and only Keil's inference is given, as one reading; Ezek. 45:1-6: what the holy portion signifies beyond the text; nothing read treats it; Ezek. 45:10-11: the Bible Dictionary's bath (about 8¼ gallons) is not a tenth of its 230-liter homer, as verse 11 requires; the note calls both figures estimates and does not reconcile them.
- **46:** Ezek. 46:1: why the inner east gate opens on the Sabbath and the new moon in particular; the text gives no reason and no source read gives a clear one; Ezek. 46:4-15: why the offerings differ from Numbers 28 (a larger Sabbath offering, a smaller new moon offering, no evening lamb); only a partial explanation of the Sabbath offering was found; Ezek. 46:2: who the prince is; no Church source read says, and the note gives one older commentary's reading as such; Ezek. 46:24: who 'the ministers of the house' are; only Keil identifies them (the Levites), and the note quotes the phrase without identifying them.
- **47:** Where the northern and southern border places were (Hethlon, Zedad, Berothah, Sibraim, Hazar-hatticon, Hazar-enan, Tamar): only Keil has them, and he says several are unknown; Which sea 'the east sea' of verse 18 is: only Keil says the Dead Sea, and he is already in a third of the notes; What the guide's question in verse 6 ('hast thou seen this?') points to: the note says the text does not say; Why the marshes are left to salt (verse 11): no Church source read comments; one outside reading is given; Whether the river and trees look back to Eden: no source read joins Ezekiel 47 to Genesis 2 directly, so the chapter no longer says so.
- **48:** 48:28, 'the waters of strife in Kadesh': not explained; the Church footnote (Meribah, Num. 20:13) is beside the verse; 48:29, why the land is divided 'by lot' when the portions are already assigned: no source read explains it; 48:1-7 and 23-27, why the tribes stand in this order: answered only in part (Dan in the north; the holy portion between Judah and Benjamin); the source says no single rule can be found; 48:9, why modern translations print twenty thousand where the King James and the Hebrew have ten thousand: no source read explains it.
