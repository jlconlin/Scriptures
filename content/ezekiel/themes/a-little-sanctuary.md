Those left in Jerusalem said of the exiles, “Get you far from the LORD” ([[Ezek. 11:15]]). The book turns that around. The glory comes to the exiles first, the Lord goes far off from His own house, and He says He will be a little sanctuary to those sent away. When the house is lost, the promise that follows is His sanctuary “in the midst of them for evermore” ([[Ezek. 37:26]]).

## Among the captives

Ezekiel sees “the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD” ([[Ezek. 1:28]]) while he is “among the captives by the river of Chebar” ([[Ezek. 1:1]]). In the plain he sees it again: “the glory of the LORD stood there, as the glory which I saw by the river of Chebar” ([[Ezek. 3:23]]). For Ezekiel the vision likely “provided assurance that the Lord could still be with the people, even though they had been exiled to Babylon.”[@scripture-helps-ezek] The exiles likely wondered, “Is God’s presence confined to his temple, or can he still be with us here?” and the visions seem to offer an answer.[@sc-knowhy-719] The LORD of these visions is Jehovah, the premortal Jesus Christ[@bd-jehovah] (see [[Ezek. 1:26]]).

## Far from His own house

The Lord asks Ezekiel whether he sees what Israel does, “that I should go far off from my sanctuary” ([[Ezek. 8:6]]). Then “the glory of the LORD departed from off the threshold of the house” ([[Ezek. 10:18]]) and “went up from the midst of the city” ([[Ezek. 11:23]]). The note on [[Ezek. 11:23]] gives the stages, and [Waters out of the Sanctuary](/ezekiel/themes/waters-out-of-the-sanctuary/) follows the house.

## “Get you far from the LORD”

Those left in Jerusalem say of the exiles, “Get you far from the LORD: unto us is this land given in possession” ([[Ezek. 11:15]]). The Lord answers: “Although I have cast them far off among the heathen… yet will I be to them as a little sanctuary in the countries where they shall come” ([[Ezek. 11:16]]). “Get you far” and “cast far off” are one Hebrew root, and so is “go far off” in 8:6.[@oshb-wlc]

“Sanctuary” is *miqdash*. The Hebrew verb is past, and “little” can be read as size or as time: the NRSVUE has “I have been a sanctuary to them for a little while,” with “a little sanctuary” in the margin.[@nrsvue] The note on [[Ezek. 11:16]] takes up both readings.

## The house lost

“I will profane my sanctuary, the excellency of your strength, the desire of your eyes” ([[Ezek. 24:21]]). After word of the city’s fall ([[Ezek. 33:21]]), the Lord says of the flock He will gather to their own land, “Thus shall they know that I the LORD their God am with them” ([[Ezek. 34:30]]).

## For evermore

The Lord says He “will set my sanctuary in the midst of them for evermore” ([[Ezek. 37:26]]), and “My tabernacle also shall be with them: yea, I will be their God, and they shall be my people” ([[Ezek. 37:27]]). The Church reads this of “a holy sanctuary or temple that would be part of the great reunification of Israel.”[@ot-manual-27] Presence beyond the temple does not make the temple unnecessary: Ezekiel “does not imply that because God’s presence is not limited to one location, temples or promised lands are unnecessary or simply symbolic.”[@sc-knowhy-719] The house itself is the subject of [Waters out of the Sanctuary](/ezekiel/themes/waters-out-of-the-sanctuary/).

## Jesus Christ

Isaiah says of the Lord, “he shall be for a sanctuary” ([[Isa. 8:14]]), the same Hebrew word.[@oshb-wlc] The Church edition’s footnote there reads “security for those who trust Him” and points to Ezek. 11:16, and the chapter heading names Christ.[@lds-scriptures] The Joseph Smith Translation footnote at 48:35 joins the city’s name to “the tabernacle of God is with men” ([[Rev. 21:3]]).[@lds-scriptures] Of that verse it is said that “the temple will be built as a symbol to Israel that the Lord is with His people.”[@ot-manual-27]

Set beside [[Ezek. 11:16]] (“yet will I be to them as a little sanctuary”), [[Rev. 21:22]] says: “I saw no temple therein: for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple of it.” In both, the Lord Himself is spoken of as the sanctuary or temple.[@lds-scriptures] The two verses are compared here only in these words.

The arc, then, is this: when the temple and the land are lost, His people are not beyond His presence. He says of the scattered, “yet will I be to them as a little sanctuary” ([[Ezek. 11:16]]), and of the flock He gathers to their own land, “I the LORD their God am with them” ([[Ezek. 34:30]]).
