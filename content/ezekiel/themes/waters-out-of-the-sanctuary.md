In Ezekiel's first temple vision the Lord's house is defiled and His glory goes out of it by the threshold. In the last, a measured house is set apart and the glory comes back and fills it. From under its threshold water goes out, grows into a river, and brings life wherever it comes.

The temple and the healing of the Dead Sea are read as realities still to come: Ezek. 47–48 is “the description of the latter-day temple in Jerusalem, the river running from the temple into the Dead Sea to heal it.”[@bd-ezekiel] Joseph Smith listed “the temple, and water come out from under the temple, and the waters of the Dead Sea be healed” among what must happen before the Son of Man appears.[@ttp-joseph-smith-21] The setting of the last vision is in [Ezekiel 40](/ezekiel/40/).

## The glory leaves the house

The Lord asks Ezekiel, “seest thou what they do, even the great abominations that the house of Israel committeth here, that I should go far off from my sanctuary?” ([[Ezek. 8:6]]). Then “the glory of the LORD departed from off the threshold of the house” ([[Ezek. 10:18]]), and it “went up from the midst of the city, and stood upon the mountain which is on the east side of the city” ([[Ezek. 11:23]]). The stages are in the note on [[Ezek. 11:23]]; what the exiles had meanwhile is in [A Little Sanctuary](/ezekiel/themes/a-little-sanctuary/).

## A house set apart

In the later vision the house has a wall round about “to make a separation between the sanctuary and the profane place” ([[Ezek. 42:20]]). The house is shown to Israel “that they may be ashamed of their iniquities,” and “the whole limit thereof round about shall be most holy” ([[Ezek. 43:10–12]]).

## The glory returns

“The glory of the God of Israel came from the way of the east,” and “the glory of the LORD filled the house” ([[Ezek. 43:2, 5]]). A voice from the house calls it “the place of my throne… where I will dwell in the midst of the children of Israel for ever” ([[Ezek. 43:6–7]]). The promise has a condition: “let them put away their whoredom… far from me, and I will dwell in the midst of them for ever” ([[Ezek. 43:9]]).

Set beside [[Ezek. 8:6]], the same Hebrew root, *rachaq*, “far,” stands in both verses: in 8:6 the Lord is made to go far off; in 43:9 Israel's whoredom is to be put far off.[@oshb-wlc]

## Water from the threshold

“Waters issued out from under the threshold of the house eastward” ([[Ezek. 47:1]]). They deepen until they are “a river that could not be passed over” ([[Ezek. 47:5]]); “every thing shall live whither the river cometh” ([[Ezek. 47:9]]); the trees grow “because their waters they issued out of the sanctuary” ([[Ezek. 47:12]]). The sea the river enters is the Dead Sea, where it “brought life and healing.”[@scripture-helps-ezek]

Set side by side, the glory “departed” from the threshold of the house in [[Ezek. 10:18]] and the waters “issued out” from under it in [[Ezek. 47:1]]. The noun (*miphtan*, “threshold”) and the verb (*yatsa'*, “go out”) are the same in both.[@oshb-wlc]

## The city's name

The book ends with a name: “the name of the city from that day shall be, The LORD is there” ([[Ezek. 48:35]]).

## Jesus Christ

“Jehovah is the premortal Jesus Christ,”[@bd-jehovah] and the LORD of Ezekiel is understood so (see the note on [[Ezek. 1:26]]). The voice of the returning glory is “like a noise of many waters” ([[Ezek. 43:2]]). The Church edition joins the word “voice” there to D&C 110:3, where the voice “as the sound of the rushing of great waters” is “the voice of Jehovah,” who says, “I am he who was slain; I am your advocate with the Father” ([[D&C 110:3–4]]).[@lds-scriptures]

The Church edition's footnote at Ezek. 47:1 sends the reader to John, who sees “a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb” ([[Rev. 22:1]]).[@lds-scriptures] Living water is “a symbol of the Lord Jesus Christ and His teachings.”[@gs-living-water] Ezekiel 47 does not use the words; Zech. 14:8 does.[@lds-scriptures] The river is also read as a symbol: the temple's ordinances “come from the source of ‘living waters,’ even Jesus Christ.”[@seminary-ezek-47]

Set beside [[Ezek. 47:1]] (see its note), Jesus' words are also of water going out: “If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink,” and “out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water” ([[John 7:37–38]]).[@lds-scriptures]

The house the glory fills is the house the waters go out of, and the life they carry reaches past its walls.
