In Ezekiel the rulers of Judah fail. The crown is taken off “until he come whose right it is”; later in the book, after word comes that the city has fallen ([[Ezek. 33:21]]), the Lord charges Israel's shepherds, says He will do their work Himself, and sets one shepherd over a flock made one. Jeremiah has a shorter prophecy against the shepherds, with the Branch: see [The Shepherds and the Branch](/jeremiah/themes/shepherds-and-the-branch/). Ezekiel never uses Jeremiah's title “Branch” for the promised king; the noun (*tsemach*) comes three times, of a bud and of a vine's growth ([[Ezek. 16:7]]; [[Ezek. 17:9–10]]).[@oshb-wlc]

## The crown taken off

Of the royal house the lament says, “she hath no strong rod to be a sceptre to rule” ([[Ezek. 19:14]]). Then the Lord says to the wicked prince: “Remove the diadem, and take off the crown” ([[Ezek. 21:26]]). He adds, “it shall be no more, until he come whose right it is; and I will give it him” ([[Ezek. 21:27]]). That verse is read of “Jesus Christ the King,”[@ot-manual-26] and the Church's edition ties it to [[Gen. 49:10]], “until Shiloh come”;[@lds-scriptures] see the note on [[Ezek. 21:27]].

## The shepherds charged

“Woe be to the shepherds of Israel that do feed themselves! should not the shepherds feed the flocks?” ([[Ezek. 34:2]]). The Lord says, “neither did my shepherds search for my flock, but the shepherds fed themselves” ([[Ezek. 34:8]]). Shepherds were likely “not only… Israel's spiritual leaders but also… its kings.”[@scripture-helps-ezek] Kings in the ancient Near East were commonly called shepherds.[@seely-messiah] See the note on [[Ezek. 34:2]].

## “I, even I”

“Behold, I, even I, will both search my sheep, and seek them out” ([[Ezek. 34:11]]). Verse 16 sets the Lord's work against the failures listed in verse 4: “I will seek that which was lost, and bring again that which was driven away, and will bind up that which was broken.” The Church's edition joins it to Luke 15:4 and 19:10.[@lds-scriptures]

## One shepherd

“And I will set up one shepherd over them, and he shall feed them, even my servant David” ([[Ezek. 34:23]]). The chapter heading reads, “The Messiah will be their Shepherd.”[@lds-scriptures] The David meant is not the ancient king but Jesus Christ, the Good Shepherd, a descendant of David;[@scripture-helps-ezek] see the note on [[Ezek. 34:23]]. Later the people are one: “one king shall be king to them all,” “they all shall have one shepherd,” and “my servant David shall be their prince for ever” ([[Ezek. 37:22]], [[Ezek. 37:24–25]]).

The temple vision also has a “prince,” the title given David in [[Ezek. 34:24]] and [[Ezek. 37:25]], but it does not say who he is, and he prepares “for himself and for all the people of the land a bullock for a sin offering” ([[Ezek. 45:22]]); see the note on [[Ezek. 44:3]].

## Jesus Christ

The LORD of Ezekiel is Jehovah, “the premortal Jesus Christ” (see the note on [[Ezek. 1:26]]).[@bd-jehovah] Jesus said, “I am the good shepherd,” and “there shall be one fold, and one shepherd” ([[John 10:11]], [[John 10:16]]); the Church's edition gives John 10:16 at Ezek. 37:22.[@lds-scriptures] Those who heard Him in Jerusalem would have had Ezekiel's passages quickly in mind.[@pike-shepherd] He goes on, “the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep” ([[John 10:11]]), an announcement of His coming sacrifice.[@pike-shepherd] To the Nephites He repeats it: “there shall be one fold, and one shepherd” ([[3 Ne. 15:17]]); Nephi says, “he shall feed his sheep” ([[1 Ne. 22:25]]).

The Hebrew *mishpat* is common in Ezekiel: 43 times in 37 verses.[@oshb-wlc] The King James renders it “right” only at [[Ezek. 21:27]] among the passages above, “whose right it is”; at [[Ezek. 34:16]] it is “judgment”: “I will feed them with judgment.”[@oshb-wlc] Setting those two verses side by side is this page's own comparison: Church sources read 21:27 and 34:23 each of Jesus Christ, and none consulted joins the two.
