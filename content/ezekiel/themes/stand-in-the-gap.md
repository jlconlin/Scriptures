To “stand in the breach” is what Moses did when “he said that he would destroy them, had not Moses his chosen stood before him in the breach, to turn away his wrath” ([[Ps. 106:23]]);[@lds-scriptures] the figure, used in [[Ezek. 13:5]] and [[Ezek. 22:30]] and compared with this psalm, means averting calamity “by instruction and (especially) intercession.”[@bdb] The figure comes twice in Ezekiel. Around it the book shows the prophet pleading, the prophets not going up, and the righteous delivering only themselves; these verses have been listed together as texts of prophetic intercession.[@sears-intercession] Set beside that search, the Lord's words in chapter 34: “I will seek.” Moses did stand; [[Ezek. 22:30]] tells of one search at one time.

| Step | Passage | The words |
|---|---|---|
| The prophet pleads | [[Ezek. 9:8]]; [[Ezek. 11:13]] | “Ah Lord GOD! wilt thou destroy all the residue of Israel” |
| The prophets do not go up | [[Ezek. 13:5]] | “Ye have not gone up into the gaps” |
| The righteous save themselves | [[Ezek. 14:14]] | “deliver but their own souls” |
| No man found | [[Ezek. 22:30]] | “I sought for a man… but I found none” |
| “I will seek” | [[Ezek. 34:4]]; [[Ezek. 34:6]]; [[Ezek. 34:16]] | “neither have ye sought that which was lost”; “I will seek that which was lost” |

## The prophet, the prophets, the righteous

Ezekiel “fell upon my face, and cried, and said, Ah Lord GOD! wilt thou destroy all the residue of Israel” ([[Ezek. 9:8]]), and again, “wilt thou make a full end of the remnant of Israel?” ([[Ezek. 11:13]]).[@lds-scriptures] The first plea is answered with the reason for the judgment ([[Ezek. 9:9–10]]), the second with a promise ([[Ezek. 11:16–20]]).[@lds-scriptures] See [Ezekiel 9](/ezekiel/9/).

Of the prophets who spoke peace the Lord says, “Ye have not gone up into the gaps, neither made up the hedge for the house of Israel to stand in the battle in the day of the LORD” ([[Ezek. 13:5]]). The Church edition glosses “gaps” as “breaks in the wall.”[@lds-scriptures] See [Ezekiel 13](/ezekiel/13/).

Even Noah, Daniel and Job, were they in the land, would deliver only themselves: “Though these three men, Noah, Daniel, and Job, were in it, they should deliver but their own souls by their righteousness” ([[Ezek. 14:14]]); “they shall deliver neither sons nor daughters” ([[Ezek. 14:16]]).[@lds-scriptures]

<h2 id="none-found">No man found</h2>

“And I sought for a man among them, that should make up the hedge, and stand in the gap before me for the land, that I should not destroy it: but I found none” ([[Ezek. 22:30]]).[@lds-scriptures] The two Hebrew roots for “make up the hedge” and “gap” are those of 13:5, and the one who stands does so “before me.”[@oshb-wlc] Who “among them” are is told in [Ezekiel 22](/ezekiel/22/).

<h2 id="i-will-seek">“I will seek”</h2>

**Connection made by this page: one verb in three places.** The verb behind “I sought” in 22:30 is the verb behind “seek after” in [[Ezek. 34:6]], “sought” in [[Ezek. 34:4]] and “I will seek” in [[Ezek. 34:16]]: *baqash*.[@oshb-wlc] The verb is in ten verses of Ezekiel, among them 34:4 and the watchman's “require” (3:18, 3:20, 33:8).[@oshb-wlc] Of the shepherds the Lord says “neither have ye sought that which was lost” ([[Ezek. 34:4]]) and “none did search or seek after them” ([[Ezek. 34:6]]); then, “I will seek that which was lost” ([[Ezek. 34:16]]).[@lds-scriptures] What is sought differs: a man to stand in the gap in 22:30, the lost sheep in chapter 34. Chapter 34 is followed in [One Shepherd](/ezekiel/themes/one-shepherd/).

## The intercessor

The LORD of Ezekiel is understood to be the premortal Jesus Christ;[@bd-jehovah] see [[Ezek. 1:26]]. Ezekiel 22 does not name an intercessor. **Set beside it: Isaiah.** [[Ezek. 22:30]], “I found none,” and [[Isa. 59:16]], “there was no man… no intercessor.” The chapter heading over Isaiah 59 says “The Messiah will intercede,” and the footnote on “intercessor” gives [[Heb. 7:25]] and [[D&C 45:3]].[@lds-scriptures] Isaiah’s Hebrew word for “intercessor” is not Ezekiel’s word for “gap.”[@bdb] See the Witness of Christ in [Ezekiel 22](/ezekiel/22/) and [The Arm of the Lord](/isaiah/themes/arm-of-the-lord/). Prophetic intercession has been read as “typological for the role of Jesus.”[@sears-intercession]

**Set beside it: Mosiah.** [[Ezek. 22:30]], “stand in the gap before me for the land, that I should not destroy it,” and Abinadi’s words, “standing betwixt them and justice” ([[Mosiah 15:9]]). The likeness is in the English words.
