The law told Israel not to vex the stranger and to love him. Ezekiel charges Jerusalem with oppressing him, and at the end of the book, where the land is divided again, the stranger who has settled and raised children in a tribe receives an inheritance there. The word matters: the King James “stranger” stands for several Hebrew words in Ezekiel, and only one of them is the stranger the law told Israel to love.[@bdb]

## The law

“Thou shalt neither vex a stranger, nor oppress him: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt” ([[Ex. 22:21]]). “The stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you, and thou shalt love him as thyself” ([[Lev. 19:34]]). The stranger is a non-Israelite living in the promised land with the Israelite authorities’ permission, and the law’s provisions for him aimed at justice and fair treatment.[@bd-stranger] Ezekiel puts him under the same sentence as Israel for setting up idols: “every one of the house of Israel, or of the stranger that sojourneth in Israel” ([[Ezek. 14:7]]).

## Wronged

Jerusalem’s sins include that “in the midst of thee have they dealt by oppression with the stranger,” beside “the fatherless and the widow” ([[Ezek. 22:7]]). Later “the people of the land” are charged: “they have oppressed the stranger wrongfully” ([[Ezek. 22:29]]). Both verses stand under the charge that the *ger* has been oppressed.[@bdb] The [note on 22:7](/ezekiel/22/) sets the list beside the law.

## A different matter

[[Ezek. 44:9]] says “No stranger, uncircumcised in heart, nor uncircumcised in flesh, shall enter into my sanctuary.” This is another word, *nekar*, another place, the sanctuary and not the land, and a condition of heart as well as flesh. One older commentary notes that the law let foreigners living in Israel offer sacrifice, and finds the apparent conflict removed by the words “uncircumcised in heart and flesh”: those shut out are “godless heathen.”[@keil-ezekiel] The text does not say that 47:22 reverses [[Ezek. 44:9]]; see the [note on 44:7](/ezekiel/44/).

## Given an inheritance

The land is to be divided “for an inheritance unto you, and to the strangers that sojourn among you, which shall beget children among you: and they shall be unto you as born in the country among the children of Israel; they shall have inheritance with you among the tribes of Israel” ([[Ezek. 47:22]]). Then: “in what tribe the stranger sojourneth, there shall ye give him his inheritance, saith the Lord GOD” ([[Ezek. 47:23]]).

The conditions are part of the text: the stranger sojourns among them and has children there. “As born in the country” is *ezrach*, the native-born, the word in “as one born among you” in [[Lev. 19:34]].[@oshb-wlc] [@bdb] One older commentator observes that the strangers “never were allowed to have any inheritance in the land.”[@gill-exposition] Latter-day Saint teaching takes it of converts outside blood Israel, though “These strangers may be some of the gentile peoples who will accept the gospel in the last days.”[@ot-manual-27] The text itself does not say who they are. See the [note on 47:22](/ezekiel/47/).

Set beside each other, [[Ezek. 22:29]] (“they have oppressed the stranger wrongfully”) and [[Ezek. 47:23]] (“there shall ye give him his inheritance”) use the same word, *ger*, the stranger of [[Ex. 22:21]] and [[Lev. 19:34]].[@oshb-wlc]

## Jesus Christ

The speaker in these chapters is the LORD, whom Latter-day Saints know as “the premortal Jesus Christ” (see [[Ezek. 1:26]]).[@bd-jehovah] Older commentaries read [[Ezek. 47:22]] beside [[Eph. 3:6]], “That the Gentiles should be fellowheirs, and of the same body, and partakers of his promise in Christ by the gospel.” One calls that verse “the best commentary on this passage,” holding that an inheritance “in a literal sense was never granted” and that the passage “respects Gospel times.”[@gill-exposition] Another says “spiritually their privileges are not to be less than those of Israel.”[@jfb-ezekiel] Both take the inheritance spiritually.

Set beside [[Ezek. 47:22]], Paul’s words to Gentiles: “strangers from the covenants of promise” ([[Eph. 2:12]]); “now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ” ([[Eph. 2:13]]); “ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints” ([[Eph. 2:19]]). This is a comparison of English words made by this page, not Ezekiel’s stated meaning: Paul names who brings the stranger near, and Ezekiel’s text does not.
