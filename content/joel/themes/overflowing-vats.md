Joel says twice that the vats overflow. “The floors shall be full of wheat, and the fats shall overflow with wine and oil” (2:24). “The press is full, the fats overflow; for their wickedness is great” (3:13). The noun for the vats, *yeqeb*, and the verb for their overflowing, *šûq*, stand together in these two verses and nowhere else in the book, and the verb in this form stands nowhere else in the Old Testament; its one other appearance is a different form in a psalm, where God “waterest” the earth (Ps. 65:9).[@oshb-wlc][@keil-joel] The first overflow is the Lord’s answer to the fast: He repays “the years that the locust hath eaten” (2:25), and the vats are the measure of what He gives back. The second is the judgment of the nations in the valley of decision, and the vats are the measure of what they have filled up. The words are the same; what fills the vats is not. The commentaries notice the repetition and say it is figurative in the second place; the lexicon files the two verses together.[@keil-joel][@cambridge-joel][@bdb] Reading them as the book’s one measure, used first of mercy and then of judgment, is this page’s.

The Church’s footnote at the second verse leads from “press” to Revelation 14:19 and to the Savior’s words at the end of the world, “I have overcome and have trodden the wine-press alone” (D&C 76:107).[@lds-scriptures] Joel names no treader. This page follows the vats through the book, from the wine cut off to the wine dripping from the mountains, and ends with the one Latter-day scripture puts in the press.

## “Cut off from your mouth”

The book opens with the wine gone. “Awake, ye drunkards, and weep; and howl, all ye drinkers of wine, because of the new wine; for it is cut off from your mouth” (1:5). The loss reaches the altar: “The meat offering and the drink offering is cut off from the house of the LORD” (1:9), and the field: “the new wine is dried up, the oil languisheth” (1:10). The verb “cut off” is the same in 1:5 and 1:9 and again of food in 1:16, and Joel uses it nowhere else.[@oshb-wlc] The Student Manual reads the drinkers of 1:5 as Judah “drunken with the wine of iniquity,” whose vineyard “was to be cut off” so “they could be drunken no more.”[@ot-manual-7] On that reading the first loss is a loss of excess as much as of plenty. The note on [[Joel 1:9]] follows the offering.

Two words for wine run through the book. The “new wine” of 1:5 is *ʿāsîs*, sweet wine, the pressed-out juice, and Joel has it only there and at 3:18. The “new wine” of 1:10 is *tîrôš*, the ordinary word for the year’s wine, and it is the word of the promise in 2:19 and of the overflowing vats in 2:24.[@oshb-wlc][@bdb]

## “The fats shall overflow with wine and oil”

Between the loss and the overflow stands the call: “turn ye even to me with all your heart … And rend your heart, and not your garments” (2:12–13). Then the Lord answers: “Behold, I will send you corn, and wine, and oil, and ye shall be satisfied therewith” (2:19), and “the floors shall be full of wheat, and the fats shall overflow with wine and oil” (2:24). The Church’s footnote on “fats” gives “vats.”[@lds-scriptures] Keil calls the verse “the counterpart to Joel 1:10–12,” and the Cambridge commentary “The reversal of Joel 1:10–12.”[@keil-joel][@cambridge-joel] Keil notes the verb: “hiphil only here and Joel 3:13, to run over, to overflow,” with the one other form in Psalm 65.[@keil-joel] That psalm has God as its subject, visiting the earth and watering it; in Joel the Lord sends the rain (2:23) and the vats run over. In the psalm and in 2:24 the overflow is the Lord’s giving; in 3:13 the verse gives another cause, “for their wickedness is great.” The contrast is this page’s.

What is repaid is harvests. “I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten … my great army which I sent among you” (2:25). The note on [[Joel 2:25]] says the repayment is in kind, full floors and overflowing vats for the years of nothing. The Student Manual reads the years more widely, as “generations of oppression for scattered and rejected Israel” before Israel’s restoration to the covenant.[@ot-manual-7] The vats of 2:24 are the Lord’s measure of mercy: not just enough, but more than the vats can hold.

## “The press is full, the fats overflow”

The second overflow is in the valley where the nations are gathered. “Let the heathen be wakened, and come up to the valley of Jehoshaphat: for there will I sit to judge all the heathen round about. Put ye in the sickle, for the harvest is ripe: come, get you down; for the press is full, the fats overflow; for their wickedness is great” (3:12–13). Keil says the words of 2:24 are “here figuratively employed to denote the great mass of men who are ripe for the judgment,” and that “their wickedness is great” “recals Genesis 6:5,” the sentence before the flood.[@keil-joel] The Cambridge commentary: “The same words (in a literal sense) Joel 2:24. Here they are meant as a significant indication of the numbers to be judged, as also of their ripeness for judgement: the grapes in the wine-press represent the nations.”[@cambridge-joel] “For their wickedness is great” it calls “A second reason, expressed without metaphor, why the judgement is to be put into execution.”[@cambridge-joel] Barnes’ Notes: “they were ripe in their sins, fit for a harvest, and as full of wickedness as ripe grapes, which fill and overflow the vats, through the abundance of the juice with which they swell.”[@barnes-joel] Of the Latter-day Saint sources read, only the *Old Testament Minute* makes the same link, sending the reader from 3:13 to “Joel 2:24 and Revelation 14:14–20.”[@ot-minute-joel]

The Hebrew names nothing that fills these vats. In 2:24 the verb has its object, “wine and oil”; in 3:13 the lexicon notes “accusative omitted.”[@bdb] The commentators supply it differently. The Cambridge commentary has the juice pressing out “simply through the weight of the superincumbent mass of grapes themselves”; Keil has the mass of men, and says the treading “does not express the idea of wading in blood”; Gill has “the great effusion of blood that will be made.”[@cambridge-joel][@keil-joel][@gill-joel] John’s vision, which takes up Joel’s harvest and press, supplies the last: “the winepress was trodden without the city, and blood came out of the winepress” (Rev. 14:20).[@lds-scriptures] The verse itself gives only the measure and the reason: full, overflowing, “for their wickedness is great.” In 2:24 the same measure was the Lord’s repayment. In 3:13 it is the nations’ own filling; Barnes’ Notes cites the Lord’s word to Abraham, “the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full” (Gen. 15:16), and the sentence over the Pharisees, “Fill ye up then the measure of your fathers.”[@barnes-joel]

Who reaps and who treads is not said. The command is plural. Keil and Ellicott give the reaping to angels, the Cambridge commentary gives the command to the “mighty ones” of 3:11, and Barnes’ Notes has the Son of Man speaking to the angels as His reapers.[@keil-joel][@ellicott-joel][@cambridge-joel][@barnes-joel] The Cambridge commentary also reads “get you down” as going down into the press to tread, “here figuratively of the destruction of foes, exactly as Isaiah 63:3.”[@cambridge-joel] The Church’s footnote at “judge” in 3:12 reads “Jesus Christ, Judge,” and the footnote at “sickle” leads to the missionary’s sickle, Alma 26:5 and D&C 4:4; the note on [[Joel 3:13]] follows that.[@lds-scriptures] The Guide to the Scriptures gives both senses of harvest, “bringing people into the Church” or “a time of judgment, such as the Second Coming of Jesus Christ,” so the one field is reaped for gathering and for cutting down.[@gs-harvest]

## “The mountains shall drop down new wine”

After the judgment the wine comes back, and in the word of 1:5. “In that day … the mountains shall drop down new wine, and the hills shall flow with milk, and all the rivers of Judah shall flow with waters, and a fountain shall come forth of the house of the LORD” (3:18). The “new wine” is *ʿāsîs*, the sweet wine “cut off from your mouth” in 1:5, and Joel has the word in those two verses only; the note on [[Joel 3:18]] follows the rest of the verse back to chapter 1.[@oshb-wlc] The commentaries do not make that join; the Cambridge commentary says the words “are evidently based upon Amos 9:13,” where “the mountains shall drop sweet wine.”[@cambridge-joel] The Student Manual reads the verse of the Jews’ future: “Upon accepting Jesus Christ as their Redeemer, the Jews will enter into a new era,” and the mountains “will flow with the riches of heaven.”[@ot-manual-7]

## “Trodden the wine-press alone”

Joel names no treader, and the Church’s edition does. The footnote at “press” in 3:13 leads to Revelation 14:19, “the great winepress of the wrath of God,” and to D&C 76:107, where Christ delivers up the kingdom “saying: I have overcome and have trodden the wine-press alone, even the wine-press of the fierceness of the wrath of Almighty God.”[@lds-scriptures] The seventh angel says the same of Him: “The Lamb of God hath overcome and trodden the wine-press alone” (D&C 88:106), and His own voice at His coming: “I have trodden the wine-press alone, and have brought judgment upon all people; and none were with me” (D&C 133:50).[@lds-scriptures] These are Isaiah’s words, “I have trodden the winepress alone; and of the people there was none with me” (Isa. 63:3), and the note on [[Isa. 63:3]] follows them. Donald Parry sets Joel 3:13 with them: at His first coming Christ “symbolically trod the winepress in offering the Atonement in the Garden of Gethsemane; but at the Second Coming, He will tread the wicked in vengeance,” and “Both acts stain His garments with blood (34:6; Joel 3:13; Lamentations 1:15).”[@ot-minute-isaiah-63] Kerry Muhlestein reads Isaiah 63 the same two ways, “his atoning sufferings in the meridian of time” and “the wrathful judgment of the Second Coming,” and says the same language “is conveyed by the latter-day Jehovah as well (see Doctrine and Covenants 76:107; 88:106).”[@muhlestein-savior-sword] The New Testament Student Manual reads John’s winepress as “God’s judgments upon the wicked and the destruction that will come upon them when they, like grapes on the vine, are fully ripe in iniquity.”[@nt-student-manual-55]

Joel’s command is to many, and the Restoration’s treader is alone. No source I read reconciles the two, and this page does not; it notes that the Church’s footnote carries the reader from Joel’s plural to the Lord’s “alone” without comment. What the passages it leads to agree on is who is in the press. The Bible Dictionary explains the two vats: the grapes “were placed in the press and then trodden under foot; the juice was collected in the ‘fat’ underneath.”[@bd-winefat-winepress] In Joel the nations are the grapes, on every commentary cited. In Isaiah and the Doctrine and Covenants the Lord is the one treading, and at His coming, in Muhlestein’s words, the red on His garments is “the blood of the wicked.”[@muhlestein-savior-sword] On Parry’s and Muhlestein’s readings the press of Gethsemane and the press of the Second Coming are one press with one Treader, and Muhlestein says of Isaiah 63 that “the mercy shown to those who follow God will at least partially take the form of destruction for those who oppress them”; that is Joel’s one measure carried to Christ, and saying so is this page’s.[@ot-minute-isaiah-63][@muhlestein-savior-sword] Jennifer Lane reads the same passages the other way as well, with Isaiah 51:22 beside them: as He treads the press alone, “he is pleading with us to accept his suffering on our behalf,” so that we “no more drink” the cup of wrath.[@lane-winepress] Elder Jeffrey R. Holland hears in “I have trodden the winepress alone” the Savior’s “solitary task of shouldering alone the burden of our salvation.”[@holland-none-with-him]

Joel’s book holds both overflows, and the Lord stands behind both. He sends the rain and the vats run over with wine and oil; He sits to judge, and the press is full. The measure is one. What fills it is the difference between “the years that the locust hath eaten,” repaid, and “their wickedness,” grown great.[@lds-scriptures] Lane reads the Treader’s own word on the difference: as He treads the press alone “he is pleading with us to accept his suffering on our behalf,” so that we “no more drink” the cup of wrath; the overflow of judgment, on that reading, is the one He has already taken for those who will let Him.[@lane-winepress]
