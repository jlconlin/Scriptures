The King James uses two English verbs for one Hebrew verb. “Sanctify a fast” ([[Joel 1:14]]; [[Joel 2:15]]), “sanctify the congregation” ([[Joel 2:16]]) and “Prepare war” ([[Joel 3:9]]) all have the same Hebrew verb, in the same form, and these four verses are its only uses in Joel.[@oshb-wlc] Three of the four pair it with the verb “call”: “call a solemn assembly” (1:14; 2:15) and “Proclaim ye this” (3:9).[@oshb-wlc] The objects differ. For the fast the verb means keeping a thing sacred; for the congregation and the war it means consecrating, the war “from custom of opening campaign by sacrifice”.[@bdb] A modern translation keeps one verb: “consecrate a fast” and “Consecrate yourselves for war.”[@nrsvue]

## The summons to Judah

“Sanctify ye a fast, call a solemn assembly, gather the elders and all the inhabitants of the land into the house of the LORD your God, and cry unto the LORD” ([[Joel 1:14]]). Again, with the trumpet: “Gather the people, sanctify the congregation, assemble the elders” ([[Joel 2:15–16]]). On one reading of Joel, “the temple is the only place of refuge from destruction in the day of the Lord.”[@vandyke-ogden-twelve] See the note at [[Joel 1:14]].

## What that gathering does

The priests “weep between the porch and the altar, and let them say, Spare thy people, O LORD, and give not thine heritage to reproach, that the heathen should rule over them” ([[Joel 2:17]]). Their prayer names the nations; “heathen” here is the Hebrew word translated “Gentiles” and “heathen” in chapter 3.[@oshb-wlc] See the note at [[Joel 2:17]].

## The Lord answers

“Then will the LORD be jealous for his land, and pity his people. Yea, the LORD will answer and say unto his people” ([[Joel 2:18–19]]). Whether the verse tells what He did or what He will do is taken up in the note at [[Joel 2:18]].

## The summons to the nations

The Lord says, “I will also gather all nations, and will bring them down into the valley of Jehoshaphat, and will plead with them there for my people and for my heritage Israel” ([[Joel 3:2]]). “Heritage” stands in Joel only here and in the priests' prayer.[@oshb-wlc] **Connection made by this page:** set beside that prayer, the Lord's words take up its two nouns, “thy people” and “thine heritage,” as “my people” and “my heritage.”[@lds-scriptures]

Then comes the summons: “Proclaim ye this among the Gentiles; Prepare war” ([[Joel 3:9]]). **Connection made by this page:** set beside the summons to Judah, this one has the same two verbs, call and sanctify, with war in place of a fast.[@lds-scriptures] See the notes at [[Joel 3:9]] and [[Joel 3:2]].

## Judgment and refuge

“Let the heathen be wakened, and come up to the valley of Jehoshaphat: for there will I sit to judge all the heathen round about” ([[Joel 3:12]]). **Connection made by this page:** set side by side, the two summonses are followed by different things: after Judah's, the Lord answers His people ([[Joel 2:18–19]]); after the nations', He sits to judge them.[@lds-scriptures]

One verse then has both: “The LORD also shall roar out of Zion, and utter his voice from Jerusalem; and the heavens and the earth shall shake: but the LORD will be the hope of his people, and the strength of the children of Israel” ([[Joel 3:16]]). “Hope” is a word for a refuge or shelter.[@bdb] See the note at [[Joel 3:16]].

## In latter-day revelation

“Call your solemn assemblies” ([[D&C 133:6]]) is listed beside Joel 1:14.[@lds-scriptures] The same revelation says, “prepare ye, prepare ye, O my people; sanctify yourselves; gather ye together” ([[D&C 133:4]]). It also says that the Lamb “shall utter his voice out of Zion” ([[D&C 133:18]], [[D&C 133:21]]), the verse to which the footnote on “utter” in Joel 3:16 points.[@lds-scriptures] **Connection made by this page:** the one revelation has both: the call to the Lord's people to sanctify themselves, gather and call solemn assemblies, and the voice out of Zion.[@lds-scriptures]

## Through Jesus Christ

In Joel the one who sits to judge and the hope of His people are the same LORD (3:12, 16), and Jehovah, the name the King James text prints as LORD, is the premortal Jesus Christ.[@bd-jehovah] The judge is cross-referenced to “Jesus Christ, Judge” and the LORD of 3:16 to “Jesus Christ, Second Coming,” and in D&C 133 the voice out of Zion is that of the Lamb, “the Lord, even the Savior” ([[D&C 133:25]]).[@lds-scriptures] “The Father judgeth no man, but hath committed all judgment unto the Son” ([[John 5:22]]).
