## Selected for development (author, 2026-10-07)

These are three proposed pages. **Do not write them yet:** the author is selecting themes across books and will separately authorize development. The earlier reports below remain research leads. Theme pages may draw their own text-grounded connections under `STANDARDS.md` §8, while sourcing factual claims and preserving the book's unanswered ending (`BRIEF.md`, “The Lord does not answer”).

1. **The Lord's hand and heart.** Follow the poems' frank attribution of Jerusalem's affliction to the Lord and their account of the people's sin (1:5, 12–18; 2:1–8, 17; 3:1–18, 39–42; 4:11), then the central statement that He does not afflict willingly and will have compassion (3:31–33), ending with the prayer that He turn the people back (5:21–22). **Include the contrast of prayer shut out and prayer heard** (3:8, 44; 3:55–57) as part of what the poems say about His hand and heart, not a fourth page. The Lord is Jehovah, identified with the premortal Christ in the book brief; do not turn these poems into a general claim that present suffering is divine punishment, or resolve chapter 5's silence.
2. **“None to comfort her.”** Follow the absence of a comforter (1:2, 9, 16–17, 21; 2:13) into the middle poem's confession of mercies and hope (3:21–26, 31–33), while the final prayer remains unanswered (5:19–22). **Include the shut-out/heard prayer contrast** (3:8, 44, 55–57) as a turn within the search for comfort, not a separate theme page. *Come, Follow Me* reads the hope of chapter 3 in Christ; preserve the difference between hope and an answer already received. Distinguish this page's felt absence and hope from page 1's judgment, confession, and the Lord's stated heart.
3. **“Look upon us.”** Trace the changing appeals that the Lord see the city's suffering (1:9, 11, 20; 2:20), the man's wait until the Lord looks down and his confidence that the Lord has seen his wrong (3:50, 59–60), and the communal “Remember, O LORD… consider” (5:1). Test whether these are a developing prayer rather than merely repeated words. The Christ connection can be drawn as a theme page's own comparison under §8, but must not put an answer in the Lord's mouth or replace the ending with comfort. Keep this page about the speakers' appeals to be seen, distinct from pages 1 and 2.

**Combined, not a fourth page:** “Prayer shut out and prayer heard” (earlier proposal 4; chapter 3 reports) belongs in both 1 and 2, with each page using it for its own arc. Link between pages or chapter notes instead of duplicating a long explanation. Other reported ideas below are not selected as separate pages.

## Reported by chapters 1–5 (not yet sorted)

- (1) 'None to comfort her': the comforter refrain (1:2, 9, 16, 17, 21; the same root as a verb in 2:13).
- (1) The Lord as the one who afflicts, with the cause stated (1:5, 12-15, 18; carried on in 2:1-8).
- (1) The city as a woman: widow, daughter of Zion, virgin daughter of Judah, unclean woman (1:1, 6, 8-9, 15, 17).
- (1) 'Behold / see, O LORD': the appeals that the Lord look (1:9, 11, 20).
- (1) Prayers against enemies left to the Lord's justice (1:21-22; 3:64-66; 4:21-22).
- (2) The Lord as the one who strikes, 'as an enemy' (2:1-8, 2:17; also 1:12-15, 3:1-18), with the book's own cause (1:18) and 3:33.
- (2) No answer from the Lord; no law and no vision (2:9; the prayers of 2:20-22 left unanswered).
- (2) moed, the appointed feast or meeting, turned against the city (1:4, 1:15, 2:6, 2:7, 2:22).
- (2) 'Behold and consider': prayers that ask the Lord only to look (2:20; also 1:11 and 5:1, not checked in this chapter).
- (2) City lament as a form, shared with Mormon 6:16-22 (2:11).
- (3) The Lord seeing: "see", "look", "behold" (1:9, 1:11, 2:20, 3:1, 3:50, 3:59-60, 5:1).
- (3) Prayer shut out and prayer heard (3:8, 3:44, 3:55-57).
- (3) The unnamed "he" who afflicts; the Lord as an enemy (2:1-8; 3:1-17).
- (3) Remembering (3:19-21; 5:1).
- (3) The book's own account of the cause, and of the Lord's heart (1:5, 1:18; 3:31-33, 3:39-42; 5:16).
- (4) The covenant curses of Deuteronomy 28 as the frame of the siege (4:10, 4:16, 4:19).
- (4) The cup of the Lord's fury passing to Edom (4:21; Jeremiah 25).
- (4) The king as "the anointed of the LORD", the people's breath and shadow (4:20).
- (4) Daughter of Zion and daughter of Edom (4:21-22).
- (4) Shifts of voice: "they", "we", "thou" (4:13-22).
- (5) "Turn" (shuv): 5:21, with Jer. 31:18 and the Lord's reply in Jer. 31:20.
- (5) "Remember" and "forget": 5:1 and 5:20.
- (5) The fathers' sin and the people's own: 5:7 and 5:16, with Jer. 31:29-30 and Ezek. 18.
- (5) The Lord does not answer: 5:19-22, and the ending of the book.
