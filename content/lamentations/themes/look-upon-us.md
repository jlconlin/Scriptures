Across Lamentations the Lord is asked to look. The city asks, then a man waits for Him to look and says He has seen, and at the end the whole people ask. The request stays the same; who asks, and the form of the asking, change. The book ends with the request before Him and no reply recorded.

| Step | Passage | The words |
|---|---|---|
| The city asks | [[Lam. 1:9]], [[Lam. 1:11]], [[Lam. 1:20]] | “behold my affliction”; “see, O LORD, and consider” |
| To whom | [[Lam. 2:20]] | “consider to whom thou hast done this” |
| A wait | [[Lam. 3:49–50]] | “Till the LORD look down” |
| Thou hast seen | [[Lam. 3:57–63]] | “thou hast seen my wrong” |
| Remember | [[Lam. 5:1]] | “Remember, O LORD… behold our reproach” |
| No reply | [[Lam. 5:20–22]] | “thou hast utterly rejected us” |

## The city asks

“O LORD, behold my affliction: for the enemy hath magnified himself” ([[Lam. 1:9]]). “See, O LORD, and consider; for I am become vile” ([[Lam. 1:11]]). “Behold, O LORD; for I am in distress” ([[Lam. 1:20]]). Each gives a reason with “for.” She asks the same of passers-by, “behold, and see if there be any sorrow like unto my sorrow” ([[Lam. 1:12]]), and of “all people” ([[Lam. 1:18]]), after the adversaries “saw her, and did mock” ([[Lam. 1:7]]). One older commentary reads the first two as one sigh, “repeated in an intensified form.”[@keil-lamentations] See [Lamentations 1](/lamentations/1/).

## “To whom thou hast done this”

“Behold, O LORD, and consider to whom thou hast done this” ([[Lam. 2:20]]). The two Hebrew verbs are those of “See… and consider” in 1:11, in the same order: *reʾeh*, then *habbiṭah*.[@oshb-wlc] The verse does not name the speaker; an older commentary calls it “the prayer which the city has been commanded to make” (see the note on [[Lam. 2:20]]).[@keil-lamentations] What He is asked to consider is now a person, the one “to whom” this was done.

## “Till the LORD look down”

“Mine eye trickleth down, and ceaseth not… Till the LORD look down, and behold from heaven” ([[Lam. 3:49–50]]). This is a wait, not a command, and “look down” (*yashqif*) is a verb that occurs only here in Lamentations.[@oshb-wlc] The note on [[Lam. 3:48]] follows “seen” through the chapter.

## “Thou hast seen”

The man says, “Thou drewest near in the day that I called upon thee: thou saidst, Fear not… O LORD, thou hast seen my wrong: judge thou my cause. Thou hast seen all their vengeance” ([[Lam. 3:57–60]]). “Fear not” is the man’s report of what the Lord said. In the King James wording it lies in the past (see the note on [[Lam. 3:56]]). An older commentary finds “no thanksgiving” for a past rescue in these verses and takes the Hebrew tenses as present confidence, “the full assurance of faith from which the request comes.”[@keil-lamentations] Then the request returns: “Behold their sitting down, and their rising up” ([[Lam. 3:63]]).

## “Remember, O LORD”

“Remember, O LORD, what is come upon us: consider, and behold our reproach” ([[Lam. 5:1]]). The speakers are “us” and “our,” and *zekhor*, “remember,” stands first.[@oshb-wlc] The pair of 1:11 and 2:20 comes in the other order here: *habbiṭah*, then *reʾeh*, “look, and see.”[@oshb-wlc] See [Lamentations 5](/lamentations/5/).

## What the verses do

Set side by side, the appeals ask one thing from first to last. Who asks, and how, changes. The speaker is the city’s “I” ([[Lam. 1:9]], [[Lam. 1:11]], [[Lam. 1:20]]), then a voice the verse does not name ([[Lam. 2:20]]), then the man’s “I” ([[Lam. 3:50]], [[Lam. 3:59–63]]), then “we” ([[Lam. 5:1]]). The form goes from commands, to a wait, to “thou hast seen,” to commands again, now beginning with “remember.” Three verses do not fit so neatly. In [[Lam. 3:19]] the verb of 5:1 is parsed as a command, “Remember mine affliction,” where the King James has “Remembering,” and the verse names no one addressed.[@oshb-wlc] [[Lam. 3:63]] is a command after “thou hast seen.” And in [[Lam. 4:16]] the verb behind “consider” is used of the Lord not looking: “he will no more regard them.”[@oshb-wlc] Reading these verses as a sequence is this page’s own; no source read treats them as one.[@lds-scriptures]

## No reply

“Wherefore dost thou forget us for ever, and forsake us so long time?… But thou hast utterly rejected us; thou art very wroth against us” ([[Lam. 5:20–22]]). The book ends on the prayer, and it contains no response from the Lord.[@scripture-helps-jer-31-33] A detail of its wording agrees: the Hebrew verb “answer” (*ʿanah*, H6030) is not used in Lamentations.[@oshb-wlc] See the notes on [[Lam. 5:21]] and [[Lam. 5:22]].

## Two verses set beside these prayers

Within Lamentations it is the man who says that the Lord has seen ([[Lam. 3:59–60]]); the Lord does not say it. He says it elsewhere, of Israel in Egypt: “I have surely seen the affliction of my people” ([[Ex. 3:7]]). The verb and the noun are those of “behold my affliction” ([[Lam. 1:9]]) in Hebrew.[@oshb-wlc] Setting the two side by side is this page’s own comparison; Exodus speaks of Egypt, not of this city.[@lds-scriptures] The LORD to whom these poems pray is Jehovah, the premortal Jesus Christ.[@bd-jehovah] The Genesis page [The God Who Sees, Hears, and Remembers](/genesis/themes/god-who-sees/) follows His seeing from Genesis to Luke 7.

Luke tells of Him looking on this same city: “when he was come near, he beheld the city, and wept over it” ([[Luke 19:41]]). Setting that verse beside “Behold, O LORD” is also this page’s own comparison; Luke is Greek and Lamentations Hebrew, and what joins them is the act and the city, not a shared word.[@lds-scriptures] It is not an answer to the prayer. Luke 19 goes on to foretell a siege of the city ([[Luke 19:43–44]]), and the prayer of Lamentations 5 is left where the book leaves it.
