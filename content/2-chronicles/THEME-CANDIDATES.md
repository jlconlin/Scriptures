## Author's selection (2026-11-14)

Three pages for Chronicles (author: “Do all 3”), two of them for this book.

### 1. “Seek My Face … and I Will Heal” (from [[2 Chr. 7:14]])

*Combines Fable's “We Sought Him Not after the Due Order” (1 Chronicles) and “Till There Was No Remedy” (2 Chronicles); both turn on 7:14 and on Hezekiah's Passover. It lives under 2 Chronicles and reaches back into 1 Chronicles.*

**The arc.** The promise has two halves: seek my face, and I will heal (7:14). Seeking fails: Saul “inquired not of the LORD” (1 Chr. 10:14); the ark “we inquired not at it” (13:3); the breach on Uzza, “for that we sought him not after the due order” (15:13); David “could not go before it to inquire of God: for he was afraid because of the sword,” and is answered by fire where he stands (21:26–22:1). *dāraš* occurs eleven times in 1 Chronicles; the one place it is used of seeking actually done has the LORD for subject: “the LORD searcheth all hearts” (28:9). Healing is sought everywhere but from the LORD: the root *rpʾ* occurs six times in 2 Chronicles (7:14; 16:12 “physicians”; 21:18 “incurable,” *lĕʾên marpēʾ*; 22:6; 30:20; 36:16 “no remedy,” *lĕʾên marpēʾ*). Once both halves meet: at Hezekiah's Passover a multitude comes “otherwise than it was written,” Hezekiah prays “The good LORD pardon” (*yĕkappēr*, the LORD as subject), and “the LORD hearkened to Hezekiah, and healed the people” (30:18–20), the reverse of Uzza. Then each condition of 7:14 is named as refused, “till there was no remedy” (36:12–16), and the land is never said to be healed.

**Thesis.** Chronicles opens its history with seeking that fails and closes it with “no healing,” and the one time the promise is kept, it is kept from the LORD's side, by atonement, for people with no claim on it: the Savior is the seeker before He is the sought, and where there is no remedy, scripture's answer is a Person who heals by bearing the wound.

**To Christ:** [[Luke 19:10]]; [[John 4:20–23]]; [[Isa. 65:1]] with [[Rom. 10:20]]; [[Heb. 10:19–22]]; [[Jer. 30:12–17]]; [[Jer. 33:6]]; [[Mal. 4:2]] with [[2 Ne. 25:13]]; [[Isa. 53:5]]; [[Mark 2:1–12]]; [[3 Ne. 9:13]]; Davis (`davis-bended-rules`) on 30:18–20. **Applies:** [[D&C 46:9]]; [[D&C 64:34]]; [[D&C 88:63]]; [[Alma 7:11–12]]; the conditions of 7:14 are how one comes to Him, not what does the healing.

**Must not claim:** that the due order stopped mattering (Uzziah, 26:16–21, and the Passover was still kept by sanctified priests); that 1 Chr. 14:10, 14 does not exist (the verb there is *šāʾal*: say so); any reason for Uzza's death beyond the notes at 13:10 and 2 Sam. 6:7; that going to physicians is faithless, or every sickness a punishment; that 36:16's judgment was softened for its generation; a meaning for “healed” at 30:20 beyond its note's readings; anything joining Alma 7's “bowels” to Jehoram's. Check every Hebrew count with `hebrew.mjs` before leaning on it. Psalms' *The Law and the Lost Sheep* already walks Ezek. 34 to Luke 19:10: link, don't repeat.

### 2. “The LORD His God Be with Him” (from [[2 Chr. 36:23]])

**The arc.** The book's first verse and last sentence hold the same three words, *YHWH ʾĕlōhāyw ʿimmô* (1:1; 36:23); Ezra 1:3 words the decree otherwise. A prophet states the terms, “The LORD is with you, while ye be with him” (15:2); Abijah has God “with us for our captain” against calves “with you” (13:8, 12); a hired army is sent home because “the LORD is not with Israel” (25:7); Ahaz refuses the sign whose name is Immanuel (28:16–23; Isa. 7:10–14), and his son says that name to a besieged city, “with him is an arm of flesh; but with us is the LORD our God” (32:7–8); then God is “with” Necho against Josiah (35:21–22); and the last sentence gives the first back as an open wish about “who is there among you,” naming no son of David.

**Thesis.** 2 Chronicles begins by saying the LORD was with David's son and ends by wishing He may be with whoever goes up, and the Gospel that opens as Chronicles does answers with a son of David named Emmanuel who says “I am with you alway”: the kings had God with them while they were with Him; in Jesus Christ God came to be with a people who were not with Him.

**To Christ:** [[Isa. 7:14]]; [[Isa. 8:8–10]]; [[Matt. 1:1]], [[Matt. 1:21–23]]; [[Matt. 28:18–20]]; [[John 1:14]]; [[John 2:19–21]]; [[Hag. 1:13]]; [[Mosiah 15:1]]. **Applies:** [[D&C 20:77]], [[D&C 20:79]]; [[D&C 88:63]]; [[John 14:23]]; [[D&C 84:88]].

**Must not claim:** that Christ removed the condition of 15:2 (the sacrament prayer keeps it); that Cyrus is a type of Christ; a motive for the Chronicler's wording against Ezra's; that the Hebrew order of the books is the order Matthew knew, beyond what a source says; that Josiah was a wicked king. Fable named scholarship from memory (Matt. 28 shaped on Cyrus's decree; Matthew leaning on Chronicles' genealogies; a copyist's variant at 36:23): the dossier confirms each or the sentence goes. List *ʾēt* (“with”) as well as *ʿim* before counting.

## Not chosen

“Deliver the Captives Again” (6:36–39; 28:5–15; 30:9; 33:11–13; 36: its way to Christ rests on reading 28:15 beside the Good Samaritan, which needs a source); “An Atonement for All Israel” (Codex's own; no insight about Christ beyond the notes on gathering); humbling, relying, preparing the heart as a list (the book's stated measures); the king and the censer; the prophets sent and refused (2 Kings' *Innocent Blood*); the face (2 Kings' *Out of His Sight*); the lamp of David; the house's history; the Passovers compared.
