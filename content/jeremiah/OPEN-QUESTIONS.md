## Chapters

- **1:5, “knew.”** The note quotes the Church's manual that *yadaʿ* “connotes a very personal, intimate relationship.” The manual takes that from a commentary that can't be read online (Thompson, NICOT); it is cited on the manual's own authority. Keep or cut?
- **1:5, the reading without a premortal life.** The note gives the Latter-day Saint reading first and then, briefly, the reading that takes the verse as God's decree only. Keep the second, or leave it out?
- **31:36, “ordinances.”** The Church's manual reads the word as priesthood ordinances; in the verse it is “the ordinances of the moon and of the stars.” The note gives both and says the manual uses the word in a different sense from the verse. Is that the right way to handle a place where a manual's reading goes beyond the verse?
- **31:22, “A woman shall compass a man.”** The lexicon says most take it as “shall protect”; the Church's manual quotes a different reading (Israel turning in love to the Lord, pointing to the new covenant). The note gives both without choosing.
- **31:2.** No note says who “the people which were left of the sword” in the wilderness are; no source read answered it.

- Jer. 3:16, the ark: the Bible Dictionary mentions “an untrustworthy tradition of its preservation by Jeremiah” (the tradition that Jeremiah hid the ark). The chapter doesn't raise it; the author may want a sentence on it.

### Jeremiah 5

- Jer. 5:7–8: Is the adultery literal or a figure for idolatry? Keil says literal. The Church footnote points to TG Adulterer, and the manual paraphrases it literally, but neither rules out the figurative sense. The note now says only what the verses name.
- Jer. 5:7: Keil's Hebrew text read 'I caused them to swear', against the Leningrad Codex's 'I fed them full'. I cut the variant paragraph. The author may want it back with the Leningrad reading stated correctly.
- Jer. 5 christ section: Setting Jer. 5:1 beside Isa. 59:16 is the chapter's own link. It now rests only on the Church footnotes to Isa. 59:16. The author may prefer a different Christ connection, or none.

- *Unanswered:* Verse 16: 'Their quiver is as an open sepulchre' has no note.

### Jeremiah 11

- Jer. 11:15: the verse is obscure. The chapter follows the Church edition's footnote ('acceptable sacrifice has ceased') and Keil's 'hypocrisy' reading. It leaves out the Septuagint's 'Can vows and holy flesh take away your evil?', which many translations follow.
- Jer. 11:23: 'no remnant' of the men of Anathoth sits beside Ezra 2:23, which lists 128 men of Anathoth who returned. The note reports both and does not reconcile them.
- Jer. 11:19: the 'lamb led to the slaughter' is tied to Christ only through the Church edition's footnote (Isa. 53:7; Mosiah 14:7; D&C 135:4). The note says Jeremiah is not the Lamb of God.
- Jer. 11:2: the chapter notes that 'the book of the covenant' also appears in Josiah's reading (2 Kgs. 23:2) but does not date the message to that reform. No source read ties them together.
- Jer. 11:13: the background on Manasseh now rests on 2 Kgs. 21:3. The manual's sentence on Manasseh and 'rampant' idolatry is in quotation marks, taken from another author, so it was not cited.
- *Unanswered:* What 'rising early and protesting' (v. 7) means. Keil only points to Jer. 7:13, and the Church edition's footnote gives only 'HEB witnessed'.
- *Unanswered:* Why Jeremiah's own prayer (v. 20) is heard when his prayer for Judah (v. 14) is forbidden.
- *Unanswered:* When the chapter was given, and whether it belongs to Josiah's covenant renewal or to Jehoiakim's reign.
- *Unanswered:* How Jeremiah's prayer for vengeance (v. 20) fits with later teaching on praying for enemies. Only a reading of the Hebrew verb from Keil is offered.
