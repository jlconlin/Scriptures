# Jeremiah: open questions for review

Judgment calls waiting for the author. Only items that need the author's judgment belong here; anything the standard already decides is just done. Delete an item once it is resolved.

The 52 chapters were written in one day (2026-10-02) and their checkers reported every call they made. Most of those were instances of rules the standard or the brief already sets, so they are recorded once below as “Decided while writing,” where the author can overturn any of them. The questions that are really the author's follow. Reader questions that no source answered are at the end, as a list for a later deepening pass, not as decisions.

## Decided while writing

Policies applied across the book. Each is already in `STANDARDS.md` or the brief, or follows from them; they are listed so the author can see them in one place.

1. **A scholar quoted inside a Church manual** is cited as the manual, for what the manual prints, and presented as one view, when the scholar's own book can't be read online (`STANDARDS.md` §4 rule 6). This covers Thompson (NICOT) in 1:5, 6:14, 7:29, 12:9, 45:1; Sperry in 13:4–7, 34:11, 40:1; the *Interpreter's Bible* in 13:23; Clarke was read on Bible Hub and is cited directly (8:1, 12:5, 33:16).
2. **A prophet's words quoted in a Church manual** (Benson 1950, McConkie, Kimball, Joseph Smith) are cited as the manual when the original wasn't opened. Where the Joseph Smith Papers have the original discourse, the chapter quotes the Papers (29:9, where *Teachings of the Prophet Joseph Smith* adds words the 1844 report lacks).
3. **An outside reading** (usually Keil) is given only where no Church, BYU, or Scripture Central source answers the question, is labelled “one reading,” and is never set beside the Church's reading as an equal (brief, “Where answers come from”). Where a Church source and Keil differ on a verse, the chapter follows the Church source (15:13–14, 18:18, 22:22, 42:20, 51:20). Chapters 15 and 29 have Keil in about half their notes because nothing else answered those questions; the alternative was to cut the notes.
4. **Scripture set beside scripture** by the writer, with no footnote or source making the link, is kept when the shared words are in the texts (13 christ, 14:7, 14:21, 24:5, 38:22 the mire, 49:13 Bozrah) and cut when it is a typology no source makes (8:19 Laman and Lemuel, 32:7 the kinsman-redeemer, 36:32 the lost 116 pages, 12:16 and 3 Ne. 21). `STANDARDS.md` §4 rule 5 and §8, “No readings of the site's own.”
5. **Dates** follow the Bible Dictionary's “Jeremiah” outline (brief). Where the Bible Dictionary's entries disagree with one another (Jehoiakim 609–598 in one, 608–597 in another), the “Jeremiah” outline wins. A year reached by arithmetic from it (Zedekiah's fourth year, “about 594 or 593 B.C.” in 27, 28, 51) is given as “about.” An undated chapter says so.
6. **Two Church sources that read a verse differently** are both given, without choosing (28:6 Jeremiah's “Amen,” 33:18 the sons of Levi, 35:13 what the Rechabites kept). **A Church source whose reading goes beyond the verse's own sense** is quoted, and the note says plainly that the verse's own sense is different (31:36 “ordinances”).
7. **Textual variants** (Septuagint, Kethib, Hebrews' wording) are reported where they change the reading, with the KJV first (2:20, 11:15, 31:32, 33:14–26, 49:1); `STANDARDS.md` §2.
8. **The `prophets` kind** is for the teaching of prophets and apostles. A note whose only latter-day voice is a Seventy or a bishop keeps the quotation under another kind (22:16, 29:13 relabelled `scripture`; 39:16 is `history`).
9. **A `christ` section** rests on scripture or a cited source; where a chapter gives little to work with (41, 43, 47), the section is short and says the chapter itself draws no connection, rather than inventing one.
10. **Repeated passages** are explained once and linked (6:12–15 and 8:10–12; 7:30–34 and 19:6; 23:5–6 and 33:15–16), as the brief says.

## For the author

- **1:5.** After the Latter-day Saint reading of “before I formed thee… I knew thee” (premortal existence and foreordination), the note briefly gives the reading that takes the verse as God's decree only. Keep that second paragraph, or leave it out?
- **7 and 26.** Chapter 7 says the arrest in chapter 26 “is probably what followed this sermon”; chapter 26 leaves it open and notes Keil's view that Jeremiah may have warned of Shiloh more than once. Should they lean the same way?
- **13:4.** The linen girdle: the note sets Keil's view that Jeremiah literally went to the Euphrates beside the manual's word “parable” (Sperry). Give only the manual's reading?
- **16:2.** The marriage ban is presented as literal; the manual's figurative reading (“may not have been a literal one”) is kept as a possibility. Keep it?
- **23:5–6.** The Branch is read as Christ reigning in the Millennium (McConkie through the manual; footnote 5d). The note gives no reading of the Branch as fulfilled at Christ's first coming. Add one?
- **25:11.** The seventy years are given only in the Church's two measures; Keil's symbolic “number of perfection” reading was cut. Raise the symbolic reading at all?
- **35:7.** The note on the Rechabites keeps a paragraph on the later *History of the Rechabites* (the Narrative of Zosimus), from KnoWhy #710. It is close to reception history, which the standard excludes, but it is the only sourced answer to what became of the promise in verse 19. Keep or cut?
- **38:6 and 1 Ne. 7:14.** Which imprisonment Nephi meant: an earlier one (Brown and Seely), an arrest in 605 B.C. (Chadwick, known only through KnoWhy #463), or this one known secondhand or by revelation. The note gives all three without choosing. Should the site lean toward one?
- **39:6, 41:10, 43:6.** The Mulek material from KnoWhy #753 (that Mulek was not in Jerusalem at the fall, or reached Egypt with the king's daughters) comes from Chadwick and Sorenson; Sorenson was read, Chadwick's article was not. The notes label it a proposal. Enough, or read Chadwick too?
- **46:10.** Carchemish is “the day of the Lord” in the text; the manual reads the phrase as the Second Coming. The note gives both as two levels. Confirm.
- **19 (christ).** The last sentence sets the jar broken in the valley of graves beside Matthew's potter's field for graves; the manual names only Jer. 18:2 and 19:1–2 as Matthew's source. Keep or cut?
- **45:4.** Keil reads “this whole land” as the whole earth (because of “all flesh” in verse 5); the plain-words section says “land.” Also, the setting and the note on verse 1 both explain why a 605 B.C. chapter sits after chapter 44; say it once?
- **52:12, 28–29, 31.** Where Jeremiah and Kings give different days and years, the chapter reports both and does not decide. Fine as is?
