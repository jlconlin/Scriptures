Use this as a lookup page while you read. The entries point to the chapter where the person, place, or image receives fuller treatment.

## People

**Jeremiah.** A priest from Anathoth, called in Josiah’s thirteenth year and active until after Jerusalem fell.[@bd-jeremiah] → [Jeremiah 1](/jeremiah/1/)

**Baruch.** Jeremiah’s scribe, who writes the prophet’s dictated scroll and reads it at the temple ([[Jer. 36]]). He remains with Jeremiah through the flight to Egypt. → [Jeremiah 36](/jeremiah/36/)

**Josiah.** The reforming king at the beginning of Jeremiah’s ministry. His death begins the rapid succession of Judah’s final kings. → [Jeremiah 1–6](/jeremiah/1/)

**Jehoiakim.** Josiah’s son, who burns Jeremiah’s scroll ([[Jer. 36:23]]). → [Jeremiah 22](/jeremiah/22/), [Jeremiah 36](/jeremiah/36/)

**Jehoiachin / Jeconiah / Coniah.** The same young king under three names; Babylon deposes and exiles him after three months. → [Jeremiah 22](/jeremiah/22/), [Jeremiah 24](/jeremiah/24/)

**Zedekiah.** Judah’s last king, installed after Jehoiachin’s deposition ([[2 Kgs. 24:17]]); he seeks Jeremiah’s counsel but resists what it asks of him. → [Jeremiah 21](/jeremiah/21/), [Jeremiah 37–39](/jeremiah/37/)

**Nebuchadnezzar / Nebuchadrezzar.** Babylon’s king. Jeremiah uses both spellings; they name the same ruler. → [Jeremiah 25](/jeremiah/25/), [Jeremiah 52](/jeremiah/52/)

**Mulek.** Zedekiah’s surviving son, whose descendants become the people of Zarahemla in the Book of Mormon ([[Hel. 8:21]]; [[Omni 1:15–16]]). → [Jeremiah 39](/jeremiah/39/)

## Places and peoples

**Anathoth.** Jeremiah’s hometown, a priestly town northeast of Jerusalem. → [Jeremiah 1](/jeremiah/1/), [Jeremiah 32](/jeremiah/32/)

**Babylon / Chaldeans.** The empire that destroys Jerusalem and carries Judah into exile. In Jeremiah, Babylon is a real historical power first, and then also the target of the book’s final prophecies. → [Jeremiah 25](/jeremiah/25/), [Jeremiah 50–51](/jeremiah/50/)

**Egypt.** The false refuge Judah seeks against Babylon; the remnant finally carries Jeremiah there. → [Jeremiah 2](/jeremiah/2/), [Jeremiah 42–44](/jeremiah/42/)

**Ramah.** A place north of Jerusalem associated with the captives and Rachel’s weeping ([[Jer. 31:15]]).[@bd-ramah] → [Jeremiah 31](/jeremiah/31/), [Jeremiah 40](/jeremiah/40/)

**Tophet / the valley of Hinnom.** The place south of Jerusalem associated with child sacrifice; Jeremiah’s broken jar there makes it an emblem of coming judgment ([[Jer. 7:31–32]]; [[Jer. 19:1–13]]). → [Jeremiah 7](/jeremiah/7/), [Jeremiah 19](/jeremiah/19/)

## Images and phrases

**Broken cisterns.** Human-made sources of security that cannot hold water, set against the Lord, the fountain of living waters ([[Jer. 2:13]]). → [Jeremiah 2](/jeremiah/2/)

**The potter and clay.** The Lord’s authority to reshape a nation, joined to the conditional nature of judgment and repentance ([[Jer. 18:1–10]]). → [Jeremiah 18](/jeremiah/18/)

**The yoke.** Jeremiah’s wooden yoke pictures Babylon’s domination; breaking it without the Lord’s word only replaces it with iron ([[Jer. 27–28]]). → [Jeremiah 27](/jeremiah/27/)

**Good and bad figs.** The figs stand for the exiles and for those remaining in Jerusalem, and they overturn easy assumptions about who is safe and who is lost ([[Jer. 24]]). → [Jeremiah 24](/jeremiah/24/)

**The cup of the Lord’s fury.** Judgment as a drink the nations must receive. → [The Cup of the Lord’s Fury](/jeremiah/themes/cup-of-fury/)

**Shepherds.** Leaders responsible to care for the Lord’s people; Jeremiah’s failed shepherds prepare for the righteous Branch. → [The Shepherds and the Branch](/jeremiah/themes/shepherds-and-the-branch/)

**“Fear on every side.”** Jeremiah’s name for Pashhur, and a recurring expression for surrounding terror ([[Jer. 20:3, 10]]). → [Jeremiah 20](/jeremiah/20/)

**To root out and to plant.** The six verbs of Jeremiah’s commission: judgment is real, but it ends in the Lord’s work of building and planting. → [To Root Out and to Plant](/jeremiah/themes/root-and-plant/)
