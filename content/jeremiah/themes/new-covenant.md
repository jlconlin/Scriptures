The Lord tells Judah what the covenant asks: “Obey my voice, and I will be your God, and ye shall be my people” ([[Jer. 7:23]]). Israel and Judah break it. Then the Lord promises a new covenant, in which He will put His law in their inward parts and write it in their hearts, and Hebrews says of Jesus that “he is the mediator of a better covenant” ([[Heb. 8:6]]).

<h2 id="broken">The covenant broken</h2>

The condition stands inside a command that [[Jer. 11:4]] says was given “in the day that I brought them forth out of the land of Egypt”: “Obey my voice… so shall ye be my people, and I will be your God.” Chapter 11 is about “breaking the covenant of obedience”:[@lds-scriptures] “The house of Israel and the house of Judah have broken my covenant which I made with their fathers” ([[Jer. 11:10]]). In Hebrew, 11:10 and 31:32 share the verb form for “broken” and the words “which I made with their fathers,” and that phrase stands nowhere else in the book.[@oshb-wlc] See [Jeremiah 11](/jeremiah/11/).

<h2 id="heart">What is written on the heart</h2>

“The sin of Judah is written with a pen of iron… it is graven upon the table of their heart” ([[Jer. 17:1]]). The metaphors “speak of how deeply sin was imbedded in Judah’s consciousness.”[@ot-manual-23] In Hebrew, 17:1 and 31:33 are the only two verses in Jeremiah where a form of “write” governs “heart.”[@oshb-wlc] See [Jeremiah 17](/jeremiah/17/), and [Jeremiah 4](/jeremiah/4/) for “circumcise… your heart.”

<h2 id="new-covenant">The new covenant</h2>

> Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah… I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people… for I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more. ([[Jer. 31:31]], [[Jer. 31:33–34]])

Three times the Lord “set before” them His law ([[Jer. 9:13]]; [[Jer. 26:4]]; [[Jer. 44:10]]); here, with the same Hebrew verb, He will “put” it “in their inward parts.”[@oshb-wlc] The Lord is the one who acts: “I will put… and write it… I will forgive.” And the two-part formula, a condition in 7:23 and 11:4, is a promise everywhere else it stands: [[Jer. 24:7]], [[Jer. 30:22]], [[Jer. 31:1]], [[Jer. 31:33]] and [[Jer. 32:38]].[@oshb-wlc]

The promise reaches the heart itself: “I will give them an heart to know me” ([[Jer. 24:7]]); “I will give them one heart, and one way… And I will make an everlasting covenant with them” ([[Jer. 32:39–40]]). Chapters 30–33 are “prophecies of the latter-day restoration of Israel and the gospel covenant.”[@bd-jeremiah] See [Jeremiah 31](/jeremiah/31/), [Jeremiah 32](/jeremiah/32/) and [Scattered and Gathered](/jeremiah/themes/scattered-and-gathered/).

<h2 id="mediator">The mediator</h2>

Hebrews quotes Jeremiah’s promise ([[Heb. 8:8–12]]) to show that the Lord “would someday make a new covenant with Israel,”[@nt-student-manual-49] and calls Jesus “the mediator of the new covenant” ([[Heb. 12:24]]). At the Last Supper He says, “This cup is the new testament in my blood” ([[Luke 22:20]]); “testament” is the Greek word for covenant,[@lds-scriptures] and in presenting the cup He “was signaling the fulfillment of the old covenant and the establishment of the new covenant.”[@nt-student-manual-9] Paul writes of an epistle “written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart” ([[2 Cor. 3:3]]), “pointing to the fulfillment of a prophecy of Jeremiah.”[@nt-student-manual-41] The Joseph Smith Translation of [[Gal. 3:20]] reads, “there is one mediator of the new covenant, who is Christ.”[@jst-gal-3]

<h2 id="when">Offered, rejected, and offered again</h2>

Jeremiah’s promise is “with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah.” Jews in New Testament times “generally rejected this new covenant,” but “it would again be offered to them at a later time.”[@nt-student-manual-49] Joseph Smith wrote in 1833 that “Christ in the days of his flesh proposed to make a covenant with them but they rejected him and his proposals,” so that “no covenant was made with them at that time but their unbelief has not rendered the promise of God of none effect.”[@smith-saxton-letter]

So Jeremiah 31:31–34 is read as speaking of “the restoration of the gospel through the Prophet Joseph Smith and the day when God’s covenant will truly be established with His people,”[@ot-manual-25] and “new” is taken to mean that the covenant “would be revealed anew to the house of Israel.”[@ot-seminary-teacher-135] [[D&C 45:9]] (“I have sent mine everlasting covenant into the world”) is cross-referenced to Jer. 31:33, and [[D&C 84:57]] to Jer. 31:31.[@lds-scriptures] For the returning people’s “perpetual covenant” ([[Jer. 50:5]]), see [Scattered and Gathered](/jeremiah/themes/scattered-and-gathered/#together).
