Every “pastor” in the KJV of Jeremiah is, in Hebrew, a shepherd.[@oshb-wlc] Chapters 21–23 are “on pastors or rulers of the people, with promise of the king Messiah (23:1–6).”[@bd-jeremiah] The shepherds fail, the house of David is weighed, and the Lord promises to gather the flock and raise a Branch to David, whom the Church’s chapter headings name as the Messiah.[@lds-scriptures]

## Shepherds who scatter

The charge comes early. In [[Jer. 2:8]] the pastors stand beside the priests, “they that handle the law,” and the prophets. In [[Jer. 10:21]] they “have not sought the LORD: therefore they shall not prosper, and all their flocks shall be scattered.” Yet in [[Jer. 3:15]] the Lord already promises “pastors according to mine heart, which shall feed you with knowledge and understanding.”

Sources differ a little on who these shepherds are: “pastors or rulers of the people,”[@bd-jeremiah] “pastors (religious leaders),”[@ot-manual-25] and, at 3:15, “faithful pastors (church leaders).”[@ot-manual-23] See [Jeremiah 3](/jeremiah/3/) and [Jeremiah 10](/jeremiah/10/).

## The house of David weighed

To the king the Lord says, “Execute ye judgment and righteousness” ([[Jer. 22:3]]), for “David’s throne stands or falls according to the obedience of the kings.”[@lds-scriptures] Jehoiakim is set beside his father: “did not thy father eat and drink, and do judgment and justice, and then it was well with him?” ([[Jer. 22:15]]). His injustices are “particularly evident when compared to the righteous deeds of his father, Josiah.”[@ot-manual-24]

Then the verdicts: Coniah’s seed will not prosper “sitting upon the throne of David” ([[Jer. 22:30]]), and of Jehoiakim, “He shall have none to sit upon the throne of David” ([[Jer. 36:30]]). The page for [Jeremiah 22](/jeremiah/22/) takes up Coniah, the signet, and his place in Matthew’s genealogy.

<h2 id="branch">The Lord gathers, and raises a Branch</h2>

“Woe be unto the pastors that destroy and scatter the sheep of my pasture!” ([[Jer. 23:1]]). Then the Lord says what He will do: “I will gather the remnant of my flock” ([[Jer. 23:3]]), “I will set up shepherds over them” ([[Jer. 23:4]]), and:

> Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper, and shall execute judgment and justice in the earth. ([[Jer. 23:5]])

This is “the Branch, who is the King (the Messiah).”[@lds-scriptures] “The Branch and the King are the Lord Jesus Christ when He returns to earth to reign.”[@ot-manual-25] The King’s work, to “execute judgment and justice,” is the Hebrew phrase commanded of the king in 22:3 and said of Josiah in 22:15; it is said of the Lord Himself in [[Jer. 9:24]] and of the Branch again in 33:15.[@oshb-wlc] For the Branch in Isaiah, see [Isaiah 4](/isaiah/4/) and [Isaiah 11](/isaiah/11/).

His name is “THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS” ([[Jer. 23:6]]). The name of Judah’s last king, Zedekiah, means “The Lord (is) righteousness.”[@bd-zedekiah] On one reading the name-title echoes his, “the last regnant king of Judah who was dethroned and exiled to Babylon, but pointing forward to the future Davidic Messiah.”[@bowen-ominous-onomastics] After the exile no descendant of David reigned, and so “this prophecy remained to be fulfilled by a future Davidic descendant.”[@bowen-ominous-onomastics] See [Jeremiah 23](/jeremiah/23/).

## The promise repeated

“David their king, whom I will raise up unto them” ([[Jer. 30:9]]) is “David, their king (the Messiah).”[@lds-scriptures] “Jesus, who is the King David of the latter days, is a branch of the ancient line of King David.”[@ot-manual-25] Then: “He that scattered Israel will gather him, and keep him, as a shepherd doth his flock” ([[Jer. 31:10]]).

“Branch” (Hebrew *ṣemaḥ*) occurs in Jeremiah only at 23:5 and 33:15.[@oshb-wlc] There the Lord says, “will I cause the Branch of righteousness to grow up unto David” ([[Jer. 33:15]]). In 33:16 the KJV gives the name to the city. Church manuals read this two ways, as the city “called after him”[@ot-manual-13] or as a rendering to be corrected so that the name stays with the Branch;[@ot-manual-25] [Jeremiah 33](/jeremiah/33/) takes it up. The verse after says, “David shall never want a man to sit upon the throne of the house of Israel” ([[Jer. 33:17]]). Prophecies of the Branch and of David’s throne “have reference almost exclusively to his second sojourn on planet earth.”[@ot-manual-13]

## The Good Shepherd

Ezekiel has the promise too: “I will set up one shepherd over them, and he shall feed them, even my servant David” ([[Ezek. 34:23]]). The footnote at 34:24 points back to Jer. 23:5 and 30:9, and “the Messiah will be their Shepherd.”[@lds-scriptures] Nephi sees the gathering end the same way: “there shall be one fold and one shepherd; and he shall feed his sheep” ([[1 Ne. 22:25]]). And Jesus says, “I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep” ([[John 10:11]]); “there shall be one fold, and one shepherd” ([[John 10:16]]).
