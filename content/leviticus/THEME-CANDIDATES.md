# Leviticus: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.**

## Author's selection (2026-10-15)

From Fable's and Codex's lists of 2026-10-15 (`.cache/batch/leviticus/reports/themes-fable.md`, `themes-codex.md`), judged by what each teaches of Christ's Atonement, mercy and redemption (`STANDARDS.md` §8, “The insight is about Christ's work”). In this order:

1. **The sacrifices and offerings beside today's ordinances** (author, 2026-10-13: “I'm curious how the various prescribed sacrifices and offerings compare to and align with today's ordinances”; “a theme page, not a guide page”; 2026-10-15: the “big” theme, “that should be first,” that “connects/compares the prescribed sacrifices and rituals with modern-day ordinances and practices”). It carries, as a section, Fable's “The bread none might eat”: the one offering Israel might not eat (6:30; 4:12; 16:27) beside Heb. 13:10–12, John 6 and the sacrament (3 Ne. 18). Before its outline, ask the author whether it spans the five books, what it should carry (one gospel taught by different ordinances, or each offering beside its counterpart), and whether temple ordinances are in scope, within what the Church's sources say publicly. The volume guides *The Law and the Covenant* and *The Temple and Worship* give sacrifice an overview: the page links to them and does not repeat them. When it is written is the author's (`IDEAS.md`: after the five books of Moses).
2. **Drawing near** (Codex 1 and 2, opening from Fable 4). Who may come near the Lord, and how: the fire that accepts the offering and consumes Nadab and Abihu (9:24; 10:2); the conditions that separate from the holy and their cleansing (11–15; 15:31, “that they die not … when they defile my tabernacle”); the one way in, once a year (16:2, 12–16, 30). Christ touches the unclean (Mark 1:40–44; 5:25–34) and opens “a new and living way” through the veil (Heb. 10:19–22). Must not claim what Nadab and Abihu did, or that ritual impurity is sin. Exodus's *That I May Dwell among Them* and Psalms' *Who May Enter* are linked, not repeated.
3. **Accepted** (Fable 2 and Codex 4, with Fable 1 folded in). How a sinner is accepted: the offering “accepted for him” (1:3–4, *ratson*, *ratsah*); confession with an offering scaled to what he has (5:5–13); the high priest's confession over the goat (16:21–22); a scattered people with no altar who “accept of the punishment of their iniquity” and confess, and the Lord who remembers His covenant and will “not cast them away” (26:40–45). The offering shrinks at each confession until there is none. Christ is the offering by which we are accepted, and He asks “a broken heart and a contrite spirit” in place of burnt offerings (3 Ne. 9:19–20). Must not claim that confession replaced sacrifice under the law (5:5 still requires the animal).
4. **I will be sanctified** (Fable 7 and Codex 5). Holiness from threat to gift: “I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me” over two dead priests (10:3); “sanctify yourselves, and be ye holy” (11:44; 20:7); “I am the LORD which sanctify you” (20:8; 21:8, 15, 23; 22:9, 16); “I will be hallowed … I am the LORD which hallow you” (22:32), the same form as 10:3. Christ: “we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all” (Heb. 10:10); “sanctified in Christ” (Moro. 10:33).
5. **The kinsman who redeems** (Fable 5, reframed for redemption, with Codex 3's jubilee). The land is the Lord's and Israel are “strangers and sojourners with me” (25:23); the stranger loved “as thyself” (19:34) is the setting. An Israelite grown poor who sells himself, even to a stranger, “may be redeemed again; one of his brethren may redeem him” (25:25, 47–49), at a price counted to the jubilee, when liberty is proclaimed (25:10). Christ the Redeemer. Bowen reads the kinsman-redeemer as a christological type: his reading. Must not draw any modern application (immigration and the like).

Not chosen: Fable 3 as a page of its own (it is a section of 1), 6 (the hand on the head), 8 (cut off); Codex's “Be holy” alone (it is in 4); the leads both lists set aside.
