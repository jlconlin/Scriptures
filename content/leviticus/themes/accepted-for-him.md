Leviticus begins with an offering that “shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him” ([[Lev. 1:4]]) and ends with a scattered people who “accept of the punishment of their iniquity” ([[Lev. 26:41]]). “Accepted” and “accept” are one Hebrew verb. “Confess” stands in the book exactly three times, at 5:5, 16:21 and 26:40.[@oshb-wlc] What is brought changes from the first verse to the last: an animal, then what the sinner is able to bring, then the priest’s confession over one goat for all, then nothing but a confession and a humbled heart.

<h2 id="accepted">Accepted for him</h2>

“He shall put his hand upon the head of the burnt offering; and it shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him” ([[Lev. 1:4]]). In the Hebrew the offering is what is accepted; the offerer is “for him.”[@oshb-wlc] “Of his own voluntary will” ([[Lev. 1:3]]) is the noun of that verb, which can mean “acceptance” as well as “will.”[@bdb] Such offerings “pointed Israel to Jesus Christ and His atoning sacrifice.”[@scripture-helps-ex-35] Of the hand on the head, one reading is that “The animal takes the place of the person in the sacrifice”;[@lane-whole-meaning] the note at [[Lev. 1:4]] gives this reading and the other.

<h2 id="confess">Confess, then bring what he has</h2>

“He shall confess that he hath sinned in that thing” ([[Lev. 5:5]]), the first of the three places.[@oshb-wlc] Atonement could be made for “crimes that a man voluntarily confessed.”[@bd-sacrifices] The confession does not replace the animal: “he shall bring his trespass offering unto the LORD… a lamb or a kid of the goats” ([[Lev. 5:6]]). Then the text scales the offering: “if he be not able to bring a lamb,” two turtledoves or two young pigeons ([[Lev. 5:7]]); if not those, “the tenth part of an ephah of fine flour” ([[Lev. 5:11]]); and “it shall be forgiven him” ([[Lev. 5:13]]).

<h2 id="one-goat">All their iniquities on one head</h2>

“Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the children of Israel… And the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited” ([[Lev. 16:21–22]]). This is the second “confess.”[@oshb-wlc] Set beside [[Lev. 5:5]] (“he shall confess”) and 16:21 (“Aaron shall… confess”), the confession has moved from the sinner, before he brings his offering, to the priest over one animal for all Israel.

<h2 id="no-altar">No altar, and “I will not cast them away”</h2>

“I will make your cities waste, and bring your sanctuaries unto desolation, and I will not smell the savour of your sweet odours” ([[Lev. 26:31]]). “Sweet odours” is the same Hebrew word as the offerings’ “sweet savour” in 1:9 and fifteen other verses,[@oshb-wlc] and the Church’s edition glosses the line “I will not accept your burnt offerings.”[@lds-scriptures]

Then: “If they shall confess their iniquity… if then their uncircumcised hearts be humbled, and they then accept of the punishment of their iniquity: Then will I remember my covenant” ([[Lev. 26:40–42]]). The promise to “remember” the covenant is “cited often toward the end of the Book of Mormon.”[@ot-minute-lev] This is the third “confess.” No priest is named, and the sanctuary is desolate (26:31); and the Church’s footnote at “humbled” is “Contrite Heart.”[@lds-scriptures] “Accept” here is the verb of 1:4, but the lexicon puts the two under different senses: “be accepted, of sacrifices” at 1:4, “satisfy (by paying off debt)” at 26:41.[@bdb] The two verses share the word, not one meaning; see the note at [[Lev. 26:41]]. The promise ends: “I will not cast them away, neither will I abhor them, to destroy them utterly… for I am the LORD their God” ([[Lev. 26:44]]).

Set beside each other, 1:4 and 26:41 use one verb: at the start an offering is accepted for the sinner; at the end the sinner has no offering and does the accepting.

<h2 id="christ">The offering, and the heart</h2>

The law’s “whole meaning” is “every whit pointing to that great and last sacrifice” ([[Alma 34:14]]). “We are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all” ([[Heb. 10:10]]), and “Animal sacrifice ended with the death of Jesus Christ.”[@scripture-helps-ex-35]

Of Christ: “he offereth himself a sacrifice for sin, to answer the ends of the law, unto all those who have a broken heart and a contrite spirit” ([[2 Ne. 2:7]]). “To have a broken heart is to be humble, contrite, repentant, and meek—that is, receptive to the will of God.”[@gs-broken-heart] The Church’s footnote files the “humbled” heart of [[Lev. 26:41]] under “Contrite Heart”;[@lds-scriptures] set beside it, 2 Nephi names the contrite spirit as the one to which Christ’s offering answers the ends of the law. The risen Lord says, “I will accept none of your sacrifices and your burnt offerings… ye shall offer for a sacrifice unto me a broken heart and a contrite spirit” ([[3 Ne. 9:19–20]]); “accept” is the King James word of 1:4 and of the gloss at 26:31, in English only. See also [the broken heart beside the altar](/psalms/themes/the-sacrifice-god-desires/#broken-heart).
