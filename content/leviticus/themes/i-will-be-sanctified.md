Twice in Leviticus the LORD says He Himself will be sanctified: “I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me” ([[Lev. 10:3]]) and “I will be hallowed among the children of Israel: I am the LORD which hallow you” ([[Lev. 22:32]]). These are the only two places in the book where the LORD uses the verb of Himself in this form (“I will be sanctified”).[@oshb-wlc] Between them Israel is told to “sanctify yourselves,” and seven times the LORD says that He sanctifies.[@oshb-wlc]

| Step | Passage | The words |
|---|---|---|
| By another's hand | [[Lev. 8:12]]; [[Lev. 8:30]] | “anointed him, to sanctify him” |
| “I will be sanctified” | [[Lev. 10:3]] | “And Aaron held his peace” |
| “Sanctify yourselves” | [[Lev. 11:44]]; [[Lev. 20:7]] | “sanctify yourselves, and ye shall be holy; for I am holy” |
| “I sanctify you” | [[Lev. 20:8]]; [[Lev. 21:8]]; [[Lev. 22:9]] | “I am the LORD which sanctify you” |
| “I will be hallowed” | [[Lev. 22:32]] | “I am the LORD which hallow you” |
| By His offering | [[Heb. 13:12]]; [[Moro. 10:33]] | “with his own blood” |

## Made holy by another's hand

In chapter 8 Moses is the one who sanctifies: he “poured of the anointing oil upon Aaron's head, and anointed him, to sanctify him” ([[Lev. 8:12]]), and with oil and blood “sanctified Aaron, and his garments, and his sons” ([[Lev. 8:30]]).[@oshb-wlc] Holiness “is extrinsic rather than intrinsic because it has its ultimate source in Yahweh.”[@bowen-law-codes] See [Leviticus 8:11](/leviticus/8/).

## “I will be sanctified”

Nadab and Abihu “offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not,” and “there went out fire from the LORD, and devoured them” ([[Lev. 10:1–2]]). Moses says: “I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me, and before all the people I will be glorified. And Aaron held his peace” ([[Lev. 10:3]]). One reading holds that the LORD had sanctified the priests by anointing, “that they might sanctify Him in their office and life. If they neglected this sanctification, He sanctified Himself in them by a penal judgment.”[@keil-leviticus] The Church's footnote on “held” sends to “all those who will not endure chastening, but deny me, cannot be sanctified” ([[D&C 101:5]]).[@lds-scriptures] See [Leviticus 10:3](/leviticus/10/).

## “Sanctify yourselves”

“Ye shall therefore sanctify yourselves, and ye shall be holy; for I am holy” ([[Lev. 11:44]]), and again, “Sanctify yourselves therefore, and be ye holy” ([[Lev. 20:7]]). Here Israel is the subject, and the verb turns back on it: Israel is to sanctify itself.[@oshb-wlc] “God's greatest desire for his people is that they become as he is.”[@strathearn-holiness-lord] See [Leviticus 11:44](/leviticus/11/).

## “I am the LORD which sanctify you”

The next verse turns: “And ye shall keep my statutes, and do them: I am the LORD which sanctify you” ([[Lev. 20:8]]). The same form, “the one who sanctifies,” stands at 20:8; 21:8, 15, 23; 22:9, 16 and 22:32: “I the LORD, which sanctify you, am holy” ([[Lev. 21:8]]); “I the LORD do sanctify them” ([[Lev. 22:9]]).[@oshb-wlc] God is “the source of holiness” and “the primary agent of sanctification.”[@strathearn-holiness-lord] Elsewhere in the book the verb has other subjects: Israel hallows the priest (21:8), the holy things (22:2–3) and the jubilee year (25:10), the high priest hallows the altar (16:19), and a man his house or field (27:14–26).[@oshb-wlc] On Joseph Smith's addition at 22:9, “if they profane not mine ordinances, I the Lord will sanctify them,” one reading is that “the blessing of sanctification is conditional upon obedience”.[@bowen-law-codes] See [Leviticus 22:9](/leviticus/22/).

## “I will be hallowed”

“Therefore shall ye keep my commandments, and do them: I am the LORD. Neither shall ye profane my holy name; but I will be hallowed among the children of Israel: I am the LORD which hallow you” ([[Lev. 22:31–32]]). In Hebrew the verb forms differ (imperfect at 10:3, perfect with “and” at 22:32), though both are translated “I will be”; the phrase after the verb also differs, “in them that come nigh me” against “among the children of Israel.”[@oshb-wlc]

Set beside [[Lev. 10:3]], where “I will be sanctified” stands over two dead priests and Aaron's silence, [[Lev. 22:32]] has the same speaker say it of Himself, and in the same verse says “I am the LORD which hallow you.” In 10:3 only His being sanctified is said; in 22:32 it and His hallowing of Israel are one sentence.[@lds-scriptures]

## Sanctified by His offering

Lev. 10:3 and 11:44 are grouped under sanctification with Heb. 10:10, Heb. 13:12, Moro. 10:33, D&C 20:31 and D&C 76:41.[@tg-sanctification] Sanctification is “the process of becoming free from sin, pure, clean, and holy through the Atonement of Jesus Christ.”[@gs-sanctification] The voice of Leviticus 20:8 is Jehovah, the premortal Jesus Christ.[@gs-jehovah] “Both he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one” ([[Heb. 2:11]]). “Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate” ([[Heb. 13:12]]), and the Church's footnote on “without” sends to Lev. 4:12.[@lds-scriptures] “We are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all” ([[Heb. 10:10]]).

“Sanctification through the grace of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ is just and true, to all those who love and serve God with all their mights, minds, and strength” ([[D&C 20:31]]). He came “to sanctify the world, and to cleanse it from all unrighteousness” ([[D&C 76:41]]).

“Then are ye sanctified in Christ by the grace of God, through the shedding of the blood of Christ, which is in the covenant of the Father unto the remission of your sins, that ye become holy, without spot” ([[Moro. 10:33]]).
