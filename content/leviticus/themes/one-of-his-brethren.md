Leviticus 25 provides for a brother who sells himself, even to a stranger: “After that he is sold he may be redeemed again; one of his brethren may redeem him” ([[Lev. 25:48]]). Only his family, or he himself, may buy him back, at a counted price, and if none does, the jubilee frees him. Jesus Christ is the Redeemer who “paid the price.”[@gs-redeemer] Where this page sets one passage beside another, the comparison is the page’s own.

<h2 id="strangers">Strangers with the LORD</h2>

“The land shall not be sold for ever: for the land is mine; for ye are strangers and sojourners with me” ([[Lev. 25:23]]). Israel held the land as “not actual owners or full possessors.”[@keil-leviticus] On the stranger, see [[Lev. 19:34]] and [the Ezekiel page](/ezekiel/themes/the-strangers-inheritance/).

<h2 id="kinsman">The kinsman who buys back</h2>

If a sojourner or stranger grows rich and a brother “sell himself unto the stranger” ([[Lev. 25:47]]), the law lists who may redeem him: “Either his uncle, or his uncle’s son, may redeem him, or any that is nigh of kin unto him of his family may redeem him; or if he be able, he may redeem himself” ([[Lev. 25:49]]). It names no one outside the family. The verb is *ga’al*, “act as kinsman, do the part of next of kin”; in 25:48–49 it is used “in redeeming from bondage.”[@bdb] It is “based on familial relationship”: the *gōʾēl* “redeemed family members who had become enslaved for whatever reason.”[@lane-redemption-gaal] See the note at [[Lev. 25:25]].

<h2 id="price">The price of his redemption</h2>

He reckons “from the year that he was sold” to the jubilee ([[Lev. 25:50]]) and gives again “the price of his redemption out of the money that he was bought for” ([[Lev. 25:51]]). [[Lev. 25:48]] is the redemption “of a slave.”[@bd-redemption] To redeem is “to free a person from bondage by payment,”[@gs-redeem] and “the law of Moses provided different ways that servants and property could be freed, or redeemed, by the payment of money (see Leviticus 25:29–32, 48–55).”[@curtis-redemption]

<h2 id="servants">Whose servants they are</h2>

“For unto me the children of Israel are servants; they are my servants whom I brought forth out of the land of Egypt” ([[Lev. 25:55]]). “Redeem” in [[Ex. 6:6]], “I will redeem you with a stretched out arm,” is the same verb as in 25:48.[@oshb-wlc] The LORD “had already graciously acted as de facto kinsman in freeing Israel from slavery in Egypt.”[@spackman-atonement]

<h2 id="jesus-christ">Jesus Christ, the kinsman who paid</h2>

“The Lord Jehovah is known throughout the Old Testament as the gōʾēl, the Redeemer of Israel… his redemption grew out of the family relationship formed through the covenants they had made.”[@lane-redemption-gaal]

*Kin.* Leviticus: “one of his brethren may redeem him” ([[Lev. 25:48]]). Hebrews: “he is not ashamed to call them brethren” ([[Heb. 2:11]]); “the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same… And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage” ([[Heb. 2:14–15]]). The redeemer the law requires is “nigh of kin”; the one who delivers from bondage took part of the same flesh and blood.

*Price.* Leviticus: “the price of his redemption… money” ([[Lev. 25:51]]). Peter: “ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold… But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot” ([[1 Pet. 1:18–19]]). Beside the money of the law stands a price that is not money. Christ “paid the price for the sins of mankind,”[@gs-redeemer] so that “if we repent, we can be forgiven of our sins, the price having been paid by our Redeemer.”[@curtis-redemption]

*Sold, and redeemed without money.* The brother “sell himself” ([[Lev. 25:47]]); Isaiah says, “Ye have sold yourselves for nought; and ye shall be redeemed without money” ([[Isa. 52:3]]). In Hebrew, Isa. 52:3 has both verbs of Lev. 25:47–48: “sold” is *makar* and “redeemed” is *ga’al*, the kinsman’s verb.[@oshb-wlc] The risen Lord speaks the verse to the Nephites ([[3 Ne. 20:38]]).[@lds-scriptures] In the law the one who sold himself is redeemed with money; in Isaiah, “without money.”

<h2 id="jubilee">The year of liberty</h2>

The brother no one redeems still goes free: “then he shall go out in the year of jubilee, both he, and his children with him” ([[Lev. 25:54]]), in the year proclaimed with “liberty throughout all the land” ([[Lev. 25:10]]). For Isaiah’s jubilee words and their fulfilment at Nazareth, see the closing note of [Leviticus 25](/leviticus/25/) and the note on [[Isa. 61:1]].
