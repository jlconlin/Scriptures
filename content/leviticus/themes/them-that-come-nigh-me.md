Leviticus asks who may come near the Lord who dwells in the camp, and how. Fire accepts the congregation’s offering, then devours two priests; the unclean are kept outside; then, after the two deaths, one way in is given. Hebrews reads that single yearly entry as a sign that the way “was not yet made manifest,”[@lds-scriptures] and on the day’s goats, “rather than destroy or drive out the sinners, God provided another way.”[@cfm-ot-2026-tabernacle]

<h2 id="fire">The fire that accepts, and the fire that devours</h2>

“All the congregation drew near and stood before the LORD” ([[Lev. 9:5]]), and “there came a fire out from before the LORD, and consumed upon the altar the burnt offering” ([[Lev. 9:24]]). Next, two priests “offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not,” and “there went out fire from the LORD, and devoured them” ([[Lev. 10:1–2]]). Moses answers: “I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me” ([[Lev. 10:3]]). The text says only that the fire was one “he commanded them not”; it is explained as “fire not taken from the altar.”[@bd-nadab]

The people “drew near” ([[Lev. 9:5]]) and the priests “offered” ([[Lev. 10:1]]) are both forms of *qarab*, the book’s ordinary word for bringing an offering (102 times in Leviticus).[@oshb-wlc] Set side by side, the two verses show the same act: chapter 9 brings “that which Moses commanded,” and the sons bring “strange fire … which he commanded them not.” [Connection made by this page.]

<h2 id="outside">Put outside, that they die not</h2>

The leper “shall dwell alone; without the camp shall his habitation be” ([[Lev. 13:46]]). Israel is to be separated from uncleanness, “that they die not in their uncleanness, when they defile my tabernacle that is among them” ([[Lev. 15:31]]).

Uncleanness was “ceremonially or ritually unclean,” not “morally evil,” and “so long as a person was unclean he was cut off from the congregation”;[@bd-clean-unclean] yet “in a spiritual sense, to be clean is to be free from sin and sinful desires.”[@gs-clean-unclean] One reading joins them: the spiritually unclean are cut off from God “until that condition of impurity was removed.”[@sc-knowhy-479] See the guide [The Law and the Covenant](/volumes/old-testament/guides/04-law-and-covenant/#clean-unclean), and for the Gospels’ healings the Witness of Christ in [[Lev. 13]], [[Lev. 14]] and [[Lev. 15]].

<h2 id="once-a-year">One way in, once a year</h2>

“After the death of the two sons of Aaron, when they offered before the LORD, and died,” Aaron is told “that he come not at all times into the holy place within the veil … that he die not” ([[Lev. 16:1–2]]). The Day of Atonement ([[Lev. 16:2]]) was “the only occasion when the high priest could enter the Holy of Holies.”[@scripture-helps-ex-35] The holy place is atoned for “because of the uncleanness of the children of Israel, and because of their transgressions in all their sins” ([[Lev. 16:16]]), so that “ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD” ([[Lev. 16:30]]).

[[Lev. 16:16]] names the uncleanness chapters 11–15 kept outside the camp, and beside it “their transgressions in all their sins.” The tent “that remaineth among them” also answers “my tabernacle that is among them” ([[Lev. 15:31]]): in Hebrew “remaineth” is the verb rendered “dwell” in “that I may dwell among them” ([[Ex. 25:8]]), and “tabernacle” at 15:31 is the noun made from that verb.[@oshb-wlc] [Connection made by this page.] The day’s purpose: “This allowed God’s presence to remain among the people.”[@cfm-ot-2026-tabernacle] See [That I May Dwell among Them](/exodus/themes/that-i-may-dwell-among-them/).

<h2 id="another-way">The way into the holiest</h2>

Hebrews: “into the second went the high priest alone once every year, not without blood … that the way into the holiest of all was not yet made manifest” ([[Heb. 9:7–8]]); Christ “by his own blood … entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us” ([[Heb. 9:12]]). “The high priest who took the sacrificial blood through the veil foreshadowed Jesus Christ.”[@scripture-helps-ex-35] “Christ’s atoning sacrifice has opened the sanctuary of God to all believers.”[@tanner-advocate]

Of the goats: “Sin must not be allowed in God’s presence. But rather than destroy or drive out the sinners, God provided another way—a goat would be killed or driven out instead.”[@cfm-ot-2026-tabernacle] Set beside the earlier chapters, “destroy” stands beside the fire that “devoured” and “that they die not,” and “drive out” beside “without the camp.” [Connection made by this page.] That sentence is about sin; uncleanness was “ceremonially or ritually unclean,” not morally evil.[@bd-clean-unclean] [[Lev. 16:16]] names both “uncleanness” and “transgressions,” and the live goat “was then driven out of the camp of Israel.”[@cfm-ot-2026-tabernacle]

Hebrews adds: “Let us draw near with a true heart” ([[Heb. 10:22]]). See [Who May Enter?](/psalms/themes/who-may-enter/#forerunner).

Leviticus answers its question with fire, the camp’s edge, and one man once a year; Hebrews says the one yearly entry was itself the sign that the way was not yet open, and that Christ, by His own blood, entered in.
