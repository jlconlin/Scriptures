# Judges: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.**

## Author's selection (2026-11-03)

Fable's and Codex's lists (`.cache/batch/judges/reports/themes-fable.md`, `themes-codex.md`) were shown to the author combined into three. **One page is chosen** (author, 2026-11-03: “Theme 2 only”): **“Right in His Own Eyes”: whose eyes judge.** For twelve chapters the narrator's measure is “evil in the sight [Hebrew: eyes] of the LORD” (2:11; 3:7, 12; 4:1; 6:1; 10:6; 13:1, the last). In the next chapter a judge measures by his own: “she pleaseth me well,” in Hebrew “she is right in my eyes” (14:3, 7); his eyes are put out (16:21), and his last prayer is to be avenged “for my two eyes” (16:28). From 17 on the whole people does “that which was right in his own eyes” (17:6; 21:25), and a host offers women to a mob with “do with them what seemeth good unto you,” in Hebrew “in your eyes” (19:24, Lot's words from Gen. 19:8). Behind the refrain is Deut. 12:8–9, where “every man whatsoever is right in his own eyes” belongs to the time before Israel is “come to the rest,” which the Church's footnote at 17:6 points to; the land has rest four times (*šāqaṭ*, 3:11, 30; 5:31; 8:28), and the word's last use is the “quiet” of Laish before Dan burns it (18:7, 27). It leads to the LORD “the Judge” (11:27; TG “Jesus Christ, Judge”; John 5:22), the Branch who “shall not judge after the sight of his eyes” (Isa. 11:3–4), and the rest that remains (Heb. 4:8–9, argued on Joshua's *Rest Round About*, which this page points to); it applies through D&C 1:16 and Prov. 3:5–7. It must not claim that the book argues for kingship (brief), or that the text calls Samson's blinding a punishment for 14:3.

**Not chosen:** “I will deliver you no more” (the cry and the Lord's compassion, 2:18; 10:10–16), because the manuals and the chapter notes at 2:18, 3:9, 10:16 and chapter 10's Christ section already make that connection; and “I cannot go back” (vows that would not relent, 11:35; 21:1–7), left optional and not taken.
