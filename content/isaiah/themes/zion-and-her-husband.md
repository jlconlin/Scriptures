Isaiah speaks of Zion as a woman: a faithful city turned harlot, a mother who thinks herself forsaken, a captive, a barren woman told to sing, and at last one over whom her God rejoices. Her condition changes from passage to passage. Scripture and the Church read the Redeemer and Husband of these chapters as Jesus Christ.[@gs-redeemer][@gs-bridegroom]

## Forsaken, not forgotten

“How is the faithful city become an harlot!” ([[Isa. 1:21]]). Later Zion herself speaks: “The LORD hath forsaken me, and my Lord hath forgotten me” ([[Isa. 49:14]]). The answer: “Can a woman forget her sucking child… yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee” ([[Isa. 49:15]]), and “Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands” ([[Isa. 49:16]]). The Savior will not forget because He has “graven [us] upon the palms of [His] hands.”[@ot-seminary-lesson-129]

Then children are given her, and she asks, “Who hath begotten me these, seeing I have lost my children, and am desolate, a captive?” ([[Isa. 49:21]]). “This imagery of Christ as the Bridegroom and His covenant people as His bride is seen elsewhere in the scriptures.”[@ot-manual-17] The [Isaiah 49](/isaiah/49/) page has the detail.

## Put away, and told to rise

“Where is the bill of your mother’s divorcement, whom I have put away?… for your iniquities have ye sold yourselves, and for your transgressions is your mother put away” ([[Isa. 50:1]]). The Lord “had no creditors; neither had He divorced His ‘wife,’ Israel. Instead, Israel had separated herself from the Lord by her sins.”[@ot-manual-17]

Then she is told, “Shake thyself from the dust; arise, and sit down, O Jerusalem: loose thyself from the bands of thy neck, O captive daughter of Zion” ([[Isa. 52:2]]); “ye shall be redeemed without money” ([[Isa. 52:3]]). Babylon is told: “Come down, and sit in the dust” ([[Isa. 47:1]]). The call to awake is traced in [The Arm of the Lord](/isaiah/themes/arm-of-the-lord/), and the [Isaiah 52](/isaiah/52/) page goes verse by verse.

<h2 id="husband">Thy Maker is thine husband</h2>

“Sing, O barren, thou that didst not bear… for more are the children of the desolate than the children of the married wife” ([[Isa. 54:1]]). “For thy Maker is thine husband; the LORD of hosts is his name; and thy Redeemer the Holy One of Israel” ([[Isa. 54:5]]). She is “a woman forsaken and grieved in spirit” ([[Isa. 54:6]]), yet: “For a small moment have I forsaken thee; but with great mercies will I gather thee” ([[Isa. 54:7]]). “Jesus Christ is the great Redeemer of mankind because He, through His Atonement, paid the price.”[@gs-redeemer]

Jesus quotes the whole chapter to the Nephites, with the verse reading “thy maker, thy husband, the Lord of Hosts is his name; and thy Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel” ([[3 Ne. 22:5]]).[@lds-scriptures] The [Isaiah 54](/isaiah/54/) page goes verse by verse.

<h2 id="married">No more Forsaken</h2>

“Thou shalt no more be termed Forsaken; neither shall thy land any more be termed Desolate: but thou shalt be called Hephzi-bah, and thy land Beulah: for the LORD delighteth in thee, and thy land shall be married” ([[Isa. 62:4]]). “Beulah” means “Married.”[@bd-beulah] “As the bridegroom rejoiceth over the bride, so shall thy God rejoice over thee” ([[Isa. 62:5]]). “Once again Zion shall be married to the Lord.”[@ot-manual-18]

The sources name the woman differently: Jerusalem and by extension the house of Israel,[@sc-knowhy-550] the covenant people as a barren wife,[@ot-manual-17] the Church as Christ’s “symbolic bride,”[@gs-bridegroom] the pure in heart,[@gs-zion] and the land.[@bd-beulah]

The same imagery runs on in later scripture: “the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready” ([[Rev. 19:7]]); “Awake and arise and go forth to meet the Bridegroom” ([[D&C 133:10]]).[@ot-manual-17]
