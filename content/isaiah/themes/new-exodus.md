Isaiah promises a second deliverance, with a highway “like as it was to Israel in the day that he came up out of the land of Egypt” ([[Isa. 11:16]]). Scripture ties two of these passages to Jesus Christ: the Gospels read [[Isa. 40:3]] of John the Baptist, who prepared His way, and the risen Savior quotes [[Isa. 52:11–12]].

## Like the day he came up out of Egypt

“The Lord shall set his hand again the second time to recover the remnant of his people” ([[Isa. 11:11]]); the LORD “shall utterly destroy the tongue of the Egyptian sea” ([[Isa. 11:15]]), to “facilitate the return, as in the days of Moses.”[@lds-scriptures] Then the highway, “like as it was” in the exodus ([[Isa. 11:16]]). Isaiah 35 gives the road a name: “it shall be called The way of holiness; the unclean shall not pass over it… the redeemed shall walk there… the ransomed of the LORD shall return, and come to Zion” ([[Isa. 35:8–10]]). See [Isaiah 11](/isaiah/11/) and [Isaiah 35](/isaiah/35/).

<h2 id="prepare-the-way">Prepare ye the way of the LORD</h2>

“The voice of him that crieth in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the LORD, make straight in the desert a highway for our God” ([[Isa. 40:3]]). Here the road is for God Himself. All four Gospels apply the verse to John the Baptist ([[Matt. 3:3]]; [[Mark 1:3]]; [[Luke 3:4]]; [[John 1:23]]),[@lds-scriptures] who says, “I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness, Make straight the way of the Lord.” Lehi had foretold that one “should go forth and cry in the wilderness: Prepare ye the way of the Lord, and make his paths straight” ([[1 Ne. 10:8]]). John “alone was entrusted with the mission of preparing the way and baptizing the Savior of the world.”[@lds-scriptures] See [Isaiah 40](/isaiah/40/).

## A new thing

The LORD “which maketh a way in the sea” says, “Remember ye not the former things… Behold, I will do a new thing… I will even make a way in the wilderness” ([[Isa. 43:16–19]]). The going out of Babylon is described with water from the rock: “they thirsted not when he led them through the deserts: he caused the waters to flow out of the rock for them” ([[Isa. 48:20–21]]). Isaiah asks of the LORD’s arm, “that hath made the depths of the sea a way for the ransomed to pass over?” ([[Isa. 51:10]]; see [The Arm of the Lord](/isaiah/themes/arm-of-the-lord/)). Paul says of the first exodus that Israel “drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ” ([[1 Cor. 10:4]]).

<h2 id="not-with-haste">Not with haste</h2>

At the first Passover the instruction was to eat “in haste” ([[Ex. 12:11]]). The Hebrew word, *chippazon*, occurs in Isaiah only at [[Isa. 52:12]], where it is negated: “ye shall not go out with haste, nor go by flight: for the LORD will go before you; and the God of Israel will be your rearward.”[@oshb-wlc]

Nephi describes the first exodus as “the Lord their God, their Redeemer, going before them” ([[1 Ne. 17:30]]). The risen Savior quotes Isaiah’s verses ([[3 Ne. 20:41–42]])[@lds-scriptures] and gives the Father’s words: “I will go before them, saith the Father, and I will be their rearward” ([[3 Ne. 21:29]]). In [[D&C 49:27]] the Lord says, “I will go before you and be your rearward.”[@lds-scriptures] See [Isaiah 52](/isaiah/52/).

## The way of the people

“Cast ye up, cast ye up, prepare the way, take up the stumblingblock out of the way of my people” ([[Isa. 57:14]]); “Go through, go through the gates; prepare ye the way of the people; cast up, cast up the highway” ([[Isa. 62:10]]). In latter-day revelation: “an highway shall be cast up in the midst of the great deep” ([[D&C 133:27]]), a way through the sea that recalls the crossing in [[Ex. 14:29]].[@lds-scriptures]
