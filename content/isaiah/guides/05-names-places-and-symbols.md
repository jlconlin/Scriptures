## People

**Ahaz.** King of Judah (c. 735–715 BC). He refused Isaiah’s offer of a sign and trusted Assyria instead of the Lord. → [Isaiah 7](/isaiah/7/)

**Cyrus.** Persian king who conquered Babylon in 539 BC and let the Jews return. Isaiah names him as the Lord’s “shepherd” and “anointed,” making him a type of the Deliverer. → [Isaiah 44](/isaiah/44/), [Isaiah 45](/isaiah/45/)

**Eliakim and Shebna.** Palace officials under Hezekiah. Proud Shebna is replaced by faithful Eliakim, who receives “the key of the house of David.” → [Isaiah 22](/isaiah/22/)

**Hezekiah.** Righteous king of Judah (c. 715–686 BC), Isaiah’s friend, delivered from Assyria and healed of a mortal illness. → [Isaiah 36–39](/isaiah/36/)

**Immanuel.** “God with us.” The sign-child of Isaiah 7:14, fulfilled in Jesus Christ. → [Isaiah 7](/isaiah/7/)

**Isaiah.** “Jehovah is salvation.” Son of Amoz, prophet in Jerusalem c. 740–700 BC. His wife is called “the prophetess”; his sons are Shear-jashub and Maher-shalal-hash-baz. → [Isaiah 1](/isaiah/1/), [Isaiah 8](/isaiah/8/)

**Lucifer.** “Shining one” or “day star.” A taunt against the king of Babylon that Latter-day Saints, with most Christians, also read as describing Satan’s fall. → [Isaiah 14](/isaiah/14/)

**Maher-shalal-hash-baz.** “Quick to the plunder, swift to the spoil,” the name of Isaiah’s second son, a living prophecy that Assyria would soon plunder Damascus and Samaria. → [Isaiah 8](/isaiah/8/)

**Merodach-baladan.** Babylonian king whose envoys Hezekiah foolishly showed his treasures. → [Isaiah 39](/isaiah/39/)

**Rabshakeh.** The Assyrian field commander who taunted Jerusalem from outside its walls. The word is a title, “chief cupbearer,” not a name. → [Isaiah 36](/isaiah/36/)

**Rezin and Pekah** (“the son of Remaliah”). Kings of Syria and Israel who attacked Judah around 735 BC. Isaiah calls them “two tails of these smoking firebrands.” → [Isaiah 7](/isaiah/7/)

**Sennacherib.** King of Assyria (705–681 BC), who invaded Judah in 701 BC. → [Isaiah 36–37](/isaiah/36/)

**The Servant.** Sometimes Israel as a nation, but in the four Servant Songs an individual who redeems Israel, fulfilled in Jesus Christ. → [Isaiah’s Witness of Christ](/isaiah/guides/isaiah-and-christ/)

**Shear-jashub.** “A remnant shall return,” the name of Isaiah’s first son. → [Isaiah 7](/isaiah/7/)

**Uzziah.** King of Judah who died about 740 BC, the year of Isaiah’s temple vision. → [Isaiah 6](/isaiah/6/)

## Places and peoples

**Ariel.** “Lion of God,” or possibly “altar hearth.” A poetic name for Jerusalem. → [Isaiah 29](/isaiah/29/)

**Assyria.** The Mesopotamian empire (capital Nineveh) that destroyed the northern kingdom in 722 BC and invaded Judah in 701 BC. The Lord’s “rod” of discipline, later judged for its arrogance. → [Isaiah 10](/isaiah/10/)

**Babylon.** Historically, the empire that destroyed Jerusalem in 587 BC. Symbolically, the proud, wicked world in any age ([[D&C 1:16]]; [[D&C 133:14]]). → [Isaiah 13–14](/isaiah/13/), [Isaiah 47](/isaiah/47/)

**Bashan.** Fertile highlands east of the Sea of Galilee, famous for great oaks and fat cattle, and a symbol of pride and strength. → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/)

**Bozrah.** A chief city of Edom, and the place the Lord comes from in red garments. → [Isaiah 63](/isaiah/63/)

**Daughter of Zion.** A poetic personification of Jerusalem and its people. → [Isaiah 1](/isaiah/1/), [Isaiah 52](/isaiah/52/)

**Edom / Idumea.** Esau’s descendants, south of the Dead Sea. In Isaiah and the D&C, a symbol of the world under judgment ([[D&C 1:36]]). → [Isaiah 34](/isaiah/34/), [Isaiah 63](/isaiah/63/)

**Egypt.** The great power on the Nile, which Judah was always tempted to trust for military help. → [Isaiah 19](/isaiah/19/), [Isaiah 30–31](/isaiah/30/)

**Ephraim.** The leading tribe of the northern kingdom, and so a name for that kingdom as a whole. In the latter days, Ephraim has a leading role in the gathering ([[D&C 133:32–34]]). → [Isaiah 7](/isaiah/7/), [Isaiah 28](/isaiah/28/)

**Jacob / Israel.** Names for the whole covenant people, descended from the patriarch Jacob, whom God renamed Israel. → [Isaiah 40–49](/isaiah/40/)

**Lebanon.** The mountain range north of Israel, famous for towering cedars, and a symbol of pride and grandeur. → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 10](/isaiah/10/)

**Moab.** A kingdom east of the Dead Sea, descended from Lot. → [Isaiah 15–16](/isaiah/15/)

**Sharon and Carmel.** Lush coastal plain and forested mountain, and symbols of fertility and beauty. → [Isaiah 35](/isaiah/35/)

**Sodom and Gomorrah.** The cities destroyed in Abraham’s day, a byword for wickedness and for total destruction. → [Isaiah 1](/isaiah/1/)

**Tarshish.** A distant port, probably in the western Mediterranean. “Ships of Tarshish” are great merchant ships, symbols of trade and wealth. → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 23](/isaiah/23/), [Isaiah 60](/isaiah/60/)

**Tyre.** A wealthy Phoenician port city, and a symbol of commercial pride. → [Isaiah 23](/isaiah/23/)

**Zion.** Originally the hill of Jerusalem where the temple stood; then Jerusalem itself; and ultimately the Lord’s pure people and the place they gather ([[Moses 7:18]]; [[D&C 97:21]]). → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 52](/isaiah/52/), [Isaiah 60](/isaiah/60/)

## Images and symbols

**Arm of the Lord.** His power in action, especially to save. “The LORD hath made bare his holy arm” ([[Isa. 52:10]]). → [Isaiah 53](/isaiah/53/)

**Blind and deaf.** Spiritual insensitivity: people who see but do not perceive. → [Isaiah 6](/isaiah/6/), [Isaiah 42](/isaiah/42/)

**Branch.** A shoot from a stump, a messianic title for the Davidic King. → [Isaiah 4](/isaiah/4/), [Isaiah 11](/isaiah/11/)

**Burden.** Hebrew *massaʾ*, a prophetic oracle, often of judgment, “lifted up” against a nation. → [Isaiah 13](/isaiah/13/)

**Cup of trembling / cup of fury.** The Lord’s judgment pictured as a drink that makes the drinker stagger. → [Isaiah 51](/isaiah/51/)

**Drunkenness.** Spiritual stupor, as well as literal debauchery. → [Isaiah 28–29](/isaiah/28/)

**Ensign.** A banner on a hill that signals where to gather. In the latter days, the restored gospel and the Church ([[D&C 45:9]]; [[D&C 64:42]]). → [Isaiah 5](/isaiah/5/), [Isaiah 11](/isaiah/11/)

**Garments.** Salvation, righteousness, and holiness put on like clothing. → [Isaiah 52](/isaiah/52/), [Isaiah 61](/isaiah/61/)

**Highway.** A raised road through the wilderness for the returning exiles, and the path of holiness home to God. → [Isaiah 35](/isaiah/35/), [Isaiah 40](/isaiah/40/)

**Hand stretched out still.** A refrain of continuing judgment: “his hand is stretched out still.” Read in context, it also holds out mercy to all who will turn ([[2 Ne. 28:32]]; [[Jacob 6:4]]). → [Isaiah 9](/isaiah/9/)

**Mountain.** The temple, the place where heaven and earth meet. → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 25](/isaiah/25/)

**Remnant.** The survivors of judgment, from whom the Lord rebuilds His people. → [Isaiah 1](/isaiah/1/), [Isaiah 10](/isaiah/10/)

**Rock.** The Lord as a sure foundation and refuge. → [Isaiah 26](/isaiah/26/), [Isaiah 28](/isaiah/28/)

**Stakes and cords.** The pegs and ropes of a tent, and the source of the Church’s term “stake.” → [Isaiah 33](/isaiah/33/), [Isaiah 54](/isaiah/54/)

**Stone of stumbling.** The Lord Himself is a sanctuary to those who trust Him and a stumbling block to those who don’t. → [Isaiah 8](/isaiah/8/)

**Tall trees** (cedars, oaks). Proud people and nations who will be felled. → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 10](/isaiah/10/)

**“That day.”** The day of the Lord, a time of judgment and deliverance, often in the last days. → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 4](/isaiah/4/)

**Vineyard.** Israel, planted and tended by the Lord. Compare Zenos’s allegory of the olive tree in [[Jacob 5]]. → [Isaiah 5](/isaiah/5/), [Isaiah 27](/isaiah/27/)

**Water in the desert.** Renewal, the Spirit, and the Lord’s provision. → [Isaiah 35](/isaiah/35/), [Isaiah 41](/isaiah/41/), [Isaiah 44](/isaiah/44/), [Isaiah 55](/isaiah/55/)

**Watchmen.** Prophets and leaders who warn the people. → [Isaiah 21](/isaiah/21/), [Isaiah 52](/isaiah/52/), [Isaiah 56](/isaiah/56/), [Isaiah 62](/isaiah/62/)

**Winepress.** Treading grapes, a picture of judgment and of the Savior’s atoning suffering. → [Isaiah 63](/isaiah/63/)

**Wings / eagle.** Renewed strength that comes from waiting on the Lord. → [Isaiah 40](/isaiah/40/)
