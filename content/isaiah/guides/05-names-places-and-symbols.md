Use this page as a lookup table while you read. Most entries point to the chapter where the name or image is explained in depth. The site search is also a good way to find every place a word appears.

## People

**Ahaz.** King of Judah. He refused Isaiah’s offer of a sign ([[Isa. 7:12]]) and allied himself with Tiglath-pileser, king of Assyria, against Syria and Israel.[@bd-ahaz] → [Isaiah 7](/isaiah/7/)

**Cyrus.** Persian king who captured Babylon and issued a decree allowing the Jews to return.[@bd-cyrus] Isaiah names him as the Lord’s “shepherd” and “anointed” ([[Isa. 44:28]]; [[Isa. 45:1]]). He was “a redeemer to Israel.”[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 102] → [Isaiah 44](/isaiah/44/), [Isaiah 45](/isaiah/45/)

**Eliakim and Shebna.** Palace officials under Hezekiah ([[Isa. 36:3]]); Eliakim was master of Hezekiah’s household.[@bd-eliakim] Shebna is replaced by Eliakim, who receives “the key of the house of David” ([[Isa. 22:19–22]]). → [Isaiah 22](/isaiah/22/)

**Hezekiah.** King of Judah and a great religious reformer, who had the help of Isaiah.[@bd-hezekiah] He was delivered from Assyria ([[Isa. 37:36]]) and healed of a mortal illness ([[Isa. 38]]). → [Isaiah 36–39](/isaiah/36/)

**Immanuel.** “God with us.” A name-title given in Isaiah 7:14 as a sign of God’s deliverance, which Matthew identifies as fulfilled in the birth of Jesus.[@bd-immanuel] → [Isaiah 7](/isaiah/7/)

**Isaiah.** “The Lord is salvation.” Son of Amoz, prophet in Jerusalem 740–701 BC.[@bd-isaiah] His wife is called “the prophetess”;[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 414] his sons are Shear-jashub and Maher-shalal-hash-baz. → [Isaiah 1](/isaiah/1/), [Isaiah 8](/isaiah/8/)

**Lucifer.** “The Shining One,” also “Lightbringer” or “Son of the Morning.” The name appears only once in the Bible ([[Isa. 14:12]]), in a taunt against the king of Babylon ([[Isa. 14:4]]). Latter-day revelation equates Lucifer with Satan,[@bd-lucifer] and the *Old Testament Student Manual* sees these chapters as reaching also to the premortal overthrow of Lucifer.[@ot-manual-e] → [Isaiah 14](/isaiah/14/)

**Maher-shalal-hash-baz.** “Speedy-spoil-quick-booty,” the name of Isaiah’s son,[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 413] a living prophecy that Assyria would soon plunder Damascus and Samaria ([[Isa. 8:4]]). → [Isaiah 8](/isaiah/8/)

**Merodach-baladan.** King of Babylon in the days of Hezekiah,[@bd-merodach-baladan] whose envoys Hezekiah showed his treasures ([[Isa. 39:1–2]]). → [Isaiah 39](/isaiah/39/)

**Rabshakeh.** The Assyrian envoy whom Sennacherib sent from Lachish with a great army to demand Hezekiah’s surrender ([[Isa. 36:2]]).[@bd-sennacherib] → [Isaiah 36](/isaiah/36/)

**Rezin and Pekah** (“the son of Remaliah”). Kings of Syria and Israel, united against Judah.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 414] Isaiah calls them “two tails of these smoking firebrands.” → [Isaiah 7](/isaiah/7/)

**Sennacherib.** King of Assyria, 705–681 BC, who invaded Judah and took some of its fenced cities.[@bd-sennacherib] Jerusalem was besieged in 701 BC.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 198] → [Isaiah 36–37](/isaiah/36/)

**The Servant.** Sometimes Israel as a nation ([[Isa. 41:8]]); in the four Servant Songs, a figure whom Latter-day Saints read as Jesus Christ, as Abinadi does in applying Isaiah 53 to the Lord who comes down among His people ([[Mosiah 15:1]]).[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 302] → [Isaiah’s Witness of Christ](/isaiah/guides/isaiah-and-christ/)

**Shear-jashub.** “A remnant shall return,” the name of one of Isaiah’s sons.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 414] → [Isaiah 7](/isaiah/7/)

**Uzziah.** King of Judah.[@bd-uzziah] Isaiah’s temple vision came “in the year that king Uzziah died” ([[Isa. 6:1]]). → [Isaiah 6](/isaiah/6/)

## Places and peoples

**Ariel.** “Altar of God,”[@bd-ariel] or “hearth of God.”[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 418] A name for Jerusalem. → [Isaiah 29](/isaiah/29/)

**Assyria.** The eastern empire whose center lay on the Tigris, where Nineveh stood.[@bd-assyria] It destroyed and exiled the northern kingdom in 722 BC[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 155] and besieged Jerusalem in 701 BC.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 198] The Lord’s “rod” of discipline ([[Isa. 10:5]]), later judged for its arrogance ([[Isa. 10:12]]). → [Isaiah 10](/isaiah/10/)

**Babylon.** Historically, the empire that destroyed Jerusalem in 586 BC.[@bd-captivities] Symbolically, “the world” ([[D&C 1:16]]),[@bd-babylon] the wickedness of the world and the dominions of Satan in it.[@ot-manual-e] Compare [[D&C 133:14]]. → [Isaiah 13–14](/isaiah/13/), [Isaiah 47](/isaiah/47/)

**Bashan.** The district east of the Jordan and north of Gilead, rich in grain and known for its cattle,[@bd-bashan] and, with Lebanon, a symbol of pride and haughtiness.[@ot-manual-e] Isaiah names its oaks ([[Isa. 2:13]]). → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/)

**Bozrah.** A city of Edom, and the place the Lord comes from in dyed garments ([[Isa. 63:1]]). → [Isaiah 63](/isaiah/63/)

**Daughter of Zion.** One of the terms Isaiah uses for Jerusalem.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 236] → [Isaiah 1](/isaiah/1/), [Isaiah 52](/isaiah/52/)

**Edom / Idumea.** Esau’s descendants, in the country south of the Dead Sea.[@bd-edom] Idumea is the same name; in the D&C it stands for the world ([[D&C 1:36]]).[@bd-idumea] Isaiah’s Edom likewise typifies worldliness.[@ot-manual-e] → [Isaiah 34](/isaiah/34/), [Isaiah 63](/isaiah/63/)

**Egypt.** The rich and prosperous kingdom of the Pharaohs,[@bd-egypt] which Judah looked to for help ([[Isa. 31:1]]) and which Isaiah also uses as a symbol of wickedness.[@ot-manual-e] → [Isaiah 19](/isaiah/19/), [Isaiah 30–31](/isaiah/30/)

**Ephraim.** The leading tribe of the Northern Kingdom, and so a name for that kingdom as a whole.[@ot-manual-e] In the latter days, Ephraim has a leading role in the gathering ([[D&C 133:32–34]]).[@bd-ephraim] → [Isaiah 7](/isaiah/7/), [Isaiah 28](/isaiah/28/)

**Jacob / Israel.** Names for the whole covenant people, descended from the patriarch Jacob, whom God renamed Israel. → [Isaiah 40–49](/isaiah/40/)

**Lebanon.** A long range of limestone hills in the far north of Palestine, once heavily forested with cedars,[@bd-lebanon] and, with Bashan, a symbol of pride and haughtiness.[@ot-manual-e] → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 10](/isaiah/10/)

**Moab.** The land east of the Dead Sea, named for a son of Lot.[@bd-moab] → [Isaiah 15–16](/isaiah/15/)

**Sharon and Carmel.** The coastal plain “celebrated for its beauty and its flowers,” extending from Carmel to Joppa.[@bd-sharon] Isaiah names them together ([[Isa. 35:2]]). → [Isaiah 35](/isaiah/35/)

**Sodom and Gomorrah.** The cities destroyed in Abraham’s day ([[Gen. 19]]), which Isaiah uses to represent unrighteousness.[@ot-manual-e] → [Isaiah 1](/isaiah/1/)

**Tarshish.** Probably Tartessus in Spain; “ships of Tarshish” came to mean the largest ships, suited to long voyages.[@bd-tarshish] Tarshish and its ships can symbolize the materialism and worldliness of the last days.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 61] → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 23](/isaiah/23/), [Isaiah 60](/isaiah/60/)

**Tyre.** An important commercial seaport,[@bd-tyre] whose fall Isaiah says is meant to “stain the pride of all glory” ([[Isa. 23:9]]). → [Isaiah 23](/isaiah/23/)

**Zion.** Jerusalem, which Judah’s historians called Zion;[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 236] and, in latter-day revelation, the Lord’s pure people and the places they gather ([[Moses 7:18]]; [[D&C 97:21]]).[@ot-manual-e] → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 52](/isaiah/52/), [Isaiah 60](/isaiah/60/)

## Images and symbols

**Arm of the Lord.** His power in action. “The LORD hath made bare his holy arm” ([[Isa. 52:10]]); “Made bare” means to “show strength.”[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 421] → [Isaiah 53](/isaiah/53/)

**Blind and deaf.** Spiritual blindness and insensitivity: people who see but do not perceive ([[Isa. 6:9]]).[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 418] → [Isaiah 6](/isaiah/6/), [Isaiah 42](/isaiah/42/)

**Branch.** A shoot from a stump ([[Isa. 11:1]]),[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 13] a messianic title for the Davidic King; Elder McConkie taught that the Branch who grew out of the house of David is Christ.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 13] → [Isaiah 4](/isaiah/4/), [Isaiah 11](/isaiah/11/)

**Burden.** Hebrew *massaʾ*, a word that means a load and also a prophetic utterance, or oracle.[@bdb, *massaʾ*] Isaiah uses it for his oracles on Babylon, Moab, Damascus, Egypt, Tyre, and others.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 72] → [Isaiah 13](/isaiah/13/)

**Cup of trembling / cup of fury.** The Lord’s judgment pictured as a drink that makes the drinker stagger ([[Isa. 51:17]]). → [Isaiah 51](/isaiah/51/)

**Drunkenness.** Spiritual stupor, as well as literal drunkenness from strong drink: Isaiah 28 condemns the latter, while Isaiah 29 pictures a “wineless drunkenness.”[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 236] → [Isaiah 28–29](/isaiah/28/)

**Ensign.** A flag or standard around which people gather, a rallying point.[@gs-ensign] The Book of Mormon and the Church are symbolic ensigns to all nations.[@gs-ensign] See also [[D&C 45:9]]; [[D&C 64:42]]. → [Isaiah 5](/isaiah/5/), [Isaiah 11](/isaiah/11/)

**Garments.** Salvation and righteousness put on like clothing ([[Isa. 61:10]]); Zion is told to “put on thy beautiful garments” ([[Isa. 52:1]]). → [Isaiah 52](/isaiah/52/), [Isaiah 61](/isaiah/61/)

**Highway.** A road through the wilderness ([[Isa. 40:3]]), called “The way of holiness” ([[Isa. 35:8]]). → [Isaiah 35](/isaiah/35/), [Isaiah 40](/isaiah/40/)

**Hand stretched out still.** A refrain of continuing judgment: “his hand is stretched out still.” The stretched-out hand is a threatening, punishing gesture; the Book of Mormon pictures mercy with a different idiom, the Lord’s arm “lengthened out” and “extended” toward all who will turn ([[2 Ne. 28:32]]; [[Jacob 6:5]]).[@gee-hand-lord] → [Isaiah 9](/isaiah/9/)

**Mountain.** The temple: “the mountain of the Lord’s house” ([[Isa. 2:2]]).[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 409] President Harold B. Lee said the phrase refers both to a place and to a righteous people.[@ot-manual-e] → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 25](/isaiah/25/)

**Remnant.** The portion that remains after judgment.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 415] The remnant is summoned and gathered again in the day of the Gentiles.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 36] → [Isaiah 1](/isaiah/1/), [Isaiah 10](/isaiah/10/)

**Rock.** The Lord as a sure foundation ([[Isa. 28:16]]) and “everlasting strength” ([[Isa. 26:4]]). → [Isaiah 26](/isaiah/26/), [Isaiah 28](/isaiah/28/)

**Stakes and cords.** The pegs and ropes of a tent ([[Isa. 54:2]]), the image behind the Church’s term “stake.”[@gs-stake] → [Isaiah 33](/isaiah/33/), [Isaiah 54](/isaiah/54/)

**Stone of stumbling.** The Lord is a “sanctuary” for some, but a “stone of stumbling” for others.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 35] → [Isaiah 8](/isaiah/8/)

**Tall trees** (cedars, oaks). Lebanon and Bashan, with their lofty cedars and oaks, connote pride and haughtiness.[@ot-manual-e] → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 10](/isaiah/10/)

**“That day.”** The “day of the Lord” ([[Isa. 2:12]]), often pointing to the last days.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 61] → [Isaiah 2](/isaiah/2/), [Isaiah 4](/isaiah/4/)

**Vineyard.** Israel, planted and tended by the Lord ([[Isa. 5:7]]). Compare Zenos’s allegory of the olive tree in [[Jacob 5]]. → [Isaiah 5](/isaiah/5/), [Isaiah 27](/isaiah/27/)

**Water in the desert.** The Lord’s provision for the thirsty, joined to the pouring out of His Spirit ([[Isa. 44:3]]). → [Isaiah 35](/isaiah/35/), [Isaiah 41](/isaiah/41/), [Isaiah 44](/isaiah/44/), [Isaiah 55](/isaiah/55/)

**Watchmen.** Sentinels on a city wall; in [[Isa. 52:8]] they are the first to see the messenger arrive, and they sing.[@isaiah-in-bom, p. 254] → [Isaiah 21](/isaiah/21/), [Isaiah 52](/isaiah/52/), [Isaiah 56](/isaiah/56/), [Isaiah 62](/isaiah/62/)

**Winepress.** Treading grapes, a picture of judgment ([[Isa. 63:3]]); compare [[D&C 76:107]]. → [Isaiah 63](/isaiah/63/)

**Wings / eagle.** Renewed strength that comes from waiting on the Lord. → [Isaiah 40](/isaiah/40/)
