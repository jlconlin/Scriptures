# Abraham: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). The author selected two on 2026-10-07. The reports below remain research leads, not additional assignments.

## Selected for development (author, 2026-10-07)

The author authorized development on 2026-10-08, and both pages are written (`content/abraham/themes/fathers-blessing.md`, `council-to-creation.md`). Under `STANDARDS.md` §8, a page may make its own connection between passages, but factual and doctrinal claims still need sources. The two pages divide at chapter 3: one follows Abraham's calling outward to the families of the earth; the other follows the premortal council into the Creation.

1. **The fathers' blessing reaches all families.** Abraham seeks the fathers' blessings and their priesthood (1:2–4), is delivered by Jehovah and charged to bear His name (1:15–19), and receives a covenant whose blessings reach his seed and those who receive the gospel, “all the families of the earth” (2:6–11). The movement is from a seeker in danger to a commissioned bearer of a blessing beyond his own family; Christ is Jehovah (1:16; 2:8), and the covenant's gospel and heirs are illuminated by Gal. 3:8, 29. Include Jehovah's deliverance and sending as the beginning of this page, **not a separate page**. Link to the detailed priesthood and covenant notes in chapters 1–2, and to Genesis's covenant themes where they bear on the promise; the Abraham page should contribute what this book says in its own words.
2. **From council to creation.** The council proposes an earth where spirits can dwell and be proved, and the Lord sends the first (3:22–28); the Gods go down, organize and form the earth (4:1, 26–31), then rest and bring to pass what they counseled (5:2–5). This cross-chapter passage from intention to execution is the page's arc, with Christ's role stated only as the Church's sources and the chapter notes identify it. Link to Genesis and Moses for shared Creation details rather than repeating them. Where Abraham 3:27–28 is read alongside Moses 4:1–4, preserve the author's rule recorded in `content/moses/THEME-CANDIDATES.md`: the Father presented one plan of salvation; Satan did not present a competing plan and there was no vote between plans. A possible comparison of the elements' obedience (4:10, 12, 18, 21, 25, 31) with the proving of spirits (3:25) was reported by chapter 4, but **was not selected as a separate page** and must not equate ordered elements with moral agents.

**Not selected as separate pages:** Jehovah delivers then sends Abraham (included in 1); the planned earth and the proving of its inhabitants (material to consider cautiously within 2). The other reports below have not been chosen for pages. The author found two enough for this short book.
