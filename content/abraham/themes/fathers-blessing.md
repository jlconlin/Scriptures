Abraham begins by seeking “the blessings of the fathers” in a house that has turned to other gods. In Haran he hears that “all the families of the earth” will be blessed through him. The one who says “my name is Jehovah” at the altar says it again when He sends him out.

## Sought, and in danger

Abraham “sought for the blessings of the fathers, and the right whereunto I should be ordained to administer the same,” wishing to be “a father of many nations, a prince of peace” ([[Abr. 1:2]]); he “sought for mine appointment unto the Priesthood” ([[Abr. 1:4]]). His fathers “utterly refused to hearken to my voice” ([[Abr. 1:5]]). On the altar, “I lifted up my voice unto the Lord my God” ([[Abr. 1:15]]).

## “My name is Jehovah”

“The angel of his presence stood by me, and immediately unloosed my bands” ([[Abr. 1:15]]). Then the voice: “Abraham, Abraham, behold, my name is Jehovah, and I have heard thee, and have come down to deliver thee, and to take thee away from thy father’s house” ([[Abr. 1:16]]). He adds: “I will take thee, to put upon thee my name, even the Priesthood of thy father, and my power shall be over thee… through thy ministry my name shall be known in the earth forever” ([[Abr. 1:18–19]]). What Abraham sought in 1:2–4 the Lord here promises; the note on [[Abr. 1:2]] has when and from whom he received it ([[D&C 84:14]]).

## Sent from Haran

In Haran the Lord appears again: “I have purposed to take thee away out of Haran, and to make of thee a minister to bear my name in a strange land” ([[Abr. 2:6]]). And: “My name is Jehovah, and I know the end from the beginning; therefore my hand shall be over thee” ([[Abr. 2:8]]). “Jehovah” stands in the book only at 1:16 and 2:8:[@lds-scriptures] the one who came down to deliver is the one who sends. See the notes on [[Abr. 2:6]] and [[Abr. 2:8]].

## All the families of the earth

“Thou shalt be a blessing unto thy seed after thee, that in their hands they shall bear this ministry and Priesthood unto all nations” ([[Abr. 2:9]]). “As many as receive this Gospel shall be called after thy name, and shall be accounted thy seed, and shall rise up and bless thee, as their father” ([[Abr. 2:10]]). “In thee… and in thy seed… shall all the families of the earth be blessed, even with the blessings of the Gospel, which are the blessings of salvation, even of life eternal” ([[Abr. 2:11]]).

“Gospel” (2:10, 11), “families” (2:11) and “all nations” (2:9, twice) are in the book only here; “nations” stands once before, in Abraham’s wish to be “a father of many nations” ([[Abr. 1:2]]).[@lds-scriptures] Genesis has “in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed” ([[Gen. 12:3]]);[@lds-scriptures] here the Lord says what the blessing is, that “this right shall continue in thee, and in thy seed after thee” ([[Abr. 2:11]]), the “right” Abraham sought in 1:2, and that those who receive the gospel are counted his seed beside “the literal seed.” See the notes on [[Abr. 2:10]] and [[Abr. 2:11]]. See [The Covenant with Abraham](/genesis/themes/covenant-with-abraham/) and [The Covenant Blesses Those Outside the Family](/genesis/themes/blessing-outsiders/).

## “Now I have found thee”

Abraham says in his heart, “Thy servant has sought thee earnestly; now I have found thee,” and then, “Thou didst send thine angel to deliver me… and I will do well to hearken unto thy voice” ([[Abr. 2:12–13]]).

## Words chapter 2 takes up

Chapter 2 takes up words of chapter 1:

- “my name”: “put upon thee my name” ([[Abr. 1:18]]), “my name shall be known” ([[Abr. 1:19]]), “bear my name” ([[Abr. 2:6]]);
- “my power shall be over thee” ([[Abr. 1:18]]) and “my hand shall be over thee” ([[Abr. 2:8]]).

For “hearken,” see the thread of [chapter 2](/abraham/2/).

## A comparison: “sought” and “father”

Set beside each other, “I sought for the blessings of the fathers” ([[Abr. 1:2]]) and “Thy servant has sought thee earnestly; now I have found thee” ([[Abr. 2:12]]) share a verb, and “a father of many nations” ([[Abr. 1:2]]) and “bless thee, as their father” ([[Abr. 2:10]]) share a noun. The objects differ: in 1:2 he seeks blessings and a right; in 2:12 it is the Lord he has sought and found. This comparison is the page’s own; no source cited here draws it, and it says nothing of what Abraham intended. Its limits: “sought” is at 1:2, 1:4 and 2:12 only, but Pharaoh too is “seeking earnestly to imitate that order established by the fathers” ([[Abr. 1:26]]). “Fathers” is in chapter 1 only (1:2–5, 26, 31; the Church’s current text has it at 1:1 as well) and includes those who “turned from their righteousness” ([[Abr. 1:5]]). “Father” is in fifteen verses: Abraham’s own father in nine, Adam (1:3), Noah (1:26), “the Priesthood of thy father” (1:18) and 5:18; of Abraham himself only at 1:2 and 2:10.[@lds-scriptures]

## Jehovah, and the seed

Jehovah is the premortal Jesus Christ[@bd-jehovah] (see the note on [[Abr. 1:16]]). “Jesus Christ—Jehovah of the Old Testament—made remarkable promises to Abraham.”[@nelson-teachings-abrahamic-covenant] Of the seed, Paul writes: “as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ… if ye be Christ’s, then are ye Abraham’s seed, and heirs according to the promise” ([[Gal. 3:27, 29]]), which the footnotes on 2:10 cite with [[Gal. 3:8]].[@lds-scriptures] To carry it “unto all nations” is the charge to take the gospel of Jesus Christ to everyone and offer its saving ordinances by priesthood authority.[@bednar-becoming-missionary]
