# 2 Samuel: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.** The candidates were Fable's and Codex's lists (`.cache/batch/2-samuel/reports/themes-fable.md` and `themes-codex.md`), shown to the author combined with 1 Samuel's on 2026-11-05; only the author's choice is recorded here.

## Author's selection (2026-11-05)

**One page: “Would God I Had Died for Thee”** (from [[2 Sam. 18:33]]). The author: “As far as themes, I like this: Would God I Had Died for Thee. The others don't seem to feel like a real connection; it seems like they are too disconnected.”

**The arc.** David's men guard his life as worth “ten thousand of us” (18:3; 21:17). Then the king tries three times, each more strongly, to spend that life for another and is not allowed to: he beseeches God for the dying child (12:16); he wishes he had died instead of Absalom (18:33, *taḥat*, “in the place of”); he asks that the LORD's hand fall on him rather than on “these sheep” (24:17). What stays the plague is not David's life but a sacrifice that costs him something (24:24), on the floor Chronicles calls Mount Moriah (2 Chr. 3:1), where a ram was offered “in the stead of” a son (Gen. 22:13). **To Christ:** the Good Shepherd who gives His life for the sheep (John 10:11) does what the best of Israel's kings wished and could not do; “while we were yet sinners” (Rom. 5:8) answers Joab's “thou lovest thine enemies” (19:6); no mortal's blood can atone (Alma 34:9–14).

**Must not claim:** that the child's death was a substitution (Ezek. 18:20; *Scripture Helps* at 12:23); a reason the text does not give for refusing David's offer in 24:17; that Absalom is a type of anything; that the two altars stood on one spot (the Gen. 22:2 note gives the Moriah tradition as tradition); anything of David's eternal state beyond the 12:13 note. The connections are the page's own and are said so.

**Not chosen:** “My Mercy Shall Not Depart” (7:14–15, the chastening and the mercy kept with David), the two threshing floors, and the rest of both lists.
