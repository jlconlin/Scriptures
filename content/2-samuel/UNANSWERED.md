# 2 Samuel: reader questions no source answered

Questions a reader would ask that no source read answered, by chapter: a list for a later deepening pass. Kept out of `BRIEF.md` so the brief stays brief.

- **1:** 2 Sam. 1:26: what David meant by comparing Jonathan's love with 'the love of women'. No faithful source read explains the comparison, so the note gives David's words and the covenant only.
- **4:** Why did the Beerothites flee to Gittaim (4:3)? The only answer found is an older commentary's guess; Why are the murder and its details told twice in 4:6–7? The note gives only the Septuagint's different telling, and no Latter-day Saint source explains it.
- **5:** Which 'hold' David went down to in 5:17 (only Keil identifies it); Whether the forty years of 5:4 is a round number, and why 5:4–5 is missing from 4QSam-a (Correa only; the writer cut it as peripheral).
- **6:** What Nachon's threshingfloor was (6:6); The fir-wood instruments of 6:5.
- **10:** What kindness had Nahash shown David (10:2)? Scripture does not say, and Keil only guesses.
- **11:** Why does 1 Chr. 20:1 tell of the siege of Rabbah but leave out the Bath-sheba story? (The writer found no source that answers it.).
- **12:** v. 30: how a crown could weigh a talent, and whether it was Milcom's (only an older commentary takes it up; left to the plain paraphrase); v. 14: what 'given great occasion to the enemies of the LORD to blaspheme' means beyond the text's own clause.
- **14:** 14:14: no Latter-day Saint source was found that reads the woman's words about God devising means for the banished as pointing to Christ or the Atonement. The writer reports that Stirling's essay in Tender Mercies, which may quote the verse, can't be read online; Why Joab worked for Absalom's return: only Keil offers a motive (that Absalom was the likely heir), and the chapter no longer gives it.
- **16:** Where David rested in 16:14: the Hebrew names no place. The writer decided this was a curiosity and gave it no note.
- **17:** 17:20: was the woman of Bahurim's deception approved? Only the Cambridge Bible discusses it; 17:25: how 'Abigail the daughter of Nahash' fits with Chronicles' Abigail as a sister of Jesse's sons; only Keil and Cambridge address it.
- **18:** Where was the wood of Ephraim, east or west of the Jordan (footnote 6a points to Josh. 17)? Only Cambridge answers; the writer cut it to limit the older commentaries; Three darts through the heart, yet ten men had to finish Absalom: only Cambridge and Keil answer (the darts were staves), and the writer cut it; Is 'would God I had died for thee' a type of Christ dying for sinners? No readable Latter-day Saint source draws the connection (Stirling's chapter can't be read online).
- **19:** Did David really forgive Shimei (19:23), given his charge to Solomon (1 Kgs. 2:8–9)? Only Keil answers, with a moral verdict (“only a sign of David's weakness”); the note sets the two passages side by side (cut 2026-11-04)
- **20:** 20:14: Who were the Berites? Only the older commentaries address it, and they say the text is uncertain; 20:5: Why did Amasa take longer than the time David set? Only the older commentaries' guesses.
- **21:** Why the Gibeonites chose Gibeah and the number seven: only Keil speaks to it, and as an outside commentary it was left out.
- **23:** What are the 'lionlike men of Moab' (23:20, Hebrew ariel)? Only Cambridge answers it, and the writer cut it to stay within the limit on older commentaries; How are these David's 'last words' when 1 Kgs. 2:1–9 gives a later charge to Solomon? No Church or BYU source read answers it.
- **24:** Why the totals differ between 2 Sam. 24:9 and 1 Chr. 21:5: the only explanation the chapter can give comes from a manual. No Latter-day Saint source beyond the Church's own was found.
