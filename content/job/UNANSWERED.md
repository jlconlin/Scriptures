# Job: reader questions no source answered

Questions a reader would ask that no source read answered, by chapter: a list for a later deepening pass. Kept out of `BRIEF.md` so the brief stays brief.

- **2:** Why Satan disappears from the book after chapter 2: the text only shows that he does (the writer's report); What the footnote's pairing of the seven days with Gen. 50:10 shows: no source read explains it.
- **3:** 3:8: why the King James renders liwyātān as 'their mourning'; no source read explains it; 3:25: whether Job's fear in some way brought on his suffering; no source addresses it.
- **6:** Who and where are Tema and Sheba (6:19)? The writer found no source read that places Tema; What does 'dig a pit for your friend' / 'overwhelm the fatherless' (6:27) mean, given the modern 'cast lots… bargain over your friend'? No note covers it; What does 'ye are nothing' (6:21) mean, where the written and read Hebrew texts differ?.
- **7:** Do the dreams and visions that terrify Job (7:14) answer Eliphaz's night vision (4:12)? No source makes the link; Is 'my soul chooseth strangling' (7:15) a thought of suicide? The chapter leaves it to the plain-words paraphrase.
- **8:** 8:9 'our days upon earth are a shadow': no source read connects it to 1 Chr. 29:15 or Ps. 144:4; 8:16–19: whether the second plant is the wicked man or the righteous one restored; the Hebrew of 17 and 19 is uncertain, and no Latter-day Saint source treats it.
- **9:** What are Arcturus, Orion, Pleiades and 'the chambers of the south' (9:9)? The writer left this out as a curiosity; Amos 5:8 on the site names the stars.
- **10:** Job 10:22: what 'without any order' means, and why Job pictures death as disorder. The writer left the rare Hebrew word out under the brief's rule, and the note gives only a modern translation's 'chaos'; Job 10:16: what the lion image means. The text is covered only in a clause.
- **11:** Does the LORD's speech from the whirlwind (38–41) answer or confirm Zophar's 'Canst thou by searching find out God?' (11:7)? No source read makes the link; What 'gather together' (11:10) means: only Delitzsch reads it as a court assembly, and the writer judged it peripheral.
- **14:** 14:4 'Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean?': does Job mean that people are born sinful, and how does this sit with Moses 6:54 and Article of Faith 2? No Latter-day Saint source read takes up the verse; only the thread gives Delitzsch's sense, labelled as one older reading; 14:1–2: what does 'born of a woman', the flower and the shadow signify? There is no note. Footnote 2a (Isa. 38:12) is beside the verse, and no source was found that goes further.
- **15:** 15:22: does 'return out of darkness' mean resurrection, as the chapter heading reads it? No source the writer read takes up the heading's reading; Delitzsch and the modern translation read it as hope of rescue; 15:34: does 'tabernacles of bribery' hint at particular charges against Job? No source read says so.
- **17:** What "My breath is corrupt" (17:1) means is left to footnote 1a; no source went further.
- **18:** 18:20: whether 'they that come after him… they that went before' means later and earlier generations, or west and east as a modern translation renders it. The writer left it in the King James sense with no note.
- **19:** 19:20, 'the skin of my teeth': only Delitzsch discusses it, and he only guesses at a medical meaning. No note was written.
- **20:** What do the 'brooks of honey and butter' (20:17) mean? The writer found only Delitzsch's link to 'milk and honey' and gave it no note; What does 'his bones are full of the sin of his youth' (20:11) mean, given that 'sin' is in italics? It is handled in the plain paraphrase only.
- **22:** Where is Ophir (22:24)? The writer judged this a curiosity, and no note covers it; What flood is meant in 22:16? Only Delitzsch says Noah's flood, and that was cut.
- **23:** 23:2 "my stroke is heavier than my groaning": the Hebrew says "my hand" and the Greek and Syriac say "his hand" (NRSVUE footnote; Delitzsch). No Latter-day Saint source explains the line, so it has no note; the plain-words section keeps the King James sense.
