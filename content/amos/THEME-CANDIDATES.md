# Amos: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.** With nine chapters, a topic that lives in one chapter belongs in that chapter's notes.

## Author's selection (2026-10-14)

Codex's list of 2026-10-13 (`.cache/batch/amos/reports/themes-codex.md`) was shown to the author numbered 1 to 17. **One page is chosen** (author, 2026-10-14: “Definitely want to do 9 and merge #2 into it”; “Yeah, let's do that one”): **the prophet who pleads** (3:7; 7:1–9; 8:1–3; 9:1, after Sears), **with “Not Utterly” merged into it** (3:12; 5:2, 15; 9:8–11, with Codex's thread from “none to raise her up” at 5:2 to “I will raise up” at 9:11), ending with Christ the Advocate (Heb. 7:25; D&C 45:3–5) and the heir of David's raised house (Luke 1:32–33).

**The others are not chosen because they were not compelling enough, not because Amos is short** (author, 2026-10-14: “the reason for not doing more is because they aren't that compelling, *not* because Amos is small”). The author found Codex's other recommendations (the famine of hearing; build and inhabit; seek not Beth-el; called by my name) not insightful: they gathered what the chapter notes already say.

## Noticed while setting up (2026-10-13; not yet tested against the chapters)

Leads from the brief's research, for the coordinator to test once the chapters are written.

1. **“Yet have ye not returned unto me.”** The Lord's warnings before judgment: five calamities sent to turn Israel (4:6–11), the principle that He reveals His secret first (3:7), the prophets told “Prophesy not” (2:12; 7:13, 16), and at last the withdrawal of the word itself (8:11–12).
2. **The poor in the gate.** The charges that recur (2:6–8; 4:1; 5:11–12; 8:4–6) and the law behind each, with the punishments aimed at houses, beds and vineyards (3:15; 5:11; 6:4, 11) and reversed in 9:14. Matson's study is the frame.
3. **The lion and the shepherd.** A herdsman's images: the roar (1:2; 3:4, 8), the shepherd's two legs and a piece of an ear (3:12), the lion and the bear (5:19), the flock he was taken from (7:15).
4. **The remnant of Joseph.** 3:12; 5:15; 6:9–10; 9:1–4, 8–10, and 9:11–15. Spencer's study is the frame.

**Belongs to another book:** the sacrifices and offerings beside today's ordinances (`IDEAS.md`; the author's idea of 2026-10-13, to be written after the five books of Moses). Amos 4:4–5 and 5:21–25 would be linked from it.
