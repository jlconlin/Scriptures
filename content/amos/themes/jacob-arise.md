Amos is shown the judgment first, and twice he pleads it away. Then he pleads no more, and the Lord speaks alone. No man answers his question, “by whom shall Jacob arise?” At the book’s end the Lord speaks the question’s verb Himself: “In that day will I raise up the tabernacle of David that is fallen” ([[Amos 9:11]]); and [[Amos 9:8]] says “I will not utterly destroy the house of Jacob.”

## Shown first, and heard

“Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets” ([[Amos 3:7]]). Shown the locusts, Amos says, “O Lord GOD, forgive, I beseech thee: by whom shall Jacob arise? for he is small. The LORD repented for this: It shall not be” ([[Amos 7:2–3]]). Shown the fire, he says “cease” and again hears “This also shall not be” ([[Amos 7:5–6]]). “Forgive” and “cease” are both commands, “even in Hebrew.”[@sears-intercession]

Abraham pleaded, and the Lord answered, “I will not destroy it for ten’s sake” ([[Gen. 18:32]]); Moses pleaded, and “the LORD repented of the evil” ([[Ex. 32:14]]). Both are instances of “the intercession of good men.”[@bd-prayer] The verb “repented” is the same in Amos 7:3, 6 and Exodus 32:12, 14.[@oshb-wlc] For that scene see [Moses the Mediator](/exodus/themes/moses-the-mediator/).

## “I will not again pass by them any more”

In the next two visions Amos says only “A plumbline” and “A basket of summer fruit.” The Lord says, “I will not again pass by them any more” ([[Amos 7:8]]; [[Amos 8:2]]). “These answers are Amos’s final words in these two visions; God does all the talking from this point forward.”[@sears-intercession] “Pass by” here most likely means “forgive” or “pardon,” and the Lord is “denying any new attempts to intercede.”[@sears-intercession] That reading rests on the arrangement of the visions; no verse states it. See [Amos 7](/amos/7/).

## What is left looks like loss

The shepherd of [[Amos 3:12]] saves “two legs, or a piece of an ear”; in one reading these are “proof that total loss was unavoidable.”[@spencer-remnant] The song says: “The virgin of Israel is fallen; she shall no more rise: she is forsaken upon her land; there is none to raise her up” ([[Amos 5:2]]). Then: “it may be that the LORD God of hosts will be gracious unto the remnant of Joseph” ([[Amos 5:15]]). Survival without God’s gracious attention “would be mere survival—not life.”[@spencer-remnant]

The plea uses the song’s verb. The song says no one will raise her; the plea asks who can make Jacob stand, and gives no name.[@oshb-wlc] Read so, the plea is for the people the song mourned as fallen.

## “Not utterly,” and “I will raise up”

The Church’s edition prints for Amos 7:3: “Jacob shall repent for this, therefore I will not utterly destroy him.”[@lds-scriptures] Set beside it is [[Amos 9:8]]: “I will destroy it from off the face of the earth; saving that I will not utterly destroy the house of Jacob.” In that edition the answer to the plea and the sentence of 9:8 are the same words, both said of Jacob.

The question’s verb returns. “Raise up” and “arise” in [[Amos 5:2]], [[Amos 7:2]], [[Amos 7:5]] and [[Amos 9:11]] are one Hebrew verb, and “fallen” in 5:2 and 9:11 is one verb.[@oshb-wlc] The verb stands in ten places in eight verses; elsewhere the Lord raises up prophets (2:11) and a nation against Israel (6:14), and rises against the house of Jeroboam (7:9); and of those who swear by Samaria’s shrines, “they shall fall, and never rise up again” (8:14).[@oshb-wlc]

Set beside [[Amos 9:11]], the question of 7:2 has its verb with the Lord as subject: by whom shall Jacob arise? “I will raise up.” The kingdom would be “raised up again from its fallen condition,” and only “through an offshoot from David’s family,” which “can be no other than the Messiah.”[@keil-amos] The chapter notes on [[Amos 9:9]] and [[Amos 9:11]] give the detail.

## The Advocate, and the heir of the house

Latter-day Saints may read these scenes “through a doctrinal lens that understands prophetic intercession as typological for the role of Jesus.”[@sears-intercession] Amos’s intercession ends with “not again… any more.” Of Christ scripture says “he ever liveth to make intercession for them” ([[Heb. 7:25]]) and “have I pleaded before the Father for them” ([[D&C 38:4]]). His advocacy “is not adversarial”: He “would not champion anything other than what the Father has wanted all along.”[@renlund-choose-you-this-day] Set beside the visions’ “not again,” the Advocate’s plea has no such word.

Gabriel says of Mary’s son, “he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end” ([[Luke 1:33]]). Set beside it, the house that [[Amos 9:8]] will not utterly destroy has the same name, “the house of Jacob.” The Lord who says “I will raise up” is Jehovah, the premortal Christ.[@gs-jehovah]
