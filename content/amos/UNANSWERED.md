# Amos: reader questions no source answered

Questions a reader would ask that no source read answered, by chapter: a list for a later deepening pass. Kept out of `BRIEF.md` so the brief stays brief.

- **1:** Where the plain of Aven, the house of Eden, Teman and Bozrah are (1:5, 12): the verses' place names are left as they stand; Which wars or raids lie behind the charges against Gaza, Edom and Ammon (1:6, 11, 13): the verses name none; only the proposal of the Philistine raid under Jehoram is given for Gaza; Whether there is physical evidence of the earthquake (1:1): no source was found.
- **2:** Who 'the Amorite' is in 2:9-10 and why his height is stressed: the Bible Dictionary entry says only that Amorites lived about Abraham's time in Palestine and Syria; only the older outside commentary joins the height to the spies' report; What 'turn aside the way of the meek' (2:7) means beyond one translation's rendering; Who the 'maid' of 2:7 was (household servant, debt-slave, or shrine woman): no source read settles it; the note says so; Which war or occasion lies behind the burning of the king of Edom's bones (2:1): only a tradition reported at second hand.
- **3:** 3:14: what cutting off the altar's horns signifies (the loss of atonement or of refuge). Neither Keil nor Matson says anything on it, and the Student Manual's 'power' reading has no support beyond the manual. The note says what the horns were and that Josiah broke the altar down; 3:15: what a 'summer house' was, beyond the words; 3:5: what a 'gin' is, beyond the Church's footnote (given only in the plain words); 3:13: who is told to 'hear and testify'. Only Keil answers (the foreign witnesses of verse 9); it is given as one reading.
- **4:** Which famine, drought, war or overthrow verses 6-11 refer to: the note says the verses do not say. Keil's dating of verses 10-11 to the Syrian wars was left out by the writer as one commentator's identification; Whether 4:4 parodies a priest's call to worship (the brief's lead): no source read says so, and the note does not claim it; Where or what "Harmon" / "the palace" in 4:3 is: the lexicon says the allusion is lost.
- **5:** 5:10 – who is 'him that rebuketh in the gate' (a judge, an elder, or a prophet)? Only Keil speaks to it; 5:13 – why do 'the prudent keep silence' in an evil time? Only Keil speaks to it; 5:5 – why does Beersheba, alone of the three shrines, receive no threat?; 5:27 and Acts 7:43 – why does Stephen say 'Babylon' where Amos has 'Damascus'? No source read gives a reason.
- **6:** 6:3: what 'put far away the evil day, and cause the seat of violence to come near' means beyond the plain-words paraphrase; no source read said more than the verse; 6:1: why Amos names Zion in a sermon to Samaria. No Church source read says the woe is aimed at Judah as well; Keil does ('the capitals of the two kingdoms'), but citing him would put Keil in a fourth note; 6:7: whether the 'banquet' (marzeach) was a known institution of feasting; the lexicon gives only 'cry of revelry' and Jer. 16:5.
- **7:** Why Amos says 'Isaac' for the nation at 7:9 and 7:16: the note says only that the name is used twice, paired with Israel; What became of Amaziah, and of Amos, after this scene, and whether the king answered; What 'the king's mowings' were: no royal right to a first cutting is known, and the only reading found is one older commentator's.
- **8:** 8:8: is the trembling of the land the earthquake of 1:1? No source read joins them; 8:9: is an eclipse meant? Only Keil discusses it, and he rejects it; 8:12: what do 'from sea to sea, and from the north even to the east' mark out?; 8:13: why are the young women and young men singled out?; 8:3: which building is the 'temple' or 'palace' (Beth-el's sanctuary or the palace at Samaria)?.
- **9:** 9:1: Who is told to "smite the lintel"? The verse does not say, and no source used answers it (Keil guesses an angel; not used); 9:3: What is the serpent at the bottom of the sea? Only a translation's cross-reference to Isaiah 27:1 was found; 9:7: Where was Kir? No source read places it.
