# Ezra: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.** The candidates were Fable's and Codex's lists (`.cache/batch/ezra/reports/themes-fable.md` and `themes-codex.md`), shown to the author combined on 2026-11-12; only the author's choice is recorded here.

## Author's selection (2026-11-12)

Two pages. The author at first found none of the four compelling as listed (“I didn't see what was compelling or new about any of the themes. Perhaps it just wasn't explained well”); the first was chosen when it was told as a story (“Yes that sounds compelling”), and the second when the author supplied its thesis.

### 1. “Till There Stood Up a Priest” (from [[Ezra 2:63]])

**The arc.** Chapter 2 makes the register the test of who may serve: priests who “sought their register… but they were not found” are put from the priesthood “till there stood up a priest with Urim and with Thummim” (2:62–63), and the book never says that priest came. It then gives Ezra the best register in it, sixteen names back to “Aaron the chief priest” (7:1–5). In the crisis that priest does not stand: he kneels, counts himself among the guilty, asks for nothing, and ends “we cannot stand before thee because of this” (9:5–6, 15). The last list in the book answers the first: “among the sons of the priests there were found” the guilty, the high priest's house first (10:18–19).

**Thesis.** The book tests its priests by their register and waits for a priest who can stand; by its end the best-registered priest in Israel says no one can, and the priest it was waiting for is the one Hebrews describes, whose “descent is not counted from them” and who “ever liveth to make intercession.”

**To Christ:** [[Heb. 7:3]] (with the Joseph Smith Translation), [[Heb. 7:11–16]], [[Heb. 7:25]]; [[Zech. 3:1–9]] (the same Jeshua “standing”); [[Mal. 3:1–3]]; [[D&C 45:3–5]]; [[D&C 85:11–12]] for the Restoration's use of 2:61–62. **Applies:** standing before God does not come by descent or record; the best of us has nothing to plead except the One who pleads.

**Must not claim:** that 2:63 prophesies Christ (the book leaves a question open and scripture answers it elsewhere; say once that joining 2:63 to 9:15 and to Hebrews 7 is the page's reading); anything that rules on the marriages of chapters 9–10 (the brief); that Ezra failed as a priest (he is the nearest the book comes to a mediator).

### 2. “A Little Reviving in Our Bondage” (from [[Ezra 9:8]])

**The author's thesis** (2026-11-12): “Just checking the boxes so that I can return to be in God's presence *is not sufficient*. I always say, ‘If I'm just going back home to live in His basement, I'm missing the point.’ Instead, the purpose of life and the power of the Atonement is to change me and my heart so that I *become* like my Heavenly Father, i.e., be redeemed.” And of the caution that Ezra does not itself speak of becoming like God: “there is powerful symbolism in returning and becoming.”

**The arc.** The captivity ends in chapter 1 as Jeremiah promised, and by chapter 6 the house is finished and everything is done “as it is written.” Yet the people are still called “the children of the captivity” after they are home, and Ezra, praying at the rebuilt house, says “we were bondmen” and calls the return “a little reviving in our bondage,” tracing it to “our iniquities” (9:7–9). The prophets of the exile had promised two things together, a return and a new heart ([[Ezek. 36:24–27]]; [[Jer. 31:33]]; [[Deut. 30:3–6]]); Ezra records the first arriving without the second. The one man of whom a prepared heart is said is Ezra himself (7:10).

**Thesis.** Ezra shows a people brought home and not yet changed: the return was never the goal, and what the journey could not do is what Christ does, making the servant in the house a son of the house.

**To Christ:** [[John 8:33–36]] (the servant “abideth not in the house for ever: but the Son abideth ever,” beside the nail “for a little space”); [[Mosiah 27:24–26]]; [[Mosiah 5:2, 7]]; [[Alma 5:14]]; [[Dan. 9:24–25]]; [[Luke 4:18–21]]; President Dallin H. Oaks, “The Challenge to Become” (October 2000). **Applies:** returning to God's presence is not the end the Atonement has in view; it changes what a disciple is.

**Must not claim:** that the book of Ezra teaches becoming like God (the return is read as a symbol, and the page says once that the reading is its own; scripture and the prophets supply the doctrine); that the return failed or Jeremiah's word was not kept (1:1); that the returned exiles' worship was mere box-checking (the book honors it); anything from “holy seed” (9:2) about descent. The author's “basement” is his own image and is not quoted on the page unless he asks.

**Keep the two apart.** Both come to Ezra's prayer. Page 1 is about the priest who can stand before God for the people; page 2 is about what God does to the people who come home.
