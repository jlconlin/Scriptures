# 1 Kings: theme candidates

Topics that run across the book and might become theme pages (`STANDARDS.md` §8). **None is written until the author chooses it.** The candidates were Fable's and Codex's lists (`.cache/batch/1-kings/reports/themes-fable.md` and `themes-codex.md`), shown to the author combined on 2026-11-05; only the author's choice is recorded here.

## Author's selection (2026-11-05)

**One page: “A Hearing Heart”** (from [[1 Kgs. 3:9]], *lēb šōmēaʿ*). The author, of the recommendation: “I agree.”

**The arc.** Solomon asks for a *hearing* heart (3:9) and prays that the LORD hear and forgive (8:30) and “incline our hearts unto him” (8:58). His wives incline his heart away, with the same verb (11:2–4, 9). Jeroboam builds the calves for fear the people's heart will turn back (12:27). Meanwhile the LORD keeps hearing, farther from the house and for less likely people, until no mediator is left: a prophet's prayer for Jeroboam at Bethel (13:6), Elijah's for the widow of Zidon (17:22), Carmel's “that thou hast turned their heart back again” (18:37), and Ahab in sackcloth (21:29). To Christ: Luke 4:25–26; Alma 33:4–16 (“because of thy Son”); Mosiah 5:2 and Hel. 15:7 (the change of heart); D&C 110:4–7, 13–16.

**Must not claim:** that Ahab was forgiven (21:29 defers the evil; 22 shows him unchanged); that the temple was unnecessary (9:3, Oaks); that plural marriage as such turned Solomon's heart (the 11:3 note); that 18:37 and Mal. 4:6 share a Hebrew verb (*sābab* and *šûb*); that the later hearings are said by the text to answer chapter 8 (that connection is the page's own). Codex judged the hearing half weak on that ground; the page argues it as its own reading.

**Not chosen:** “Thy Son Liveth” (3:27; 17:23; Luke 7:15), “Return Every Man to His House in Peace” (8:66; 12:24; 22:17; John 18:8), “Not in Thy Days” (11:12; 21:29; JST 11:33), and the rest of both lists.
