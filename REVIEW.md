# The review of the theme pages and the guides

Begun 2026-10-25. This file holds the plan and the decisions; **`REVIEW-TASKS.md` holds the list of pages and where each stands**, one line a page. What has been done is in git (`git log -- REVIEW.md REVIEW-TASKS.md`, and each page's own history). Both files are deleted when the review is finished. Keep this file short: a decision is a paragraph, and the reasons behind a page's bin are in the commit that binned it.

## Why

The author, 2026-10-25: the theme pages were “rather bland and boring.” “The themes are opportunities to identify new connections across multiple chapters that a scholar has not made before… These pages can be longer essays instead of simple ‘devotionals’… They are intended to be semi-academic.” And: “It's okay if no insights are found; we just won't make a theme.”

Two causes, found over two days and three drafts of the same two essays. The form rules that had accumulated for theme pages (a table of steps, a few sentences per step, “set beside,” a hedge after every reading, an outline relayed to a writer who could add nothing) produced prose the author called “*very* AI-generated.” Under those, a second: when every sentence must have a ledger row, writers write one sentence per row in the source's words, and the prose takes the shape of the evidence file. That second cause reaches the guides and the chapter notes too (decision 4).

## What has changed

- `STANDARDS.md` §8, “How a theme page is written”: six instructions and no rules about form; the Fable session writes the essay itself; the reviewer judges thesis, sourcing, doctrine, application and padding, not form. `DECISIONS.md` records it. The models: Deuteronomy's *Let It Suffice Thee* and Leviticus's *Accepted for Him*, in preview on the branch `claude/theme-pages-review-7ba220` (author: “Yes! this is so much easier to read”).
- `STANDARDS.md` §5: the ledger documents the page, the page is not written to the ledger; paraphrase the default, one row may stand behind a paragraph. The `chapter-writer`'s instructions carry it.
- `STANDARDS.md` §8, “How a guide is written”: a new guide gets the essay's deal minus readings of its own; one mind writes it.
- `check-content` no longer warns a theme page for naming a source in running text.

## The decisions (all made 2026-10-26, with the author, one page or one question at a time)

1. **The triage.** Every page was binned: A, an argument worth an essay; B, a single insight worth a shorter page at the new pace; C, sound but carried by the manuals; D, no connection at all. Bin D was gone over one page at a time (three deleted, one moved to B). Bin A was approved whole. Bin B's 25 were read by a Sonnet agent for the single insight each makes; 19 were approved as a block and six doubtful ones taken one at a time (three to B, two to C, one deleted). Two merges: *Moses the Mediator* + *Hear Ye Him* into one page in B, *What Was Offered at Sinai* kept apart; the three Lamentations pages into one essay in A. **The test the author set** (of *That Ye May Know*: “even though there are lots of others who have made this connection, I want to include it on this site… even if we don't add anything else, I still want to keep it”): a page is kept when its connection is worth having on the site, not only when no scholar has made it; “no insight” means no connection at all, as in the Abraham pages. §8 already says so.
2. **Bin C** is rewritten at the new pace, all of it, so the site ends with one form, “but it may not be the step right after A and B.” In the old form until its turn; a page the author reads and finds flat moves to B at once.
3. **Guides.** New guides under the guide's deal (above). The narrative guides join C's queue; the period guides and the Isaiah reference guide stay. The §5 sentence reaches chapters, guides and essays alike.
4. **Chapter notes.** Spot-checked in eight chapters (Genesis 12, Exodus 32, Numbers 21, Psalm 22, Isaiah 53, Jeremiah 31, Hosea 11, Zechariah 3): the one-sentence-per-row voice is in all eight, worst in notes with many sources and in “one reading… another reading” stacks; “christ” notes clean; Isaiah 53 the best (19 sentences on 12 rows); later books no better. No chapter is rewritten in this review. **Next: a count before a plan.** The author runs a Codex task (`REVIEW-codex-scoring.md`) that scores every note and tables the worst; then the flagged notes are rewritten a volume at a time, in the language pass's shape (Codex reads, Opus applies), under the author's standing rule of a volume when the author returns to it. Before the next book, one batch of chapters is written under the new writer's instruction and read against the spot check's worst examples.
5. **Order and pace** (author: “Agreed”). Essays first, Numbers' *Between the Dead and the Living* first of all so the author can read it against the models; then the shorter pages; grouped by book where a book has several; two or three a session; each read by the author in preview as it is finished, and a flat one goes back before the next starts. Fable writes; **Sonnet researches** (author, asked whether a more capable model was needed: “approved” the arrangement; the session frames the questions and always includes the adversarial ones, *who has already made this connection* and *what in the text works against it*; a dossier thin at the crux is finished by the session; Opus by exception only, measured first). Codex's tokens go to the scoring and to reading, not to applying edits or writing essays. **Amended 2026-10-29 for bin B's shorter pages** (author, after asking that most of the work go to Opus and Sonnet: “Yes, Opus writes”): Sonnet researches; the Fable session reads the dossier's analytic sections and writes a one-page outline (the thesis, the honest findings the page must state, the sections, the ledger rules); an Opus `theme-writer` writes the page and its ledger from the outline and the dossier; an Opus `theme-reviewer` reviews; the writer applies the review and Codex's clarity items; the Fable session reads the draft once, decides what Codex's items to apply, and commits. First used on the Numbers pair of 2026-10-28 (f5416690, 5f4f850d). Essays (bin A, finished) were written by Fable.

## How a page is done

1. Start the dossier (`node scripts/theme-dossier.mjs …`) and run one `theme-researcher` (Sonnet) with the old page's ledger as verified evidence to carry in and the questions the essay needs answered beyond it, the adversarial ones among them. For a merged page, the dossier starts from every old ledger.
2. The Fable session reads the dossier whole, decides whether there is an insight, and writes the essay and its ledger under the six instructions (`STANDARDS.md` §8). The old page and ledger are removed from the working tree first (git keeps them) so nothing of the old form is inherited.
3. One `theme-reviewer` (Opus). The session makes the repairs and applies Codex's reading without compressing.
4. `check-page.mjs`, `merge-sources.mjs`, commit with `status: preview`; the chapter notes that point to the page are reworded to say what it argues; `REVIEW-TASKS.md` updated in the same commit; the author reads it in the preview. Then the dashboard is refreshed: `node scripts/review-dashboard.mjs` exports `review-tasks.json` and `review-commits.json`, the session uploads them as the artifact's blobs and points its `datasets/tasks` and `datasets/commits` documents at them (the dashboard is the author's claude.ai Artifact “Theme page review”; its progress counts merged and deleted rows as resolved, not as pages left to write).
5. A page judged to have no insight is deleted with its ledger, and the notes that pointed to it lose the pointer; a candidate for an essay that the research bears (*Seek Not after Your Own Eyes*) is written as one, and the author told which it became.
